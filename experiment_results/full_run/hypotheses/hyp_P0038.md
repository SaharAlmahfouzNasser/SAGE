# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that although high tumor proliferation (MKI67) and transcriptional HER2 programs explain aggressiveness and sensitivity to HER2 blockade, some tumors with robust immune infiltration still fail to achieve pathologic response—why does local immune presence (TILs) not reliably translate to effective therapy-induced tumor elimination? 2) Tier 1 paths used: Core Path 1 (Ki67/proliferation linked to pertuzumab response and pCR) and Core Path 2 (GE profiles / ERBB2-driven transcriptional programs). I built on these because they give a direct mechanistic axis (proliferation and HER2 signaling) that can modulate how immune infiltration impacts response to HER2-targeted therapy. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: circulating biomarkers (CA15-3, ctDNA, LINE-1 methylation) and systemic inflammatory markers (CRP, PLR) provide orthogonal, longitudinal measures of tumor burden, epigenomic state, and host inflammation that can help test whether proliferative/HER2-driven tumors truly outpace or suppress local immune function. 4) Why therapy_response was chosen as the endpoint: the core paths directly connect proliferation and HER2 signaling to pCR on pertuzumab-containing regimens—therapy_response (pCR) is the most mechanistically linked, actionable, and testable endpoint in available TCGA/clinical-neoadjuvant contexts. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded exploratory Tier 3 items (TB markers, dietary RPI links, broad frailty/age routine biomarker frameworks) from the core hypothesis to keep the causal question focused on tumor-intrinsic proliferation, HER2 signaling, and local immune infiltration; systemic inflammatory measures and liquid-biopsy markers were relegated to secondary modifiers rather than anchors to preserve hypothesis parsimony?

## Working Hypothesis

In HER2-positive breast cancer treated with pertuzumab-containing neoadjuvant therapy, high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density does not guarantee a pCR when tumors exhibit high MKI67 proliferation and strong ERBB2-driven signaling. The mechanistic claim is that simultaneous high proliferation (MKI67) and amplified ERBB2 signaling create an immune-averse microenvironment or accelerate tumor outgrowth in a way that impairs local immune cytotoxicity, leading to reduced pCR compared with tumors that are TIL-high but MKI67-low and/or ERBB2-low. The predicted clinical consequence is that among TIL-high tumors, those with MKI67-high and ERBB2-high will have lower pCR rates to pertuzumab-containing regimens than TIL-high, MKI67-low tumors, identifying a biologically distinct subset that may benefit from combined strategies (e.g., concurrent HER2 blockade with anti-proliferative or immune-modulatory approaches). Anchor genes: MKI67 and ERBB2. Secondary modifiers (used to refine and test the model): NLR, PLR, CA15-3, ctDNA, LINE-1 methylation, CRP, lipids, and an autoantibody panel to capture systemic/inflammatory and tumor-agnostic signals. The hypothesis further predicts that incorporating these modifiers will improve prediction of pCR beyond TIL density and anchor gene status alone, and that immune profiling will reveal an exhausted or suppressed TIL phenotype in MKI67-high/ERBB2-high tumors despite high TIL counts. Experimental testing will focus on (1) stratified analyses by MKI67 and ERBB2 status within TIL-high tumors, (2) interaction models testing whether MKI67/ERBB2 modify the effect of TIL on pCR, and (3) prospective validation with integrated immune phenotyping and circulating biomarkers.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
