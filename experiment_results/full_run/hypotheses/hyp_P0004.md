# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do only some muscle-invasive bladder tumors respond to intravesical BCG despite similar clinical staging? The provided single path proposes that pre-existing TLS explain this heterogeneity because TLS can serve as local antigen-experienced immune niches that BCG can amplify. 2) Data used: Single-path evidence linking pre-treatment tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) on H&E to BCG treatment outcome; no molecular (gene) anchors were provided, so synthesis focuses on morphology and spatial organization. 3) Supporting evidence (operationalized as secondary modifiers): TIL density, TLS maturity (germinal centers), TLS proximity to tumor nests/invasive front, and tumor stage/grade are plausible modulators of the TLS→BCG effect and will be included in downstream adjustment/stratification. 4) Endpoint choice: therapy_response (objective response/recurrence-free interval after BCG) was selected because the clinical question is about predicting benefit from BCG specifically. 5) Exclusions and rationale: No genes were included because the ontologist provided no molecular anchors; broader immune-phenotype genes or exhaustion markers were deliberately excluded to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on an H&E-assessable, mechanistic imaging question. I therefore propose a focused, mechanistic hypothesis linking pre-existing TLS morphology/spatial context to BCG responsiveness?

## Working Hypothesis

Pre-treatment muscle-invasive bladder tumors that contain high-density, mature TLS-like lymphoid aggregates located at or near the tumor invasive front will demonstrate superior objective response rates and longer recurrence-free survival following intravesical BCG, independent of baseline tumor stage/grade and overall TIL density. The effect size is driven by (i) TLS density, (ii) TLS maturity characterized by germinal-center-like features, and (iii) spatial proximity of TLS to tumor nests/invasive front, which collectively create local antigen-presenting hubs that amplify BCG-induced adaptive immunity. A composite TLS score combining density, maturity, and proximity will stratify patients into responder vs non-responder phenotypes and predict RFS, enabling prospective validation in independent cohorts. Mechanistically, pre-existing mature TLS provide on-site B and T cell priming and clonal expansion in response to BCG-derived danger signals, thereby converting intravesical immunotherapy into a localized, efficacious antitumor immune response. The hypothesis remains anchored to H&E-detectable features to maintain assay simplicity and translational readiness, with targeted IHC in a validation subset to corroborate TLS identity and maturation without changing the primary imaging-based framework.
