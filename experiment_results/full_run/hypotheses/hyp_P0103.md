# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist identified that PARP-inhibitor (Olaparib)/BRCA-related tumors sometimes show imaging- and pathomics-linked survival benefits, yet the local immune contexture and inflammatory/metabolic signatures vary—i.e., why do TLS- or TIL-rich tumors not always translate to improved survival after DNA-damage–driven therapy? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on Core Path 1 (therapy/OS ↔ imaging/pathomics → in-situ immune infiltrate) because it links survival and imaging-derived immune readouts; Core Path 2 (BRCA → PD-L1 → T-cell exhaustion → PTGER4) because it provides a mechanistic immune-suppressive axis (PGE2/PTGER4 driving PD-L1–associated exhaustion); and Core Path 3 (Olaparib → predictive immune markers → long-term outcomes) because it frames the clinical consequence of immune modulation. These three together support a focused question about when local immune structures (TLS) fail to produce survival benefit due to active immunosuppression. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: metabolic/inflammatory modifiers (EDIP/EDIH, insulin-response and inflammation biomarkers such as IL6/CRP) can amplify PGE2-driven immunosuppression or blunt T-cell function; prognostic genomic classifiers (Decipher GC, CENPA) can define intrinsically aggressive tumors where immune architecture is insufficient. These lend biological plausibility for effect modification. 4) Why OS as the endpoint: OS is the most robust, clinically meaningful endpoint present in the core paths and captures the long-term consequence of immune evasion despite apparent immune infiltration; it aligns with the Ontologist’s path linking Olaparib → OS → nomograms → pathomics. 5) Deliberate exclusions and why: I excluded making BRCA1/2 a core anchor to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the immune-suppressive mechanism that modulates TLS benefit (BRCA status is retained as a secondary modifier). I also excluded other imaging features (TIL density, TSR) because TLS-like aggregates more directly represent organized antigen-presentation niches relevant to PD-L1/T-cell exhaustion dynamics. I excluded exploratory Tier 3 nodes (SPC, urinary biomarkers) to avoid diluting the mechanistic claim?

## Working Hypothesis

In BRCA-associated breast cancers treated in the context of PARP-inhibitor biology, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates confer an OS advantage only when the tumor immune microenvironment exhibits low PGE2-PTGER4 signaling and low PD-L1–associated T-cell exhaustion. Specifically, TLS-rich tumors with high PTGER4 expression and high CD274 (PD-L1) expression will show attenuated or absent OS benefit compared with TLS-rich, PTGER4/CD274-low tumors, indicating that PGE2-driven immunosuppression impairs TLS function. This effect modification by the PGE2-PTGER4–PD-L1 axis should persist after adjusting for BRCA1/2 status and other clinical covariates. Mechanistically, PGE2 signaling via PTGER4 promotes PD-L1–mediated T-cell exhaustion and dampens TLS-mediated antigen presentation, undermining local anti-tumor immunity. Preclinical models will test whether pharmacologic inhibition of the PGE2/PTGER4 axis or PD-1/PD-L1 blockade can restore TLS-associated immune activity and improve survival in TLS-high tumors, supporting a combinatorial strategy in patients where TLS are present but suppressed by the PGE2 axis.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
