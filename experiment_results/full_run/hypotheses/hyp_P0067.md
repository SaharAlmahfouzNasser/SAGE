# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Pretreatment cytolytic activity (CA)/high cytotoxic effector expression should predict higher rates of pCR to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in TNBC, but many CA-high/TIL-rich tumors nonetheless fail to achieve pCR — why is cytolytic transcriptional activation sometimes functionally ineffective?  
2) Tier 1 paths used and why: I built on two complementary mechanistic axes: (a) cytotoxic effector execution (GZMB as a representative granzyme driving target-cell killing) and (b) IFN-γ signaling (IFNG) as a double-edged axis that both fosters immune recruitment/activation and induces adaptive resistance programs in tumor cells (e.g., checkpoint induction, antigen-presentation modulation). These two paths directly address the puzzle of ‘‘active-looking’’ immune transcriptional programs not yielding tumor eradication.  
3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: CD8A, PRF1, GNLY, NKG7 and CTSW reinforce the cytotoxic module; PDCD1, CD274 (PD-L1), LAG3, TIGIT, STAT1, and CXCL9/10/11 describe IFN-driven recruitment and adaptive-exhaustion/resistance mechanisms; tumor-intrinsic modulators (HLA class I expression, antigen-presentation genes) mediate effectiveness of cytotoxicity. These support testing whether IFNG-associated adaptive programs blunt the cytolytic effect.  
4) Why this endpoint: therapy_response (neoadjuvant chemotherapy pCR) is the most relevant clinical consequence for pretreatment CA in TNBC (well-supported in the literature, actionable for treatment decisions, and measurable in available NAC cohorts).  
5) What I excluded and why: I deliberately excluded multi-gene poly-models and extra imaging features (TLS-like aggregates, TSR) to keep the mechanistic question sharp (one imaging axis + two molecular anchors). I also excluded metabolic/oxidative-stress modulators (e.g., NAMPT, SLC16A3) and broad proliferation markers from the core hypothesis to avoid diluting the causal claim linking cytolytic execution vs. IFN-driven adaptive resistance; those can be explored later as secondary modifiers if needed?

## Working Hypothesis

In pretreatment TNBC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy, high Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte density with elevated GZMB denotes active cytotoxic engagement. However, concurrent high IFNG signaling induces adaptive resistance programs in tumor cells—including PD-L1 upregulation and alterations in antigen-presentation pathways—that blunt cytotoxic efficacy. As a result, tumors with high TIL density, high GZMB, and high IFNG activity will have lower therapy_response (pCR) rates to NAC than similarly profiled tumors with lower IFNG-driven adaptive resistance. This yields a testable interaction: IFNG status moderates the positive relationship between cytolytic activity (TIL density + GZMB) and therapy_response.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).

**Prediction 2:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
