## Clinical Question

Tumor budding (TBS) at the invasive front is reproducibly visible on H&E and associates with worse outcomes in MIBC, but it is unclear why TBS provides prognostic information beyond conventional stage/grade — specifically, what biological processes make budding prognostically adverse. 2) Tier 1 path used: the single provided path (H&E → Tumor budding score → Prognostic outcomes). ## Working Hypothesis

High tumor budding score (TBS) at the invasive front in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) reflects a localized epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)-like state in single cells or small cell clusters that detach from the primary tumor and seed occult micro-metastases. This biological state promotes early dissemination, leading to inferior overall survival (OS) independent of traditional clinicopathologic factors. In a rigorously characterized MIBC cohort with standardized TBS assessment, high TBS will independently predict worse OS after adjustment for pathologic stage, tumor grade, lymphovascular invasion, nodal status, and tumor size. The prognostic impact of high TBS will be mediated by measurable occult dissemination at diagnosis (e.g., detectable circulating tumor cells and/or circulating tumor DNA) and by histologic/surrogate EMT features localized to budding cells (e.g., loss of cohesive cell–cell contacts and acquisition of mesenchymal-like morphology in buds). Furthermore, TBS dynamics during neoadjuvant therapy or early postoperative follow-up (persistent or increasing budding) will correlate with therapeutic resistance and poorer outcomes. Integrating TBS with the tumor immune microenvironment (e.g., immune cell infiltration and stromal context) will enhance risk stratification and identify subgroups that may benefit from intensified systemic therapy, adjuvant surveillance, or therapeutic strategies targeting EMT-like biology in budding cells, without requiring predefined molecular anchor genes in the core hypothesis.”,
