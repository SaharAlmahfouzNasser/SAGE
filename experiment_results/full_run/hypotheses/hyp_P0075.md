# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Organized intratumoral lymphoid structures (TLS-like aggregates) usually indicate anti-tumor immunity, yet in some ER-positive breast cancers they do not translate into improved therapy response to endocrine treatment. Why does local immune organization fail to deliver clinical benefit in this context? 2) Tier 1 paths built on: I prioritized (a) imaging axis = TLS-like lymphoid aggregates because it encodes organized local immunity visible on H&E, and (b) two molecular anchors available in the dataset that mechanistically modulate immune efficacy and therapy sensitivity: PDCD1 (a T-cell exhaustion marker) and ESR1 (estrogen receptor signaling / endocrine therapy axis). These directly address the puzzle of TLS presence without benefit in ER+ tumors. 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: other T-cell exhaustion markers (CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT) and stromal/matrix programs (NABA_CORE_MATRISOME genes such as COL1A1, FN1) plausibly modulate immune function or physical access of immune cells to tumor cells and therefore go into secondary_modifiers. 4) Why I chose the endpoint (therapy_response): Endocrine therapy response is the most problem-relevant clinical question for ESR1-driven tumors (actionable and interpretable), and it aligns mechanistically with ESR1 as a core anchor. 5) What I deliberately EXCLUDED and why: I excluded broader antigen-presentation genes and metabolic regulators (e.g., HLA family, NAMPT) and proliferation markers to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the interaction between organized local immunity (TLS), T-cell functional state (PDCD1), and ER-driven therapy sensitivity (ESR1). These can be explored later as secondary hypotheses?

## Working Hypothesis

In ER-positive (ESR1-high) breast tumors, TLS-like aggregates indicate organized local immunity but do not guarantee endocrine therapy benefit when intratumoral T cells are exhausted. Specifically, TLS-positive tumors with high PDCD1 expression on T cells will exhibit poorer endocrine therapy response due to an exhausted T cell state that cannot mount effective anti-tumor activity; this effect is amplified by CXA-like exhaustion signals (CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT) and reinforced by ECM-associated barrier genes (COL1A1, FN1) that limit immune cell access and sustain exhaustion. Consequently, TLS presence plus ESR1-dominant signaling predicts reduced therapy response when PDCD1 and other exhaustion markers are elevated, whereas TLS with low PDCD1 (less exhausted T cells) may correlate with better endocrine therapy outcomes. This framework yields testable, clinically relevant stratifications (TLS+/ESR1+/PDCD1high vs TLS+/ESR1+/PDCD1low) that can be refined by measuring CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT and COL1A1/FN1 as secondary modifiers.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
