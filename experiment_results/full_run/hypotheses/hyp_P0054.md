## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2− breast cancer, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) alone do not predict endocrine therapy response; their predictive value is contingent on the tumor’s intrinsic proliferation state and estrogen receptor (ER) pathway activity, as well as systemic inflammatory signals. We hypothesize that: (1) high TIL density combined with high proliferation (MKI67) and reduced ER pathway activity (lower ESR1 signaling) predicts endocrine resistance; (2) high TIL density with low proliferation and intact ER signaling predicts endocrine sensitivity; (3) integrating TIL density with MKI67, ESR1 pathway activity, and secondary modifiers (95GC-H signature, circulating miRNAs/isomiRs, CA125) yields a predictive model for endocrine therapy response with superior accuracy over any single biomarker; (4) spatial analysis will show that, in MKI67-high tumors, TILs are more often functionally exhausted and less proximal to tumor cells, explaining diminished therapeutic efficacy; (5) these mechanistic interactions delineate actionable strata for potential combinatorial strategies (e.g., endocrine therapy plus agents that modulate proliferation or reinvigorate T cells) to overcome resistance. Endpoints will include short-term endocrine response (Ki67 reduction after short-term therapy) and long-term outcomes (PFS/DFS).
