# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2− breast cancers with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL-high), concurrent dominance of MARCO-expressing tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and a tumor transcriptomic TP53-mutant-like program define a functionally immunosuppressive microenvironment that blunts the therapeutic benefit of endocrine therapy (ET). Specifically: (i) this triad (TIL-high + MARCO-high TAMs + TP53-mutant-like signature) will correlate with higher ET resistance rates and shorter time-to-progression compared with TIL-high tumors lacking MARCO dominance or TP53-mutant-like transcriptional features; (ii) mechanistically, MARCO+ TAMs suppress antigen presentation (lower MHC-I/MHC-II and co-stimulatory signals), secrete immunosuppressive cytokines, and interact with TP53-contextual programs to dampen interferon signaling and T cell–mediated anti-tumor activity; (iii) disrupting MARCO+ TAMs or reprogramming TP53-associated immunomodulation will restore antigen presentation and T cell function, thereby improving ET response in preclinical models and in prospective translational studies. Therefore, a minimal core readout set—imaging-based TIL density plus MARCO expression and a TP53-mutant-like transcriptional score—will identify the patient subset most likely to exhibit ET resistance and who may benefit from macrophage-targeted or TP53-contextualized combination therapies to restore endocrine sensitivity.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
