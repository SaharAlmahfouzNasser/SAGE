# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do organized intratumoral immune structures (TLS-like aggregates) not consistently translate into effective therapeutic responses to PARP inhibition in mCRPC? I built on these because they offer a mechanistic chain connecting tumor metabolism, immune organization on H&E, and functional immune suppression that would blunt targeted therapy benefit. 3) Tier 2 support: f/tPSA, urinary biomarkers (PCA3), CENPA, and established immune-response predictive markers offer orthogonal, noninvasive readouts and molecular context (tumor burden, chromosomal instability, and immune program signatures) to stratify or validate the hypothesized interactions. 4) Endpoint choice: I chose therapy_response (specifically Olaparib/PARP-inhibitor response) because the Ontologist highlighted PARP inhibition as an actionable therapy with documented effects on tumor immunogenicity; therapy response is the most clinically actionable question for integrating imaging, metabolic, and microenvironment biomarkers. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded SPC, CSF1, f/tPSA, PCA3, CENPA and other Tier 2 markers from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (max 2 anchors + 1 imaging feature); these are listed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses. ## Working Hypothesis

In metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), the presence of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates does not reliably predict favorable response to the PARP inhibitor Olaparib when the tumor exhibits a high EDIP metabolic/inflammatory signature and high osteopontin (SPP1) expression. The mechanistic chain is: EDIP-driven metabolic inflammation upregulates osteopontin (SPP1), which in turn promotes CSF1 production and CSF1R+ myeloid cell recruitment, skewing macrophage polarization toward a suppressive phenotype. This myeloid milieu impairs TLS antigen presentation and T cell–mediated anti-tumor activity, blunting the immunomodulatory benefits that TLS might confer in the context of PARP inhibition. Therefore, the predictive value of TLS for Olaparib response is conditional on the EDIP/SPP1 axis and the associated myeloid suppression. Consequently, the following predictions should hold: (1) TLS-high tumors that are also EDIP-high and SPP1-high will have poorer objective response rates and shorter PFS to Olaparib than TLS-high tumors with low EDIP and/or low SPP1; (2) TLS-high, EDIP-low, SPP1-low tumors will show relatively better responses, reflecting functional TLS activity; (3) TLS-low tumors will show limited improvement with Olaparib irrespective of EDIP/SPP1 status, highlighting the importance of TLS presence but within a permissive inflammatory/myeoid context. This framework supports testing for an interaction between TLS status and the EDIP/SPP1-CSF1 axis to predict Olaparib efficacy, enabling refined patient stratification and informing combination strategies to overcome myeloid-mediated resistance.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions
