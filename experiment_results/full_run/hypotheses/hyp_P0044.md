# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates enhance local anti-tumor immunity in HER2+ breast cancer predominantly when tumor-intrinsic programs reflect a favorable state (low PAM50 risk of recurrence). In this context, TLS-associated B and T cell collaboration can synergize with HER2-directed therapy to yield higher pathologic complete response (pCR). However, when intrinsic tumor programs captured by a high PAM50 risk score induce robust PD-L1–mediated adaptive immune resistance, or when PD-L1 expression is elevated, TLS-driven responses are functionally dampened by exhaustion of effector cells and by myeloid remodeling that suppresses TLS function. Consequently, TLS-rich tumors with high PAM50 risk and/or high PD-L1 will exhibit diminished or no additional benefit from TLS organization, resulting in pCR rates that approach those of TLS-poor tumors. Systemic host factors (e.g., anemia indices Hgb/RDW, on-treatment neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio) and CHI3L3-associated macrophage remodeling will further modulate TLS efficacy by shaping the local inflammatory milieu and antigen presentation capacity. Therefore, the predictive value of TLS for neoadjuvant response in HER2+ disease is contingent on the combined state of: (1) intrinsic tumor biology (PAM50 risk), (2) PD-L1–mediated immune suppression, and (3) macrophage/matrix remodeling cues (CHI3L3), with systemic inflammation as a secondary modifier. The testable prediction is a significant three-way interaction: TLS presence predicts higher pCR only in tumors with low PAM50 risk and low PD-L1; in tumors with high PAM50 risk and/or high PD-L1, TLS presence will not predict improved pCR.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TLSs promote localized antigen presentation and T/B cell collaboration, which correlates with better outcomes in several breast cancer contexts but is not uniformly predictive in HER2+ disease;

**Prediction 2:** PAM50-based intrinsic tumor programs influence proliferative signaling and immune susceptibility, and high PAM50 risk is associated with mechanisms that can dampen immune efficacy and upregulate PD-L1;

**Prediction 3:** PD-L1–mediated adaptive resistance and myeloid-driven remodeling (e.g., CHI3L3-associated pathways) can neutralize TLS-driven immunity, converting a potential biomarker (TLS) into a context-dependent indicator. The combined model explains why TLS-rich HER2+ tumors sometimes underperform despite organized local immunity.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Caveats & Risks
