Biomarker name: Glandular Architectural Complexity (GAC)

Definition
- GAC is the mean boundary fractal dimension (or another measure of boundary irregularity) of tumor glands within the prostate cancer region on H&E-stained slides. It captures how complex and irregular the gland outlines are across the tumor, reflecting architectural disorganization and stromal remodeling that may relate to androgen receptor (AR) signaling dependencies and response to androgen deprivation therapy (ADT).

Rationale
- ADT response in localized prostate cancer is influenced by tumor architecture and microenvironmental organization, which are visible in H&E images.
- Gland boundary irregularity and architectural disorganization have been associated with tumor aggressiveness and treatment response in several cancers; in prostate cancer, irregular gland morphology often correlates with Gleason patterns and tumor-stroma interactions.
- Measuring GAC from H&E avoids molecular assays and is scalable to routine pathology workflows, enabling a purely morphological/spatial biomarker for predicting ADT response.

How to measure (workflow)
- Input: Digitized H&E slides from patients with localized low-to-intermediate risk prostate cancer treated (or considered for treatment) with ADT.
- Step 1: Delineate tumor regions (manual annotation or automated tumor segmentation).
- Step 2: Segment individual glands within the tumor region (gland segmentation can be automatic with a pathology-aware model or semi-automatic with pathologist refinement).
- Step 3: For each gland, extract the boundary contour.
- Step 4: Compute fractal dimension of each gland boundary using a box-counting method (or an equivalent boundary-roughness metric).
- Step 5: Aggregate across glands to obtain GAC:
  - Primary metric: mean boundary fractal dimension across glands
  - Secondary metrics (optional): standard deviation, median, and distributional descriptors of fractal dimensions
- Step 6: Normalize across slides to reduce staining/scan variability (e.g., color normalization, standardization of gland size scale).
- Output: A single GAC score per patient (or per tumor region) that can be fed into a predictive model using only morphological features.

- Outcome definition: Response to ADT (e.g., PSA decline trajectory, biochemical recurrence-free survival, or radiographic progression-free survival) within a defined follow-up.
