- Key candidate: Tumor-Stroma Ratio (TSR) measured on H&E-stained histopathology

Rationale (why this could work)
- Drug delivery and microenvironment: A higher fraction of stroma within the tumor area can impede cisplatin diffusion and create a microenvironment linked to therapy resistance.
- Morphology from H&E: TSR is assessable directly from standard H&E slides without special stains, making it suitable for a morphology-focused workflow.
- Spatial context: TSR captures the spatial balance between tumor epithelium and surrounding stroma, a fundamental aspect of tumor architecture that may influence chemosensitivity.

What to measure and how
- Data: Digitized whole-slide images (WSIs) of pretreatment H&E sections from NMIBC patients treated with cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
- Segmentation (two approaches, can be combined):
  - Tumor epithelium vs. stroma segmentation within the tumor region (use a trained segmentation model or manual/semi-automatic annotation).
  - Optionally, delineate invasive fronts to ensure TSR reflects the relevant tumor compartment.
- TSR calculation:
  - TSR = stromal area / (stromal area + tumoral epithelial area) within the defined tumor region.
  - Classify tumors as stroma-high or stroma-low using a data-driven threshold (e.g., optimized on a training cohort via ROC analysis or survival-based criteria).
- Optional augmentations (morphological/spatial additions on H&E):
  - Tumor budding or single-cell invasion at the invasive front (counts of single cells/small clusters).
  - Lymphocyte density and spatial clustering in the tumor and peritumoral areas (estimated on H&E as a morphological surrogate for immune infiltration).
  - Spatial heterogeneity metrics (e.g., distribution entropy of tumor vs stroma, nearest-neighbor distances between tumor nests and stromal cells).

Validation approach (how to establish predictive value)
- Cohort: Retrospective NMIBC patients who received cisplatin-based neoadjuvant therapy with known treatment response (pathologic response or defined clinical response) and follow-up data.
- Analysis plan:
  - Primary outcome: treatment response (responder vs non-responder) or pathologic downstaging.
  - Statistical model: logistic regression or tree-based methods with TSR as a predictor, adjusted for clinical covariates (age, stage/grade, tumor size, multiplicity, prior recurrence).
  - Performance: AUC/ROC, accuracy, sensitivity/specificity; calibration plots; decision-curve analysis.
  Subgroup analyses: examine robustness in different NMIBC subtypes and in datasets from multiple centers.
- Validation strategy: internal cross-validation and, if possible, external validation on an independent NMIBC cohort.

Practical considerations and caveats
- Data quality: Ensure pretreatment H&E slides are of adequate quality and the tumor region is well-represented in the section analyzed.
- Segmentation reliability: TSR is sensitive to accurate segmentation of tumor vs stroma. Consider combining automated segmentation with pathologist review.
- Generalizability: Thresholds may vary by scanner, staining, and patient cohort; consider site-specific calibration or transfer learning strategies for multi-center data.
- Complementary features: If available, combining TSR with additional morphology-based features (e.g., tumor budding, peritumoral lymphocyte patterns, spatial organization metrics) may improve predictive performance.
