# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some pretreatment TNBCs with high stromal TILs still fail to achieve pCR after NAC? The working mechanistic question is whether immune presence (sTILs) is only beneficial when those lymphocytes are functionally activated (IFN-γ signaling) and not counterbalanced by adaptive immunosuppression?

## Working Hypothesis

In pretreatment TNBC, high stromal TIL density predicts higher likelihood of achieving pathological complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant chemotherapy only when IFN-γ signaling is functionally active and IDO1–mediated immunosuppression is minimal, such that sTIL-high tumors with high IFNG and low IDO1 exhibit a robust cytotoxic/inflammatory program conducive to pCR. An integrated biomarker combining sTIL density with IFNG and IDO1 expression will more accurately predict pCR than sTIL density alone by capturing the balance between effector activation (and associated Tier 2 signals such as CXCL9/10, GZMB, PRF1, CD8A, HLA-DRA) and immunosuppressive counter-regulation.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
