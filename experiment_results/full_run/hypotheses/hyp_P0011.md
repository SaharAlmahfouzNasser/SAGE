# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a tension—tumour mutational burden (TMB) and DNA-repair status (MSH6) define an intrinsic immunogenic potential, yet noninvasive and microenvironmental readouts (e.g., urine methylation, histologic patterns) show that this potential does not always translate into clinical benefit from immunotherapy. Why do some TLS-rich tumors fail to respond despite high mutation-derived neoantigen loads? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the TMB path (mechanistic link to neoantigen load and immunotherapy sensitivity) and the MSH6 path (DNA mismatch-repair → altered mutation spectrum and genome instability). These two provide a direct mechanistic axis—repair defect → altered mutational landscape → altered immune recognition—that can explain mismatches between local immune organization and treatment response. 3) Tier 2 support: Immunotherapy-biomarker concepts justify focusing on response as the endpoint; HDL-C and urine/stool methylation biomarkers indicate systemic and epigenetic modifiers that could influence tumor antigenicity or immune infiltration; SARIFA and other histologic phenotypes support the idea that microenvironment architecture modulates whether immune structures (TLS) are functional. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: The most actionable and mechanistic question from these paths concerns predicting/understanding response to immune checkpoint blockade (immunotherapy) in MIBC—TMB and MSH6 are directly implicated in immunogenicity, so a therapy_response proxy is the most informative clinical endpoint for probing mechanism. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded making urine DNA methylation an anchor to keep the hypothesis sharp (it remains a secondary modifier). I omitted exploratory/cryptic markers (pZVR) and distant fluid biomarkers (CSF AD markers) from the core claim because they lack direct mechanistic ties to anti-tumor immunity in bladder cancer. I also avoided naming additional genes to respect the 2-anchor limit and to keep the hypothesis focused on the repair → mutational landscape → immune-function axis?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are insufficient predictors of response to immune checkpoint blockade because MSH6 deficiency creates an ultramutated but functionally suboptimal neoantigen landscape and concomitant defects in antigen presentation. Specifically, TLS-rich MIBC tumors with MSH6 loss will show: (a) a high but qualitatively poor neoantigen pool (low predicted MHC binding quality and reduced clonality), and (b) downregulation or dysfunction of antigen presentation machinery (MHC I/II, B2M, TAP1/2, NLRC5) leading to impaired neoantigen display to T cells. This results in a discordance where TLS are structurally present but functionally inert, yielding poor immunotherapy response. Conversely, TLS-rich tumors with intact antigen presentation and high-quality neoantigens will exhibit active TLS-associated T/B cell interactions, robust CD8+ T cell activation within and around TLS, and favorable responses to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. Therefore, the predictive value for immunotherapy in MIBC is driven by the interaction between: (i) TLS maturation/functional status, (ii) TMB-driven neoantigen quality, and (iii) antigen presentation competence (MHC, B2M, processing). The study aims to test whether a composite model incorporating TLS metrics, MSH6 status, TMB, and antigen-presentation readouts better predict therapy_response than TLS or TMB alone, and to delineate mechanistic pathways underlying TLS dysfunction in MIBC.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
