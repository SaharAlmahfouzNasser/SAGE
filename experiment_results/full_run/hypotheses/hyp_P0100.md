# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Docetaxel is linked to changes in tumor immune/inflammatory signaling, but imaging evidence of lymphoid infiltration (TILs) does not always translate to improved survival — why do some TIL-rich tumors fail to benefit? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the 'status of in-situ immune cell infiltrate' path (connects imaging/pathomics to immune context and OS) and the osteopontin→CSF-1 axis (mechanistically ties chemo to macrophage recruitment/polarization). These directly address an immune–microenvironment-mediated explanation for discordant TIL prognostic effects. 3) Tier 2 support: inflammatory indices (EDIP/EDIH) and CENPA provide complementary evidence that systemic inflammation/metabolism and tumor proliferative/genomic instability can modulate treatment effects and immune context, reinforcing a model where tumor-intrinsic and host metabolic states influence whether immune infiltrates are effective. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) was chosen because it is the most definitive clinical outcome available across cohorts and best captures the net effect of competing tumor-promoting and immune-mediated processes after chemotherapy. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded TNFα-R2 from the core claim to keep the mechanism focused on the macrophage-recruiting osteopontin–CSF1 axis and T cell suppression observable via TIL density; TNFα-R2 and CENPA are relegated to secondary modifiers for follow-up analyses. I also excluded non-imaging biomarkers (urinary panels, f/tPSA) from the core variables to keep the hypothesis sharply testable with H&E-derived TIL density and transcript/protein measures of SPP1/CSF1?

## Working Hypothesis

In taxane-treated tumors, high intratumoral TIL density alone does not guarantee OS benefit if the tumor microenvironment exhibits concurrent high SPP1 (osteopontin) and CSF1 expression, which promote recruitment and M2-like polarization of macrophages via the CSF1-CSF1R axis. This macrophage milieu suppresses cytotoxic T cell activity, rendering TILs functionally inert and leading to poorer OS compared with TIL-high tumors with low SPP1/CSF1. The magnitude of this effect is modulated by systemic inflammatory/metabolic state (as reflected by EDIP/EDIH) and tumor proliferative/genomic instability signals (e.g., CENPA). Therefore, the prognostic value of TILs is conditional on the SPP1-CSF1 axis and host/tumor-intrinsic states, and combining CSF1R/macro-phage–targeted strategies (or SPP1-axis inhibition) with docetaxel could reveal or enhance the survival advantage of TIL-rich tumors.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
