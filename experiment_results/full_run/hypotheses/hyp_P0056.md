# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a tension—organized lymphoid structures (TLS) or neoepitope-bearing tumors should enable effective antitumor T-cell responses, yet some HR+/HER2- tumors treated with tamoxifen still show endocrine therapy failure. Why does apparent immune organization or neoantigenicity fail to translate into endocrine therapy benefit? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on CORE PATH 1 (MARCO) because MARCO+ macrophages offer a plausible mechanism for local immune suppression that can neutralize TLS function; and CORE PATH 2 (PIK3CA→neoepitope) because PIK3CA mutations simultaneously create neoepitopes (immune visibility) and drive tumor-intrinsic signaling that can modulate the microenvironment. These two together directly address the puzzle of immune presence vs. immune efficacy. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: CCND1 amplification (endocrine resistance) and FGFR1/4–LAMP1 trafficking (altered receptor signaling/turnover) provide alternative tumor-intrinsic paths to therapy failure and could interact with MARCO-driven suppression; senescence (SA-β-gal) and serum markers (CEA) represent additional microenvironmental/systemic modifiers that can confound TLS benefit. These support testing combinatorial mechanisms (macrophage polarization × oncogenic signaling) rather than attributing outcome solely to TLS presence. 4) Why therapy_response (endocrine resistance) as the endpoint: The scientific puzzle is explicitly about tamoxifen-treated HR+ tumors failing therapy despite immune features; therapy_response is the most actionable, mechanistically specific endpoint (identifies patients for combination interventions) and is testable using available proxies (hormone signaling, mutation status, outcome surrogates). OS/PFI are broader and more confounded by downstream treatments. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded FGFR1/4+LAMP1 from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (it is placed as a secondary modifier); I excluded imaging features 'TIL density' and 'TSR' to avoid diluting the focus—TLS specifically captures organized antigen-presentation niches relevant to neoepitope-driven responses. Tier 3 exploratory items (CD34, HGH1, CA markers) were omitted from the core claim to maintain a testable, two-anchor-gene hypothesis?

## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2- breast tumors treated with tamoxifen, organized TLS features alone do not predict therapy benefit unless the tumor microenvironment is not dominated by MARCO+ suppressive macrophages. Specifically, MARCO+ macrophage–mediated immunosuppression dampens TLS-driven T-cell priming and cytotoxicity, and concurrent PIK3CA-driven signaling reduces neoepitope–directed cytotoxicity, collectively converting TLS presence into endocrine therapy resistance. Clinically, tumors with both TLS-like organization and high MARCO expression plus PIK3CA mutations will show reduced tamoxifen response, defining a subgroup that may require macrophage-targeting and PI3K/immune-modulatory combination therapy. Secondary modifiers (CCND1 amplification and FGFR1/4–LAMP1 trafficking) can modulate the magnitude of resistance and should be considered as interaction terms rather than primary drivers. The two-anchor framework (MARCO+ macrophage immunosuppression and PIK3CA-driven neoepitope signaling) provides a focused, testable hypothesis with clear therapeutic implications.
