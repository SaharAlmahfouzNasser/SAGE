# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Among men with localized prostate cancer assigned low-to-intermediate risk by Gleason grade group (GGG1–2) on routine H&E, a subset nonetheless experiences progression; why does morphology-based GGG fail to identify these higher-risk tumors? 2) Tier 1 path used: the single provided path linking Gleason grade group (GGG) to H&E-detected Gleason patterns and gland architecture — I built on this because the ontology frames GGG as a morphology-derived risk label whose accuracy depends on captured architecture. 3) Tier 2 / supporting evidence: nodes describing interobserver variability and the need for standardized or image-based scoring support the idea that sampling, observer differences, and unquantified architectural heterogeneity modulate GGG’s prognostic fidelity. 4) Why PFI as the endpoint: progression-free interval directly maps to the clinical decision dilemma (active surveillance vs definitive therapy) in localized low-to-intermediate risk disease and is the most relevant outcome to test whether morphology-based grading misses clinically meaningful progression. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded molecular features (genes, methylation, expression), other imaging biomarkers, and broad clinical covariates to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the mechanistic role of architectural heterogeneity and sampling/interpretation limits in causing GGG misclassification and subsequent progression risk?

## Working Hypothesis

Imaging feature: Gleason grade group (GGG) derived from H&E. Endpoint: progression-free interval (PFI). Core hypothesis: In localized prostate cancer patients initially classified as GGG1–2, intratumoral architectural heterogeneity and sampling limitations cause underestimation of aggressiveness, so a subset will experience progression sooner than predicted by the initial GGG. A blinded, centralized re-evaluation (consensus grading) or reference-standard grading on complete histology will reclassify a proportion of GGG1–2 cases to a higher grade, and those upgraded cases will have significantly shorter PFI compared with concordant cases. This would demonstrate that the prognostic value of initial GGG1–2 for PFI is limited by sampling and interobserver variability, and that standardized pathology review improves risk stratification for localized disease, informing management decisions between active surveillance and definitive therapy. No anchor genes are included; the analysis remains confined to the Gleason-based imaging feature and its relation to PFI.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Assemble a retrospective multicenter cohort of localized prostate cancer with initial GGG1–2 and long-term follow-up for PFI.

**Prediction 2:** Obtain reference histology (whole-mount or RP specimens) and have a blinded panel of pathologists assign consensus/ upgraded grades.

**Prediction 3:** Classify cases into concordant (no upgrade) and upgraded (GGG1–2 to higher GGG).
