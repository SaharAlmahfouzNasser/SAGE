# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that a genomic recurrence score (RS ≥16) and proliferation (Ki-67/TBS) each signal prognosis and potential chemotherapy benefit in breast cancer, yet TNBC is heterogeneous in immune infiltration; therefore the puzzle is why a genomic score that predicts chemo benefit (RS ≥16) does not uniformly translate into treatment responsiveness across TNBC cases. 2) Tier 1 paths used: CORE PATH 1 (RS ≥16 → chemo benefit) provides the predictive axis for adjuvant therapy; CORE PATH 2 (Ki-67 → prognosis/TBS) supplies the proliferation-dependent mechanism that underpins chemotherapy sensitivity. I built on these because they form a mechanistic contrast—genomic risk versus cell-intrinsic proliferative sensitivity. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: immune/epigenetic and liquid-biopsy modalities (PD-L1/HGH1, LINE-1 methylation in ctDNA, ALK-PHOS, DBS, PIVKA-II, CYC) supply plausible modifiers—immune phenotype and systemic/epigenetic tumor state could substitute for or mask proliferation-driven chemo effects and thus modulate the RS–chemo relationship. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: The core path explicitly links RS ≥16 to benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy, so a therapy-response proxy (chemo benefit) is the most direct, clinically actionable endpoint to test the mechanistic interaction between RS, proliferation, and immune context. 5) What I excluded and why: I deliberately excluded HGH1/PD-L1 from the two anchors to keep the mechanistic claim sharply focused on the interplay between genomic risk (RS) and proliferation (Ki-67) with immune status measured morphologically (TILs) as the modulator; other Tier 2 modifiers (LINE-1 methylation, ctDNA, ALK-PHOS, DBS, PIVKA-II, CYC) are placed in secondary_modifiers for follow-up mechanistic or biomarker studies. I also excluded Tier 3 exploratory metabolites (4-EPS, RPI, ER 1–10%) from both core and secondary lists to preserve focus?

## Working Hypothesis

In triple-negative breast cancer, the predictive value of a genomic recurrence score (RS ≥16) for benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy is not universal but is modulated by the tumor’s immune context and its proliferation rate. Specifically, there is a three-way interaction where RS≥16 confers substantial chemotherapy benefit primarily in tumors with high proliferation (high Ki-67) and/or low tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density. In tumors with high TIL density (immune-inflamed microenvironment), the incremental benefit of adding chemotherapy to surgery is attenuated or modified due to immune-mediated tumor control, potentially diminishing the added value of RS≥16–driven chemotherapy decisions. Conversely, in tumors with low Ki-67 (low proliferation) but low-to-moderate TILs, RS≥16 may still identify subgroups with benefit, though the magnitude may be smaller and influenced by immune context. Therefore, a composite model integrating RS status, Ki-67 proliferation index, and TIL density will stratify TNBC patients into distinct groups with high, intermediate, or low likelihood of chemotherapy benefit, enabling safe de-escalation for certain RS≥16 patients when the immune context and proliferation status predict limited additional chemo sensitivity, while preserving standard chemotherapy for those with high proliferation and/or low immune infiltration. Mechanistically, chemotherapy efficacy arises from a combination of proliferation-dependent cytotoxicity and immune-mediated tumor cell killing; in tumors with robust immune infiltration, immune mechanisms may suffice or dominate, reducing the reliance on high proliferation-driven chemosensitivity. This three-way framework can be prospectively tested in clinical cohorts and complemented by translational studies to elucidate the biological underpinnings of RS–Ki-67–TIL interactions.
