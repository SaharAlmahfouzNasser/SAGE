What it is
- TSR is the proportion of stroma relative to the total tumor area within an invasive breast cancer specimen, quantified from hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained images.
- It is a morphological/spatial biomarker derived purely from histology.

Why it’s prognostic in HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer
- A higher stromal content (stroma-rich tumors) has been associated with worse outcomes and higher risk of recurrence in breast cancer, including some HR-positive/HER2-negative cases.
- TSR uses only tissue morphology and spatial arrangement, so it fits the requirement of deriving prognosis from H&E features without molecular assays.

How to derive from H&E images
- Digitize slides (whole-slide imaging) and identify representative invasive tumor areas.
- Segment or classify regions into tumor and stroma (manual annotation or automated image analysis/AI segmentation).
- Compute TSR as either:
  - TSR = stromal area / (tumor area + stromal area), or
