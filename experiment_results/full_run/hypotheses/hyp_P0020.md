# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

NMIBC patients often show organized local immune structures (TLS/TILs) after BCG yet some fail to achieve durable clinical benefit — why does local immune organization not always translate into therapy response? 2) Tier‑1 basis: I built on Core Path 1 (BCG → biomarker loop), Core Path 2 (PD‑L1 ↔ bTMB / immune checkpoint dynamics), and Core Path 3 (MSH6 as a mismatch‑repair/prognostic axis). These provide a mechanistic chain: mismatch‑repair status (MSH6) alters mutation/neoantigen load, which modulates PD‑L1–mediated adaptive resistance and thereby the functional output of TLS. 3) Tier‑2 support: noninvasive urine DNA methylation panels and cross‑cancer methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2) provide feasible stratification/readout routes; TP53/ER axis and pZVR supply orthogonal prognostic signals that can validate or refine the mechanistic subgrouping. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected therapy_response (BCG response proxy) because the core question asks why an immune therapy (BCG) sometimes fails despite apparent local immune engagement — a therapy‑response endpoint directly tests that mechanism. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I limited the hypothesis to one imaging feature (TLS) and two anchor genes (MSH6, PD‑L1) to meet the focusing rule; I excluded explicit mention of bTMB and methylation markers from the hypothesis sentence (they are in secondary_modifiers) to avoid overloading the mechanistic claim and to keep the question sharply testable?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in NMIBC predict a reduced response to intravesical BCG in tumors that harbor MSH6 loss due to increased neoantigen burden driving PD-L1 upregulation and adaptive immune resistance within TLS; consequently, TLS-mediated anti-tumor activity is blunted when MSH6 is deficient. Clinically, TLS-rich NMIBC with MSH6 deficiency and high PD-L1 expression will show lower therapy_response to BCG, and these patients may benefit from PD-1/PD-L1 blockade to restore TLS-driven immunity and improve clinical outcomes.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Retrospective cohort analysis of NMIBC patients treated with BCG with data on TLS density/maturation, MSH6 status, PD-L1 expression, and bTMB;

**Prediction 2:** prospective collection of tumor biopsies for IHC (TLS, PD-L1), targeted sequencing for MSH6, calculation of bTMB, and outcome data on therapy_response;

**Prediction 3:** in vitro/in vivo models to test whether MSH6 loss leads to PD-L1 upregulation and TLS dysfunction, and whether PD-1/PD-L1 blockade can restore TLS activity and improve BCG-like responses.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
