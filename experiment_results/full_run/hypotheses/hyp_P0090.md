# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 analysis identifies a tension between apparent local immune organization (PD-L1/TLS signals) and poor clinical outcomes under standard therapy — why do immune-structured tumors sometimes fail to derive benefit from cytotoxic therapy in localized high‑risk prostate cancer?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary mechanistic hypothesis: In localized high-risk prostate cancer, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates indicate organized anti-tumor immunity, but their effectiveness is contingent on tumor-intrinsic PGE2/EP4 signaling. When PTGER4 expression is high within tumor and/or immune cells, PGE2-EP4 signaling upregulates PD-L1 (CD274), promoting T-cell exhaustion and damping TLS-driven cytotoxic responses, thereby reducing docetaxel sensitivity. Clinically, TLS-high tumors with concurrent high PTGER4/CD274 expression will exhibit diminished therapy_response to docetaxel relative to TLS-low or PTGER4/CD274-low tumors. Secondary hypothesis: pharmacologic co-targeting of EP4 (PTGER4) and PD-L1 can restore TLS-mediated anti-tumor activity and enhance docetaxel response in TLS-high, PTGER4/CD274-high tumors. In addition, genomic risk (Decipher_GC), cell-cycle signals (CENPA), and inflammatory/urinary biomarkers, as secondary modifiers, will modulate this axis by affecting baseline tumor aggressiveness and the inflammatory milieu, thereby improving the predictive model without being primary causal drivers. Endpoint specification: therapy_response will be defined in the neoadjuvant/localized setting by objective measures of docetaxel sensitivity (e.g., radiographic/biochemical response, pathologic tumor viability) and will be correlated with TLS status, PTGER4/CD274 expression, and modifier biomarkers.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** mediates PGE2-driven immunosuppression and has been linked to increased PD-L1 expression in various cancers; high PTGER4/CD274 activity within an otherwise TLS-rich microenvironment provides a plausible mechanistic bridge explaining observed discordance between TLS presence and docetaxel response in localized prostate cancer.

**Prediction 2:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.

**Prediction 3:** In functional experiments, restoring or enhancing the anchor gene's signaling will improve immune-mediated tumor control in imaging-feature-positive models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Multi-modal validation pipeline: computational -> spatial -> functional, providing mechanistic depth beyond association.

## Caveats & Risks

- Gene expression does not equal protein activity; phospho-protein or functional assays are needed for confirmation.

## Alternative Directions

- Decipher_GC: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- CENPA: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
