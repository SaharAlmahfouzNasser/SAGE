# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

In HR+/HER2− breast cancer, why do tumors with abundant stromal content and imaging features of a pro-vascular microenvironment sometimes fail endocrine therapy — i.e., what microenvironmental mechanisms convert stromal/angiogenic phenotypes into endocrine therapy resistance? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the VEGF (angiogenesis ↔ imaging/perfusion) path and the BIRC5 (survivin → apoptosis resistance ↔ tamoxifen outcome) path because together they provide a plausible mechanistic axis: a VEGF-driven stromal niche can enhance survival signaling while BIRC5 confers tumor-intrinsic apoptosis resistance. I deprioritized HSP70 from the core only to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the angiogenesis–apoptosis interaction. 3) Tier 2 support: metabolic and physiologic modifiers (HbA1c, insulin/METS-IR, skeletal muscle index) can modulate stromal activation, systemic inflammation, and drug pharmacodynamics—these factors plausibly gate VEGF signaling and survivin induction, offering testable modifiers of the core axis. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (endocrine therapy resistance) because the paths consistently implicate tamoxifen/endocrine therapy and because a mechanistic explanation of resistance has immediate therapeutic implications (combination strategies). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded exploratory/indirect entities (EHD2, generic 'breast cancer biomarker status', and the broad HR status node) from the core hypothesis to avoid diluting the mechanistic claim; HSP70 and metabolic/body-composition markers are retained as secondary_modifiers rather than primary anchors to keep the question crisp and testable?

## Working Hypothesis

A high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) identifies a VEGFA-driven pro-angiogenic microenvironment that upregulates tumor-cell survival signaling and BIRC5 (survivin) expression, mechanistically promoting endocrine therapy resistance in HR+/HER2− breast cancer. Metabolic and body-composition factors (HbA1c, insulin/METS-IR, skeletal muscle index) modulate this axis by shaping stromal activation, systemic inflammation, and drug pharmacodynamics, such that TSR-high tumors with elevated VEGFA and BIRC5 exhibit reduced endocrine therapy benefit and may be more responsive to combination strategies that target angiogenesis or survivin.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TSR correlates with a pro-angiogenic, stromal-rich microenvironment that supports tumor survival signals;

**Prediction 2:** VEGFA signaling can induce prosurvival pathways and transcriptional upregulation of BIRC5 (survivin), contributing to endocrine resistance in HR+/HER2− cancers;

**Prediction 3:** systemic metabolic status modulates inflammation, insulin signaling, and drug exposure, potentially gating the strength of VEGFA- and survivin-mediated resistance.
