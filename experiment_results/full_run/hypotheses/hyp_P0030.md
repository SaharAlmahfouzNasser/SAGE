# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights three converging biomarker axes—CTC-derived systemic indicators, MSI/AI-derived signatures that predict immunogenicity, and TGF-β–driven stromal remodeling—raising the question: under what microenvironmental conditions does a tumor that appears immunogenic nevertheless fail to respond to immune therapy? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) the CTC/liquid-biopsy axis because it reports systemic dissemination and tumor plasticity, and (b) the TGF-β stromal axis because TGF-β mechanistically drives fibrosis/immune exclusion; these two provide a testable mechanism linking local stroma to systemic tumor behavior. (MSI/AI is a core context but I used it as a secondary modifier to keep the mechanistic claim sharp.) 3) Tier 2 support: MSH6/MSI status (neoantigen load) can create an immunogenic tumor that nevertheless is excluded by a fibrotic stroma; PTEN loss and GREM1-mediated BMP/TGF modulation can amplify EMT and dissemination (increasing CTCs); SDC2/TFPI2 methylation and HDL-C provide complementary noninvasive readouts to validate tumor origin and metabolic contribution. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: The most actionable, clinically urgent puzzle here is therapy failure—particularly immune checkpoint blockade—despite apparent immunogenicity (MSI); TGF-β–mediated exclusion and CTC-mediated dissemination offer a mechanistic explanation that directly informs treatment selection. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded Tier 3 exploratory paths (CSF/AD biomarkers, pZVR) and broader multi-feature AI models from the core claim to avoid dilution of the mechanistic focus; MSI/AI-derived signatures and other Tier 2 genes are kept as secondary modifiers rather than anchors to conform to the Focusing Rule?

## Working Hypothesis

In solid tumors, high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) reflecting a TGF-β–driven fibrotic microenvironment promotes EMT and immune exclusion, leading to increased circulating tumor cell (CTC) burden and diminished anti-tumor immune activity. Consequently, tumors with high TSR and elevated TGFB1 signaling will show reduced response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) regardless of MSI-associated neoantigen load, whereas TSR-low tumors with lower TGFB1 activity will exhibit robust ICB responses. The predictive value is enhanced when integrating noninvasive and genomic markers—MSH6/MSI status, PTEN loss, GREM1 expression, SDC2 and TFPI2 methylation, and HDL-C—creating a multi-parameter score that outperforms MSI status alone. This hypothesis will be tested in bladder cancer and other solid tumors, with the expectation that TGF-β–targeted combination therapy may restore ICB sensitivity in TSR-high contexts.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
