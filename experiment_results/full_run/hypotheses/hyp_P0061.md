# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 analysis shows co-existing but divergent microenvironmental signals in TNBC — B-cell/TLS features (CD20, TIL/TLS) that usually predict better outcomes coexist with macrophage/inflammatory signatures (CHI3L3) and vascular phenotypes (CD34). The puzzle is: why do lymphoid structures/TILs sometimes fail to translate into improved survival in TNBC, and what microenvironmental mediator(s) determine when immune organization is functionally beneficial? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built primarily on CORE PATH 3 (CD20/TIL/TLS axis) and CORE PATH 1 (CHI3L3/macrophage-inflammation). I prioritized CD20 because TLS and B-cell biology are tightly connected to the chosen imaging feature (TLS-like lymphoid aggregates), and CHI3L3 because it provides a plausible macrophage-driven immunosuppressive mechanism that could negate TLS benefit. I deliberately deprioritized CD34 from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic question focused on immune organization vs. immune suppression. 3) Tier 2 support: GE profiles and multigene assays (supporting path 2 and 4) provide transcriptional classifiers to identify TLS-rich and CHI3L3-high tumors; CCND1/GLI1/Hedgehog and MKI67 (supporting path 1 and Tier2 entities) offer orthogonal axes (proliferation/stemness) that may modulate immune efficacy; baseline hemoglobin offers a systemic modifier affecting outcomes and treatment tolerance. These elements will be treated as secondary modifiers to refine stratification and test mechanism. 4) Endpoint choice: I chose overall survival (OS) because TCGA-BRCA provides robust OS data for hypothesis testing and because the central puzzle concerns why organized immune infiltration does or does not translate to durable clinical benefit. 5) Exclusions: I excluded Tier 3 exploratory entities (visceral adiposity index, isoprostanes, HR-negativity as a general fact) from the hypothesis to avoid diluting the mechanistic focus; CD34 (vascularity) was left out of the core claim to respect the Focusing Rule (max 2 anchors) though it remains relevant as a secondary axis for future work?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In TNBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are prognostically favorable only when intratumoral macrophages exhibit a low CHI3L3-driven inflammatory program; high CHI3L3 activity remodels TLS niches into an immunosuppressive microenvironment that blunts B-cell–mediated antitumor immunity, thus negating TLS-associated survival benefits. Consequently, TLS-rich tumors stratified as CD20-positive/CHI3L3-low will have superior overall survival (OS) compared with TLS-rich/CD20-positive/CHI3L3-high tumors. A subset of TLS-rich/CHI3L3-high tumors will show activity that may be unmasked by macrophage-targeted therapies (e.g., CHI3L1/CHI3L3 blockade or CSF1R inhibition), suggesting a targeted combination approach to realize TLS-derived benefits. Secondary modifiers (CD34 vascularity, CCND1, GLI1, SHH, MKI67, and baseline hemoglobin) will modulate the magnitude of TLS benefit and identify contexts in which macrophage-directed strategies are most effective.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
