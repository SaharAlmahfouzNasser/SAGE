# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, the key puzzle is why some HER2+ tumors with markers of high proliferation (mitotic count) still fail to derive expected benefit from trastuzumab — i.e., what tumor-intrinsic or microenvironmental factors decouple proliferation from trastuzumab sensitivity. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on Core Path 2 (mitotic count as a proliferation readout tied to trastuzumab action) and Core Path 3 (a diagnostic gene signature that correlates with clinical findings/therapy outcomes). These provide a mechanistic backbone linking tumor proliferation, molecular diagnostics, and variable therapy response. I did not place NT-pro-BNP in the core mechanistic claim because it relates to cardiotoxicity (an important clinical axis) but does not directly explain tumor-level trastuzumab resistance; it was moved to secondary_modifiers. 3) Tier 2 support: VEGF-A/angiogenesis (affects vasculature and drug delivery), Oncotype DX/genomic proliferation signatures and IHC receptor status (cross-validate molecular and histopathologic proliferation/HER2 heterogeneity), on-treatment dNLR (host inflammation that can influence therapy efficacy), and circulating miRNA/isomiRs (noninvasive indicators of microenvironmental or vascular states) all reinforce a model where microenvironmental features (ECM, vasculature, inflammation) modulate access and effectiveness of trastuzumab despite high tumor proliferation. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected a therapy_response proxy (trastuzumab response) because the core question is mechanistic — why expected sensitivity (based on proliferation) does not translate into clinical benefit under HER2 blockade, and because TCGA/clinical contexts and the Tier 1 paths are framed around trastuzumab outcomes. Therapy response is the most direct, testable endpoint for this mechanistic question. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded NT-pro-BNP from the two-anchor core claim to keep the hypothesis focused on tumor-level resistance mechanisms (it remains a secondary modifier to enable testing cardiotoxicity trade-offs later). I also excluded exploratory Tier 3 items (e.g., CYC, five-biomarker zanidatamab panel, socioeconomic "education") from the hypothesis to preserve mechanistic clarity. Overall I prioritized a crisp question about how tumor stroma/vasculature (imaging TSR) modulates the relationship between proliferation (mitotic count) and trastuzumab response as captured by a diagnostic gene signature?

## Working Hypothesis

In HER2-positive tumors with high mitotic count, a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) coupled with an ECM/angiogenesis-dominant diagnostic gene signature reduces trastuzumab penetration and engagement with HER2+ tumor cells, leading to diminished therapeutic response compared with tumors having low TSR and non-ECM/angiogenesis-dominant signatures. This resistance phenotype is modulated by microenvironmental factors (ECM density, abnormal VEGF-driven vasculature) that impede antibody delivery, independent of NT-pro-BNP-driven cardiotoxicity risk. Consequently, TSR and the associated ECM/angiogenesis transcriptional programs can stratify patients for combination strategies (e.g., stromal remodeling or anti-angiogenic approaches) to restore trastuzumab efficacy in high-mitotic tumors.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
