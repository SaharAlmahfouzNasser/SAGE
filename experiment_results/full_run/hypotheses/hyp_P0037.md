# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In HER2-positive breast cancers that express ER at 1–10% and have Ki-67 ≥10%, a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) identifies a desmoplastic, immune-excluded microenvironment in which trastuzumab-based therapy yields diminished tumor response due to reduced antibody penetration and impaired effector cell engagement, resulting in a lower objective response rate (ORR) compared to ER 1–10% and Ki-67 ≥10% tumors with low TSR. Mechanistic basis includes: (a) dense ECM and activated stroma creating physical and biochemical barriers to trastuzumab diffusion and Fc-mediated ADCC; (b) potential shift in tumor dependence away from HER2 signaling in an ER-low, highly proliferative state; (c) altered immune contexture (reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration, NK cell activity) associated with high TSR. This triad (ER 1–10% + Ki-67 ≥10% + high TSR) defines a distinct, less HER2-addicted phenotype with reduced responsiveness to trastuzumab-containing regimens. The effect should be detectable after adjusting for HER2 amplification level and established prognostic signatures (e.g., LobSig, RS). Secondary modifiers (e.g., LINE-1 methylation, HbA1c) may modulate magnitude but are not essential to the core mechanism. The predictive model will integrate ER status, Ki-67, TSR, and stromal/immune signatures to stratify patients for HER2-targeted therapy intensity and to identify candidates for combination approaches targeting the stroma or boosting immune effector function.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** retrospective clinical cohorts with quantified TSR, ER, Ki-67, and response data;

**Prediction 2:** mechanistic correlative studies assessing intratumoral trastuzumab levels and immune-effector markers (CD8, NK markers, FcγR engagement) and stromal signatures;

**Prediction 3:** preclinical models (PDX or organoids with controlled stromal content) to test trastuzumab efficacy and the impact of stromal modulation. If validated, TSR-guided stratification could inform treatment intensification or combination strategies (e.g., stromal remodeling agents, immune activation) for the high-TSR ER1–10% Ki-67≥10% subset to improve outcomes.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
