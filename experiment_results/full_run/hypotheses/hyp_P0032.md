## Working Hypothesis

TGFB1-driven stromal remodeling increases the stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) in solid tumors, creating a fibrotic and immune-excluding microenvironment that diminishes intratumoral CD8+ T cell activity and antigen presentation, thereby reducing clinical benefit from immune checkpoint blockade. This primary mechanistic link is observable in bladder cancer as tumors with high TGFB1 expression and high TSR exhibit lower objective response rates to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors compared with TGFB1-low/low-TSR tumors. The strength of TGFB1-induced fibrosis and consequent immune exclusion is further modulated by (i) GREM1, which can potentiate TGF-β–driven signaling and fibroblast activation, and (ii) eIF4E-driven translational control, which may influence the expression of immune-regulatory proteins (e.g., MHC/Ii components, checkpoint ligands) and cytokines. Noninvasive readouts—urine DNA methylation biomarkers and circulating tumor cells (CTCs)—will correlate with TGFB1/TSR status and predict immunotherapy response, enabling risk stratification and dynamic monitoring. The hypothesis is testable through (a) correlative analyses in existing datasets linking TGFB1, TSR, T cell infiltration, and ICB response; (b) a prospective cohort validating imaging/biomarker readouts against ICB outcomes; and (c) mechanistic in vitro/in vivo experiments demonstrating that TGFB1 inhibition or TSR reduction rescues T cell infiltration and enhances antitumor activity in the presence of immune checkpoint blockade.

- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)

## C. Functional Validation

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
