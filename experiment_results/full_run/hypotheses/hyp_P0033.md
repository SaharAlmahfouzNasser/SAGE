# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are variably predictive of immunotherapy benefit in bladder cancer — why do some TIL-high tumors respond to immune checkpoint blockade while others do not? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built the hypothesis on the TMB path because mutational/neoantigen load is a mechanistic driver of productive anti-tumor T cell responses, and on the MSH6 path because MSH6/MMR deficiency is a concrete mechanism that elevates indel/frameshift neoantigens and TMB. These two together explain when TILs are likely to be functionally tumor-reactive. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: immunotherapy-linked translational control (eIF4E) and Immunoscore concepts provide orthogonal evidence that immune contexture and tumor-intrinsic programs modulate T cell functionality; urine DNA methylation and liquid biopsy (CTCs) offer non-invasive readouts to validate and monitor the proposed interactions. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: the core axes (TMB, MSH6, immune infiltration) are directly tied to sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitors, making therapy response the most clinically actionable and mechanistically relevant endpoint. 5) Deliberately excluded: I excluded exploratory/cross-disease markers (PSA, pZVR, CSF AD biomarkers) and kept CTCs/urine methylation out of the core to maintain a sharp, testable mechanistic question focused on how neoantigen generation (TMB/MSH6) conditions the functional impact of TILs on immunotherapy response?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, high tumoral TIL density predicts response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade only when accompanied by a high neoantigen burden driven by MSH6 deficiency or other MMR-related mutational processes that generate abundant clonal, immunogenic neoepitopes. Thus, TIL-high tumors with MSH6 deficiency/TMB-high will show higher objective response rates and longer progression-free survival compared with TIL-high/TMB-low or TIL-high/MSH6-intact tumors, where TILs are present but largely non-reactive or exhausted. This predictive axis is modulated by (i) antigen presentation competence (HLA genotype, IFN-γ signaling, PD-L1 expression), (ii) the functional state of TILs (exhaustion vs. cytotoxic activity, supported by TCR clonality data), and (iii) translational control and immune contexture (eIF4E–mediated regulation, Immunoscore/IFN-γ signatures). Non-invasive longitudinal readouts (urine DNA methylation markers and circulating tumor cells) will reflect dynamic changes in neoantigen generation and immune engagement, serving as complementary biomarkers to monitor response and resistance. Practically, the model predicts: (A) TIL-high + MSH6-deficient/TA high TMB patients have the highest response rates to anti-PD-(L)1 therapy; (B) TIL-high + MSH6-wildtype/low TMB patients have reduced benefit unless other factors (e.g., high immunoscore, robust antigen presentation) compensate; (C) TIL-low tumors show limited benefit regardless of MSH6/TMB status unless combination strategies augment neoantigen visibility or T cell priming.

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
