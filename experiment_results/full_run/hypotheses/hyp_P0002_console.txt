Proposed biomarker: Tumor budding score (TBS) at the invasive front from H&E-stained histopathology

Why this fits your constraint
- It is a morphological and spatial feature observable on standard H&E slides.
- Tumor budding reflects invasive potential and early metastatic spread, making it a plausible prognostic marker.
- It is evaluated directly from tissue architecture without any molecular assays.

What to measure (practical workflow)
- Define the invasive front of the muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) tumor on the H&E slide.
- Identify budding foci: single tumor cells or small clusters (typically up to 4 tumor cells) at the tumor–stroma interface.
- Count budding foci in representative high-power fields (HPFs) at the invasive front. Some workflows use a defined length of invasive front (e.g., per millimeter) or a fixed number of HPFs (e.g., 10 HPFs) to standardize.
- Compute a budding density or score:
  - Budding density = number of budding foci per specified unit length/area.
  - Categorize into risk groups using data-driven cutoffs (e.g., low vs high budding) or retain as a continuous prognostic score.
- Optional: use automated or semi-automated image analysis to improve reproducibility:
  - Nuclei/cluster segmentation to identify single cells vs small clusters.
  - Spatial filtering to delineate the invasive front and count buds systematically.
- Validation steps:
  - Correlate TBS with clinical outcomes (overall survival, disease-specific survival, recurrence) in your MIBC cohort.
  - Adjust for confounders (e.g., stage, grade) in multivariable analysis.
  - If possible, compare with inter-observer agreement metrics and consider standardizing scoring per established guidelines or published thresholds in urothelial carcinoma.

Notes and alternatives
- Tumor-stroma ratio (TSR) is another strong morphology-based, image-derived biomarker from H&E slides. It measures the proportion of stroma vs tumor and has been studied as a prognostic factor in various cancers, including bladder cancer. It can be a complementary marker if you want to capture the broader tumor microenvironment.
- Be aware that manual budding scoring can have inter-observer variability; a standardized protocol and, ideally, automated image analysis can improve reproducibility.
