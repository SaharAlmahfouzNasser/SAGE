# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Immunologically 'hot' bladder tumors with visible lymphoid organization (high TLS/TIL) do not always benefit from immunotherapy—why do some TLS-rich tumors fail to respond?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-rich bladder tumors fail to mount productive anti-tumor immunity when high TGFB1 signaling and tumor SDC2 promoter hypermethylation cooperate to generate a fibrotic, immune-excluded niche that sequesters lymphoid aggregates and impairs effective antigen presentation. Consequently, TLS-high tumors with concurrent high TGFB1 expression and SDC2 methylation will show reduced response rates to immune checkpoint blockade compared with TLS-high tumors lacking these molecular features. The mechanism is supported by three interconnected axes: (i) TGFB1/SMAD-driven fibrosis and ECM remodeling that physically and chemically barrier cytotoxic T cells from TLS zones; (ii) SDC2 promoter methylation altering proteoglycan-mediated cell–cell signaling and stromal–epithelial communication, leading to diminished dendritic cell activation and decreased MHC-I–restricted antigen presentation; and (iii) modulation by secondary modifiers (TFPI2, MSH6, GREM1) that influence mutational load, neoantigen availability, and stromal signaling, thereby shaping the magnitude and quality of TLS-driven immunity. This yields two testable predictions: (P1) within TLS-rich bladder tumors, TGFB1high/SDC2methylated status associates with lower objective response rates to ICI and shorter progression-free survival; (P2) pharmacologic attenuation of TGFB1 signaling and/or reversal of SDC2 methylation will enhance TLS function and improve ICI efficacy in preclinical models and, prospectively, in treated patient cohorts.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** retrospective multi-omics analyses of bladder cancer patients treated with ICIs to correlate TLS metrics, TGFB1/SMAD readouts, and SDC2 promoter methylation with response and survival;

**Prediction 2:** spatial profiling (multiplex IHC, spatial transcriptomics) to map T cells relative to fibrotic ECM and SDC2-rich zones;

**Prediction 3:** in vitro models modulating TGFB1 signaling and SDC2 methylation (CRISPR/dCas9-based epigenetic editing and DNMT inhibitors) to assess effects on antigen presentation pathways;


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.

## Alternative Directions

- TFPI2: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- MSH6: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
