## Clinical Question

## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2+ breast cancers, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates co-occurring with high TROP2 expression and elevated IL-18 define a tumor-immune microenvironment in which anti-HER2 therapy yields reduced therapy_response due to IL-18–driven immunosuppression and TLS functional impairment. In contrast, a TROP2-directed ADC (e.g., datopotamab deruxtecan) delivers cytotoxic payload directly to TROP2-expressing tumor cells and thus achieves superior therapy_response in the same TLS-high/IL-18-high/TROP2-high subgroup, effectively bypassing reliance on immune effector functions such as ADCC. This differential response is modulated by secondary modifiers (leptin signaling, Ki-67 proliferation, PD-L1 expression), which may amplify or mitigate the observed effects in a given tumor context. The primary endpoint is therapy_response; the imaging feature is TLS-like lymphoid aggregates; anchor genes are TROP2 and IL-18; an ambiguous ER-negative risk biomarker is excluded to maintain mechanistic clarity. Testable predictions include: (i) TLS-high/IL-18-high/TROP2-high tumors show limited benefit from HER2-directed therapy; (ii) Dato-DXd yields higher response rates in the same subgroup; (iii) perturbing IL-18 or TROP2 expression in preclinical models alters these responses; and (iv) adding a TROP2-directed ADC to HER2-directed therapy yields additive or synergistic benefit in TLS-wealthy microenvironments.
