# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, many HER2+ tumors show abundant immune infiltration yet still recur after standard HER2-directed therapy; why does apparent anti-tumor immunity (high TILs) fail to translate into durable control? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the BIRC5 (survivin) path because it implicates a tumor-intrinsic apoptosis/resistance program that can protect cancer cells from immune- and therapy-mediated killing, and on the MPO path because MPO marks neutrophil-driven oxidative stress that can both suppress T-cell function in the tumor microenvironment and reflect systemic cardiotoxic risk that undermines therapy delivery. These two provide a coherent mechanistic axis linking tumor survival and host/innate inflammation to failed immune control. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: immunogenic markers and CD4 expression indicate the presence/quality of anti-tumor immunity (explaining why TILs might be present), while CAB/five-biomarker and multigene assays show that composite tumor signatures modify therapy benefit — all consistent with an interaction between immune infiltration and tumor-intrinsic programs shaping outcome. 4) Why PFI as the endpoint: the Ontologist emphasized recurrence/reduction in recurrence as the clinically relevant outcome; PFI (progression-free interval) most directly captures early recurrence/therapy failure in adjuvant and perioperative HER2+ settings. 5) What I deliberately excluded and why: I excluded CA12-5 from the core mechanistic claim to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the interaction between immune infiltration and resistance/oxidative-modulatory programs (CA12-5 and broader multigene panels are placed as secondary modifiers for follow-up analyses), and I did not include cardiotoxicity as the primary endpoint to avoid conflating tumor control and treatment toxicity into a single primary outcome?

## Working Hypothesis

In HER2-positive breast cancer, high intratumoral TIL density does not consistently predict prolonged progression-free interval (PFI) when tumors simultaneously express high BIRC5 (survivin) and exhibit elevated MPO activity. Mechanistically, survivin confers tumor-cell resistance to cytotoxic T-cell–mediated apoptosis, while MPO-driven oxidative stress suppresses T-cell function within the tumor microenvironment. The combination creates an immune-excluded/adaptive-resistance phenotype in which TILs are present but ineffective, resulting in shorter PFI compared with tumors that are TIL-high but BIRC5low and/or MPOlow. This interaction defines a biologically distinct subset of HER2+ tumors that may benefit from combinatorial strategies targeting survivin signaling and MPO activity to restore T-cell cytotoxicity and extend PFI. This hypothesis is testable by integrating tumor immunoprofiling (TILs, CD4/CD8 subsets, immunogenic markers), BIRC5 and MPO expression/activity assessments, and functional assays in vitro/ex vivo and in vivo, with PFI as the primary clinical readout.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
