# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a mismatch: TNBC tumors can show organized immune structures or immune-activation readouts (TLS/CRAM-A/IFN-γ, senescence marker p21, miRNA-223) yet do not always translate into durable clinical benefit. Why does apparent local immune organization fail to generate tumor control in some TNBCs?

## Working Hypothesis

In TNBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are not uniformly associated with durable tumor control when tumors co-express high CRAM-A (an IFN-γ–driven immune signature) and elevated p21Cip1 (senescence marker). This combo reflects a tumor-intrinsic state where PI3K/IFN-γ–skewed signaling and senescence-associated programs reprogram TLS-resident lymphocytes toward functional exhaustion or regulatory phenotypes, impairing cytotoxic anti-tumor activity. Consequently, TLS-high tumors with CRAM-A-high and p21Cip1-high status will have poorer overall survival than TLS-high tumors with lower p21Cip1 or CRAM-A, TLS-low tumors, or TLS-high tumors lacking this dual high signature. This detrimental effect is modulated by systemic metabolic and proteostatic stress signals (e.g., HbA1c levels, CHI3L1, HSP70, NAD+-related metabolism) that shape SASP, antigen presentation, and immune-effector fitness, thereby converting an apparently immune-organized microenvironment into a dysfunctional one. Importantly, the model predicts that: (i) high CRAM-A and p21Cip1 co-expression will correlate with increased T cell exhaustion markers (PD-1, TIM-3, LAG-3) and reduced cytotoxic function (granzyme B, perforin) within TLS; (ii) tumor cells will show altered antigen presentation (MHC-I) and/or SASP signaling that recruits suppressive myeloid/tolerogenic niches; (iii) disrupting the PI3K/IFN-γ axis or modulating SASP/NAD+ dynamics will restore TLS cytotoxic function and improve survival in preclinical models. The hypothesis remains testable through integrative analyses of patient data and controlled experimental perturbations across in vitro, ex vivo, and in vivo systems.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TLS-high, CRAM-A-high, p21Cip1-high tumors exhibit higher CD8+ T cell exhaustion and reduced cytotoxic function compared with TLS-high, CRAM-A-high, but p21Cip1-low tumors;

**Prediction 2:** these tumors show impaired antigen presentation and a pro-tumor SASP milieu;

**Prediction 3:** interventions targeting PI3K, SASP components (e.g., CHI3L1 inhibitors), or NAD+ restoration can rejuvenate TLS function and improve survival in preclinical models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
