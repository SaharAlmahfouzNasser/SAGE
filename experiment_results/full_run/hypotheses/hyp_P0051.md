# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

HR+/HER2− tumors vary in endocrine therapy sensitivity, yet it is unclear why some ER-positive tumors fail endocrine therapy despite apparent ER dependence; the Ontologist’s path centered on H&E-derived immune morphology (TIL/TLS) predicting endocrine response, so the key puzzle is: under what immune-context conditions does endocrine therapy succeed or fail? 2) Tier 1 paths used: the single provided path linking tumor immune morphology (TLS/TIL) to endocrine therapy response — I built on this because TLS represent organized local immune competence that could mechanistically interact with ER-driven tumor cell dynamics. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: tumor ER signaling (ESR1 expression/mutation) and local T cell activation/exhaustion signatures (PDCD1, CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT) are measurable in TCGA and can modulate how immune surveillance controls emergent endocrine-resistant clones; proliferative and antigen-presentation markers (MKI67, PGR, HLA family) are plausible modifiers. 4) Why therapy_response as endpoint: the proposal’s clinical goal is to predict endocrine therapy benefit (a therapy-response proxy) rather than long-term survival, and response is the most direct, mechanistically linked endpoint for assessing whether local immune architecture modifies drug effect. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I chose TLS (not TIL density) as the single imaging feature to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on organized local immunity; I excluded spatial interface metrics and broader stromal signatures from the core claim to satisfy the 1-imaging/≤2-gene constraint and relegated them to secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2− breast cancers, the presence and maturity of organized TLS within or immediately adjacent to the tumor correlate positively with clinical response to estrogen receptor–directed therapy. Specifically, mature TLS (germinal center–containing structures) sustain local antigen presentation and T cell activation (reflected by PDCD1/PD-1 axis activity) that synergizes with ER signaling status (ESR1 expression and absence of ESR1 mutations) to constrain emergence of endocrine-resistant subclones. Consequently, TLS-high, mature TLS–positive tumors show higher response rates to endocrine therapy, lower residual proliferation (Ki67) after initiation of therapy, delayed progression, and improved short- to mid-term clinical benefit compared with TLS-poor or immature TLS tumors. The effect is modulated by, and can be partly predicted by, anchor immuno-genomic features (ESR1 expression/mutation, PDCD1 expression) and secondary modifiers (PGR, MKI67, CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT, HLA gene expression). This framework supports a clinically testable imaging biomarker (TLS presence and maturity) that integrates with tumor ER signaling status to predict endocrine therapy sensitivity.

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
