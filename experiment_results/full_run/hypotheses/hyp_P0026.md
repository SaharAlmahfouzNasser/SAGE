# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do tumors with abundant stroma (high stromal-to-tumor ratio) not always show better containment of disease, and under what molecular conditions does a fibrotic TME drive early progression? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TGFB1/TGF-β fibrogenic signaling (Path 1) — gives mechanistic basis for stromal expansion and immune modulation; (b) Immune contexture (Immunoscore/CD8) (Path 7) — provides the anti-tumor effector axis whose exclusion could explain worse outcomes. I built on these because they directly link stromal biology to immune exclusion and clinical outcome. 3) Tier 2 support: GREM1 (metastasis/EMT), SMAD4 (downstream mediator of TGF-β transcriptional programs), EPCAM/CTC concepts (dissemination readouts) reinforce a pathway in which TGF-β–driven stroma promotes invasion/dissemination and immune evasion; AI/radiomic approaches provide ways to measure TSR and integrate multimodal signals. 4) Endpoint choice: PFI was chosen because the mechanistic model implicates stromal-driven immune exclusion and dissemination as drivers of disease progression (PFI captures time to progression), and PFI is robustly available in TCGA-BRCA-like datasets for testing progression hypotheses. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded imaging features focused on lymphoid aggregates/TIL density as the primary imaging variable to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on stromal/fibrotic mechanisms; I also excluded broad multi-gene signature claims to satisfy the 1–2 anchor constraint and to emphasize an explicit mechanism (TGF-β–mediated immune exclusion) rather than an associative predictor?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, high stromal-to-tumor ratio reflects TGF-β–driven fibrotic remodeling (via TGFB1 signaling) that promotes an immune-excluded microenvironment. Consequently, tumors with high TSR and elevated TGFB1 but low CD8A expression will have a shortened progression-free interval (PFI) compared with tumors lacking this triad, highlighting a mechanism where fibrosis-mediated immune exclusion accelerates progression.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** induces fibroblast activation and ECM deposition, expanding fibrotic stroma that correlates with higher TSR. This fibrotic milieu can create physical and biochemical barriers that limit CD8+ T cell infiltration, reflected by lower CD8A expression, thereby enabling tumor progression and potential resistance to immune therapies.

**Prediction 2:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.

**Prediction 3:** In functional experiments, restoring or enhancing the anchor gene's signaling will improve immune-mediated tumor control in imaging-feature-positive models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
