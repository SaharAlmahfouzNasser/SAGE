## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates indicate a latent anti-tumor immune potential, but their ability to mediate PARP inhibitor (PARPi)–driven tumor control is contingent on (i) the macrophage-influenced immunosuppressive milieu governed by CSF1/CSF1R signaling and (ii) the tumor’s chromosomal instability profile as reflected by CENPA activity, which shapes neoantigen quality and immune visibility. Specifically, TLS-high tumors with low CSF1 signaling and low CIN/CENPA activity will exhibit robust PARPi-induced anti-tumor immunity and clinical response. In contrast, TLS-high tumors with high CSF1 expression and/or high CIN/CENPA activity will show blunted PARPi responses due to macrophage-mediated suppression and suboptimal neoantigen presentation. Consequently, two actionable refinements emerge: (1) combining PARPi with CSF1R blockade or macrophage-repolarizing therapies should improve responses in TLS-high, CSF1-high/CIN-high tumors, and (2) stratification by TLS density plus CSF1 and CIN/CENPA status will identify a subset of patients most likely to benefit from PARPi monotherapy or combination therapy. This framework yields testable predictions across in vitro, in vivo, and patient-derived models and can be validated with noninvasive radiomics/pathomics readouts as orthogonal stratifiers.
