# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that tumor-intrinsic signaling (TGF-β), DNA repair status (MSH6), and systemic metabolism (HDL-C) converge to shape prognosis; the most incisive puzzle is: why do some tumors with genomically immunogenic features (e.g., MSH6 loss → higher mutation/neoantigen load) still progress rapidly? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the TGF-β path because TGF-β mechanistically drives stromal fibrosis/EMT and immune exclusion, and on the MSH6 path because mismatch-repair defects increase neoantigens and are expected to slow progression via immune surveillance — the tension between these two yields a clear mechanistic question. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: histopathologic and cross-cancer biomarkers (SARIFA, pZVR, tumor-biomarker frameworks) and considerations about biomarker specificity (PSA) support that tissue architecture and non–tumor cell signals (stromal features) can modify molecular biomarker effects; HDL-C (Tier 1) suggests metabolic state could further modulate the stroma–immune axis and will be tested as a secondary modifier. 4) Why PFI chosen: progression-free interval directly captures early tumor control and is the most sensitive clinical endpoint for interactions between microenvironment-mediated immune evasion and DNA-repair–driven immunogenicity. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded forming the hypothesis around HDL-C, PSA, pZVR, or SARIFA from the core two-gene statement to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (they are placed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up); I also excluded cross-disease (CSF/AD) and prostate-specific Gleason concepts from the core hypothesis because they are inspirational but not mechanistically central to the stroma–MSH6 interaction in bladder cancer?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In bladder cancer, a TGF-β–driven stromal reaction characterized by a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) creates an immune-excluded, fibrosis-rich microenvironment that functionally diminishes neoantigen-driven anti-tumor immunity, thereby nullifying the prognostic advantage conferred by MSH6 deficiency. Consequently, MSH6-deficient tumors with high TSR have shorter progression-free intervals (PFI) than MSH6-deficient tumors with low TSR. Secondary modifiers (HDL-C, pZVR, SARIFA, and broader tumor biomarker context) modulate the magnitude of this interaction by influencing stromal activation, vascular/immune architecture, or signals from non-tumor cells. This framework predicts that disrupting TGF-β–driven stroma or altering metabolic context could restore neoantigen-driven control in MSH6-deficient, high-TSR tumors and improve PFI.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
