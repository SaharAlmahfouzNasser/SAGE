# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Among localized prostate cancers with cribriform gland architecture, tumors exhibiting high AR signaling activity (as indicated by elevated AR expression and a robust AR-target transcriptional program) will be more sensitive to androgen-deprivation therapy (ADT), showing greater PSA decline at defined early timepoints and longer biochemical progression-free survival compared with cribriform-pattern tumors with low AR signaling activity. A composite ME-ARS score that integrates a normalized Cribriform Index (CI) with AR activity (AR expression plus AR-target transcriptional score) will predict ADT response more accurately than CI or AR metrics alone. Proliferation modifiers (e.g., MKI67, PCNA) will modulate this relationship, with high proliferation potentially reducing AR-dependence and attenuating ADT response within cribriform tumors. This framework supports stratification for neoadjuvant or definitive ADT in localized disease and clarifies when cribriform morphology indicates true AR-driven vulnerability versus AR-independent resistance.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Assemble a cohort of localized prostate cancer patients treated with ADT (with available pretreatment tissue) and collect outcome data on PSA response and progression-free metrics.

**Prediction 2:** Quantify CI from digital pathology of pretreatment slides; quantify AR activity via (a) AR protein expression by IHC and (b) an AR-target gene expression score from RNA-seq or targeted panels; compute a composite AR activity score.

**Prediction 3:** Build a ME-ARS model testing the interaction between CI and AR activity to predict therapy_response; include MKI67 and PCNA as secondary modifiers to explore proliferation-driven resistance.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Gene expression does not equal protein activity; phospho-protein or functional assays are needed for confirmation.
