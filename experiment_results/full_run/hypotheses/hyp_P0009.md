# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

TGF-β–driven fibrotic microenvironments in MIBC (which should create immune exclusion) do not uniformly predict lack of benefit from immunotherapy — some fibrotic tumors nevertheless respond. ## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) denotes a TGFB1-driven fibrotic microenvironment that creates immune exclusion and reduces anti-tumor immunity, contributing to resistance to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB). However, concurrent loss or deficiency of MSH6 (reflecting mismatch repair deficiency with elevated neoantigen burden) can partially overcome this exclusion by enhancing neoantigen-driven T cell recruitment and reactivity, generating targetable yet dysfunctional T cells. Therefore, among TSR-high tumors, those with MSH6 deficiency will exhibit higher objective response rates, longer durations of response, and improved progression-free survival when treated with ICB compared to TSR-high tumors with intact MSH6. In TSR-low tumors, the predictive power of MSH6 status is reduced, and responses to ICB are more favorable overall, reflecting lower fibrosis-associated exclusion and more accessible antitumor immunity. Urine DNA methylation markers and other Tier-2 modifiers (e.g., HDL-C, ALP, TG, SHBG, IGF1, AI-based biomarker signatures) should be evaluated as secondary modifiers to refine response predictions but are not required for the core mechanism. The anchor genes remain TGFB1 (fibrosis/TSR mediator) and MSH6 (MMR/neoantigen burden). The primary endpoints will be therapy_response, with secondary endpoints including duration of response and progression-free survival. Feasible testing approaches include retrospective analyses integrating digital pathology-based TSR assessment, MSH6 status via sequencing/IHC, and ICI-treated cohorts; prospective validation in trials with comprehensive biomarker collection; and the incorporation of noninvasive methylation panels as modifiers to enhance predictive power.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TGF-β signaling fosters fibrotic stroma and immune exclusion in bladder cancer, with TSR serving as a practical readout of this hostile microenvironment and often predicting poor ICI response;

**Prediction 2:** MSH6 deficiency increases tumor mutational burden and neoantigen landscape, which can elicit robust or at least more recruitable T cell–mediated responses even within an excluded stromal context.

**Prediction 3:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
