# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that inflammatory/immune readouts (HMGB1, CHI3L1, and a nine-protein signature) are prominent in TNBC, yet robust immune organization (e.g., TLS/TILs) does not always translate into effective anti-tumor control. I considered the nine-protein proteomic signature (Core Path 1) as contextual but kept it out of the core claim to preserve mechanistic clarity. 3) Tier 2 support: Proliferation and risk-score signals (Ki-67 / RS), cancer-stem-cell markers, EMT/ECM effectors (vimentin, MMP9) and the 5q15 genomic context provide plausible modifiers—e.g., high proliferation/CSC or active ECM remodeling could amplify HMGB1/CHI3L1-driven myeloid recruitment and TLS dysfunction. 4) Why PFI endpoint: Progression-free interval is selected because the central question concerns failure of local immune control to prevent recurrence/progression (PFI is more sensitive than OS to early immune-related control/relapse signals in TNBC within TCGA). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded socioeconomic/external-context nodes (education), distant/systemic biomarkers not mechanistically tied to local TLS function (t-tau, CK), and folded the composite nine-biomarker signature into secondary_modifiers rather than the core claim to avoid conflating descriptive panels with mechanistic drivers?

## Working Hypothesis

In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), the presence of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates is not invariably associated with durable tumor control if tumor-derived alarmins HMGB1 and CHI3L1 are highly expressed. The expanded hypothesis posits that high HMGB1 and CHI3L1 reprogram the TLS microenvironment through recruitment and polarization of myeloid cells (e.g., MDSCs, M2-like TAMs), upregulation of ECM remodeling (e.g., MMP9, vimentin) and suppression of antigen presentation (diminished dendritic cell maturation and HLA class II activity). This creates a dysfunctional TLS that supports local immune evasion, T cell exhaustion, and early relapse, leading to shorter progression-free interval (PFI) despite TLS presence. In TLS-rich, HMGB1/CHI3L1-low tumors, TLSs remain functionally competent with effective antigen presentation and T cell activation, correlating with longer PFI. Secondary modifiers (Ki-67/proliferation, cancer stem cell markers, EMT/ECM effectors, and 5q15 genomic context) modulate the strength of HMGB1/CHI3L1-driven TLS dysfunction and thus influence the magnitude of PFI differences. Therapeutically, disrupting HMGB1/CHI3L1 signaling or downstream myeloid recruitment should restore TLS functionality and improve anti-tumor immunity, offering a mechanistic and clinically testable avenue to convert TLS presence into durable control.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
