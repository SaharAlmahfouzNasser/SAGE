# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Decipher GC predicts metastasis/progression risk but many low-to-intermediate grade tumors show discordant clinical behavior; why do some genomically high-risk tumors not progress while others do? Urine PCA3 and local immune organization (TLS) may modulate or reveal mechanisms of immune containment vs immune evasion that determine progression. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the urinary biomarker (PCA3) and Decipher GC genomic-classifier paths because they provide complementary, clinically actionable readouts—PCA3 as a non-invasive marker of tumor transcriptional activity/shedding and Decipher GC as a tissue-level genomic risk signal. 3) Tier 2 support: Imaging/radiomics provides spatial proxies for immune organization (here operationalized as TLS-like lymphoid aggregates); predictive immune-response markers and in-situ immune infiltrate characterize functional immune competence; metabolite biomarkers reflect tumor metabolic programs that can enable immune escape—these support the mechanism that immune containment (TLS) can modulate genomic risk depending on tumor activity/metabolism (PCA3). 4) Endpoint choice: I selected Progression-Free Interval (PFI) because Decipher is tied to metastasis/progression risk and PFI best captures the clinically relevant progression events we aim to explain. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded weaker, indirect or exploratory links (e.g., SPC/CSF-1 axis, insulin-response, alpha-fetoprotein, therapy-nomogram–Olaparib paths) to keep the hypothesis tightly focused on the PCA3–Decipher–immune imaging interaction that directly addresses progression risk in localized disease?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In localized prostate cancer, the adverse association between a high Decipher GC score and shorter progression-free interval (PFI) is attenuated when TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are present in the tumor and urinary PCA3 levels are low; conversely, absence of TLS-like aggregates or high PCA3 amplify the risk associated with a high Decipher GC score. Secondary hypotheses: radiomics/imaging biomarkers, systemic/metabolic signatures, and in-situ immune infiltrate status will reflect or mediate this interaction and improve non-invasive prediction of PFI. A combined risk model incorporating Decipher GC, TLS status, PCA3, radiomic features, and metabolite markers will outperform single-modality predictors in identifying patients at highest risk for progression and guiding surveillance versus early intervention.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).

**Prediction 2:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks
