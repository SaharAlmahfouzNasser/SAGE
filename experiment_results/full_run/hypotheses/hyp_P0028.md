## Working Hypothesis

In TLS-rich solid tumors, does TLS-driven anti-tumor immunity depend on a specific molecular context where (i) MSH6 loss increases neoantigen burden, (ii) eIF4E activity is low to permit robust antigen presentation, and (iii) TLS-like aggregates provide localized T cell activation? Specifically, can high eIF4E activity uncouple TLS abundance from productive antigen presentation and thereby blunt immunotherapy responses? If correct, TLS-high tumors that are MSH6-deficient and have low eIF4E will exhibit superior responses to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade compared with TLS-high MSH6-deficient tumors with high eIF4E or TLS-low tumors, identifying eIF4E as a targetable resistance modifier and a predictive biomarker. Mechanistically, MSH6 loss is expected to raise neoantigen load, while low eIF4E supports intact antigen-processing machinery and MHC-I presentation, allowing TLS-primed T cells to recognize and kill tumor cells; conversely, high eIF4E may promote immunosuppressive translation programs that reduce antigen presentation and TLS effectiveness. Experimental validation should test this triad across in silico analyses, murine models engineered for MSH6 deficiency and TLS induction, and patient-derived systems, with immunotherapy response as the primary endpoint and secondary readouts including neoantigen burden, MHC-I surface expression, TLS organization, and T cell effector activity. Secondary modifiers (e.g., liquid biopsy markers, ctDNA methylation) can stratify responses and reflect tumor burden and systemic context. Outcome expectations: TLS-high/MSH6-deficient/low-eIF4E tumors will show higher objective response rates, longer progression-free survival, and greater immune infiltration on checkpoint blockade than comparison groups, supporting eIF4E as both a resistance modifier and a predictive biomarker.
