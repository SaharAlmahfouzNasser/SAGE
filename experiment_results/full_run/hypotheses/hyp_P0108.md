# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist framed a core question around why chemotherapy (docetaxel)–linked immune/inflammatory states only sometimes translate into better long-term outcomes; specifically, CENPA-linked chromosomal instability (CIN) emerged as a potential modifier of immune-related prognostic signals. In short: why do some immune-infiltrated tumors fail to benefit clinically? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on CORE PATH 1 (CENPA as an independent prognostic marker tied to stage/CIN), CORE PATH 2 (therapy–immune interface / predictive markers for immune response), and CORE PATH 3 (therapy-linked inflammation signals). These collectively point to an interaction between lymphoid infiltration, tumor CIN (CENPA), and inflammation that determines outcome. 3) Tier 2 support: the OPN→CSF-1 macrophage axis, SPC readouts, and imaging/radiomics signals provide plausible mechanisms by which CIN could recruit/activate suppressive myeloid programs and reshape the microenvironment — thereby blunting lymphoid efficacy. These are placed as secondary_modifiers (mechanistic mediators to test). 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected progression-free interval (PFI) because it captures treatment-modulated tumor control (sensitive to chemo/immune interactions), is available in TCGA clinical annotations, and is more proximate to docetaxel-related biology than OS. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded prostate-specific urinary biomarkers (PCA3) and general urinary biomarker concepts from the core hypothesis because they are not biologically or clinically transferable to the TCGA-BRCA/histology imaging context; Tier‑3 exploratory items (TNFα-R2, spermine) were omitted from both core and secondary lists to keep the hypothesis sharp?

## Working Hypothesis

Among breast tumors with high TIL density, high CENPA-driven CIN predicts shorter progression-free interval (PFI) after therapy compared with high TIL tumors with low CIN. This effect is mediated by CIN-induced recruitment and polarization of macrophages via the CSF1-OPN (SPP1) axis, which dampens antigen presentation and cytotoxic T cell activity, thereby reducing the clinical benefit of lymphocyte-rich tumors. Radiomics features reflecting immune/inflammatory patterns will modulate (interact with) this CIN-TIL relationship. Secondary modifiers (OPN/SPP1 and CSF1) act as mediators of this immunosuppressive microenvironment; in CIN-low contexts, high TIL density remains associated with prolonged PFI, whereas in CIN-high contexts, the TIL advantage is attenuated or reversed. The hypothesis remains testable using TCGA-BRCA data ( PF I as endpoint ), with validation in external, taxane-containing cohorts and complementary mechanistic in vitro/in vivo experiments to establish causality between CENPA-driven CIN, CSF1/OPN signaling, macrophage polarization, and T cell function.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
