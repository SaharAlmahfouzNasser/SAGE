# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In urothelial carcinoma, the therapeutic efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 blockade is determined by an integrated axis of (a) neoantigen burden driven by MSI/dMMR and high TMB, (b) the presence and organization of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) as captured by Immunoscore-like measures, and (c) secondary modifiers that influence antigen presentation, T cell recruitment/activation, and metabolic/epigenetic immune regulation. I propose that: (1) MSI/dMMR or high TMB tumors will only achieve meaningful ICI responses if accompanied by high TIL density and favorable immune architecture; (2) MSI/dMMR or high TMB tumors with low TIL density (immune-excluded/ccold tumors) will show attenuated or absent responses despite neoantigen richness; (3) secondary modifiers (bTMB, Immunoscore, PD-L1 expression, HDL-C/metabolic state, SDC2/TFPI2 methylation, and other immune-epigenetic markers) modulate this relationship by affecting chemokine signaling and antigen presentation or by promoting immune suppression, thereby shifting the probability of response up or down. The resulting predictive model is an integrated biomarker: Neoantigen Load (MSI/dMMR/TMB) × Immune Contexture (TIL density/Immunoscore) × Modifiers (PD-L1, bTMB, HDL-C, SDC2/TFPI2 methylation, etc.). Clinically, patients with high neoantigen load and favorable immune context are expected to have high response rates to ICIs; those with discordant signals may require combination strategies to recruit/activate T cells or reprogram the tumor epigenome/metabolism to convert non-responders into responders.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Retrospective multi-omic correlative analyses in bladder cancer cohorts treated with ICIs to test interactions between MSI/dMMR, TMB, Immunoscore, and secondary modifiers with response.

**Prediction 2:** Spatially resolved assays (multiplex IHC/IF, spatial transcriptomics) to quantify TIL density and organization relative to tumor nests in MSI/TMB-high tumors.

**Prediction 3:** Functional assays in organoid–immune co-cultures and patient-derived xenografts with manipulation of TIL recruitment signals and epigenetic/metabolic modifiers to assess changes in ICI responsiveness.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.
- Multi-modal validation pipeline: computational -> spatial -> functional, providing mechanistic depth beyond association.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.

## Alternative Directions

- bTMB: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- Immunoscore: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
