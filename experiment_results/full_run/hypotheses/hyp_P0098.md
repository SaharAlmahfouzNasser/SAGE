# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, the core question is why some Olaparib-treated, BRCA1/2-deficient mCRPC tumors with apparent favorable immune imaging (e.g., abundant intratumoral lymphoid organization) do not translate that immune presence into improved survival. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on Core Path 1 (imaging/pathomics link to in-situ immune infiltrate and outcome) and Core Path 2 (BRCA1/2 → PD-L1 → T-cell exhaustion → PTGER4 axis) because together they provide a coherent mechanistic chain connecting DNA-repair status, an immunosuppressive PGE2/PTGER4 program, and the functional competence of local immune structures. I chose BRCA1/2 (as the DNA-repair anchor) and PTGER4 (as the immunomodulatory anchor) to directly test what modulates the expected immune benefit. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: PSA nadir and urinary PCA3 offer early, clinical readouts of tumor response; systemic inflammation indices (EDIH/EDIP) and radiomics/pathomics features can corroborate immune–metabolic states and noninvasively map the immune architecture. These support testing whether PTGER4 modulates the link between imaging-detected lymphoid structures and survival. 4) Why OS: Overall survival is the most definitive clinical endpoint available in TCGA/clinical context and is the outcome most relevant for assessing whether immune-contexture confers real clinical benefit under Olaparib. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded PD-L1 from the core anchor (it remains a key mechanistic mediator and is placed among secondary modifiers) to respect the 1–2 anchor-gene limit and to keep the mechanistic hypothesis sharp (PTGER4 as the proximate immunosuppressive effector). Tier 3 exploratory items (CENPA, SPC, broad urinary biomarker category) were omitted from the hypothesis because their links are indirect and would dilute mechanistic focus?

## Working Hypothesis

In Olaparib-treated BRCA1/2-deficient metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), TLS-rich tumors will not necessarily exhibit an OS advantage if tumor-intrinsic PTGER4 (PGE2-EP4 signaling) is high, because PGE2-EP4 drives T-cell exhaustion and impairs TLS-mediated antitumor immunity (with PD-L1 upregulation acting downstream). Conversely, TLS-rich BRCA1/2-mutant tumors with low PTGER4 signaling—and thus reduced PGE2-EP4–driven suppression—will show an OS benefit, reflecting functional TLS activity. Blocking EP4 signaling or reversing exhaustion (e.g., EP4 antagonism alone or in combination with PD-1 blockade) should restore or augment the TLS-associated survival benefit. The mechanistic chain is: BRCA1/2 deficiency + PARP inhibition enhances DNA damage/neoantigen exposure and fosters TLS formation, but PGE2-EP4 signaling can suppress TLS effector function and promote exhaustion, thereby modulating OS. PD-L1 status serves as a secondary mediator of exhaustion without eclipsing the primary PTGER4–TLS interaction. Secondary modifiers (PSA nadir, PCA3, radiomics/pathomics features, and inflammatory indices like EDIH/EDIP) may refine early predictive readouts and correlate with TLS functionality and survival.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** PARP inhibitors in BRCA1/2-deficient tumors can increase DNA damage and immunogenic cues, potentially promoting TLS formation and local anti-tumor immunity;

**Prediction 2:** PGE2 through the EP4 receptor suppresses T-cell function and can induce an immunosuppressive microenvironment, dampening TLS-driven responses; and

**Prediction 3:** TLS presence alone has variable prognostic value depending on immune context, including PD-L1–mediated exhaustion.
