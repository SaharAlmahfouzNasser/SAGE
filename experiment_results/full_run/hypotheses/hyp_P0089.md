## Clinical Question

## Working Hypothesis

Among TLS-rich localized high-risk tumors, tumor-cell expression of PTGER4 and concurrent SOX2-driven neuroendocrine programs define biologically distinct subgroups with divergent overall survival (OS). Specifically, high PTGER4 signaling (PGE2–PTGER4 axis) in TLS-rich tumors promotes local T-cell dysfunction and functional impairment within TLS, attenuating TLS-mediated anti-tumor immunity. This effect is amplified by SOX2-associated neuroendocrine programs that reprogram the tumor immune microenvironment by reducing antigen presentation and suppressing TLS-supportive chemokine signaling. Consequently, TLS-high, PTGER4-high, and SOX2-high tumors will exhibit the poorest OS, whereas TLS-high tumors that are PTGER4-low and/or SOX2-low will retain TLS-associated survival benefits. Secondary modifiers (Decipher_GC, CENPA, EDIH, and urinary biomarkers) will correlate with PTGER4 activity and NE state, improving prognostic stratification within TLS-rich cohorts. This framework yields testable predictions: (1) within TLS-high tumors, PTGER4 expression and SOX2 co-segregate with worse OS; (2) spatial proximity of PTGER4-high tumor cells to TLS regions correlates with reduced T-cell activation/exhaustion marker patterns locally; (3) functional assays will show PTGER4 dependency for PGE2-mediated T-cell suppression, and (4) multi-omic signatures will associate with outcome across independent cohorts.
