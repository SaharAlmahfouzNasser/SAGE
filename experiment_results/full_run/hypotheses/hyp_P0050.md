# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

In HR+/HER2− breast cancer, why do stroma-rich (high TSR) tumors have higher recurrence risk despite similar clinicopathologic features? The key question is whether the histologic stromal abundance reflects an active, pro-survival extracellular matrix program that mechanistically promotes recurrence. 2) Tier 1 paths chosen: I focused on ECM/matrisome-driven mechanisms (NABA_CORE_MATRISOME genes) because TSR is an anatomy/matrix-derived metric; COL1A1 and FN1 are canonical fibrillar ECM components most directly tied to desmoplasia and altered cell–matrix signaling. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: hormone receptor signaling (ESR1/PGR) and proliferation (MKI67) can modulate tumor sensitivity to endocrine therapy and interact with ECM-mediated survival signals; additional matrisome members (COL3A1, LAMA2) plausibly reinforce a stiff, drug-impermeable stroma. 4) Why PFI as the endpoint: PFI (progression/recurrence interval) most closely maps to the clinical problem of recurrence/DFS described for HR+/HER2− disease and is available in TCGA; it tests the hypothesis that TSR-linked ECM programs shorten time to recurrence. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded immune-centric explanations (e.g., TIL density) and metabolic or rarer mutational modifiers to keep the mechanistic claim tightly focused on ECM-driven tumor resilience; I also limited anchors to two matrisome genes to satisfy the focusing rule and maintain a testable mechanism?

## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2− breast cancer, high stromal content (high TSR) reflects an activated ECM program with elevated FN1 and COL1A1 expression, creating a desmoplastic, stiff microenvironment that enhances integrin-β1/FAK signaling in tumor cells. This signaling promotes survival and reduces sensitivity to endocrine therapy, accelerating recurrence. Consequently, tumors that are TSR-high and exhibit high FN1 and COL1A1 expression will have shorter progression-free intervals (PFI) than TSR-low or FN1/COL1A1-low tumors, independent of ESR1, PGR, MKI67, and other standard covariates. Secondary matrisome modifiers (COL3A1, LAMA2) may amplify this effect, while ESR1/PGR status and tumor proliferation (MKI67) may modulate the magnitude of ECM-driven resistance. The hypothesis is testable by (a) demonstrating a TSR × FN1/COL1A1 interaction predicting shorter PFI in clinical datasets, and (b) showing that ECM-enhanced survival signals and reduced endocrine responsiveness can be recapitulated in ECM-mimetic in vitro models and reversed with inhibitors of integrin/FAK signaling.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** observational analyses using public datasets (e.g., TCGA, METABRIC) to test the TSR × FN1/COL1A1 interaction on PFI, adjusting for ESR1, PGR, MKI67 and other covariates;

**Prediction 2:** mediation/interaction analyses to assess the contribution of integrin/FAK pathway genes;

**Prediction 3:** in vitro/organotypic 3D models with ECM coatings enriched for FN1/COL1A1 to assess endocrine therapy response, matrix stiffness effects, and rescue with integrin-FAK inhibitors;


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions
