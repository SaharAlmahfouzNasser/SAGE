# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist, the key puzzle is why immune-structured tumors (TLS/TIL-rich) in NMIBC do not uniformly show favorable clinical trajectories — specifically, what tumor-intrinsic genomic features modulate whether local immune organization (TLS) restrains progression. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on MSH6 (mismatch-repair axis) and TMB (tumor mutational burden) because they provide a mechanistic route from DNA-repair status → neoantigen landscape → immune visibility, which directly interacts with TLS-driven local immunity. I prioritized these over CTCs for the mechanistic core because they explain immune effectiveness rather than simply reflecting dissemination. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: TGF-β (stromal remodeling/immune exclusion), E-FABP (metabolic reprogramming that can suppress immunity), and PTEN (growth signaling impacting immune microenvironment) are plausible modifiers that can exacerbate TLS failure; systemic/inflammatory markers (pZVR, GLU, urinary creatinine, microalbuminuria) and CTCs can act as complementary readouts of host/tumor state. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: PFI (progression-free interval) is the most problem-relevant clinical endpoint in NMIBC (primary concern is progression to muscle-invasive disease), and it directly reflects the clinical consequence of failed local immune control. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded making CTCs a primary anchor to keep the mechanistic hypothesis focused on antigenicity vs. local immune organization (CTCs are listed as secondary_modifiers). I also excluded exploratory elements (Gleason analogy, SARIFA as a core) because they are cross-domain and less directly tied to TLS–neoantigen mechanistic interactions?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates alone are insufficient to guarantee durable local tumor control. The efficacy of TLS depends on tumor-intrinsic genomic context, particularly MSH6-associated mismatch repair status and resultant neoantigen landscape. TLS-high tumors with MSH6 deficiency and high tumor mutational burden (TMB) are predicted to have elevated neoantigen load but increased subclonal heterogeneity, which impairs clonal antigen presentation and durable T cell–mediated tumor clearance, leading to shorter progression-free interval (PFI) compared with TLS-high, MSH6-intact tumors. Secondary modifiers—TGF-β–driven stromal remodeling, E-FABP–mediated metabolic suppression, and PTEN loss—can further attenuate TLS function by promoting immune exclusion or dampened effector activity. Systemic reads (pZVR, GLU, urinary creatinine, microalbuminuria) reflect host metabolic/inflammatory milieu that correlates with TLS competency and PFI. The working model predicts: (a) TLS-high/MSH6-deficient/high-TMB tumors have worse PFI than TLS-high/MSH6-intact tumors; (b) clonal vs subclonal neoantigen burden will modulate TLS effectiveness within TLS-high groups; (c) signatures of TGF-β activity, E-FABP expression, and PTEN pathway disruption will correlate with reduced TLS maturation and T cell infiltration; (d) non-invasive systemic markers will correlate with TLS status and PFI; (e) targeted interventions (e.g., TGF-β blockade, metabolic modulation, or PTEN pathway restoration) could restore TLS function and improve local control in a subset of tumors. This yields testable predictions that can be evaluated with integrated histology, genomics, transcriptomics, spatial profiling, and ex vivo functional assays.

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
