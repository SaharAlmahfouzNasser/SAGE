# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

PAM50-defined Luminal A tumors (transcriptomically ER-high, low-proliferation) are expected to be endocrine-sensitive, yet a subset still exhibits primary or early acquired resistance — what microenvironmental features convert an otherwise endocrine‑sensitive program into clinical nonresponse? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) Pretreatment transcriptome → PAM50 → Luminal A → endocrine therapy response (mechanistic backbone linking ESR1-driven hormone signaling and low proliferation to endocrine sensitivity); (b) Pretreatment transcriptome → proliferation gene signature (MKI67 et al.) → endocrine response (proliferation modulates sensitivity). I built on these because they directly explain why tumors classified as Luminal A may or may not respond. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: stromal/ECM programs (NABA_CORE_MATRISOME genes such as FN1, COL1A1), and growth‑factor paracrine signaling from stroma can sustain estrogen‑independent proliferation — these provide a plausible mechanism by which high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) blunts endocrine efficacy. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: the translational question is explicitly about endocrine benefit (prediction and mechanism of endocrine resistance), so a therapy_response endpoint directly tests the clinical consequence of the mechanistic interaction. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded immune-organizing features (TIL density, TLS) to keep focus on tumor–stroma interactions; I limited core molecular anchors to ESR1 and MKI67 to maintain the 2-gene maximum and relegated other proliferation and ECM genes (CCNB1, BIRC5, AURKA, FN1, COL1A1) to secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In HR+/HER2− breast cancers classified as PAM50 Luminal A (characterized by high ESR1 expression and low MKI67), a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) independently predicts reduced response to endocrine therapy. The proposed mechanism is that TSR-rich stroma enhances extracellular matrix and paracrine signaling (driven by ECM genes such as FN1 and COL1A1) that activate alternative pro-proliferative pathways, enabling estrogen-independent tumor cell growth despite ER-targeted therapy. ESR1-driven transcription remains active but is functionally overridden by stromal cues in TSR-high tumors, diminishing endocrine efficacy. MKI67 serves as a secondary modifier, where higher baseline proliferation within Luminal A tumors may amplify resistance in the TSR-high context. Clinically, Luminal A tumors with high TSR will show lower therapy_response rates than TSR-low counterparts, even after adjusting for ESR1 expression and MKI67 level. This hypothesis implies ESR1 and MKI67 are necessary but not sufficient determinants of response; the TSR context modulates therapeutic outcome through microenvironmental signaling that sustains proliferation independent of estrogen signaling.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
