# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Ontologist synthesis asks why CRPC tumors show persistent AR pathway activity and therapy resistance despite AR-targeted therapy and whether transcriptional/epigenetic reprogramming (full-length AR mRNA mutations, MGMT, KRT7P1 activation) underlies this. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) full-length AR mRNA alterations linked to enzalutamide resistance — chosen because direct AR-transcript alterations are a primary, mechanistic route to antiandrogen failure; (b) KRT7P1 epigenetic activation under PARP/epigenetic perturbation — chosen because epithelial transcriptional reprogramming can reflect cell-state shifts that sustain malignant programs. I built the core claim on these two because they provide a mechanistic route (transcript-level AR robustness + epigenetic epithelial state change) that can be modulated by the tumor microenvironment. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: STAT3/SOX2–driven transcriptional plasticity (supporting stemness and transcriptome remodeling) and transcriptome assembly/transfrag analyses indicate that therapy (Olaparib/cabazitaxel) can induce novel/altered transcripts and regulatory programs; these reinforce that microenvironmental cues could preserve aberrant full-length AR transcripts and activate keratin-related programs. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (enzalutamide response) because the strongest clinical problem highlighted by the Tier 1 paths is failure of AR-directed therapy driven by AR transcript alterations; therapy response is the most direct, actionable endpoint for guiding treatment selection. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded MGMT mRNA from the core clause to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (MGMT is more relevant to PARP/DDR context and would dilute focus on AR-driven enzalutamide resistance). I also excluded broader transcriptional assembly concepts (transfrag/Oases) from the core hypothesis and placed them in secondary_modifiers as supportive methodological/contextual evidence?

## Working Hypothesis

High stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) in castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) creates a paracrine/epigenetic niche that preserves AR signaling under enzalutamide by two non–mutually exclusive mechanisms: (1) stromal-derived cues enhance stability and processing of full-length AR mRNA, and (2) stromal-epigenetic signaling activates KRT7P1, facilitating regulatory networks that sustain the AR transcriptional program. Consequently, tumors with high TSR exhibit continued AR target gene activity and reduced response to enzalutamide, defining a clinically actionable resistant subset. This model predicts: (a) TSR-high tumors will have higher levels of full-length AR mRNA relative to AR variants and elevated KRT7P1 expression; (b) stromal signals (e.g., IL-6/STAT3 axis) will increase AR mRNA stability and KRT7P1 activation; (c) disrupting stromal signaling or KRT7P1 function will restore sensitivity to enzalutamide or synergize with AR blockade. Experimental approach to test this: (i) analyze patient datasets to correlate TSR proxies with full-length AR mRNA and KRT7P1, AR target gene signatures, and clinical enzalutamide response; (ii) in vitro co-culture of prostate cancer cells with CAFs or stromal-conditioned media to assess AR mRNA stability (actinomycin D chase), processing, and AR target gene expression under enzalutamide; (iii) manipulate KRT7P1 by CRISPRi/ASO to evaluate effects on AR signaling and enzalutamide sensitivity; (iv) use organoid and PDX models with high TSR to test combination strategies targeting stromal signaling (STAT3 inhibitors), epigenetic modulation (DZNep), or PARP inhibitors (Olaparib) to overcome resistance; (v) validate imaging-phenotype correlations using TSR measurements with RNA in situ hybridization for full-length AR and KRT7P1. Outcomes supporting the hypothesis would include linkage of high TSR with preserved AR signaling despite enzalutamide and attenuation of this resistance when KRT7P1 or stromal signals are inhibited.
