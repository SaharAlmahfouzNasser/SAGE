# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

High stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR), as a readout of TGF-β–driven fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix deposition, is associated with worse overall survival (OS) in breast cancer; this adverse effect is amplified when circulating HDL-C is low because reduced HDL-C fails to counteract TGF-β–mediated fibrotic and immunosuppressive signaling, promoting an immune-excluded microenvironment. Consequently, patients with high TSR and low HDL-C will have the poorest OS, identifying a subgroup that may benefit from anti–TGF-β or lipid-modulating interventions and can be stratified using AI-enabled biomarker signatures alongside the Tier 1 anchors.


| Element | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Imaging feature | Stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) |
| Anchor gene(s) | TGF-β, HDL-C |
| Primary endpoint | OS |


# 2. Why This Gene — Not Others

## Primary hypothesis gene(s)

**TGF-β:** TGF-β (fibrogenic/stroma) path because it mechanistically explains dense, immune-excluding stroma, and on the HDL-C (systemic lipid/metabolic) path because systemic lipid status can modulate inflammation, ECM remodeling, and immune function—together these provide a plausible interaction.

**HDL-C:** HDL-C), and computational/accessible biomarkers—suggesting that stromal-rich tumors do not always confer the same prognosis; the key question is what molecular conditions determine when high stromal content is prognostically adverse.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** retrospective analysis in TCGA-BRCA and external cohorts to assess TSR × HDL-C interaction on OS using multivariate Cox models with robust adjustment for confounders;

**Prediction 2:** mediation analyses to evaluate whether TGF-β pathway activation and ECM gene signatures mediate the TSR–OS relationship;

**Prediction 3:** immune-infiltration profiling (e.g., CIBERSORT/xCell) to link TSR/HDL-C with immune contexture;


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.

## Alternative Directions

- pZVR: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- PTGDS: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
