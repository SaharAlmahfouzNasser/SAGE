# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some localized prostate cancers with adverse histology (cribriform architecture) not progress unless they also activate specific transcriptomic metastatic programs, and conversely why some high-risk transcriptomic tumors do not rapidly progress unless accompanied by invasive morphologies? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) Cribriform architecture → morphological axis of collective invasion/structural routes for dissemination; (b) Decipher score → transcriptomic programs that predict metastatic competency. I built the hypothesis on the mechanistic intersection of these two paths (morphology enabling dissemination × transcriptional metastatic potential). 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: published associations of cribriform pattern with worse outcomes and with invasive features; validation of Decipher as a predictor of metastasis/recurrence; biological plausibility that morphologic invasion and cell-intrinsic metastatic programs synergize. 4) Endpoint selection: PFI (progression-free interval) maps best to the clinical outcomes of interest here—biochemical recurrence and metastasis—and is sensitive to earlier clinical events that the biomarker aims to predict. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broader clinical covariates (PSA, treatment modalities) and multiple additional genomic modifiers from the core hypothesis to keep focus on the mechanistic interaction between morphology and transcriptomic metastatic programs; those factors are listed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up analysis?

## Working Hypothesis

Cribriform architecture creates a dominant collective invasion axis that enables tumor cells to disseminate along defined morphological routes. High Decipher transcriptional programs encode metastatic competence, including motility, invasion, and extracellular matrix remodeling. I hypothesize a synergistic interaction: cribriform architecture is necessary to realize the full metastatic potential of tumors with high Decipher scores, such that tumors positive for cribriform pattern and with high Decipher scores exhibit a greater risk of progression to metastasis and shorter progression-free interval (PFI) than would be predicted by either feature alone. In non-cribriform tumors, even a high Decipher score may have limited impact on progression due to the absence of coherent invasion routes. The mechanistic basis for this interaction likely involves spatial proximity of Decipher-driven invasion programs to cribriform invasion fronts, enabling efficient cell shedding and colonization along collective migratory paths. This interaction should be detectable as a statistically significant Cribriform × Decipher interaction predicting PFI after adjusting for standard clinical covariates, and should be supported by (a) spatial transcriptomic mapping showing enrichment of Decipher-program genes at cribriform invasion fronts, (b) functional readouts in model systems where cribriform-like architecture and Decipher-encoded programs are concomitantly manipulated to alter invasion and metastatic potential. Collectively, these data would support a combined histology-transcriptomic biomarker that identifies patients at highest risk who may benefit from treatment intensification or intensified surveillance, beyond what either cribriform status or Decipher score provides alone. Specific testable predictions: (1) In cribriform-positive tumors, high Decipher score confers a significantly shorter PFI than cribriform-positive tumors with low Decipher; (2) In non-cribriform tumors, Decipher score stratifies risk to a lesser extent; (3) There is a statistically significant interaction term between cribriform status and Decipher score in Cox models predicting PFI; (4) Spatial transcriptomics demonstrates co-localization of Decipher-related transcripts with invasion-front markers specifically in cribriform tumors; (5) In organoid/animal models, inducing cribriform-like architecture together with high Decipher gene expression results in greater invasion and metastatic spread than either manipulation alone, while disrupting one component mitigates dissemination despite the other.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
