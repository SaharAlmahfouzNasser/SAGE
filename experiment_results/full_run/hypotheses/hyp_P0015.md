# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC treated with cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy, a higher pretreatment tumor-stroma ratio (TSR) measured on H&E sections is causally associated with reduced therapeutic efficacy, due to ECM/stromal density creating diffusion barriers and stromal fibroblasts sequestering cisplatin, resulting in lower intratumoral cisplatin exposure and diminished tumor cell kill. This effect is most evident when TSR is assessed at the invasive front and is modulated by additional morphological features such as tumor budding and lymphocyte density, which reflect microenvironmental dynamics and invasion. The hypothesis predicts: (i) higher pretreatment TSR will be associated with lower rates of favorable therapy response (e.g., downstaging or pathologic complete response) after standardized cisplatin-based neoadjuvant therapy, independent of clinical covariates; (ii) ex vivo models with higher ECM density or fibroblast-rich stroma will show reduced cisplatin penetration and greater chemoresistance; (iii) quantitative shifts in TSR coupled with the proposed modifiers will improve the predictive performance beyond TSR alone in independent cohorts. No molecular/genomic markers or multi-gene signatures are required for this imaging-centered mechanism.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** retrospective and prospective digital pathology analyses to test the TSR–response association,

**Prediction 2:** validation across independent cohorts with standardized TSR measurement (invasive-front emphasis), and

**Prediction 3:** mechanistic ex vivo/in vitro diffusion studies using ECM-rich and fibroblast-containing models to demonstrate reduced cisplatin diffusion and efficacy under high stromal conditions. This plan intentionally excludes molecular markers to maintain a morphology-centric, mechanistic framework and to avoid overfitting in single-path models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.

## Alternative Directions

- tumor budding / single-cell invasion at invasive front: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- lymphocyte density (intratumoral and peritumoral): Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
