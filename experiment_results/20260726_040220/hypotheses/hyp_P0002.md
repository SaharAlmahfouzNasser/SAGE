## Working Hypothesis

Among muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) tumors, MSH6 deficiency (a driver of dMMR) yields high neoantigen load but does not guarantee PD-1/PD-L1 blockade responsiveness unless the tumor microenvironment supports local antigen presentation and T cell priming via TLS-like lymphoid aggregates. Specifically, TLS-positive, MSH6-deficient tumors will exhibit higher objective response rates (ORR), longer progression-free survival (PFS), and greater Immunoscore (CD3/CD8 densities) compared with TLS-negative, MSH6-deficient tumors; PD-L1 expression will enhance this effect. In tumors with intact MSH6, TLS status will still stratify response, confirming TLS as a critical determinant beyond MMR status. PD-L1 positivity plus TLS presence will yield the strongest responses, while TLS-poor, genomically hot tumors will be prone to resistance, suggesting the need for combination strategies to induce or compensate for immune organization. The TLS axis will correlate with six core biomarkers: MSI/dMMR status, tumor mutational burden (TMB), CD3/CD8 Immunoscore, urine DNA methylation markers, AI-derived imaging/omics signatures, and TLS presence on histology/imaging, and non-invasively predicted TLS status via urine methylation and AI features will enable pre-treatment stratification. This hypothesis is testable in prospective multi-omic studies of MIBC patients treated with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, with TLS status serving as a mediator/modifier of therapy response and as a guide for combination therapies in TLS-poor cases.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** MSH6 loss contributes to MMR deficiency and elevates neoantigen burden, but effective anti-tumor immunity requires functional local immune architecture;

**Prediction 2:** TLSs act as ectopic lymphoid organs within tumors, enabling in situ antigen presentation, B and T cell priming, and have been linked to better responses to checkpoint blockade across cancer types, including bladder cancer;

**Prediction 3:** PD-L1 expression alone is an imperfect predictor of response. Integrating TLS status with MMR-linked markers (MSH6), TMB, and immune contexture (CD3/CD8 Immunoscore) improves predictive power.
