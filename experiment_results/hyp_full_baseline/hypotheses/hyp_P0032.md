# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Noninvasive tumor-derived readouts (urine DNA methylation panels) detect bladder tumors but variably predict clinical course — why do some methylation-positive tumors nonetheless progress while others do not? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on CORE PATH 1 (selected urine-DNA methylation biomarkers) as the tumor-derived, noninvasive molecular readout, and CORE PATH 3 (TGF-β) as a microenvironmental regulator that can reshape immune architecture and matrix remodeling. These two directly suggest a mechanism linking tumor epigenetic signals, local immune organization, and clinical outcome. 3) Tier 2 support: metabolic/urinary markers (GLU, creatinine, microalbuminuria) and HDL-C could modulate tumor shedding or host clearance of tumor DNA; Immunoscore/immune-contexture evidence supports the importance of TLS/TIL architecture; AI-enabled integration supports combining imaging and urine signals to improve performance. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected progression-free interval (PFI) because the clinical need for noninvasive surveillance is focused on detecting/anticipating progression/recurrence, and PFI best captures surveillance-relevant prognostic performance. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded making CTCs a core variable (moved to secondary_modifiers) to obey the 2-anchor limit and keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the interaction between TGF-β–driven microenvironmental remodeling and urine methylation signal interpretability; exploratory cross-disease markers (CSF AD biomarkers, PSA, pZVR) were excluded as they lack direct mechanistic linkage to bladder tumor microenvironment in this triage?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, TGFB1 signaling drives fibrotic remodeling that leads to immune exclusion and disruption of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates. This microenvironmental change modulates tumor DNA shedding and the interpretability of urine-DNA methylation signatures. Consequently, tumors with high TGFB1 activity and disrupted TLS architecture will show altered or less prognostic urine methylation signals and shorter progression-free intervals (PFI) compared with tumors exhibiting organized TLS and lower TGFB1 activity, where urine methylation more accurately reflects prognosis. A combined model incorporating (i) TLS status/organization as observed by histology, (ii) TGFB1 activity (TGFB1 expression and downstream pSMAD2/3 signaling), and (iii) urine-DNA methylation signature will outperform urine methylation alone in predicting PFI in bladder cancer. This will be tested in a prospective patient cohort with matched tumor and urine samples, followed by an independent validation cohort, and complemented by mechanistic in vitro work showing TGFB1-induced fibrosis disrupts TLS formation and alters methylation shedding.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Gene expression does not equal protein activity; phospho-protein or functional assays are needed for confirmation.
