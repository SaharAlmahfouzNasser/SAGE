## Clinical Question

Tumor budding is observed at the invasive front and is associated with progression in NMIBC, but it is unclear why some budding-rich tumors progress rapidly while others remain non‑muscle invasive. ## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, high density of tumor buds at the invasive front, quantified on diagnostic H&E sections, independently predicts shorter progression-free interval due to EMT-driven dissemination that facilitates breach of stromal containment and local invasion. This effect is modulated by the tumor–stroma ratio and the peri-bud immune microenvironment, such that BuddingHigh tumors with permissive stroma and/or limited peri-bud cytotoxic immune infiltration exhibit the greatest risk of progression. The association remains after adjusting for standard clinicopathologic factors and can be validated in an independent cohort. Mechanistically, buds co-localize with EMT markers, indicating EMT activation within buds, and occur in a microenvironment conducive to invasion, supporting a causal link between budding and progression in NMIBC.
