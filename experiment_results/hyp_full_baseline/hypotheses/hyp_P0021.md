# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

TLS-rich immune organization is often associated with better anti-tumor immunity, yet many NMIBC tumors that display lymphoid aggregates still fail intravesical BCG therapy—why does organized local immunity not always translate into durable therapy response? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the PDCD1 (PD-1/PD-L1) path because BCG can induce PD-L1 and thereby engage T cell inhibitory signaling, and on the VEGFA/BMAL1 angiogenesis path because VEGF-driven vascular remodeling is a well-characterized mechanism that creates immune-excluding and immunosuppressive microenvironments; together these provide a plausible mechanism linking TLS function to therapy failure. 3) Tier 2 support: EP300, HCFC2 and TNPO3 (circRNA) supply epigenetic and post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms that could modulate PD-L1/PDCD1 and VEGFA programs; AURKA and TP53/KRAS context suggest cell-cycle and oncogenic pressures that may further blunt TLS efficacy. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: BCG response is the most clinically actionable, mechanistically linked outcome in NMIBC (the PD-L1 induction path centers on BCG), and is more sensitive than OS for testing microenvironment-driven mechanisms in this setting. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded using KRAS as a core anchor to keep the hypothesis mechanistically focused on immune–vascular crosstalk; other Tier 2/Tier 3 genes and broad signatures (79-gene, stem-like, micropapillary) were relegated to secondary_modifiers to avoid overcomplicating the core causal question?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In NMIBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are not universally associated with favorable BCG response; in TLS-positive tumors with high VEGFA expression (VEGFAhigh), VEGF-driven vascular remodeling creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment that promotes PD-1/PD-L1–mediated T cell dysfunction within the TLS, leading to reduced BCG efficacy. This results in a distinct non-responder subset defined by TLS presence, VEGFAhigh status, and elevated PDCD1/PD-L1 signaling on intratumoral immune cells. Predictions: (1) TLS+VEGFAhigh tumors will exhibit increased PDCD1 expression on CD8+ T cells and other exhausted/exhausted-like states within TLS, reduced effector function (lower granzyme B/IFN-γ), and diminished BCG response compared with TLS+VEGFAlow or TLS− tumors. (2) Spatial analyses will show immature or dysfunctional TLS architecture with abnormal vasculature (abnormal vessel density, pericyte detachment) and immune exclusion in TLS+VEGFAhigh tumors. (3) Epigenetic/post-transcriptional regulators (EP300, HCFC2, TNPO3) will modulate PD-L1/PDCD1 axis and VEGFA signaling, such that perturbations elevating EP300/HCFC2/TNPO3 activity correlate with higher PD-L1 and VEGFA expression and poorer BCG response. (4) Oncogenic context (AURKA, TP53, KRAS) will modulate TLS maturation and sensitivity to vascular/ checkpoint perturbations, with AURKA amplification and TP53 loss associated with more suppressive TLS microenvironments. (5) Therapeutically, combining intravesical BCG with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade and anti-angiogenic modulation will restore TLS integrity, enhance CD8+ T cell activity within TLS, and improve therapy response in TLS+VEGFAhigh tumors. Biomarker signature for prediction: TLS maturity status (presence of germinal centers, follicular dendritic cell networks), VEGFA expression level, and PDCD1/PD-L1 axis activity on intratumoral immune cells.
