## Working Hypothesis

Preexisting, high-density TLS-like aggregates located at the tumor–stroma boundary, particularly when organized as compact, germinal center–like structures and in direct or near-direct contact with invasive tumor nests, prime local antigen presentation and lymphocyte activation such that intravesical BCG elicits robust anti-tumor immunity. Pretreatment boundary TLS density, quantified as structures per square millimeter within a defined boundary zone, will predict clinical response to BCG. This predictive value will be strengthened by: (a) TLS organization and size (more mature, larger TLS with germinal center features showing greater predictive power), (b) spatial proximity of TLS to tumor nests (closer TLS associated with higher response rates), and (c) the combined effect with overall TIL density. In multivariable analyses adjusting for standard clinicopathologic factors (stage, grade, tumor burden) and non-boundary TLS metrics, boundary TLS density and organization will remain independently associated with therapy response, supporting an H&E‑based, clinically translatable biomarker for patient stratification in BCG therapy.
