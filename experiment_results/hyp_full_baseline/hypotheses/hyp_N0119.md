# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma treated with pembrolizumab, high intratumoral TIL density predicts higher objective response rates only in TP53 wild-type tumors; TP53-mutant tumors do not show this association. This implies a TP53-dependent mechanism linking immune infiltration to PD-1 blockade efficacy, potentially via TP53-regulated immunogenic cell death and antigen presentation pathways. Secondary modifiers such as KRAS and other urothelial-associated cell-death pathway genes may modulate the magnitude of this interaction by further shaping tumor immunogenicity and apoptotic responses. The endpoint for testing is therapy_response (objective response rate). This hypothesis supports identifying a TIL-density + TP53-status biomarker subset to select patients for PD-1 blockade and to inform combination strategies in TP53-mutant cases.


| Element | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Imaging feature | Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte (TIL) density |
| Anchor gene(s) | TP53 |
| Primary endpoint | therapy_response |

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Clinical evidence shows variable pembrolizumab responses in MIBC, with TIL density as a known correlate of immunotherapy efficacy but not uniformly predictive.

**Prediction 2:** TP53 status influences genomic instability, immunogenic cell death, and antigen presentation, providing a plausible mechanism by which TP53 wild-type tumors could translate TIL presence into effective PD-1 blockade–mediated cytotoxicity, whereas TP53-mutant tumors may dampen this process.

**Prediction 3:** Testing this interaction in existing clinical datasets is feasible and would yield a clinically actionable biomarker pair (TIL density plus TP53 status) to refine patient selection and inform combinatorial approaches for TP53-mutant tumors. Covariates such as PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden can be considered in sensitivity analyses but are not central to the core hypothesis.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Caveats & Risks
