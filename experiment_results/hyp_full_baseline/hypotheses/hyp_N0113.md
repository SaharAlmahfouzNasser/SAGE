# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In TCGA-BRCA, TLS-high tumors that overexpress SLC4A1 do not exhibit the expected OS benefit because SLC4A1-driven pH/metabolic remodeling reprograms CXCL13 signaling and TLS composition toward dysfunctional, non-productive immune niches. This tumor-intrinsic modifier alters TLS quality (architecture, B cell/Tfh organization, and chemokine milieu) via downstream cross-talk with PI3K/AKT, MAPK, and TGF-β pathways, thereby diminishing TLS-mediated anti-tumor immunity. Testable predictions: (A) There is a significant interaction between TLS status and SLC4A1 expression on OS in TCGA-BRCA, such that TLS-high/SLC4A1-high tumors have attenuated or no OS advantage compared with TLS-high/SLC4A1-low tumors. (B) In TLS-high tumors, high SLC4A1 correlates with reduced CXCL13 activity and altered TLS architecture (e.g., fragmented or less mature B cell–rich zones, disrupted Tfh networks) relative to TLS-high/SLC4A1-low tumors. (C) Blocking SLC4A1-related pH modulation or CXCL13-CXCR5 signaling, or inhibiting downstream PI3K/MAPK/TGF-β cross-talk, restores TLS functionality and enhances anti-tumor immune responses in preclinical models. (D) The observed effect remains after adjusting for treatment modalities (ICB, RT) and is modulated by Wnt/β-catenin signaling context. Consequently, SLC4A1 serves as a tumor-intrinsic modifier of TLS prognostic value and a potential biomarker/therapeutic target to restore TLS efficacy.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** orchestrates TLS organization. It is plausible that SLC4A1-driven metabolic remodeling reprograms CXCL13 signaling and subsequent TLS architecture via cross-talk with PI3K/AKT, MAPK, and TGF-β pathways, and that this reprogramming is detectable in TCGA-BRCA data.

**Prediction 2:** to stratify TLS benefit, and suggest combinatorial strategies (pH/metabolism modulation, CXCL13 axis targeting, and pathway inhibitors) to restore TLS efficacy.

**Prediction 3:** while allowing mechanistic expansion via Tier 2 pathways (PI3K, MAPK, TGF-β, Wnt) to contextualize potential therapeutic interventions.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
