# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist's synthesis highlights an inconsistency: tumors with organized local immune structures (TLS-like lymphoid aggregates) and high apparent antigenicity (high TMB/neoantigen load) do not uniformly benefit from immune checkpoint–based therapies. ## Working Hypothesis

TLS-rich tumors that simultaneously harbor high tumor mutational burden (TMB) and high FerrScore exhibit impaired antitumor immunity and reduced objective response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade compared with TLS-rich tumors that have high TMB but low FerrScore. This impairment arises because iron/ferroptosis-associated metabolic programs in tumor cells dampen antigen processing/presentation and suppress effector T-cell function within TLS niches, despite organized immune architecture. Consequently, FerrScore-high, TLS-high, TMB-high tumors are less likely to achieve durable responses to PD-1/PD-L1 therapy unless co-therapies targeting iron metabolism/ferroptosis and/or vascular/epigenetic modulators are applied. The hypothesis implies three testable tiers: (A) within TLS-high and TMB-high tumors, FerrScore stratifies response; (B) mechanistic assays show reduced MHC-I antigen presentation and CD8+/T cell effector activity in FerrScore-high contexts; (C) combination strategies that mitigate ferroptosis-linked suppression or normalize the vasculature or epigenetically enhance antigen presentation can restore checkpoint blockade responsiveness in FerrScore-high cases.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TLS presence correlates with antigen exposure yet does not guarantee responsiveness to checkpoint blockade in some contexts, indicating additional suppressive drivers within the microenvironment;

**Prediction 2:** high TMB/neoantigen load provides targets but may be undermined by metabolic states that impair antigen presentation and T-cell function;

**Prediction 3:** iron metabolism and ferroptosis modulate tumor immunogenicity and immune cell activity, influence MHC-I expression, and shape the inflammatory milieu.
