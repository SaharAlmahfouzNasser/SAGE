# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

High intratumoral TIL density and high mutational/neoantigen load should predict immunotherapy benefit, yet many TIL-high tumors fail to respond — why is immune infiltration sometimes ineffective? 2) I build on two mechanistic branches in the single path: (a) the IL-6→STAT3→STAM2 S375 O-GlcNAcylation axis that stabilizes STAM2 and drives proliferation/lymph node metastasis (mechanism that could sustain immune evasion despite TILs), and (b) the association of SRD5A3 with TMB and overall survival (a modifier of tumor immunogenicity). These together suggest a collision between cell-intrinsic pro‑growth/immune‑suppressive signaling and mutational immunogenicity. 3) Supporting evidence (Tier 2-like) in the path: MSI/TMB→neoantigens→immunotherapy response establishes why TIL/TMB should predict response, and SRD5A3’s link to TMB/OS provides a plausible molecular moderator. IL-6/STAT3 supports the mechanistic route to STAM2 stabilization. 4) I chose therapy_response (immunotherapy response proxy) as the endpoint because the path centers on MSI/TMB-driven immunogenicity and clinical sensitivity to immune checkpoint blockade; this is the most directly relevant, actionable outcome. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded naming IL6/STAT3 in the hypothesis as primary anchors to keep the core focused (they are in secondary_modifiers as mechanistic mediators). MSI, neoantigens, and TMB are placed in secondary_modifiers rather than the core to avoid overloading the primary claim and to keep the hypothesis testable with 1 imaging feature + 2 anchors?

## Working Hypothesis

In tumors with high TIL density, tumor-intrinsic IL‑6/STAT3 signaling promotes STAM2 S375 O‑GlcNAcylation, which stabilizes STAM2 and enhances endosomal sorting pathways that support tumor cell proliferation and immune‑evasive programs. This STAM2 stabilization diminishes the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB). The magnitude of this effect is modulated by SRD5A3-associated differences in tumor mutational burden (TMB) and neoantigen landscape. Specifically, among TIL‑high tumors, those with high STAM2 S375 O‑GlcNAcylation and high SRD5A3-associated TMB have reduced objective response rates to ICB compared with TIL‑high tumors lacking this axis. The hypothesis implies three testable predictions: (1) STAM2 S375 O‑GlcNAcylation correlates with STAM2 protein stability and with proliferation/immune‑evading gene signatures in vitro and in vivo; (2) in patient cohorts treated with ICB, among TIL‑high tumors, higher STAM2 S375 O‑GlcNAcylation (and SRD5A3‑linked high TMB) predicts poorer response; (3) pharmacologic or genetic disruption of STAM2 S375 O‑GlcNAcylation (or IL‑6/STAT3 signaling) enhances ICB responsiveness, particularly in SRD5A3‑high/TMB‑high contexts. Secondary variables (MSI, neoantigens) inform the degree of immunogenicity, but the primary focus remains the STAM2 S375 O‑GlcNAcylation axis as the intrinsic mediator of response in TIL‑high tumors.
