# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

DDR-deficient NMIBC tumors (low ERCC-family/HR activity) should be more sensitive to cisplatin, yet some DDR-low tumors fail to achieve pathologic response. What tumor-extrinsic features modulate or blunt intrinsic DDR-related chemosensitivity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: ERCC2 (well-established cisplatin-sensitizing lesion in bladder cancer) and ERCC1 (NER mediator linked to platinum resistance). I built on these because they form the core mechanistic axis tying DNA-repair capacity to cisplatin cytotoxicity. 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: HR genes (BRCA1/2, RAD51), broader NER/DSB sensors (ATM, CHEK1, ERCC4/5), and immune-context markers (CD274, CD8A) can modify tumor cell sensitivity or microenvironmental elimination; importantly, stromal barriers (TSR) are mechanistically plausible modulators of drug delivery and pro-survival signaling. 4) Endpoint choice: therapy_response (pathologic response / downstaging after cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy) is the direct clinical question the IMED score is designed to predict and is the most actionable endpoint for treatment selection. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded TIL/TLS as core imaging features to preserve focus on a delivery/barrier hypothesis (TSR chosen instead); I limited anchor genes to ERCC2 and ERCC1 to remain mechanistically sharp and put other DDR/immune genes into secondary_modifiers for exploratory adjustment and model refinement?

## Working Hypothesis

In DDR-deficient NMIBC, low ERCC2 and low ERCC1 expression increase cisplatin sensitivity, but this sensitivity is attenuated in tumors with a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) due to reduced intratumoral drug delivery and stromal-derived pro-survival signaling; clinically, patients with low ERCC2/ERCC1 and low TSR will have higher therapy_response to cisplatin-based neoadjuvant therapy, whereas patients with low ERCC2/ERCC1 but high TSR will exhibit diminished responses, indicating a significant ERCC1/ERCC2 × TSR interaction that predicts cisplatin outcomes.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** and a single imaging feature (TSR) creates a focused mechanistic axis (DDR deficiency plus delivery barrier) to test a clear interaction effect on a clinically meaningful endpoint (therapy_response). Secondary modifiers (Tier 2 DDR/immune genes) are acknowledged as potential refiners of the effect size and direction but are not required for the primary hypothesis.

**Prediction 2:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
