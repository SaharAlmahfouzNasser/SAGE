## Working Hypothesis

In urothelial carcinoma, the ability of mTOR inhibition to convert autophagy into tumor-cell death depends on TP53 functional status and the tumor immune context, such that TIL-high tumors with wild-type TP53 are more likely to undergo autophagy-dependent cell death upon mTOR blockade, whereas TP53-mutant tumors or tumors with low TIL density favor cytoprotective autophagy. Clinically, TP53-wildtype, TIL-high urothelial carcinomas are predicted to show higher therapy_response to mTOR inhibitors than TP53-mutant or TIL-low tumors, with imaging-detected TIL density correlating with autophagy markers and treatment response as mechanistic readouts.
