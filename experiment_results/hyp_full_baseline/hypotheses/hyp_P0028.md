# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Diverse non-invasive biomarker modalities (stromal remodeling/TGF-β, urine DNA methylation, and CTCs) each signal important aspects of bladder tumor biology, but it is unclear how stromal remodeling specifically modulates tumor cell dissemination and thereby the prognostic value of liquid/epigenetic biomarkers — i.e., why some tumors with detectable tumor-derived signals progress rapidly while others do not. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the TGFB1/TGF-β signaling path because it provides a clear mechanistic link to stromal remodeling and fibrosis, and on the CTC/liquid-biopsy path because CTC burden is the proximal mechanism for metastatic dissemination; these together explain how microenvironmental state could drive progression. I selected TSR (stromal-to-tumor ratio) as the imaging feature because it is an H&E-derivable readout that directly reflects TGF-β–mediated stromal remodeling and is measurable in archived pathology. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: urine-DNA methylation signatures and GREM1 support tumor-origin detection and stromal-tumor crosstalk respectively and can act as orthogonal readouts of shedding/EMT; Immunoscore/immune-context and HDL-C/metabolic state are plausible modifiers of dissemination and progression but are supportive rather than central. 4) Endpoint rationale: Progression-free interval (PFI) was chosen as the single primary endpoint because the mechanistic chain (TGF-β → stromal fibrosis/EMT → increased CTCs) specifically predicts earlier recurrence/progression (PFI) and is more directly testable with CTC and urine methylation dynamics than long-term OS. 5) Deliberate exclusions and why: I excluded putting urine-DNA methylation signature into the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic focus tight (stroma → dissemination); urine methylation, GREM1, Immunoscore, HDL-C, MSI/AI, and radiomic correlates are listed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses rather than being crammed into the primary mechanistic claim?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) detected in diagnostic tissue indicates a TGFB1-driven fibrotic microenvironment that promotes epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and increases circulating tumor cell (CTC) burden. Consequently, tumors with high TSR and elevated TGFB1-associated CTCs will exhibit shorter progression-free intervals (PFI) than tumors with low TSR and/or lower CTC burden; integrating TSR with TGFB1 signaling and CTC measures will provide superior early risk stratification for progression compared with any single biomarker.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** retrospective analyses in bladder cancer cohorts with matched pathology (for TSR) and liquid biopsy data (for CTC/CTC-related signals) plus TGFB1 pathway activity from transcriptomics to test for additive and interaction effects on PFI;

**Prediction 2:** mechanistic in vitro studies using bladder cancer cells co-cultured with TGFB1-activated fibroblasts to assess EMT markers and invasive behavior, with TGFB1 inhibition as a control;

**Prediction 3:** in vivo validation in orthotopic bladder cancer models evaluating stromal fibrosis, EMT markers, CTCs, and time to progression. Potential confounders (stage, treatment, smoking, age) will be controlled.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
