# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist frames a core question—why RNA-based regulatory programs (m6A-modified lncRNAs and other noncoding RNAs) and tumor metabolic state (one‑carbon/mitochondrial metabolism) together determine whether local immune organization leads to effective anti-tumor immunity and therapy benefit in MIBC (e.g., BCG). In other words: why do some tumors with robust local immune structures (TLS/TILs) still fail to respond to immune-based therapy? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built the hypothesis from the SHMT2 perturbation path (one‑carbon/mitochondrial metabolism) and the m6A-related lncRNA (AL161431.1) path because they provide a clear, testable mechanism linking tumor metabolic flux to epitranscriptomic stabilization of oncogenic lncRNA programs that could suppress effective anti-tumor immunity. I considered DDIT4 important (links immune stimulation to stress/mTOR), but kept it out of the core pair to preserve a focused two‑feature mechanistic claim. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: PTEN/mTOR axis (PTEN) and stress-response signaling (DDIT4) intersect with metabolic control and immune signaling, providing plausible modulatory nodes; MAFG‑AS1, circMMP9 and HRG1 indicate complementary RNA regulatory and redox/metabolic routes that could modify immune/therapy outcomes. These Tier 2 nodes justify biological plausibility and provide candidate covariates for follow-up stratified analyses. 4) Why I chose therapy_response as the primary endpoint: The Ontologist explicitly linked DDIT4 to BCG and HRG1 to cisplatin effects, and the central puzzle concerns why immune activation (BCG/TLS) does or does not translate to clinical benefit—thus a therapy response proxy (BCG responsiveness) is the most directly relevant, actionable clinical endpoint for translational follow-up. 5) What I deliberately excluded and why: I excluded DDIT4 from the 1–2 anchor genes to keep the core hypothesis tightly focused on metabolic (SHMT2) and epitranscriptomic (AL161431.1) drivers of immune evasion; DDIT4 and other Tier 2 genes were moved to secondary_modifiers to be tested as effect modifiers in downstream analyses. ## Working Hypothesis

In muscle invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) treated with intravesical BCG, tumors that exhibit TLS-like lymphoid aggregates and harbor high activity of mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism (high SHMT2) together with high expression of the m6A-stabilized oncogenic lncRNA AL161431.1 form a distinct immunosuppressive/metabolically reprogrammed phenotype. This AL161431.1↑/SHMT2↑ state, supported by TLS-like organization, blunts antigen presentation and effector T cell function, leading to reduced and less durable BCG responses. Clinically, patients with TLS-high AND SHMT2-high AND AL161431.1-high tumors will have poorer therapy_response to BCG compared with TLS-high tumors lacking this metabolic–epitranscriptomic signature. DDIT4 and other Tier2 modifiers (PTEN, MAFG-AS1, HRG1, circMMP9) may modulate the magnitude of this effect and serve as covariates or co-targets in combination strategies.
