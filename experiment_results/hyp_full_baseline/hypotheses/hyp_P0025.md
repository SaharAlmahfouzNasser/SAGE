# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do tumors with apparent organized local immunity (TLS/TLS-like aggregates) sometimes fail to translate that immune presence into clinical benefit—specifically, why do some TLS-rich bladder cancers not respond to checkpoint blockade despite high mutational loads? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSH6 (MMR) path because MSH6 status mechanistically drives MSI-like hypermutation, and on the TMB path because TMB is the proximate surrogate of neoantigen load; these together frame how mutation source and architecture influence immune visibility. I deliberately did not include Immunoscore as a main anchor to keep the testable mechanism tight (it remains a key readout). 3) Tier 2 support: MSI/MMR context, eIF4E (translation control), HDL-C (metabolic/ inflammatory modulation), and liquid biopsy (CTCs) support mechanisms that can modulate antigen presentation, neoantigen quality, or systemic immune tone and provide orthogonal ways to validate the primary mechanism. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected therapy_response (immune checkpoint inhibitor response) because MSH6-driven mutational processes and TMB most directly inform immunotherapy sensitivity; this endpoint yields the clearest mechanistic test. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broad prognostic endpoints (OS/PFI) to avoid confounding by non-immune factors and omitted many exploratory/tertiary paths (pZVR, CSF-AD, SARIFA) because they lack direct mechanistic linkage to MSH6→TMB→immune organization in bladder cancer?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates predict improved therapy response primarily when the tumor mutational burden reflects predominantly clonal neoantigens. MSH6 deficiency tends to drive hypermutation with a higher proportion of subclonal neoantigens, which attenuates the beneficial impact of TLS on immunotherapy response. Therefore: (1) TLS presence + high TMB with clonal neoantigens and intact MSH6 will show the strongest therapy response; (2) TLS presence with high TMB driven by subclonal neoantigens in MSH6-deficient tumors will have a reduced or absent therapy response advantage; (3) TLS absent or low will correlate with poorer response irrespective of TMB, unless clonal neoantigen burden and MSH6 status align favorably. This yields a testable interaction: TLS × neoantigen clonality × MSH6 status predicting immunotherapy response.
