# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that bladder tumors with apparent immune infiltration show heterogeneous outcomes and immunotherapy benefit despite links to DNA repair (MSH6), mutational burden (TMB), and epigenetic signals. Why does immune infiltration (visible on H&E) sometimes fail to translate into clinical response? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSH6 path (mismatch-repair → altered neoantigen generation) and the TMB path (mutational load → neoantigen quantity) because together they most directly determine neoantigen quality/clonality and hence whether a TIL-rich microenvironment can mount an effective anti-tumor response. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: eIF4E (translation control) can modulate immunogenic protein expression, CTC dynamics reflect tumor heterogeneity and subclonality that undermine systemic immunity, and Immunoscore/TIL metrics quantify immune infiltrates—these support a model in which neoantigen clonality and tumor-intrinsic programs modulate whether TILs are productive. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: The clinical question from the Ontologist centers on who benefits from immunotherapy; therapy response is the most mechanistically relevant and actionable endpoint linking TMB/MSH6, immune infiltration, and translational/epigenetic modifiers. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded PSA, pZVR, and CSF/AD biomarker paths as irrelevant cross-domain signals; I excluded TLS-like aggregates and TSR from the core variable set to keep the hypothesis maximally focused (one imaging feature only). I also relegated urine DNA methylation (Tier 1) to secondary modifiers to preserve the 2-anchor limit and keep the mechanistic claim crisp?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors, high intratumoral TIL density predicts objective therapy_response only when MSH6 function is intact and the tumor harbors a high burden of clonal neoantigens (i.e., high TMB with a predominance of clonal neoantigens). MSH6 loss elevates overall mutational load but yields largely subclonal neoantigen landscapes, which diminish T cell priming and enable immune evasion despite dense infiltration. A combined biomarker model that integrates TIL density, MSH6 status, and TMB clonality will predict therapy_response more accurately than TIL density alone. Secondary modifiers (urine DNA methylation biomarkers, eIF4E expression, circulating tumor cells, and Immunoscore) may refine predictions by capturing additional tumor-intrinsic and immune-context signals but are not essential to the primary two-anchor framework.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Assemble retrospective cohorts of bladder cancer patients treated with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 with available tumor and blood/tissue samples.

**Prediction 2:** Quantify TIL density (digital pathology/IHC), assess MSH6 status (IHC and sequencing for mutations/LOH), and measure TMB and neoantigen clonality (whole-exome/genome sequencing with subclonal vs clonal neoantigen calling).

**Prediction 3:** Build predictive models for therapy_response using (a) TIL alone, (b) TIL + MSH6 + TMB, and (c) TIL + MSH6 + TMB + secondary modifiers.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
