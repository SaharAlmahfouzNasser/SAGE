# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist synthesis highlights that some MIBC tumors display organized immune architecture (high TLS / immune infiltration) yet fail to respond to immune therapies; why does immune presence not translate into effective anti-tumor immunity in those cases? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the TGFB1/TGF-β fibrogenic signaling path (core path 1) because it provides a clear, targetable mechanism for stromal remodeling and immune suppression, and on the Immunoscore/immune-contexture path (core path 2) because it frames TLS/T-cell organization as the primary immune readout whose functional competence is in question. I therefore paired TGFB1 (tumor fibrogenic/immunosuppressive driver) with CD8A (CD8+ T-cell effector marker) linked to TLS-like aggregates as the imaging readout. 3) Tier 2 support: Epigenetic markers (SDC2/TFPI2) and microenvironment patterns (SARIFA) provide orthogonal evidence that stromal and epigenetic remodeling accompany aggressive, immune-excluded phenotypes; MSI status and AI-based integration support feasibility of deriving composite, clinically usable classifiers. 4) Why this endpoint: I selected therapy_response (immune checkpoint inhibitor response proxy) because the central clinical puzzle concerns functional immune competence and therapeutic sensitivity rather than long-term survival alone; therapy_response is the most mechanistically informative endpoint to test immune–fibrosis interactions. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded additional signaling mediators (e.g., explicit SMAD genes) from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim focused (max 2 anchors). Exploratory cross-domain paths (e.g., CSF AD biomarkers, PSA/pZVR cross-cancer links) were omitted from the core claim due to weak bladder-specific mechanistic support but are retained in secondary_modifiers for follow-up studies?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), the presence of TLS-like aggregates co-localizing with CD8A-expressing T cells is associated with improved response to immune checkpoint blockade only when TGFB1 signaling is low or inhibited. When TGFB1 expression/signaling is high in the tumor microenvironment, TGF-β–driven stromal remodeling suppresses CD8A+ T cell effector function and TLS maturation, leading to poor or unchanged therapy_response despite TLS presence. Consequently, TLS-density becomes a context-dependent predictor of ICB benefit, and combining TGFB1/TGF-β pathway inhibition with ICB unmasks the positive association between TLSs and therapy_response in TGFB1-high tumors. This framework uses two anchors (TGFB1 and CD8A) and treats TLS-like aggregates as the readout of organized immunity whose predictive value for ICB depends on TGFB1-driven immunosuppression; testing this requires demonstrating: (1) TLS density and CD8A expression correlate with ICB response only when TGFB1 signaling is low, (2) TGFB1 blockade rescues the TLS-CD8A–dependent prediction of response, and (3) functional assays show TGFB1 reduces CD8A+ T cell cytotoxicity within TLS-like microenvironments.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
