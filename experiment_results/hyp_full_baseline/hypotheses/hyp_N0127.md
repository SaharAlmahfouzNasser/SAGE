# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Ontologist synthesis highlights that MSI-H/IS-high bladder tumors often show better survival and are candidates for PD-1 blockade, yet response is heterogeneous — why do some TLS- or immune-rich MSI-H tumors fail to benefit from checkpoint blockade?

## Working Hypothesis

In MSI-H bladder tumors, the presence of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates correlates with better responses to PD-1 blockade only when tumor-intrinsic translation activity is not driving a suppressive immunomodulatory transcript. Specifically, elevated eIF4E-driven translation sustains the transcript AL662844.4, which in turn attenuates antigen presentation and/or effector T cell activation within the TLS-rich microenvironment, reducing therapy_response to PD-1 inhibitors. Consequently, MSI-H bladder tumors with high TLS density but concomitant high eIF4E activity and AL662844.4 expression define a distinct, immunotherapy-resistant subset that can be predicted by integrating TLS metrics with eIF4E/AL662844.4 status. In contrast, TLS-rich tumors with low eIF4E activity and/or low AL662844.4 expression will exhibit enhanced therapy_response to PD-1 blockade. This mechanistic axis implies that effective immunotherapy in this setting requires either (a) direct disruption of the translation program (eIF4E axis) or (b) targeted modulation of AL662844.4, in combination with PD-1 blockade, to restore antigen presentation and T cell effector function. The key testable predictions are: (i) eIF4E activity and AL662844.4 levels will stratify PD-1 response within TLS-high MSI-H bladder cancers; (ii) manipulation of eIF4E or AL662844.4 will modify antigen presentation genes and T cell–mediated cytotoxicity in vitro; (iii) combination therapy with PD-1 blockade and a translation-targeting strategy or AL662844.4–directed approach will improve response in TLS-high/eIF4E-AL662844.4–high models; (iv) RP116 in combination with PD-1 blockade will show enhanced efficacy in relevant preclinical models by dampening the translation-immunity axis. Endpoints should include objective therapy_response, pCR in neoadjuvant contexts, and mechanistic readouts (MHC expression, antigen presentation pathway activity, TIL phenotypes).

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
