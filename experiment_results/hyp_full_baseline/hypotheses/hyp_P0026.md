# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates do not reliably predict immunotherapy benefit in MSH6-deficient, TMB-high bladder tumors. The refined hypothesis posits that: (i) MSH6 loss drives a very high mutational burden with neoantigen heterogeneity, which can impede effective clonal expansion of tumor-reactive T cells; (ii) antigen presentation pathways (MHC I surface expression, TAP/processing machinery) are variably intact and can be suppressed in this context, limiting neoantigen display to cytotoxic T cells; (iii) translation control via eIF4E and systemic/metabolic cues involving HDL-C modulate the production and presentation of neoantigens and the tumor’s immunogenic milieu; (iv) TLS quality (maturation status, germinal center activity, B/T cell composition) determines functional activity, so presence alone may not translate to effective cytotoxicity when antigen presentation is compromised; (v) TLS may be present in an immunosuppressive microenvironment with exhausted T cells or regulatory elements, diminishing predictive value for therapy response. Therefore, in MSH6-deficient, TMB-high bladder tumors, therapy_response to checkpoint inhibitors will be predicted not by TLS presence alone but by the integration of TLS quality with antigen presentation competency and translation/metabolic state. Specifically, TLS-high, MSH6-deficient, TMB-high tumors will show reduced therapy_response unless antigen presentation and/or translation/metabolic pathways are favorable or pharmacologically modulated. The hypothesis yields testable predictions: (a) TLS-high/MSH6-deficient/TMB-high tumors will have lower objective response rates to immunotherapy than TLS-high tumors with intact antigen presentation or TLS-low tumors; (b) reduced expression of MHC I and antigen-processing genes, and elevated eIF4E activity or adverse HDL-C associations, will correlate with poor response within the TLS-high subset; (c) interventions that boost antigen presentation or modulate translation/metabolism will enhance therapy_response in TLS-high, MSH6-deficient, TMB-high models; (d) integrated TLS quality scores plus antigen presentation/metabolic markers will outperform TLS presence alone in predicting response. Feasibility is supported by existing platforms: WES/TMB and MSH6 status in cohorts, IHC and RNA-seq for MHC I/APM and IFN-signaling, ribosome profiling and proteomics for translation targets, HLA peptidomics, AI-imaging to quantify TLS maturation, and preclinical models for combination therapies.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
