# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Clinical trials test pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy because chemo can induce tumor cell death that synergizes with PD-1 blockade, yet response is heterogeneous — specifically, why do some TIL-rich muscle-invasive tumors fail to benefit from the chemo+PD-1 combination? 2) Tier 1 paths used: Core Path 1 (pembrolizumab + chemotherapy → cell death in muscle-invasive patients) provides the clinical/therapeutic backbone; Core Paths 2–3 (KRAS signaling in colon and KRAS mutations in lung) motivate KRAS as a cross-tissue oncogenic modifier that can alter cell-death modalities and immune visibility. I built the hypothesis by asking whether KRAS activity mechanistically interferes with chemo-induced immunogenic cell death and TIL function, thereby blunting pembrolizumab synergy. 3) Tier 2 support: tissue-specific occurrence of KRAS mutations (Tissue → mutated_in → KRAS), colon/lung mutation contexts, and smoking-associated mutational patterns provide biological plausibility that KRAS-mutant tumors differ in mutational landscape, immunogenicity, and microenvironmental conditioning that could reduce chemo+IO benefit. 4) Why therapy response endpoint: The core question is about whether chemo-induced cell death synergizes with PD-1 blockade to produce tumor regression — a therapy response proxy (clinical benefit to pembrolizumab+chemotherapy) is the most proximal and testable endpoint in available trial-like datasets, more directly tied to the mechanistic claim than OS/PFI which are distal and confounded. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded the TB/Mycobacterium-related paths and other organism/resistance edges as off-topic for the cancer–IO/chemo question; I also limited anchors to KRAS (did not add PD-1/PD-L1 gene names) to keep the hypothesis sharp and mechanistic rather than multi-feature/predictive?

## Working Hypothesis

Within muscle-invasive tumors, activating KRAS signaling modulates the effectiveness of chemotherapy-induced immunogenic cell death (ICD) and tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) function such that high TIL density predicts a strong response to pembrolizumab+chemotherapy only in KRAS-wildtype tumors; KRAS-mutant tumors with high TIL density show attenuated clinical benefit. Therefore, TIL-high/KRAS-mutant tumors will have lower objective response rates and shorter duration of response to pembrolizumab+chemotherapy than TIL-high/KRAS-wildtype tumors, with the magnitude of this effect influenced by tissue context (colon vs lung) and smoking-associated mutational patterns. In TIL-low tumors, benefits from pembrolizumab+chemotherapy will be limited regardless of KRAS status. The imaging readout remains TIL density, while KRAS status serves as a mechanistic modifier of chemo-induced ICD and subsequent TIL efficacy, explaining heterogeneity in therapeutic response across KRAS-driven solid tumors.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
