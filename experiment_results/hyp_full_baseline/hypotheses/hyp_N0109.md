# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do organized lymphoid structures (TLS) sometimes fail to confer a survival advantage? The path implicates a TGF-β→Treg immunosuppressive axis together with tumor-suppressor (RASSF1A) alterations and context-dependent roles of LAG3, suggesting that tumor-intrinsic alterations may convert otherwise ‘protective’ TLS into functionally suppressed niches. 2) Tier 1 path(s) used: the single mechanistic chain linking promoter/TGF-β → Tregs → cancer context → RASSF1A inactivation and the cross-cancer observation that LAG3 has a context-dependent protective association (BLCA). I built on RASSF1A (tumor-intrinsic modifier of microenvironmental signaling) and LAG3 (immune-checkpoint shaping T cell effector state) because they offer a tumor-intrinsic × immune-regulatory interaction that plausibly modulates TLS function. 3) Tier 2/supporting evidence: TGF-β driven Treg enrichment provides the suppressive mechanism that can negate TLS benefit; cross-cancer LAG3 associations (protective in BLCA, low in READ) highlight that checkpoint expression/function is context-dependent and can mark whether TLS are productive. These support testing whether tumor suppressor loss (RASSF1A) cooperates with TGF-β/Tregs to disable TLS effectiveness via LAG3-related T cell dysfunction. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) was chosen because it is the most robust, clinically meaningful endpoint available across TCGA cohorts and best captures the net effect of immune–tumor interactions on patient outcome. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded imaging modalities/methods (ultrasound) and organ-specific nodes (prostate, bladder, BLCA, READ) from the core hypothesis to keep the question mechanistically focused and generalizable; I also excluded explicitly listing TGF-β/Tregs from the core variables to obey the 1–2 anchor gene limit and to center the hypothesis on tumor-intrinsic (RASSF1A) modulation of immune checkpoint (LAG3) function within TLS-rich microenvironments?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates confer a survival benefit only when tumor cells maintain functional RASSF1A. Loss of RASSF1A potentiates TGF-β signaling, driving recruitment and/or function of regulatory T cells within TLS and attenuating the anti-tumor activity of LAG3-expressing T cells. Consequently, in TLS-rich BRCA tumors, RASSF1A inactivation reshapes the TLS microenvironment from pro-immunogenic to immunosuppressive, reducing LAG3-associated effector activity and leading to worse overall survival. Predictions: (1) In TCGA-BRCA, the survival advantage of TLS-rich tumors is attenuated or reversed when RASSF1A is inactivated (low expression and/or promoter hypermethylation). (2) Within TLS-rich, RASSF1A-intact tumors, higher LAG3 signatures relate to functional TLS and better OS, whereas in TLS-rich, RASSF1A-low tumors, elevated TGFB1 and Treg signatures correlate with poor OS despite TLS presence. (3) A significant interaction between TLS status and RASSF1A status will be observed for OS, with mediation by TGF-β/Treg axis (TGFB1, FOXP3/CD25) and a modulation of LAG3 signaling within TLS that reflects TLS functional status.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
