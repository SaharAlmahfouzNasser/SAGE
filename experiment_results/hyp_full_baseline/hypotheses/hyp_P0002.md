## Working Hypothesis

We hypothesize that in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), a high tumor mutational burden (TMB) and TP53-mutant status create a baseline propensity for neoantigen-driven T cell responses, but this pro-immunogenic effect is functionally overridden by a tumor microenvironment with a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR). A high TSR—reflecting dense extracellular matrix, cancer-associated fibroblast activity, and physical immunosuppressive barriers—restricts neoantigen presentation and CD8+ T cell infiltration/effector function, thereby dampening response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICI) even in TMB-high tumors. This interaction between genomic drivers (TMB, TP53) and microenvironmental architecture (TSR) yields a distinct resistance phenotype that can be detected clinically on pre-treatment pathology and imaging and stratifies patients for combination therapies targeting stroma alongside ICI. The hypothesis implies testable predictions: (1) among TMB-high MIBC, TSR will be inversely associated with ICI response, and (2) TP53-mutant, TSR-high tumors will show the weakest response compared with TSR-low counterparts, while TSR-low, TMB-high TP53-mutant tumors will have comparatively better responses. Secondary modifiers (MSI/dMMR status, SARIFA patterns, HDL-C–associated metabolism, DPYD/PTGDS signatures, and circulating tumor cells) may modulate the magnitude of this effect but are not required for the core mechanism. Experimental approaches will test this axis across clinical cohorts, spatial biology, and mechanistic systems. Feasibly, TSR can be quantified from standard H&E or multiplex IHC using digital pathology; the proposed work will integrate TMB (and selective MSI/dMMR assessment), TP53 status, and TSR with immune contexture to predict ICI response and guide rational combination strategies (e.g., stromal/CAF targeting or TGF-β pathway inhibition alongside PD-1/PD-L1 blockade). This framework yields testable hypotheses and actionable clinical implications for stratified therapy in MIBC.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TMB and neoantigen load correlate with ICI responsiveness but do not guarantee benefit in MIBC, indicating modulators beyond neoantigen quantity influence outcomes;

**Prediction 2:** TP53 mutations are common in MIBC and associate with genomic instability and immune-evasive tumor phenotypes, potentially dampening immunogenicity despite high TMB;

**Prediction 3:** the tumor stroma and ECM (captured by TSR) can physically impede immune infiltration and foster immunosuppressive signaling, a phenomenon supported by literature on SARIFA, stromal remodeling, and CAF-driven TGF-β signaling. By integrating TSR with TMB and TP53 status, we can identify a microenvironment-mediated resistance phenotype and develop strategies to overcome this barrier.
