# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Expanded hypothesis: There exists a human bladder cancer biomarker network in which a prioritized set of nodes—MKI67, TP53, RB1, FGFR3, CCND1, CDKN2A, ERCC2 and related DNA-damage repair factors, PD-L1/CD274 and other immune-checkpoint genes, EMT markers (CDH1, SNAI1, ZEB1), and APOBEC-signature–related nodes—exhibits distinct dysregulation patterns between NMIBC and MIBC. The network will be characterized by a combination of direct mechanistic edges (e.g., regulatory interactions, mutation-driven pathway activation) and strong co-expression/prognostic edges (e.g., edges that correlate with recurrence, progression, chemotherapy or immunotherapy response). Specific testable predictions include: (1) differential expression and mutation spectra of the gene set between NMIBC and MIBC that associate with recurrence-free and progression-free survival; (2) DDR/TP53/RB1 alterations and FGFR3 status predicting chemo-sensitivity (cisplatin-based regimens) and, separately, responsiveness to immune-checkpoint blockade; (3) immune-checkpoint and EMT signatures correlating with tumor immune microenvironment status (inflamed vs. excluded) and predicting response to BCG or PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors; (4) integration of mechanistic and co-expression edges into a network whose edge weights stratify patients by prognosis and therapy response, enabling a concise biomarker model capped at ~30 genes/edges. Edge metadata will be captured (evidence type: literature, curated databases, and patient cohorts) to enable Tier 1 high-confidence edges. Experimental testing plan: (i) assemble human bladder cancer cohorts with explicit NMIBC/MIBC annotation and comprehensive omics data (transcriptomics, mutation, CNV) plus clinical outcomes and treatment history; (ii) perform differential expression/mutation analyses for the prioritized genes and compute associations with recurrence/progression and therapy response; (iii) estimate immune infiltration and examine co-expression patterns with immune/EMT markers; (iv) construct a multi-layer network integrating curated regulatory/pathway edges and data-driven co-expression edges, weight edges by supporting evidence, and derive a predictive signature using penalized regression or network-based learning; (v) validate the top edges and the predictive model in independent cohorts and, where feasible, corroborate with protein-level assays (IHC, targeted proteomics).

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
