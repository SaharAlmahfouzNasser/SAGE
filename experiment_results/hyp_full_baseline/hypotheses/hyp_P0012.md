# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-rich MIBC tumors fail to translate TLS architecture into effective anti-tumor immunity when tumor MSH6 deficiency reshapes neoantigen quality/quantity and fosters a PD-L1–dominant, S100A9+ myeloid-inflamed microenvironment that functionally suppresses TLS-driven T-cell activity. Specifically, MSH6-deficient, PD-L1–high tumors will exhibit: (a) impaired TLS T-cell activation and reduced TCR clonal expansion despite abundant TLS; (b) higher PD-L1 expression on tumor and/or myeloid cells and enriched S100A9+ myeloid infiltrates in TLS-adjacent zones; (c) diminished cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell effector responses and increased exhaustion markers within TLS neighborhoods; (d) lower objective/durable responses to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade compared with TLS-rich, MSH6-proficient tumors. Contextual modifiers (HDL-C, TP53 status, ER signaling, PTGDS) will modulate the strength of this suppression by affecting PD-L1 regulation, myeloid polarization, or TLS maintenance. Consequently, TLS abundance will be a poor predictor of response to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors specifically in the subset of MIBC tumors that are MSH6-deficient and PD-L1–high; TLS will retain predictive value in MSH6-proficient or PD-L1-low contexts. The model also predicts that strategic disruption of the myeloid/S100A9 axis or co-targeting PD-L1 will restore TLS functionality and improve therapeutic responses in preclinical systems and some patient subsets.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** among TLS-rich tumors, MSH6def/PD-L1high cases show poorer PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor responses than MSH6proficient cases;

**Prediction 2:** single-cell/spatial analyses reveal proximate S100A9+ myeloid cells suppressing TLS-associated T-cell activity in MSH6def/PD-L1high tumors;

**Prediction 3:** combining PD-L1 blockade with myeloid-targeting or metabolic modulators can rescue TLS function and improve responses;


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
