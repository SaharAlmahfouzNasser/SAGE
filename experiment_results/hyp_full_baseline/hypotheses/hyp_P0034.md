# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that urine DNA methylation panels (notably SDC2/TFPI2) and liquid biopsy signals detect bladder tumors noninvasively, but it is unclear why some immune-infiltrated tumors still fail therapy — i.e., when does noninvasive detection or high TILs not translate into effective anti-tumor immunity or therapy response? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built primarily on the 'selected urine-DNA methylation biomarkers' path and the 'SDC2 and TFPI2 methylation' concept because these provide concrete, testable molecular anchors (gene-specific methylation) that can mechanistically alter tumor/ECM biology; I considered CTCs as complementary but not central. 3) Tier 2 support: TGF-β (fibrogenic signaling/EMT/ECM remodeling) supports a mechanism of immune exclusion/function impairment; MSI/AI-based biomarker concepts inform potential stratification and detection sensitivity; HDL-C and prognostic histopathologic signals provide metabolic and aggressiveness context for interpreting methylation–immune interactions. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response because (a) TIL density is a functional imaging correlate of anti-tumor immunity relevant to response to immune-based therapies in bladder cancer and (b) resolving why some TIL-high tumors do not respond has immediate translational impact for patient selection and combination strategies. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded cross-disease readouts with weak bladder mechanistic links (CSF AD biomarkers, PSA, pZVR, and the Gleason/prostate framing) from the core hypothesis to keep the question mechanistically focused on bladder-derived methylation (SDC2/TFPI2) and immune microenvironment interactions?

## Working Hypothesis

Mechanistically, methylation-induced silencing of SDC2 and TFPI2 in bladder cancer remodels the extracellular matrix and pericellular signaling to create an immunosuppressive niche that reduces TIL functionality despite their presence. Clinically, bladder tumors with high TIL density and SDC2/TFPI2 methylation will show reduced response to checkpoint inhibitors compared with TIL-high tumors lacking this methylation, defining a methylation-defined subset that may benefit from ECM-targeting co-therapies to restore immunotherapy sensitivity.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
