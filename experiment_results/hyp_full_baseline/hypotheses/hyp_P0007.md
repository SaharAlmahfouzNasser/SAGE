# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some tumors with high histologic TIL density nevertheless progress rapidly? The ontologist's analysis suggests competing influences from tumor-intrinsic metabolism (SHMT2) and nuclear lncRNA programs (NEAT1) that could blunt effective anti-tumor immunity despite apparent immune infiltration. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on SHMT2 (metabolic vulnerability path) because it provides a druggable mechanism that can create a hostile metabolic microenvironment for T cells, and NEAT1 (paraspeckle/lncRNA path) because it can reprogram gene expression and sequester immune-regulatory factors — together these give a plausible mechanistic route for immune failure in TIL-high tumors. 3) Tier 2 support: HCP5, LINC00456, MAFG-AS1 and exosomal miR-21 support an ncRNA network and intercellular miRNA signaling that can modulate immune visibility and metastatic potential, reinforcing the idea that ncRNA modules interact with metabolism to shape immune efficacy. 4) Why PFI: Progression-free interval is the most sensitive clinical endpoint for detecting early failure of immune control (tumor progression/recurrence) and is well-captured in cohort-level datasets, making it optimal to test whether immune infiltration fails to translate into durable control. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded AC010503.4 from the 1-2 anchor genes (it remains a secondary_modifier) to keep the core mechanistic claim tightly focused on a metabolic (SHMT2) versus nuclear regulatory (NEAT1) interaction; I also excluded TLS-like aggregates and TSR as imaging choices because TIL density most directly reflects effector immune presence/function relevant to the posed puzzle?

## Working Hypothesis

Among tumors with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density, concurrent metabolic reprogramming driven by elevated SHMT2 and NEAT1-mediated transcriptional regulation creates a metabolically hostile and immunosuppressive microenvironment. This decouples immune cell presence from effective anti-tumor activity, resulting in shorter progression-free intervals (PFI). Secondary modifiers (AC010503.4, HCP5, LINC00456, MAFG-AS1, and exosomal miR-21) modulate the magnitude of this decoupling by influencing antigen presentation, interferon signaling, and intercellular communication, but do not redefine the core axis; SHMT2 and NEAT1 remain the primary anchors. Clinically, patients with high TIL density but elevated SHMT2 and NEAT1 will have reduced PFI, identifying a subset that may benefit from combination strategies targeting tumor metabolism and NEAT1-associated regulatory networks to restore TIL functionality.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
