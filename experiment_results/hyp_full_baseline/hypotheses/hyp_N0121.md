# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights two opposing forces — tumor immunogenicity driven by defective mismatch repair (MSH6/MSI) that should promote antitumor immunity, versus TGF-β–driven EMT/fibrosis that remodels stroma and can exclude or suppress immune effectors. Why do some immunogenic (MMR-deficient) bladder tumors with histologic immune organization nevertheless fail to yield better patient survival?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder tumors, high MSH6 deficiency (a driver of elevated mutational burden and neoantigen load) fosters tumor immunogenicity and can promote TLS-like lymphoid structures. However, concurrently high TGF-β signaling within the tumor stroma induces fibrosis and immune exclusion, undermining TLS maturation/function and preventing effective anti-tumor immunity from translating into improved survival. The proposed mechanistic model predicts a significant interaction among three factors: (a) TLS-like lymphoid aggregates present within the tumor, (b) MSH6 status indicating antigenicity, and (c) intratumoral TGF-β signaling indicating stromal immune suppression. Specifically, TLS-high and MSH6-deficient tumors with low TGF-β signaling will exhibit the best overall survival (OS) due to functional TLS-driven immunity, whereas TLS-high and MSH6-deficient tumors with high TGF-β signaling will have worsened OS, reflecting immune exclusion and TLS dysfunction. This interaction identifies a clinically relevant subphenotype likely to benefit from combination therapy that targets TGF-β–driven stroma and augments immune effector function. To test this, the following is proposed: (1) retrospective, multivariable analyses in bladder cancer cohorts to assess OS with an interaction term TLS_status × MSH6_status × TGF-β_signature; (2) spatial/single-cell profiling to characterize TLS maturation, TGF-β pathway activity (pSMAD2/3), EMT/fibrosis markers, and immune cell composition in TLS-high vs TLS-low, MSH6-deficient vs proficient tumors; (3) functional preclinical models (in vitro organoid/T-cell co-cultures and in vivo syngeneic bladder cancer models) to manipulate TGF-β signaling (blockade vs. control) in MSH6-deficient TLS contexts and evaluate TLS integrity, T-cell infiltration, cytokine milieu, tumor growth, and survival; (4) pilot translational readouts to evaluate combination strategies (anti–TGF-β ± PD-1/PD-L1) in preclinical models and identify biomarkers that predict response. Secondary modifiers (HDL-C, GREM1, SDC2, TFPI2, pZVR) can be incorporated as exploratory strata to refine magnitude of effect but are not required for establishing the core interaction.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
