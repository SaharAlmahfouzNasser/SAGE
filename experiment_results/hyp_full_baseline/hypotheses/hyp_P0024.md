# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 synthesis highlights three noninvasive modalities (urine-DNA methylation, CTC-derived ADNP mRNA, and blood TMB) that do not always agree in predicting NMIBC clinical benefit from intravesical BCG — why do histologic signs of local immune organization (e.g., TLS) sometimes fail to translate into effective BCG responses? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on Core Path 3 (bTMB linking systemic neoantigen supply to immune responsiveness) and Core Path 2 (ADNP mRNA in CTCs as a circulating tumor transcriptional program) because together they offer a mechanism by which systemic antigenic context and tumor-intrinsic transcriptional state modulate the functional output of local immune structures. I included the imaging axis implicitly (TLS-like lymphoid aggregates) because the Ontologist's synthesis asked why noninvasive readouts can be discordant with immune-organization on tissue. 3) Tier 2 support: MSH6 (mismatch repair) provides a genomic mechanism that can raise TMB/bTMB; PD-L1 links immune activation to adaptive suppression; and HDL-C/metabolic factors can modulate inflammation and immune cell function — these modifiers plausibly alter the TLS–bTMB–ADNP interaction. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected a therapy_response proxy (BCG response) because the core paths explicitly connect bTMB and PD-L1 to immunotherapy context and BCG is the principal NMIBC therapy where predictive biomarkers are clinically actionable. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded packing the hypothesis with urine-methylation panels as a core anchor to keep the mechanistic question focused on the immune-effector failure (TLS vs systemic neoantigen and tumor transcriptional programs); urine methylation remains in secondary_modifiers for complementary diagnostic value. I also excluded weaker Tier3/ancillary paths (PSA, pZVR, SARIFA) because they lack mechanistic relevance to the TLS–bTMB–ADNP axis?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In NMIBC patients treated with intravesical BCG, presence and maturity of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates predict favorable treatment response only when systemic neoantigen supply is high (elevated bTMB) and tumor-derived ADNP transcriptional activity is low (low ADNP mRNA in circulating tumor cells). If ADNP mRNA in CTCs is high, the tumor-intrinsic transcriptional program suppresses TLS-driven anti-tumor immunity, leading to BCG resistance despite TLS presence. Therefore, the strongest predictor of BCG response is the combined state: TLS-competent microenvironment plus high bTMB and low ADNP mRNA in CTCs, which yields the highest probability of clinical benefit. Conversely, TLS presence with low bTMB or high ADNP mRNA predicts reduced response, suggesting consideration of alternative or combination immunotherapies. Secondary hypotheses and mechanistic modifiers: (1) MSH6 deficiency (mismatch repair deficiency) increases bTMB, amplifying the TLS–bTMB–ADNP interaction and improving BCG responsiveness in the TLS-high/bTMB-high/ADNP-low subgroup. (2) PD-L1 expression modulates the effect by reflecting adaptive immune suppression; high PD-L1 may attenuate TLS efficacy even with favorable TLS/bTMB/ADNP profiles. (3) HDL-C and metabolic factors shape systemic inflammation and immune cell function, modulating TLS maturation and its functional output; higher HDL-C may support TLS efficiency and improve response when other supportive signals are present. (4) Urine DNA methylation biomarkers act as secondary modifiers that reflect mucosal pathology and could refine risk stratification but are not core anchors of the TLS–bTMB–ADNP axis. Experimental approach: test in a prospective NMIBC cohort with integrated sampling at baseline and during/after BCG; perform TLS assessment by at least H&E and multiplex IHC (CD3, CD8, CD20, PNAd, CXCL13, CXCL9/10, BCL6); quantify bTMB from plasma cell-free DNA via targeted or shallow whole-genome sequencing; quantify ADNP mRNA in CTCs using validated isolation (e.g., microfluidics) and digital PCR; determine MSH6 status and PD-L1 expression in tumor tissue; measure HDL-C and urine methylation panels; track objective endpoints such as recurrence-free survival and progression-free survival with predefined time points. Data analysis: construct a multivariable predictive model incorporating TLS metrics, bTMB, ADNP mRNA, MSH6, PD-L1, HDL-C, and urine methylation; compare model performance to single-biomarker models using AUC/ROC, net reclassification improvement, and decision-curve analysis; validate within-sample with cross-validation and, if possible, in an independent cohort. Expected outcomes: a validated composite biomarker model that improves prediction of BCG response over individual markers and provides a rationale for stratified therapy (BCG alone vs. BCG plus adjunctive immunotherapies) in NMIBC.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
