# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist framed a core question of why robust local immune contexture (Immunoscore/TILs) and/or high tumor mutational burden (MSI/dMMR) do not uniformly predict durable benefit from immune-stimulating therapies, and how non‑invasive readouts (ctDNA) might resolve this discordance. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) the Immunoscore/immune-contexture axis by operationalizing it as TIL density (imaging proxy of Immunoscore) because it is directly measurable on H&E/WSI, (b) the MSI/dMMR path as a genomic feature that modulates neoantigen load and immune engagement, and (c) the ctDNA/liquid-biopsy path as a dynamic, non‑invasive readout of emerging immune‑evasive clones. These three form a mechanistic chain: neoantigen load (MSI/dMMR) -> local immune infiltration (TIL density) -> selection/escape dynamics detectable by ctDNA. 3) Tier 2 support: SARIFA, PDIA2, pZVR and methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2) provide orthogonal evidence that gene-expression/epigenetic programs and cross‑disease protein biomarkers can mark high‑risk or immune‑modulating tumor states; they support the plausibility that tumor-intrinsic programs modulate how TILs translate into control. 4) Why PFI: Progression-free interval is the most sensitive clinical endpoint here for capturing early failure/escape after an immune stimulus and aligns with ctDNA kinetics as an early surrogate of recurrence; it therefore best tests whether immune infiltration + genomic context produce durable disease control. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broad cross-disease markers (PSA/PDIA2 used in prostate contexts) and higher-level labels (the umbrella term Immunoscore) from the formal hypothesis to keep it tightly focused and testable in TCGA-style data: I used TIL density as the specific imaging feature and limited anchors to MSI/dMMR and ctDNA. Other Tier2/Tier3 candidates (SARIFA, stool methylation, pZVR) are listed as secondary_modifiers rather than included in the core claim to avoid overfitting and preserve mechanistic clarity?

## Working Hypothesis

Baseline tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density predicts improved progression-free interval (PFI) only in the context of MSI/dMMR-associated high neoantigen load; in MSI/dMMR tumors, sustained neoantigen exposure can promote T-cell dysfunction and rapid emergence of immune-evasive ctDNA-detectable subclones, limiting PFI despite high TIL density. Therefore, combining baseline TIL density with MSI/dMMR status and early on-treatment ctDNA kinetics will provide superior stratification of durable PFI than TIL density alone.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
