## Working Hypothesis

Expanded hypothesis: In breast cancer, the prognostic impact of tumor-derived SPP1 is amplified by a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) due to enhanced ECM remodeling and pro-tumor signaling, leading to worse overall survival (OS). Activating FGFR3 signaling mitigates this adverse SPP1–TSR axis by promoting differentiation-associated programs and dampening SPP1-driven invasion, resulting in relatively better OS within TSR-high tumors. EPB41L2 acts upstream to boost SPP1 expression/secretion and thereby modulates the TSR–SPP1–prognosis pathway, making EPB41L2 a secondary modifier in tumors with high TSR. The two core anchors are SPP1 and FGFR3, with TSR as the key imaging mediator; OS (and DFS, if available) are the primary endpoints. Testable predictions include: (1) in TSR-high tumors, high SPP1 associates with worse OS; (2) within TSR-high strata, FGFR3-activating alterations weaken the SPP1–OS association; (3) EPB41L2 expression correlates with higher SPP1 and poorer prognosis in TSR-high contexts; (4) a multi-variable model integrating TSR, SPP1, FGFR3 status, and EPB41L2 improves prognostic accuracy over any single marker.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
