# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tumors with dense, organized immune infiltrates (TLS/Immunoscore) often predict better outcomes, yet many TLS-rich MIBC tumors fail to respond to immunotherapy — why does local immune organization sometimes not translate into effective anti-tumor immunity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TGFB1/TGF-β signaling (core driver of fibrosis, EMT, and immune exclusion) provides a mechanistic axis that can physically and functionally prevent immune cells from engaging tumor cells; (b) Immunoscore/TLS-like lymphoid aggregates capture the imaging/immune organization whose effectiveness is in question; (c) Urine DNA methylation markers represent tumor-side epigenetic programs (e.g., antigen-presentation or EMT-associated methylation) that could silence immune-recognition. I built the hypothesis by linking TGFB1-mediated stromal/immune exclusion with epigenetic silencing signatures that together blunt TLS function. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: PTGDS and HDL-C point to local inflammatory/prostaglandin and systemic lipid–immune modulation that can amplify or attenuate TGF-β effects and influence immune cell function; lymph node metastasis as a clinical correlate reinforces that an immune-excluded, fibrotic phenotype is clinically aggressive. 4) Why therapy_response chosen as the endpoint: The key translational question is not only prognosis but whether TLS presence predicts benefit from immune checkpoint blockade; TGF-β and methylation mechanisms are well-positioned to determine immunotherapy responsiveness, making therapy response the most relevant primary endpoint. 5) What I excluded and why: I deliberately excluded Tier 3 exploratory links (PSA cross-disease signals, CSF/AD biomarker analogies, stool SDC2/TFPI2 markers) because they are indirect to bladder-specific immune–fibrosis–epigenetic biology and would diffuse the mechanistic focus?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), TLS-like lymphoid aggregates indicate potential local anti-tumor immunity, but high TGFB1 activity drives fibrotic stromal remodeling and immune exclusion, compromising TLS maturation and function. Concurrent tumor DNA hypermethylation silences antigen presentation programs, further diminishing TLS-driven immune engagement. As a result, TLS-rich tumors with elevated TGFB1 activity and high urine DNA methylation scores will exhibit reduced response to immune checkpoint blockade compared with TLS-rich tumors that have low TGFB1 activity and low methylation; this effect will be detectable as a statistical interaction between TGFB1 activity and urine DNA methylation on therapy_response, independent of TLS presence.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
