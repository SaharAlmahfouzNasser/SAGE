## Working Hypothesis

Primary mechanistic claim: In TLS-rich NMIBC, MSH6 deficiency combined with ultra-high TMB impairs antigen presentation, resulting in reduced T-cell priming and diminished response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) compared with MSH6-intact tumors possessing moderate-to-high TMB. Secondary mechanistic elements: MSH6 deficiency shapes the mutational spectrum (e.g., frameshift neoantigens) but without competent antigen presentation (MHC I/II, B2M, TAP1/2, CIITA pathways), neoantigens are poorly presented, blunting cytotoxic T cell activity despite TLS formation. The presence of TLS indicates organized local immunity, but its effectiveness is contingent on antigen presentation quality; thus TLS-rich, MSH6-deficient, TMB-high tumors will show lower therapy response rates to ICB than TLS-rich, MSH6-wildtype tumors with comparable TLS density and similar TMB. Testable predictions: (1) Surface expression and function of antigen presentation machinery (MHC I/II, B2M, TAP1/2) will be reduced in MSH6-deficient, TMB-high TLS-rich NMIBC compared with MSH6-wildtype controls. (2) T-cell receptor clonality and effector function within TLS regions will be diminished in the MSH6-deficient/TMB-high group. (3) Objective response to ICB will be lowest in TLS-rich, MSH6-deficient, TMB-high tumors and higher in TLS-rich, MSH6-wildtype tumors with similar TLS density and TMB. (4) Modifiers such as TP53 status, HLA-DQB1 alleles, and SARIFA/AI biomarkers will modulate the magnitude of antigen-presenting impairment and, consequently, response. Testing strategy: (a) retrospective NMIBC cohorts treated with ICIs with data on MSH6 status, TMB, TLS metrics, and antigen presentation gene expression; (b) immunohistochemistry and flow cytometry for MHC I/II, B2M, TAP1/2 in TLS-rich regions; (c) spatial transcriptomics and single-cell RNA-seq to map antigen presentation pathways and T cell activity within TLS; (d) in vitro NMIBC models (MSH6 knockout with controlled TMB) to measure MHC expression, neoantigen presentation, and T-cell activation; (e) in vivo models to test ICB response with or without agents that boost antigen presentation (e.g., IFN-γ mimetics, cGAS-STING agonists, or epigenetic modulators). This framework yields a concise, testable biomarker signature (MSH6 status × TMB × antigen presentation competence) to predict ICB benefit in TLS-rich NMIBC and to guide combination strategies.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
