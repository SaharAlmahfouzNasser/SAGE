# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Although high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density (Immunoscore-like) is generally prognostic and predictive of immunotherapy benefit, many TIL-rich bladder tumors do not respond to immune checkpoint blockade; the Ontologist's Tier 1 analysis implicates opposing influences of fibrogenic TGF-β signaling and MMR-driven immunogenicity (MSH6) in shaping this discordance. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TGF-β (core path 1) — selected because TGF-β is mechanistically linked to stromal fibrosis, T cell exclusion/dysfunction and immunotherapy resistance; (b) MSH6/MMR (core path 3) — selected because MSH6 loss increases neoantigen load and can potentiate immune responsiveness. I built the hypothesis around how these two axes interact with the observable imaging readout (TIL density). 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: non-invasive readouts (urine DNA methylation, CTCs) and AI-based biomarkers can provide complementary measures of tumor burden, epigenetic state, and standardized spatial immune metrics to test the proposed interaction; MSI status from Tier 2 further supports the role of mutational burden in modulating immune sensitivity. 4) Choice of endpoint: I selected therapy_response (immune checkpoint blockade response proxy) because the mechanistic puzzle is explicitly about why an ostensibly favorable immune microenvironment (TIL-high) sometimes fails to produce clinical benefit, and both TGF-β and MMR status are known to directly influence immunotherapy sensitivity. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broader non-mechanistic tumor biomarker lists and exploratory cross-cancer stool methylation markers from the core hypothesis to keep it sharply focused on a mechanistic interaction between stromal immunosuppression (TGFB1) and neoantigen-driven immunogenicity (MSH6) as they relate to TIL imaging; other entities (urine methylation, CTCs, AI-readouts, SDC2/TFPI2) are placed as secondary modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density, the likelihood of response to immune checkpoint blockade is determined by a bidirectional interaction between TGFB1-driven stromal remodeling and MSH6-dependent neoantigen load. Specifically: (i) High TGFB1 pathway activity promotes fibrotic ECM remodeling and T cell exclusion/exhaustion, reducing ICI efficacy even when TIL density is high. (ii) MSH6 deficiency increases neoantigen burden, enhancing T cell recognition and potential anti-tumor activity, but this benefit is attenuated if TGFB1 signaling remains strong. (iii) When TGFB1 activity is low or therapeutically inhibited, MSH6-deficient tumors exhibit substantially higher response rates to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade due to greater neoantigen visibility and improved T cell infiltration. (iv) There is a synergistic interaction such that the predictive value of MSH6 status for ICI response is conditional on the TGFB1 pathway state; thus, the best responses are anticipated in MSH6-deficient tumors with low TGFB1 activity or with concurrent TGFB1 blockade. This model yields testable predictions: a) TGFB1 pathway activity inversely correlates with ICI response in TIL-high bladder cancers, regardless of MSH6 status; b) MSH6-deficient tumors with low TGFB1 signaling have the highest response probability; c) Combining TGFB1 blockade with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade will enhance TIL infiltration and tumor control, especially in MSH6-deficient contexts. Operationally, a composite biomarker integrating TGFB1 pathway activity (and SMAD/ECM signatures) with MSH6 status will outperform TIL density alone in predicting response. The hypothesis accommodates non-invasive and AI-enabled readouts (urine DNA methylation, CTCs, AI-based spatial immune classifiers) as correlative, translationally actionable biomarkers that can be integrated into prospective testing.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).

**Prediction 2:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.

**Prediction 3:** In functional experiments, restoring or enhancing the anchor gene's signaling will improve immune-mediated tumor control in imaging-feature-positive models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
