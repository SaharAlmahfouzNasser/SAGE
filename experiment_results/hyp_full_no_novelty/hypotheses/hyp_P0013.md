# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tumor-stroma ratio (TSR) is an image-derived prognostic signal in NMIBC, but why stroma-rich tumors more frequently progress after TURBT is unclear — does the stromal compartment actively enable residual tumor survival, invasion, or immune evasion that drives progression? 2) Data basis / paths used: only a single, morphology→outcome path was provided (TSR → NMIBC prognosis), so I built the hypothesis directly on that mechanistic link: TSR as a readout of a pro-invasive, immune-excluding microenvironment. 3) Supporting evidence (Tier 2 style): spatial morphologic phenomena (tumor budding, CAF-rich ECM, and lymphocyte exclusion) conceptually support that high stromal fraction can create physical/biochemical niches favoring residual cell survival and local invasion after resection. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: Progression-free interval (PFI) is the clinically decisive outcome in NMIBC (transition to MIBC drives morbidity and treatment change), so it is the most relevant and measurable endpoint for a TSR-based prognostic mechanism. 5) Deliberate exclusions: No molecular anchor genes were included because the provided input contained only morphologic/spatial nodes (introducing specific genes would invent data not supplied). Conventional clinicopathologic covariates (grade, stage Ta/T1, multiplicity, prior recurrences, adjuvant intravesical therapy) are placed as secondary modifiers to be adjusted for in analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In NMIBC patients treated with TURBT, a high tumor stroma ratio (TSR) in diagnostic tumor sections is independently associated with a shorter progression-free interval (PFI) owing to an activated stromal microenvironment that (i) promotes residual tumor cell survival and local invasion through CAF-driven ECM remodeling, and (ii) facilitates immune exclusion, reducing anti-tumor immune responses. Consequently, TSR provides incremental prognostic value beyond conventional clinicopathologic factors, improving risk stratification for progression when incorporated into a multivariable model focused on PFI.
