# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The path links TP53 status, autophagy-driven cell death, mTORC1 dependence (rapamycin sensitivity), and immune context (LAG3/TILs). The puzzle is why some tumors with TP53 alteration are rapamycin-sensitive while others in an immune-active microenvironment may not benefit — i.e., what tumor-immune conditions modulate mTOR inhibitor efficacy. 2) Tier 1 path used: TP53 ↔ autophagy → cell death → mTORC1/rapamycin and the immune checkpoint node LAG3; I built on TP53 because it mechanistically connects stress/autophagy to survival signaling and on LAG3 because immune-exhaustion states (reflected by LAG3) could alter immunogenic consequences of therapy. 3) Tier 2/supporting evidence: rapamycin directly targets mTORC1 (mechanistic drug-target link) and the high-risk cohort showed lowest IC50 for rapamycin, suggesting a subgroupal mTOR dependency; autophagy promotes cell death linking TP53 function to therapy-induced cytotoxicity. PTEN/mTOR axis evidence (from glioma) reinforces that PI3K/AKT/PTEN status can modulate mTOR dependence. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: the chain explicitly highlights rapamycin efficacy signals (lowest IC50 in a high-risk group and links between TP53 mutation and rapamycin response), making therapy sensitivity the most directly testable and clinically actionable endpoint. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broader clinical endpoints (OS/PFI) to keep the question mechanistic and focused on actionable drug sensitivity; I omitted other contextual cancers (glioma/BLCA/READ) from the core claim to avoid conflating tissue-specific effects — they remain in secondary_modifiers for exploratory follow-up?

## Working Hypothesis

In breast tumors, loss of TP53 function creates a survival dependency on mTORC1 signaling when autophagy is suppressed. In TP53-mutant, TIL-low tumors, impaired autophagy plus mTORC1 dependence renders these tumors more sensitive to rapamycin (therapy_response proxy). Conversely, in tumors with high TIL density and elevated LAG3 expression indicative of T cell exhaustion, rapamycin efficacy is attenuated due to immune-context limitations; thus rapamycin alone yields a lower therapy_response in TP53-wildtype or TIL-high/LAG3-high settings. These hypotheses imply that TP53-mutant/TIL-low tumors will show greater rapamycin-mediated tumor control, whereas TP53-wildtype or TIL-high/LAG3-high tumors will require combination strategies (e.g., rapamycin plus LAG3 blockade) to achieve comparable responses. The anchor genes are TP53 and LAG3; the imaging feature is TIL density; the endpoint is therapy_response to rapamycin.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
