## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are not universally predictive of intravesical BCG response in NMIBC. Their predictive value is contingent on two tumor-derived states: (1) ADNP-enriched circulating tumor cell (CTC) shedding, which reflects systemic tumor payload and may indicate dynamic antigen release/immune interaction, and (2) MSH6 status-driven mismatch repair dysfunction, which elevates mutational load and neoantigen quality. We hypothesize that: a) in patients with low ADNP-CTC shedding and intact MSH6, TLS maturity correlates positively with BCG response; b) in patients with high ADNP-CTC shedding and/or MSH6 deficiency (high bTMB), TLS presence fails to predict response due to altered antigen presentation and potential immune escape; c) a composite biomarker integrating TLS metrics (density and maturation), ADNP-CTC signal, and MSH6/bTMB will predict BCG responders vs non-responders with higher accuracy than any single marker. This model implies mechanistic interactions where local immune organization is necessary but not sufficient, and systemic tumor payload plus repair-defective mutational landscapes modulate whether TLS translates into effective anti-tumor immunity. Predictions include: (i) TLS maturity alone will predict response only in MSH6-intact and low ADNP-CTC contexts; (ii) ADNP-CTC positivity will associate with non-response even when TLS is present; (iii) the best discriminatory power will come from a combined scoring system (TLS maturity × ADNP-CTC status × MSH6/bTMB) with superior AUC over single-parameter models; (iv) non-invasive methylation markers (SDC2, TFPI2) will parallel shedding signals and reinforce the interpretation of ADNP-CTC data. Feasibility-wise, this requires concurrent tissue TLS assessment, CTC RNA analysis, germline/sporadic MSH6 evaluation, bTMB calculation, and cfDNA methylation profiling in a prospective NMIBC cohort undergoing BCG.
