# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 analysis highlights a paradox in MIBC — tumors with high neoantigen load (MSI/dMMR or high bTMB) should be immunogenic and respond to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, yet many fail to benefit; convergent paths implicate TGF-β–driven stromal/fibrotic programs that may physically and functionally exclude effector T cells and blunt immunotherapy. 2) Tier 1 paths used: the TGFB (TGF-β) → fibrosis/EMT → stromalization axis (CORE PATH 1) provides the mechanism for a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR), and the MSI/dMMR → neoantigen → immunotherapy-sensitivity axis (CORE PATH 3) supplies the immunogenicity axis; these directly set up the puzzle of why immunogenic tumors sometimes fail to respond. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: S100A9/PD-L1 and bTMB (CORE PATH 2) explain inflammatory modulation of checkpoint expression and neoantigen burden as modifiers of response; Immunoscore and urine-DNA methylation provide orthogonal, clinically accessible assays to stratify immune context and tumor burden, and PTGDS/SARIFA offer cross-context phenotypes that could refine stratification in validation studies. 4) Choice of endpoint: therapy_response (PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint blockade efficacy proxy) is the most directly relevant clinical consequence of the immune vs stromal balance and is actionable in trials — testing this endpoint can immediately inform combination therapy strategies (e.g., anti–TGF-β + ICI). 5) Deliberate exclusions and why: I excluded exploratory/systemic markers with weak MIBC mechanistic links (CSF AD biomarkers, systemic metabolic panel, Gleason-analogs) to keep the hypothesis sharp; I also excluded PD-L1 and S100A9 from the core two-anchor pair to respect the 2-anchor limit and because they are best treated as secondary modifiers that modulate the core TGFB1–MSI interaction?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) driven by elevated TGFB1 signaling establishes a fibrotic, TGF-β–mediated tumor microenvironment that functionally excludes CD8+ T cells and suppresses neoantigen-driven immunity, even in tumors with MSI/dMMR (or high bTMB). Consequently, MSI/dMMR MIBC with high TSR will exhibit reduced response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade compared with MSI/dMMR MIBC with low TSR; and preclinical or clinical co-inhibition of TGFB1/TGF-β signaling with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade will restore T-cell infiltration and improve therapeutic responses in the high-TSR, MSI/dMMR subset. Secondary modifiers (e.g., PD-L1 expression, Immunoscore, S100A9, and noninvasive markers like urine DNA methylation) may modulate the magnitude of effect but are not required for the core mechanistic link.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
