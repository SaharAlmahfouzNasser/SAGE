# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

In MIBC, histologic T-cell infiltration (TILs) should predict benefit from PD-1 blockade (pembrolizumab), but many TIL-high tumors fail to respond; what immune-inhibitory mechanism blunts pembrolizumab efficacy? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) ClinicalTrial evidence that pembrolizumab is being tested in muscle-invasive bladder cancer establishes the therapy-response clinical context; (b) BLCA/urothelial carcinoma anchoring ensures disease- and histology-specific relevance; (c) LAG3 as a core immune-checkpoint node provides a plausible mechanism of resistance. These together motivate testing whether LAG3-mediated inhibition explains nonresponse in TIL-rich MIBC. 3) Tier 2 support: the path indicating LAG3 expression in cancer cells strengthens the biological plausibility that intratumoral LAG3 (on tumor cells and/or T cells) can maintain T-cell dysfunction; the BCG path provides a clinical precedent that modulating local immunity can alter responsiveness and suggests combination/priming strategies. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response because the Ontologist centered the analysis on pembrolizumab clinical trials in MIBC—response to PD-1 blockade is the most direct, actionable endpoint for trial-informed mechanistic hypotheses. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded ambiguous cross-cancer edges (e.g., READ↔LAG3, BLCA-as-protective-for-LAG3) because their directionality is unclear; I did not introduce PDCD1 as a separate anchor gene to avoid inventing genes not present in the Ontologist output—pembrolizumab defines the PD-1 axis context without adding new anchors. I chose TIL density over TLS/TSR because the central puzzle concerns why TIL-high tumors sometimes fail to benefit from PD-1 blockade?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder/urothelial carcinoma (MIBC), tumors with high Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte (TIL) density and elevated LAG-3 expression within the tumor microenvironment exhibit impaired responses to pembrolizumab (PD-1 blockade) due to LAG-3–mediated T-cell exhaustion that is not fully reversible by PD-1 inhibition alone. This predicts that: (a) within TIL-high tumors, LAG-3–high status correlates with lower objective response rates to pembrolizumab compared with LAG-3–low status; (b) dual blockade of PD-1 and LAG-3 will improve response rates in the TIL-high/LAG-3–high subset compared with PD-1 blockade alone; and (c) intravesical BCG therapy or priming strategies modulate the TME to reduce LAG-3–mediated inhibition or enhance T-cell activation, thereby shifting nonresponders toward responders when followed by PD-1–targeted therapy or enabling more pronounced benefit from PD-1+LAG-3 blockade. Testing plan: (i) correlate pre-treatment TIL density and LAG-3 expression (on T cells and, if present, tumor cells) with pembrolizumab response in MIBC cohorts; (ii) in preclinical models and available organoid/immune-competent systems, compare PD-1 blockade alone vs PD-1+LAG-3 blockade in TIL-high tumors and assess functional restoration (cytokine production, proliferation, cytotoxicity); (iii) evaluate whether prior BCG exposure alters LAG-3 expression or TIL activation and modifies subsequent response to PD-1–targeted therapy. Endpoint to test is therapy_response, with secondary endpoints including progression-free survival and biomarker readouts (LAG-3 expression level, spatial co-localization with T cells, co-expression of other exhaustion markers).
