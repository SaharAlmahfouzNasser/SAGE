## Working Hypothesis

In epithelial tumors with high TIL density, loss of CDH1 drives EMT-associated architectural changes that create immune-excluded microdomains, reducing productive T cell–tumor cell interactions. Concurrently, upregulation of LAG3 on infiltrating T cells reflects an exhausted/dysfunctional state that suppresses cytotoxic activity. The combination of CDH1-low and LAG3-high defines a TIL-rich but functionally immunosuppressive phenotype that predicts poor response to immune checkpoint blockade (anti-PD-1/PD-L1), even relative to CDH1-high/LAG3-low tumors with similar TIL densities. MSI status and KRAS-driven signaling modulate this axis by shaping antigenicity and inflammatory signaling, thereby altering the magnitude of the CDH1–LAG3–TIL interaction and its impact on therapy response. Therefore, the following testable predictions arise: (1) In TIL-high tumors, regions with CDH1 downregulation will co-localize with immune-excluded zones and reduced T cell activation markers; (2) LAG3 expression on T cells will correlate with exhaustion markers (e.g., PD-1, TIM-3) and be higher in CDH1-low tumors; (3) In vitro and ex vivo systems where CDH1 is knocked down or knocked out in epithelial tumor models will show reduced T cell–mediated killing unless LAG3 signaling is blocked; (4) In vivo models and patient cohorts, a combined signature of CDH1-low/LAG3-high among TIL-rich tumors will predict inferior response to ICIs, with the magnitude of the effect modulated by MSI status and KRAS signaling; (5) Therapeutic benefit may be observed with combination strategies targeting both PD-1/PD-L1 and LAG3 specifically in CDH1-low contexts, particularly where MSI KRAS-related signaling amplifies the immunosuppressive milieu.
