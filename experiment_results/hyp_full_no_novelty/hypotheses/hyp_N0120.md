# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer, high intratumoral TIL density does not predict pembrolizumab response when tumors harbor oncogenic KRAS mutations because KRAS-driven signaling programs a locally immunosuppressive microenvironment that impairs CD8+ T cell effector function and diminishes PD-1 blockade efficacy; TP53 mutations modulate this effect by reshaping the neoantigen landscape and antigen presentation context. Therefore, among TIL-high MIBC tumors, KRAS-mutant status will be associated with a lower objective response rate to pembrolizumab compared with KRAS-wildtype tumors, with TP53 mutation status providing context-dependent modulation of response via changes in neoantigen burden. This yields testable predictions: (1) the ORR is lower in TIL-high/KRAS-mutant vs. TIL-high/KRAS-wildtype tumors; (2) the magnitude of this effect is modulated by TP53 status (mutant vs. wild-type) due to differential neoantigen load and recognition potential.

# 2. Why This Gene — Not Others

## Primary hypothesis gene(s)

**KRAS:** KRAS because oncogenic KRAS signaling is mechanistically poised to reprogram the tumor microenvironment toward immunosuppression and impair T cell function; I included TP53 as a second anchor because TP53 mutation status is tightly associated with urothelial carcinoma and can reshape the tumor mutational/antigen landscape.

**TP53:** TP53↔urothelial carcinoma association suggest DNA-damage–driven changes in neoantigen burden and genetic context that could modulate immune recognition and checkpoint efficacy.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
