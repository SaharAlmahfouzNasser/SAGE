## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), tumors that harbor MSH6 deficiency exhibit elevated mutational/neoantigen load (high TMB) and are thus primed for immune recognition. However, an activated TGFB1–driven fibrotic stroma creates a physical and signaling barrier that impedes CD8+ T-cell infiltration and effector function. The net immunotherapy response in MIBC is governed by the interaction between antigenicity (MSH6-driven TMB/neoantigen landscape) and stromal gatekeeping (TGFB1 signaling manifested as high stromal content). Specifically, among MSH6-deficient/high-TMB MIBC tumors, a low stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR_low) predicts a higher likelihood of response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, whereas a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR_high) predicts primary resistance due to T-cell exclusion mediated by TGFB1 signaling. TGFB1 activity, as reflected by pSMAD2/3 signaling and desmoplastic/stromal markers (e.g., α-SMA, COL1A1), should correlate positively with TSR and negatively with therapy response. Consequently, combining TGF-β pathway inhibition with immune checkpoint blockade will preferentially improve responses in TSR_high/MSH6-deficient tumors by converting a “cold/excluded” microenvironment into a more permissive, T-cell–inflamed state. This model yields testable predictions and supports a biomarker strategy centered on (i) TSR from digital pathology, (ii) MSH6 status/TMB, and (iii) TGFB1 pathway activation as a functional readout.
