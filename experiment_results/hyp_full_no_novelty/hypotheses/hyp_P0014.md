# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

NMIBC tumors with molecular signs of high immunogenicity (high TMB and MSI/dMMR) nonetheless show variable clinical benefit from immunotherapies—why does neoantigen richness not always translate into effective local anti-tumor immunity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the Immunoscore/TIL axis by selecting a spatial immune-organization imaging feature (TLS-like lymphoid aggregates) and combined it with the two Tier-1 molecular immunogenicity anchors (MSI/dMMR and TMB) because these three elements form the mechanistic triad: neoantigen supply (TMB/MSI) → local immune organization (TLS/TIL) → effector function. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: MSH6 (MMR component) supports MSI biology; liquid biopsy (ctDNA/CTCs) provides a way to measure dynamic TMB/MSI changes and detect emerging resistance; ER status, GARS1 and SARIFA provide orthogonal tumor-intrinsic or microenvironmental modifiers that could explain discordant outcomes. 4) Why I chose therapy_response as the endpoint: the central translational question is about variable benefit from immunotherapy in NMIBC (clinical decision-making and trial stratification), so a therapy-response proxy (anti–PD-1/PD-L1 or BCG response) is the most actionable endpoint. 5) What I deliberately EXCLUDED and why: I excluded broad/general 'biomarkers' and non-specific tumor-biomarker paths (Tier 3 generics) to keep the hypothesis mechanistically tight; I also kept Tier-2 modulators out of the core claim to respect the 1 imaging + max 2-anchor constraint and to preserve a clear causal question about how spatial immune structures gate neoantigen-driven responses?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC treated with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are required to convert high neoantigen burden (as indicated by MSI/dMMR and high TMB) into an effective local anti-tumor T cell response. Specifically, tumors that are TLS-high and MSI/dMMR-positive or TMB-high will show higher objective therapy response rates than TLS-low counterparts with equivalent TMB/MSI status. Conversely, TLS-low tumors with high TMB or MSI/dMMR will exhibit limited responses due to immune exclusion and poor local priming. TLS-high tumors with low TMB/MSI may have intermediate responses, underscoring that TLS acts as a critical context for neoantigen-driven immunity. The primary testable prediction is a significant interaction between TLS status (imaging feature) and neoantigen burden (TMB and MSI/dMMR) on therapy_response in NMIBC patients receiving checkpoint blockade.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
