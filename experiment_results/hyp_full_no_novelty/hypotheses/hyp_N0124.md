# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The provided path shows PROS1 is expressed in tumors and associated with Ki-67, raising the question of why immune organization seen on H&E (e.g., TLS/TIL) does not always translate into better clinical outcomes — could tumor-expressed PROS1 drive proliferation/immune modulation that negates lymphoid aggregate benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Within TCGA-BRCA, TLS-rich breast tumors that also have high tumor-derived PROS1 expression and high MKI67 (Ki-67) exhibit a net pro-proliferative and immune-modulatory phenotype driven by PROS1-TAM receptor signaling, which compromises TLS-associated anti-tumor activity and leads to worse overall survival (OS) compared with TLS-rich tumors with low PROS1 expression. This implies a PROS1–MKI67 axis that links tumor PROS1 to proliferation and macrophage-mediated immune modulation, such that: (1) among TLS-high tumors, PROS1 is positively correlated with MKI67; (2) TLS-high/PROS1-high tumors have poorer OS than TLS-high/PROS1-low tumors after adjusting for covariates (age, stage, subtype, tumor purity, etc.); (3) a TAM-receptor signaling signature mediates this association; and (4) in vitro assays show that PROS1 overexpression or PROS1-containing conditioned media increases proliferation and skews macrophages toward a pro-tumor (M2-like) phenotype in a MerTK-dependent manner. This framework yields testable predictions and supports a mechanistic link between PROS1, tumor proliferation, TLS architecture, and clinical outcome in breast cancer.
