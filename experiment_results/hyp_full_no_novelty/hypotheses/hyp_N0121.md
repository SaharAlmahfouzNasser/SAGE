# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist's path raises the question why organized immune architecture (e.g., TLS) that normally portends effective antitumor immunity does not always translate into improved therapeutic efficacy — specifically, why immune-adjuvant agents (like Fc–IL-4) or T cell–directed therapies fail in some tumors despite visible lymphoid organization. 2) Tier 1 path used: the single high-priority path linking tumor-associated Etv5 → negative regulation of antitumor immunity → modulation of Fc–IL-4 activity → altered efficacy of CD19-CAR-T therapy; I built the hypothesis on the Etv5 → antitumor immunity node because it provides a tumor-intrinsic mechanistic lever that can disable otherwise favorable immune architecture. 3) Tier 2/supporting evidence: the path elements 'antitumor immunity' and 'Fc–IL-4' support a model where immune functional state (not just presence) gates cytokine/adjuvant effects; this justifies using an imaging proxy of organized immunity (TLS-like aggregates) to probe functional competence. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected a therapy_response endpoint because the path explicitly concerns enhancement of cellular immunotherapy efficacy (Fc–IL-4 boosting CAR-T), and a therapy-response question is the most actionable clinical problem here. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded specifying CAR-T antigen biology (CD19) and other downstream molecular actors from the core hypothesis to keep focus on the tumor-intrinsic immunosuppressive mechanism (ETV5) that could broadly blunt T cell–directed therapies; those factors and broader immune gene sets are listed as secondary modifiers for follow-up?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-rich tumors with high tumor-cell ETV5 expression fail to mount effective T cell–mediated antitumor responses due to an ETV5-driven transcriptional program that promotes immunosuppressive mediators and dampens TLS effector functions, thereby blunting the therapeutic benefits of T cell–directed therapies and Fc–IL-4–mediated adjuvant effects. Reducing ETV5 activity (genetic knockdown/CRISPRi or pharmacologic targeting) or applying Fc–IL-4–type adjuvant therapy will restore TLS effector function and improve therapy response. These effects should be detectable in models of CD19-CAR-T–like therapy and general T cell–directed immunotherapies, enabling a biomarker-driven strategy to stratify patients who may need ETV5-targeted or Fc–IL-4–modulated regimens.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In vitro: manipulate ETV5 in tumor cells (overexpression/knockdown) and co-culture with engineered or patient-derived T cells; assess T cell activation (CD69, IFN-γ), cytotoxicity (granzyme B, perforin), and TLS-associated markers; test Fc–IL-4 effects on cytokine release and myeloid/TLS remodeling.

**Prediction 2:** In vivo: use mouse models with ETV5-modulated tumors, implement adoptive T cell transfer or CAR-T–like therapy, with/without Fc–IL-4; quantify tumor growth, TLS density/organization (MECA-79, CD3/CD8, B cells), and intratumoral cytokine profiles.

**Prediction 3:** Omics: RNA-seq and scRNA-seq to define ETV5 targets and TLS-immune cell states; ChIP-seq to map direct ETV5 targets.
