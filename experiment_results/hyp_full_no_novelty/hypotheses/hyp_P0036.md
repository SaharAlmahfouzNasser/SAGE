# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder tumors that are MSI/dMMR and exhibit TLS-like immune organization, tumor cell–intrinsic upregulation of the translation initiation factor eIF4E reprograms the tumor translatome to suppress effective TLS-driven anti-tumor immunity. Specifically, elevated eIF4E promotes translation programs that diminish antigen presentation capacity (e.g., reduced surface MHC class I/antigen-processing machinery) and concurrently increase immunosuppressive mediators and/or checkpoint ligands, leading to impaired TLS maturation/function and reduced CD8+ T cell cytotoxic activity. Clinically, among MSI/dMMR TLS-positive bladder cancers, high tumor eIF4E expression will predict poorer therapy_response to immune checkpoint blockade (anti–PD-1/PD-L1) despite the presence of organized TLS. Conversely, strategies that inhibit eIF4E‑dependent translation or combine translational control with checkpoint blockade will restore TLS functionality and enhance therapy_response in this subset. This hypothesis explicitly links a tumor-intrinsic translational regulator to immune microenvironment functionality and clinically actionable immunotherapy outcomes, while integrating TLS presence as a context-dependent readout rather than a sole predictor.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** MSI/dMMR tumors exhibit high neoantigen load and are often associated with TLS formation and improved response to checkpoint inhibitors, yet response heterogeneity suggests additional regulators of anti-tumor immunity.

**Prediction 2:** eIF4E is a pivotal translation initiation factor whose upregulation reprograms the translatome to drive oncogenic traits and has been implicated in modulating components of immune recognition and cytokine signaling, potentially affecting antigen presentation and the immunosuppressive milieu.

**Prediction 3:** Disrupting translation initiation or its downstream outputs has been proposed as a strategy to re-sensitize tumors to immunotherapy. Therefore, testing whether eIF4E-driven translational control suppresses TLS efficacy in MSI/dMMR bladder cancers and predicting therapy_response based on eIF4E status provides a focused, testable mechanism with clear therapeutic implications.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
