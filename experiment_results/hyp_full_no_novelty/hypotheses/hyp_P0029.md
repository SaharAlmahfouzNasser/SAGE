## Working Hypothesis

Hypothesis 1: In bladder cancer, the predictive value of high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density for response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade is contingent on concurrent mismatch-repair deficiency with MSH6 loss (MSH6 LOF), which drives MSI/dMMR and elevates tumor neoantigen burden—particularly indel-derived neoantigens—that license effective TIL-mediated cytotoxicity. As a result, within the TIL-high cohort, tumors that are MSI/dMMR due to MSH6 LOF will exhibit higher objective response rates (ORR), longer duration of response (DOR), and improved progression-free and overall survival (PFS/OS) to PD-1 blockade compared with TIL-high, microsatellite-stable (MSS) tumors. Hypothesis 2: Among TIL-high bladder cancers, those that are MSS will show limited benefit from PD-1 blockade despite immune infiltration, due to a relatively lower neoantigen load and/or a more suppressive microenvironment; noninvasive biomarkers such as circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) dynamics and urine DNA methylation will correlate with therapeutic response and can monitor early treatment effects. Together, these imply that TMB, ctDNA clearance, and urine methylation changes will align with clinical benefit primarily in the TIL-high MSI/dMMR (MSH6 LOF) subgroup, validating an integrated biomarker strategy for patient selection and dynamic monitoring.
