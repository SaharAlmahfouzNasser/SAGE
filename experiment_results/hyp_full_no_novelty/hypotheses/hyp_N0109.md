# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In prostate cancer with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), tumor-intrinsic loss of RASSF1A promotes an immunosuppressive microenvironment by enhancing TGF-β–driven Treg differentiation and maintaining an exhausted, LAG-3–dominant state in CD8+ T cells. This combination (RASSF1A inactivation plus elevated LAG3 and active TGF-β signaling) renders TILs functionally inert and predictive of poor response to single-agent checkpoint blockade (e.g., anti–PD-1/PD-L1). The hypothesis predicts that: (1) RASSF1A loss correlates with heightened TGF-β pathway activity and higher LAG3 expression on TILs; (2) blocking TGF-β signaling reduces Treg frequency and lowers LAG-3–driven exhaustion; (3) LAG-3 blockade alone will have limited efficacy in RASSF1A-null tumors but will show improved activity when combined with anti–PD-1 or anti–TGF-β in these contexts; (4) combinatorial strategies (anti–TGF-β plus anti-LAG-3, with or without anti–PD-1) will synergistically restore T cell effector function and enhance tumor control in RASSF1A-null, LAG3-high, TGF-β–active tumors. Clinically, this defines a biomarker-guided subset of TIL-high prostate cancers that are resistant to monotherapy but responsive to rational combination immunotherapies.

# 2. Why This Gene — Not Others

## Primary hypothesis gene(s)

**RASSF1A:** RASSF1A → is inactivated in → prostate cancer together with immune-checkpoint relevance of LAG3.

**LAG3:** LAG3 expression differences (BLCA/READ edges) and the promoter-like process acting as TGF-β provide ancillary support that immune-checkpoint context and TGF-β–like signals modulate checkpoint dominance; these strengthen the plausibility but are treated as secondary.


# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).

**Prediction 2:** In functional experiments, restoring or enhancing the anchor gene's signaling will improve immune-mediated tumor control in imaging-feature-positive models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
