# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that immune contexture (Immunoscore/TILs), genomic instability (MSI/dMMR), and dynamic tumor burden (ctDNA) each predict immunogenicity, yet some MIBC tumors with apparently favorable immune contexture (high TILs) or high mutational load still fail to derive durable benefit from immune checkpoint blockade; why are immune-infiltrated tumors sometimes non‑responsive?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), the predictive value of pre-treatment tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density for response to immune checkpoint blockade is contingent on mismatch repair (MMR) status defined by MLH1 and MSH2. Specifically, MLH1/MSH2 loss-of-function (MMR-deficient) tumors with high TIL density will show superior immunotherapy response (higher objective response rate and longer duration of response) compared with MLH1/MSH2-intact tumors, where high TIL density will have limited predictive value due to predominance of bystander or exhausted T cells. A combined biomarker model incorporating TIL density and MLH1/MSH2 LOF will outperform TIL density alone in discriminating responders from non-responders (therapy_response). Secondary modifiers—TP53 mutation status, GARS1 expression, urine DNA methylation panels, and HPV status—will modulate the strength of the TIL/MMR interaction and further refine misclassification, providing a more robust, multilevel framework for predicting immunotherapy outcomes in MIBC.
