A strong candidate biomarker that fits your constraints is the Tumor-Stroma Ratio (TSR) measured on H&E-stained slides.

What TSR is
- TSR quantifies the proportion of stroma relative to tumor epithelial tissue within the tumor region. A higher stromal fraction (i.e., more stroma and less tumor) is associated with a worse prognosis in several cancers, and accumulating evidence supports its prognostic value in bladder cancer, including non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC).

Why it’s suitable for NMIBC with only morphological/spatial features
- It relies solely on tissue morphology and spatial organization seen in H&E images.
- It captures the tumor microenvironmental context (stroma-rich environments can facilitate invasion and recurrence) without molecular assays.
- It provides a simple, interpretable metric (continuous stromal fraction or a binary high/low stroma category).

How to compute TSR from H&E images
- Delineate the tumor region on the H&E slide (can be done manually or via an automated tissue/region segmentation).
- Within the tumor region, classify tissue into two components: tumor epithelium and stroma. This can be done by:
  - Manual annotation by a pathologist, or
  - Automated image analysis (color-based segmentation, or a trained classifier/segmentation model trained on labeled H&E patches).
- Compute stromal fraction: stromal_area / (tumor_area + stromal_area) within the defined region.
- Interpret results:
  - Higher stromal fraction = stroma-high
  - Lower stromal fraction = stroma-low
  - You can use a predefined cutoff (commonly around 0.5 in many studies) or treat TSR as a continuous prognostic variable.

How to validate its prognostic value in NMIBC
- Data: NMIBC patients with TURBT, pathology slides, and adequate follow-up for progression and/or recurrence.
- Analyses:
  - Primary outcome: progression to muscle-invasive disease; secondary: recurrence.
  - Model: Cox proportional hazards with TSR (as a continuous predictor or a dichotomous variable) adjusting for known clinical factors (e.g., grade, stage Ta/T1, number of tumors, prior recurrences, adjuvant therapy).
  - Performance: discrimination (C-index), time-to-event curves, calibration.
- Practical considerations:
  - Ensure standardized slide preparation and scanning to minimize tissue processing variability.
  - Consider interobserver variability if manual segmentation is used; automated pipelines can improve reproducibility.
  - If possible, validate in an independent external cohort.

Optional complementary approach (still morphological/spatial)
- Tumor budding or immune cell spatial patterns (e.g., spatial distribution of lymphocytes) on the same H&E slides can be studied alongside TSR to build a multi-feature morphometric prognostic score for NMIBC.
