# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do organized local immune structures (TLS/TIL) in MIBC sometimes fail to predict benefit from immune checkpoint blockade—i.e., under what tumor-intrinsic conditions does local immune organization translate into a systemic, therapy-responsive anti-tumor immune state? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the bTMB and MSH6/TMB core paths because they form a mechanistic triad linking DNA-repair status (MSH6) to neoantigen generation (TMB/bTMB) and thereby to the effectiveness of pre-existing immune infiltrates. bTMB was prioritized as an anchor alongside MSH6 to emphasize a clinically deployable, non-invasive readout that captures systemic mutational heterogeneity relevant to therapy response. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: PD-L1 and S100A9 link innate/inflammatory regulation to checkpoint expression and may modulate whether lymphoid aggregates are functionally suppressive or activating; MSI/dMMR provides a mechanistic precedent for MMR-driven neoantigenicity; AI/accessibility arguments justify using bTMB as a scalable assay to triage patients. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected therapy_response (immune checkpoint therapy response) because the core mechanistic question connects neoantigen load and MMR status to immunotherapy sensitivity, and bTMB/msr biomarkers are most actionable in the context of treatment selection. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded exploratory metabolic and circulating metabolic marker paths (ALP/HDL-C/TG/SHBG/IGF-1), stool methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2), and peripheral prognostic signals (pZVR) from the core hypothesis because they are indirect, cross-tissue, or insufficiently mechanistic for the TLS–TMB–MMR interaction I focus on. I also relegated PD-L1, S100A9, TP53, MSI/dMMR and GARS1 to secondary modifiers rather than anchors to keep the hypothesis sharply mechanistic and testable?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary refined hypothesis: In urothelial bladder cancer (MIBC), the presence of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates predicts responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade only when the tumor exhibits high tumor-intrinsic neoantigen generation driven by MSH6 deficiency and elevated systemic neoantigen load as captured by high bTMB. Specifically, TLS-high, MSH6-deficient, and bTMB-high tumors will show superior objective response rates and longer progression-free survival to anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy compared with TLS-high tumors that are MSH6-proficient or have low bTMB, and TLS-high status alone will not reliably predict response in the absence of high neoantigen burden. This core relationship is modulated by secondary factors such as PD-L1 expression, S100A9+ myeloid cells, MSI/dMMR status, TP53 alterations, and GARS1, which can amplify or dampen the TLS-driven immunogenic potential but are not required for the fundamental TLS–neoantigen–ICI axis. Mechanistically, TLS provide localized antigen presentation and T cell priming, but productive systemic anti-tumor immunity under checkpoint blockade requires a sufficient pool of neoantigens to drive clonal expansion of neoantigen-specific T cells; MSH6 deficiency increases mutational load and neoantigen diversity, enabling TLS-driven priming to translate into systemic, therapy-responsive immunity. bTMB serves as a clinically actionable surrogate for systemic neoantigen load that, when integrated with TLS status and MSH6, yields improved prediction of ICI response beyond either biomarker alone. The hypothesis generates testable predictions: (i) TLS-high/MSH6-deficient/bTMB-high tumors will have higher response rates and longer PFS than TLS-high/MSH6-proficient/bTMB-low tumors; (ii) TLS-high/MSH6-deficient/bTMB-high tumors will show enriched cytotoxic T cell effector signatures and higher TCR clonality expansion post-therapy; (iii) TLS-high with low bTMB or intact MSH6 will not consistently predict response, even if TLS are present.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
