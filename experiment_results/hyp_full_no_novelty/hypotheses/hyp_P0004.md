# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that Immunoscore/MSI status are strong predictors of immunotherapy benefit in some cancers, yet many tumors with dense immune infiltration or high mutational load do not respond—why is immune presence sometimes decoupled from clinical benefit? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built primarily on CORE PATH 1 (Immunoscore → immune infiltration) and CORE PATH 3 (PD-L1/B7-H1 and eIF4E as immunotherapy-relevant tumor programs). I also excluded broad noninvasive panels (stool/urine methylation, radiomics) from the core claim to keep the mechanistic question sharp and experimentally tractable?

## Working Hypothesis

Expanded hypothesis: In breast tumors with high TIL density (immunoscore-high), elevated tumor-intrinsic translational activity driven by EIF4E results in increased PD-L1 (CD274) protein expression through enhanced cap-dependent translation, creating an adaptive immune resistance state that diminishes clinical benefit from anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy. Therefore, TIL-high / EIF4E-high / CD274-high tumors will show poorer therapy_response to checkpoint inhibition compared with TIL-high / EIF4E-low tumors. Furthermore, reducing EIF4E-driven translation (genetic knockdown or pharmacologic inhibition) will lower PD-L1 protein and restore T-cell–mediated cytotoxicity, and combination therapy with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 plus EIF4E pathway inhibition will produce superior tumor control in EIF4E-high models. Secondary markers (ctDNA dynamics, TMB-based radiomics, urine methylation markers, and MSI/dMMR status as a stratifier) can track and refine the predictive signal and distinguish alternative resistance mechanisms.
