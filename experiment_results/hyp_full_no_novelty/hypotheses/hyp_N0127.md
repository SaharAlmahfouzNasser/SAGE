# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that MSI-H tumors are "immune-bright" (higher immune cell infiltration) across cancers but show variable clinical outcomes; key tension is why immune infiltration (TILs) does not uniformly translate into tumor control. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSI-H → higher immune infiltration axis and the involvement of MSH6 (Tier 1) because MSH6 links the MSI-H genotype to the mutational processes that generate neoantigens and potentially alter clonal architecture and immune-recognition. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: regulators noted in Tier 2 (XBP1, PAQR4, IRF8, DICER1) offer plausible secondary mechanisms — metabolic/stress adaptation (XBP1/PAQR4) or immune-regulatory/miRNA modulation (IRF8/DICER1) — that can modify whether TILs effectuate tumor control, so they are included as secondary modifiers. 4) Why PFI as endpoint: Progression-free interval is the most sensitive clinical readout of whether local anti-tumor immunity achieves durable tumor control (less confounded by subsequent lines of therapy than OS), making it ideal to test immune–editing hypotheses in TCGA cohorts. 5) What I excluded/deliberately de-emphasized and why: I did not include cancer-type nodes like stage II melanoma or cRCC in the core hypothesis because the dataset context available (TCGA-BRCA H&E + genomics) favors a breast-focused, imaging-genomics test; I also excluded additional Tier 1/2 genes from the core mechanistic sentence to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on a single imaging feature (TIL density) and the MSI-H/MSH6 axis, moving other candidates (XBP1, PAQR4, IRF8, DICER1) into secondary_modifiers for follow-up interrogation?

## Working Hypothesis

In breast cancer cohorts with reliable MSI-H annotation, among tumors with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL-high), MSI-H tumors harboring loss or pathogenic mutation of MSH6 will exhibit shorter progression-free interval (PFI) than MSI-H tumors with intact MSH6, after adjusting for clinical covariates. This effect reflects MSH6-associated mismatch repair defects generating subclonal neoantigen heterogeneity and ongoing immune editing that uncouples TIL presence from effective tumor suppression. Secondary modifiers XBP1, PAQR4, IRF8, and DICER1 modulate the magnitude and direction of this effect by shaping tumor cell stress responses, antigen presentation, and immune signaling; for example, shifts in XBP1-driven UPR activity or altered IRF8/DICER1–mediated miRNA pathways may enhance immune escape, amplifying the PFI difference, whereas alternative states may diminish it. The primary prediction is that within the TIL-high MSI-H subset, MSH6 loss correlates with reduced PFI, and stratification by MSH6 status (and by the four modifiers) will improve prognostic discrimination beyond TIL density alone.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
