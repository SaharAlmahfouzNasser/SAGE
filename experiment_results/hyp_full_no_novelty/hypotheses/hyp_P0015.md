# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) treated with intravesical BCG or systemic immune checkpoint therapy, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in pretreatment tumors reflect an antigen-driven local response but their predictive value for clinical benefit is conditional on TLS maturation status and the systemic immuno-biological context. Specifically, TLS-rich tumors with high mutational/neoantigen burden due to high tumor mutational burden (TMB) or MSI/dMMR will more frequently form or attract mature TLS with germinal centers, supporting productive anti-tumor immunity and favorable responses. Conversely, TLS-rich tumors where TLS are immature or functionally exhausted (dense PD-1+, LAG-3+ T cells, suppressive myeloid cells) will exhibit limited durable responses, despite TLS presence, due to immunosuppressive microenvironments and impaired cytotoxic function. This dichotomy yields discordant clinical outcomes in TLS-positive NMIBC when stratified by mutational context. Importantly, circulating/urinary biomarkers (ADNP mRNA, circulating tumor cells [CTCs], urine DNA methylation patterns, GARS1) reflect tumor evolution and systemic immune milieu that modify TLS function and can predict discordance between tissue TLS status and treatment response. A composite model integrating (1) TLS maturation/status (e.g., germinal center markers, B cell and T cell composition, spatial organization), (2) tumor mutational/neoantigen context (TMB, MSI/dMMR), and (3) non-invasive liquid biomarkers (ADNP mRNA, CTC phenotype, urine DNA methylation, GARS1) will predict therapy response more accurately than TLS presence alone. This framework also implies that strategies aimed at promoting TLS maturation or reversing exhaustion could convert TLS-rich, high-neoantigen NMIBC into more responsive disease when combined with BCG or checkpoint inhibitors.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.
- Multi-modal validation pipeline: computational -> spatial -> functional, providing mechanistic depth beyond association.

## Caveats & Risks
