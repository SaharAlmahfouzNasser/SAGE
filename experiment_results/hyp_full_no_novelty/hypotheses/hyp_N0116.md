# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist's single-path analysis suggests EPB41L2-associated adhesion changes manifest as altered osteopontin-driven stromal remodeling, yet this state paradoxically aligns with signals of good prognosis linked to FGFR3. The puzzle is: under what microenvironmental configuration do adhesion-driven stromal changes translate into favorable, rather than adverse, clinical behavior? 2) Tier 1 path used: I built directly on the single EPB41L2 → Osteopontin → prognosis → FGFR3 path because it provides a clear mechanistic chain from tumor-cell adhesion (EPB41L2) to stromal remodeling (observable as TSR) and to a differentiation/prognosis signal (FGFR3). 3) Supporting evidence (Tier 2-style): EPB41L2 biology (cytoskeletal/adherence effects) plausibly drives ECM secretion/remodeling; osteopontin (SPP1) is a secreted ECM mediator that alters stromal composition; FGFR3 signaling is contextually linked to differentiation and better outcomes in some tumors—together these support a model where stromal state modulates FGFR3-associated tumor behavior. 4) Why OS chosen: the Ontologist framed outcomes as prognosis broadly; overall survival (OS) is the most robust, unambiguous clinical endpoint available in TCGA-BRCA and best captures the net impact of microenvironment–tumor interactions on patient outcome. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded SPP1 (osteopontin) from the core variable set to respect the 1–2 anchor gene limit and to keep the mechanistic hypothesis sharply focused on the adhesion→stroma→differentiation axis; SPP1 is retained as a secondary modifier to be tested as the putative mediator. I also excluded any additional speculative genes or immune features because they were not in the provided path?

## Working Hypothesis

EPB41L2-driven alterations in tumor cell adhesion promote a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR). When FGFR3 signaling is intact, this stromal-rich, adhesion-modulated state activates a differentiation-associated program that suppresses invasion and improves overall survival (OS). Osteopontin (SPP1) functions as a secondary ECM mediator that modulates this ECM-stroma–FGFR3 axis and influences the magnitude of the OS effect. Therefore, in breast cancer cohorts, tumors with high TSR and high EPB41L2 expression plus functional FGFR3 signaling will show the strongest OS advantage, with SPP1 levels modifying (amplifying or attenuating) this association.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
