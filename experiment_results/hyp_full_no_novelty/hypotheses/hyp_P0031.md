# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density is associated with improved cisplatin response only when the tumor circRNA and lncRNA landscape does not promote immune evasion. Specifically, elevated hsa_circ_0003221 in cancer stem cells drives stemness and immune-exclusion, attenuating TIL-mediated cytotoxicity and contributing to cisplatin resistance, whereas expression of lncRNA MEG3 supports tumor suppressive and immune-activating states that preserve chemosensitivity. Therefore, the predictive value of TIL density for cisplatin response is contingent on (i) low-to-moderate hsa_circ_0003221 expression and (ii) adequate MEG3 expression; combined assessment of these three factors (TIL density, hsa_circ_0003221, MEG3) provides superior prediction of therapy_response than TIL density alone. Mechanistically, hsa_circ_0003221 promotes CSC programs and immune evasion, potentially by miRNA sponging and modulation of pathways overlapping MEG3's tumor-suppressive/p53-related signaling; MEG3 loss removes a barrier to proliferation and dampens immune signaling. EIF4A3 and other RNA-processing factors may regulate circRNA abundance and thus modulate this axis. Testable predictions include: (a) in patient samples, the strongest cisplatin responders are TIL-high with low hsa_circ_0003221 and high MEG3; (b) in vitro, knockdown of hsa_circ_0003221 or overexpression of MEG3 increases cisplatin sensitivity in CSC-enriched bladder cancer models and enhances TIL/immune effector activity in co-culture; (c) in vivo, tumors with high circ_0003221 and low MEG3 show poor cisplatin response despite TIL presence, which can be rescued by targeting circRNA biogenesis or by MEG3 restoration or immune modulation.).

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).

**Prediction 2:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
