# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, the predictive value of tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density for PD-1 inhibitor response is contingent upon MSH6-driven mismatch repair dysfunction and a functional exhaustion state of T cells. Specifically, among dMMR/MSI-H bladder tumors, high TIL density coupled with elevated PDCD1 expression predicts poor response when accompanied by an exhaustion program (co-expression of PD-1 with LAG-3/TIM-3, high TOX, reduced cytotoxic markers), reflecting chronic antigen stimulation driven by MSH6-associated neoantigen landscapes. Conversely, high TIL density paired with a non-exhausted or less-exhausted T cell profile, or with intact MSH6 signaling, predicts better PD-1–blockade responses. A composite biomarker model that integrates (i) MSH6 status/dMMR cluster, (ii) PDCD1-expressing TIL functional state (exhaustion signature), (iii) quantitative TIL density, and (iv) secondary modifiers (GARS1 expression, stool methylation markers SDC2/TFPI2, TP53 status) will more accurately stratify patients by likelihood of PD-1 inhibitor benefit. This framework also identifies subgroups that may require combination strategies to reverse exhaustion or target compensatory tumor programs.
