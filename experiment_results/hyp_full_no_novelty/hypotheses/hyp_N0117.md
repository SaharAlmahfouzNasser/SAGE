# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Intravesical BCG provokes local immune infiltration (TILs) and upregulates PD-L1, yet many urothelial carcinomas fail to respond; why does TIL presence sometimes not translate into BCG clinical benefit? 2) Tier 1 paths used: TMPRSS2/ERG → cooperativity with PI3K pathway activation → increased PD-L1 (CD274). I built on this because it provides a direct tumor-intrinsic route (oncogenic fusion → PI3K signaling) to immune-checkpoint upregulation that can negate local immune effects. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: BCG itself up-regulates PD-L1 and binds urothelial cells (providing the setting where tumor PD-L1 matters); PSMA/FOLH1 and prostate-stem-cell links indicate how tumor lineage/oncogenic context can shape immune-modulatory programs; androgen-axis/AR signaling and PTEN/PIK3CA alterations are plausible modifiers of PI3K–PD-L1 coupling. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: the mechanistic chain centers on BCG-induced immunity and PD-L1–mediated suppression, so 'therapy response to BCG' is the most direct, actionable clinical endpoint and has immediate translational implications (predicting and reversing BCG failure). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded high-level nodes (Carcinoma, bladder organ anatomy) and peripheral prostate-treatment nodes (androgen deprivation) from the core hypothesis to keep focus on the tumor-intrinsic oncogenic → immune-checkpoint axis; AR and PSMA/lineage signals are retained only as secondary modifiers rather than core anchors?

## Working Hypothesis

In urothelial carcinoma treated with intravesical BCG, high TIL density alone does not guarantee an anti-tumor response if tumor-intrinsic PI3K pathway activation is present, as indicated by PTEN loss and/or PIK3CA mutation leading to PI3K-AKT-mTOR–driven upregulation of PD-L1 (CD274) on tumor cells. This PD-L1 overexpression suppresses infiltrating T cells, producing a dysfunctional immune milieu that undermines BCG efficacy. Therefore, tumors that are PD-L1 high (CD274 high) and PI3K-activated (PTEN loss and/or PIK3CA mutation) with prominent TILs will show poorer response to intravesical BCG compared with tumors lacking PI3K activation. This mechanistic axis predicts that combining BCG with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade and/or PI3K pathway inhibition will restore or enhance BCG responsiveness in TIL-high, CD274-high, PI3K-activated urothelial tumors.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
