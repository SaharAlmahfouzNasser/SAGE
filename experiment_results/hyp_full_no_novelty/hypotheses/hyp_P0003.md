# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some MIBC tumors respond to intravesical BCG while others do not, and can pretreatment H&E-detectable TLS morphology explain which tumors are ‘primed’ for effective BCG-driven adaptive immunity? 2) Tier 1 path used: the single provided path (Node 1 → Node 2): TLS morphology on H&E → BCG response in MIBC. I build directly on this because it links an H&E-accessible, spatial immune structure to therapy outcome and is immediately testable in retrospective cohorts. 3) Supporting evidence (Tier 2-like rationale): published immunology and pathology findings show that mature TLS (with germinal-center–like architecture) support local antigen presentation, B-cell maturation and T-cell priming in other tumor types, mechanisms plausibly required for BCG-induced tumor control. 4) Endpoint choice: therapy_response (BCG response) was chosen because the clinical question is specifically which patients will benefit from BCG; using a therapy-response endpoint aligns the mechanism (local adaptive priming) with the clinical action (BCG efficacy). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded molecular/genomic features and survival endpoints (OS, PFI) to keep the hypothesis tightly focused on an H&E-only, mechanistic explanation for differential BCG response and to allow direct validation from routine pathology slides; broader multi-omic modifiers and non-therapy endpoints can be explored later as secondary analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

BCG efficacy in bladder cancer requires local antigen presentation and B-cell/T-cell priming, and pretreatment histology that shows mature TLS with germinal-center–like architecture and proximal localization to tumor nests on H&E provides the structural substrate for effective BCG-driven immune activation; tumors with high density of mature TLS near tumor areas will thus have higher therapy_response to BCG. Therefore, in MIBC patients treated with intravesical BCG, pretreatment slides demonstrating abundant mature TLS (GC-like) and favorable peritumoral proximity will predict higher therapy_response rates compared with tumors lacking such TLS features.
