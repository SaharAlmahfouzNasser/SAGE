# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density does not reliably predict favorable response to intravesical BCG when tumors harbor TP53 loss-of-function and elevated estrogen receptor (ER) signaling. TP53 dysfunction impairs antigen processing/presentation and reshapes the local cytokine milieu toward immune suppression, while ER-driven transcription dampens interferon signaling and upregulates immune checkpoint pathways (e.g., PD-L1), collectively creating a nonpermissive microenvironment that overrides the beneficial impact of TILs. Consequently, a combined phenotype of TIL_high + TP53_mutant/ER_high will exhibit lower BCG response rates and higher recurrence compared with other immune-phenotypes. A composite biomarker integrating TIL density, TP53 status, and ER activity will outperform TIL density alone for patient stratification and will identify candidates for combination therapies (e.g., BCG plus PD-1/PD-L1 blockade or agents that enhance antigen presentation or counteract ER-driven immunosuppression). This hypothesis generates testable predictions across clinical outcomes, molecular signatures, and functional assays, with noninvasive urine methylation readouts serving as correlative biomarkers of tumor immune-phenotype and treatment response.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In NMIBC patients treated with BCG, those with TIL_high and TP53_mut/ER_high tumors will have lower complete response rates and shorter recurrence-free survival than other groups.

**Prediction 2:** Tumor tissue from TP53_mut/ER_high tumors will show reduced MHC-I/antigen presentation machinery and elevated PD-L1 with weakened IFN-γ signaling signatures.

**Prediction 3:** Functional assays will reveal that TP53_LOF + ER_activation blunts BCG-induced immune activation in vitro/ex vivo.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Gene expression does not equal protein activity; phospho-protein or functional assays are needed for confirmation.

## Alternative Directions

- Immunoscore: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- PD-L1: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
