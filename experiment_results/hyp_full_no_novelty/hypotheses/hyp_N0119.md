# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Clinical trials combine pembrolizumab with chemotherapy (cisplatin-containing regimens) expecting TLS-rich tumors to benefit from enhanced T-cell immunity, yet KRAS-driven signaling and therapy-induced DNA damage could modulate this expected benefit. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the KRAS → cell death / DNA-damage context and the cisplatin → mutation axis because they directly suggest mechanisms by which tumor-intrinsic KRAS activity could alter the antigenic consequences of cisplatin and thereby affect immune-mediated therapy. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: the clinical-trial/pembrolizumab context and the notion that chemotherapy induces cell death and mutations support testing whether chemo-induced neoantigen generation or DNA-damage responses are altered by KRAS status. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response because the path centers on pembrolizumab + chemotherapy combinations and the immediate translational question is who benefits from that regimen. 5) Exclusions: I deliberately excluded tissue-specific (colon) generalization and treating "Mutation" as the primary anchor to keep the hypothesis mechanistically sharp; those remain as secondary_modifiers for exploratory stratification?

## Working Hypothesis

In TLS-rich tumors, activating KRAS mutations rewire the cisplatin-induced DNA-damage response and antigen processing pathways, leading to a qualitatively altered neoantigen landscape and reduced MHC-mediated presentation. This dampens TLS-driven T-cell activation and effector function, thereby reducing the efficacy of pembrolizumab plus cisplatin-containing chemotherapy compared with TLS-rich KRAS-wild-type tumors. The magnitude of this effect will correlate with measurable DDR/antigen-presentation perturbations (e.g., increased/f altered γH2AX signaling, reduced TAP1/MHC I expression) and with TLS-associated immune activity markers (lower IFN-γ signature, diminished TLS B/T cell activity) in KRAS-mutant versus KRAS-wild-type TLS-rich tumors.
