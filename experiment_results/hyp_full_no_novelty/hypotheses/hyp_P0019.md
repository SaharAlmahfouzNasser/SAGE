# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Although intravesical BCG elicits local immune activation that should clear residual tumor and reduce NMIBC recurrence, a subset of patients still progress to muscle-invasive disease—what tumor-intrinsic features blunt BCG-driven local immunity and cause BCG failure? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) BCG as the principal intravesical immune therapy establishes the importance of local immune organization; (b) CDKN2A silencing provides a concrete tumor-intrinsic mechanism that drives cell-cycle deregulation and aggressive behavior that could oppose immune control. I built the hypothesis on the tension between BCG-driven local immunity (manifested as TLS-like lymphoid aggregates) and CDKN2A loss. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: transcriptomic/protein-coding readouts can detect CDKN2A silencing and immune programs; progression markers (e.g., hydronephrosis) and broader drug activity context (cisplatin) frame the clinical consequences and alternative therapies for tumors that fail BCG. 4) Why I chose therapy_response as the endpoint: the ontologist’s core axis centers on BCG efficacy in NMIBC (a treatment-response problem), so asking why some tumors fail BCG directly targets a clinical decision point (who benefits from BCG vs. early cystectomy or systemic therapy). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded cisplatin as a core anchor in the hypothesis sentence to keep the mechanistic question tightly focused on BCG–tumor interactions and CDKN2A; other specific genes not present in Tier 1 were omitted to respect the 1–2 anchor-gene limit and avoid speculative catch-all models?

## Working Hypothesis

Expanded hypothesis: In non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) treated with intravesical BCG, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates form in the tumor microenvironment and are associated with improved local tumor control, but the durability of this immune response is determined by tumor CDKN2A status. Specifically, CDKN2A silencing (via promoter hypermethylation, homozygous deletion/LOH, or reduced transcript/protein expression) drives cell-cycle deregulation and reprograms tumor-intrinsic pathways toward immune evasion, blunting TLS-mediated clearance. Consequently: (i) TLS-high, CDKN2A-intact tumors will exhibit higher BCG response rates and longer recurrence-free survival (RFS) compared with TLS-high, CDKN2A-silenced tumors; (ii) TLS-low tumors will exhibit poorer response irrespective of CDKN2A status, but CDKN2A silencing may still predict worse outcomes within TLS-high groups; (iii) in patients with TLS-high but CDKN2A-silenced tumors, early progression or recurrence will be enriched, indicating a need for alternative perioperative or systemic strategies. This framework yields testable predictions and a clinically actionable stratification of NMIBC patients receiving BCG.
