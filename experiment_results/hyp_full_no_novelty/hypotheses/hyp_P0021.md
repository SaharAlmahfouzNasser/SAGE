# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist identified three complementary noninvasive biomarker axes—local epigenetic signals in urine, tumor transcriptional programs captured in CTCs (ADNP), and systemic mutational load (bTMB)—but it remains unclear why organized local immune structures (TLS/TIL) sometimes fail to translate into effective anti-tumor responses after BCG in NMIBC. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) urine-DNA methylation as a local tumor epigenetic readout (diagnostic/monitoring axis), (b) ADNP mRNA in CTCs as a tumor-intrinsic transcriptional/programmatic indicator of aggressiveness and immune modulation, and (c) bTMB as a systemic proxy for neoantigen load and immunogenicity; together these offer a mechanistic triad linking tumor-intrinsic programs, antigenicity, and local immune organization. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: MSH6 (MMR) and PD-L1 provide mechanistic links between mutational processes and checkpoint-mediated dysfunction; GARS1 links translation-related stress/immune signaling that could alter TLS maturation; these support plausible mechanisms by which ADNP and low bTMB might blunt TLS functionality. 4) Why therapy_response was chosen as the endpoint: the strongest clinical need and mechanistic links in the Ontologist analysis center on BCG and immunotherapy responsiveness in NMIBC—harmonizing local and systemic biomarkers has greatest impact as a therapy-response stratifier. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded cross-cancer stool-methylation concepts, systemic metabolic markers (HDL-C/IGF-1 panel), and other exploratory paths from the core hypothesis to preserve mechanistic focus on immune organization (TLS), tumor transcriptional state (ADNP), and antigenicity (bTMB); these remain as exploratory secondary analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, high ADNP expression in circulating tumor cells (CTCs) drives an tumor-intrinsic transcriptional program that disrupts TLS maturation and function, as reflected by imaging-detected TLS-like aggregates that are structurally or functionally immature; this effect is amplified when systemic bTMB is low, reducing neoantigen supply and TLS-driven anti-tumor activity. Consequently, NMIBC tumors with TLS present but elevated ADNP and low bTMB will show poor therapy_response to intravesical BCG, identifying patients who may require alternative or combination immunomodulatory strategies.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
