# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some MIBC tumors with apparent genomic immunogenicity (high mutational/repair deficiency signals) still fail to respond to immune checkpoint blockade? The Ontologist's synthesis framed this as an interaction between tumor-intrinsic genomic instability (MSH6/TMB) and a TGF-β–driven, fibrotic/immunosuppressive stroma that may exclude or incapacitate effector immunity?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), MSH6 deficiency elevates neoantigen burden and immunogenic potential, yet this potential remains unrealized when the tumor microenvironment is dominated by a TGF-β–driven, fibrotic stroma that excludes or impairs effector T cells. Therefore, the predictive strength of MSH6 deficiency for durable response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICI) is contingent on stromal context, specifically stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) and TGF-β signaling activity. The refined, testable proposition is: 

- Primary prediction: Among MIBC tumors, those with high TSR (TGF-β–driven fibrosis) will exhibit poor response to ICI even if MSH6 is deficient, whereas low-TSR, MSH6-deficient tumors will show superior ICI responses. 
- Secondary prediction: In MSH6-deficient, high-TSR tumors, concurrent TGF-β pathway inhibition will remodel the stroma, reduce ECM density, increase CD8+ T-cell infiltration and activation, and convert non-responders to responders or improve response durability when combined with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy. 
- Tertiary prediction: In MSH6-proficient tumors, TSR/TGF-β status will similarly modulate response to ICI, but the magnitude will be smaller and could still be augmented by TGF-β blockade. 
- Mechanistic bridge: MSH6 loss increases neoantigen load, creating targetable T cells, whereas TGF-β–driven CAF activation enforces an immune-exclusion barrier; removing or remodeling this barrier enables neoantigen-specific T cells to engage tumor cells, restoring or enhancing ICI efficacy. 
- Biomarker framework: Baseline TSR and MSH6 status will be primary predictors of ICI response; secondary modifiers include TGF-β signaling readouts (pSMAD2/3, TGFB signature), immunoscore, and noninvasive surrogates (urine DNA methylation, AI-derived TSR/TGF-β activity scores, bTMB) to stratify patients and monitor dynamic changes during therapy.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** MSH6 loss contributes to high mutational burden and potential neoantigen generation, which can sensitize tumors to ICI;

**Prediction 2:** TGF-β–driven fibrotic stroma promotes immune exclusion by increasing ECM deposition and CAF activity, impeding CD8+ T-cell infiltration and function. Spatially resolved analyses across cancers have shown that high TSR and active TGF-β signaling correlate with poor ICI responses, while TGF-β blockade or stromal remodeling can synergize with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy.

**Prediction 3:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
