# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist framed that high neoantigen-generating states (high TMB or dMMR/MSI) and intratumoral immune infiltration (CD4/CD8 TILs) each associate with immunotherapy benefit in MIBC, yet not all TIL-rich tumors respond — why does immune presence sometimes fail to translate into checkpoint-blockade benefit? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the TMB path (core mechanistic link between mutational load → neoantigens → immune recognition) and the dMMR/MSI path (mechanism producing high TMB/MSI-H phenotype), because these most directly explain when TILs are likely to be productive effectors. 3) Tier 2 support: non‑invasive readouts (CTCs, urine methylation) and PD‑1/PD‑L1 biology provide orthogonal ways to detect tumor antigenicity, systemic tumor burden, and immunosuppressive signaling that can modulate the TIL–neoantigen interaction; GARS1 offers a candidate tumor-intrinsic modifier of immune biology for follow-up. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (anti–PD‑1/PD‑L1 responsiveness) because the core question is mechanistic—under what molecular conditions does immune infiltration convert into clinical benefit from checkpoint blockade in MIBC. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded exploratory Tier 3 items (BCG/urothelial mucosal immunity, TP53/ER links) to keep a focused, testable mechanistic hypothesis driven by neoantigen load and local immune contexture; I also avoided adding more than two molecular anchors to preserve clarity and falsifiability?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), the clinical benefit of anti–PD-1/PD-L1 therapy requires both sufficient tumor antigenicity and an activated local immune effector milieu. We hypothesize a combinatorial, mechanistic model: (1) High tumor antigenicity, proxied by elevated tumor mutational burden (TMB) or dMMR/MSI-H status, increases neoantigen load available for T cell recognition; (2) Robust effector TIL presence (high CD8+ and/or CD4+ TIL density with productive functional status) is necessary to translate neoantigen recognition into tumor cell killing upon checkpoint blockade. Consequently, anti–PD-1/PD-L1 therapy will yield the greatest objective responses and longer PFS/OS in tumors that (a) are TMB-high or MSI-H/dMMR and (b) exhibit high TIL density and functional T cell activity; tumors with high TMB/MSI-H but low TILs, or high TILs but low antigenicity, will show attenuated benefits. A two-step refinement is anticipated: (i) neoantigen quality (e.g., clonality, MHC-binding predicted affinity) and T cell exhaustion markers will modulate the TIL–neoantigen interaction and the magnitude of response; (ii) non-invasive readouts (circulating tumor cells [CTCs], urine DNA methylation signatures) and a tumor-intrinsic modifier (GARS1) will further stratify responders by reflecting antigen presentation dynamics, tumor burden, and immune signaling. In summary, combining TIL density with TMB or MSI/dMMR status will outperform either metric alone in predicting anti–PD-1/PD-L1 response in MIBC, and incorporating neoantigen quality, T cell functional status, and non-invasive biomarkers will yield a robust, testable predictive model.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
