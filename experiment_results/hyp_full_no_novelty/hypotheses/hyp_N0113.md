# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

BCG elicits local anti-tumor immunity in the urothelium, yet some tumors still recur/metastasize — why do immune-recruiting treatments (BCG) fail in certain cases? ## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: CTNNB1-driven activation of Wnt/beta-catenin signaling enforces EMT programs in urothelial tumors, creating a tumor microenvironment in which intravesical BCG-triggered anti-tumor immunity is impaired, leading to reduced therapy_response. Ancillary hypothesis: EMT status modulates the relationship between TIL density and BCG response such that tumors with high TIL density but concurrent high CTNNB1 activity and EMT signatures exhibit poorer BCG response than tumors with low CTNNB1 activity or low EMT signaling. Mechanistically, CTNNB1 activation reprograms chemokine/cytokine profiles and antigen presentation pathways (e.g., reduced T cell recruiting chemokines, altered MHC expression) and promotes T cell exclusion or dysfunction, diminishing local immune cascade initiation by BCG. Clinically, CTNNB1-driven EMT status plus high TIL density will stratify patients into distinct BCG response groups, independent of tumor mutational burden alone. Secondary modifiers (CDH1, SNAI1, ZEB1, MMP9) may modulate the magnitude of immune evasion but are not essential drivers of the primary CTNNB1–EMT–immune interaction; their inclusion will refine risk stratification and mechanistic nuance.
