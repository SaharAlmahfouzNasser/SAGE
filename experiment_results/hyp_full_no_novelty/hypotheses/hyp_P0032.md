# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that dMMR/MSI and high TMB create neoantigen-rich tumors and that TIL presence (CD4/CD8) is linked to anti–PD-1 benefit, yet not all TIL-rich bladder cancers respond to checkpoint blockade. The puzzle is: why does TIL density sometimes fail to translate into anti–PD-1 responsiveness despite high mutational/immune signatures? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the dMMR/MSI → neoantigen → ICI sensitivity axis and the TMB → neoantigen burden axis, plus the anti–PD-1 → CD4/CD8 TILs → prognostic biomarker path, because together they provide a mechanistic backbone linking tumor genomic instability, neoantigen quality, and intratumoral T cells to therapy response. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: PD-L1 expression, liquid biopsy (ctDNA/CTC) and methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2) offer orthogonal ways to (a) measure immune activation/exhaustion states, (b) monitor neoantigen-bearing clones noninvasively, and (c) assess tumor heterogeneity and epigenetic suppression — all relevant to whether TILs are effective. 4) Why this endpoint: a therapy-response proxy (anti–PD-1 response) is the most direct, clinically actionable endpoint given the core paths focus on immunotherapy predictive biomarkers. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broader prognostic biomarker aggregates, non-specific serum markers (PSA, pZVR), and exploratory early-detection stool methylation as core anchors to keep the hypothesis sharply mechanistic; these remain in secondary_modifiers or future exploratory work?

## Working Hypothesis

High TIL density predicts anti–PD-1 response in bladder cancer only when the tumor harbors high-quality neoantigens that are predominantly clonal and effectively presented by the antigen presentation machinery. In tumors with MSI/dMMR or high TMB but low clonal neoantigen fraction or impaired antigen presentation (e.g., B2M/MHC-I defects, weak IFN-γ signaling, or epigenetic silencing), even abundant TILs fail to generate productive anti-tumor activity. Therefore, a composite biomarker that integrates (i) TIL density, (ii) MSI/dMMR status and total TMB, (iii) fraction of clonal (neoantigenic) neoantigens, (iv) antigen presentation integrity (MHC-I/B2M status, antigen-processing/gene signature), (v) PD-L1 expression, and (vi) methylation-based epigenetic suppression markers (SDC2/TFPI2) will superiorly stratify bladder cancer patients for anti–PD-1 therapy. Additionally, noninvasive monitoring with ctDNA methylation markers will reflect clonal dynamics linked to response. Testable predictions: (1) TIL-high, clonal-neoantigen–high, intact antigen presentation tumors have the highest response rates to anti–PD-1; (2) TIL-high but clonal-neoantigen–low or antigen-presentation–deficient tumors have attenuated responses; (3) the proposed composite biomarker outperforms TIL density or TMB alone in predicting response; (4) ctDNA methylation dynamics correlate with radiographic/clinical responses and clonal evolution.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
