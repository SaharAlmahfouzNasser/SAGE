## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer (BLCA), the therapeutic efficacy of cisplatin-based regimens augmented with 9-ING-41 in TLS-rich tumors depends on therapy-induced upregulation of the tumor-suppressive long noncoding RNA CASC2. This CASC2 induction requires permissive transcriptional/epitranscriptomic states and is antagonized by high SOX2 activity, which reprograms chromatin and transcriptional outputs via TP53 and CTNNB1 networks. Consequently, TLS-high/SOX2-low tumors will robustly induce CASC2 upon cisplatin+9-ING-41 exposure and exhibit enhanced therapy_response, whereas TLS-high/SOX2-high tumors will fail to induce CASC2 and show resistance despite organized TLS, with TLS-low tumors displaying variable responses depending on their intrinsic CASC2/SOX2 axis dynamics. The proposed mediators (AL161431.1, AC090181.2, DEFB103) and epitranscriptomic regulators (m6A/m7G lncRNA modifiers) modulate this axis and can shift outcomes, but the core dependence is CASC2 upregulation enabled by TLS context and constrained by SOX2-driven transcriptional reprogramming.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** CASC2 is a tumor-suppressive lncRNA whose induction may contribute to chemotherapy-driven cytostasis;

**Prediction 2:** SOX2 drives stem-like transcriptional programs and can interact with TP53/CTNNB1 signaling to rewire gene expression, potentially suppressing CASC2 induction;

**Prediction 3:** TLS structures reflect a distinct immune microenvironment that can influence drug response but does not guarantee benefit, suggesting a context where CASC2 induction is a key determinant of chemosensitivity.
