# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights an apparent contradiction — immune-rich tumor microenvironments (Immunoscore/TLS) should protect against progression, yet some NMIBC patients still progress. These offer a direct mechanistic contrast between local immune presence and systemic indicators of escape. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: Immunoscore®/immune-context rationale supports choosing an imaging immune readout (TLS); MSI and AI-based biomarkers provide frameworks showing that tumor-intrinsic genomic states can be captured and translated into classifiers; metabolic/hormonal signals (HDL-C, IGF-1) illustrate systemic modulators that could further influence immune efficacy or tumor aggressiveness. 4) Why PFI as endpoint: Progression-free interval is the most problem-relevant clinical readout in NMIBC (distinguishing tumors that invade/morbidity escalate vs. those controlled locally), and it is well represented in TCGA clinical annotations and suitable for testing interactions between local immune features and systemic dissemination markers. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded making Immunoscore® itself a core anchor (it informed the imaging choice instead) and omitted ER status and the broader metabolic panel from the core hypothesis to preserve focus and mechanistic clarity; these are listed as secondary_modifiers for subsequent modeling. I also excluded Tier 3 exploratory/weakly linked paths (CSF/psychiatric signals, pZVR/PSA cross-disease inferences) because they lack direct mechanistic grounding in NMIBC for the primary question?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, the protective association of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates with longer progression-free interval (PFI) is conditional on tumor TP53 status and the systemic tumor dissemination signal captured by ADNP mRNA in circulating tumor cells (CTCs). Specifically, TLS presence predicts longer PFI only when TP53 is functionally intact and ADNP mRNA in CTCs is low. When TP53 is dysfunctional (e.g., pathogenic mutations or functional inactivation) and ADNP mRNA in CTCs is elevated, tumor cells acquire or express immune-evasive phenotypes and increased disseminative potential, which can override the local immune organization and drive progression. Therefore, a composite biomarker combining TLS status (imaging/histology-based assessment of TLS-like aggregates), TP53 status, and ADNP mRNA levels in CTCs will more accurately stratify NMIBC patients by progression risk than TLS alone or TP53/ADNP alone. The study should test for a significant three-way interaction (TLS × TP53 × ADNP-CTC) on PFI, with the highest risk defined by TLS-positive tumors that harbor TP53 dysfunction and high ADNP-CTC burden, predicting the shortest PFI and informing more intensive surveillance or adjuvant strategies.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
