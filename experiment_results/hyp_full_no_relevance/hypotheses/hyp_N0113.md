# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

EMT and Wnt/beta-catenin activation drive metastatic, radioresistant phenotypes, yet radiotherapy can potentiate immunotherapy; why do some immune-infiltrated tumors still fail to respond to combined RT+immunotherapy? 2) Tier 1 path used: the single-path mechanistic backbone linking EMT ↔ Wnt/beta-catenin ↔ metastatic spread and modulation of radiosensitivity; I built on the beta-catenin node (CTNNB1) as the pathway effector and the EMT program as the cellular state. 3) Supporting (Tier 2) evidence: RT’s ability to increase tumor immunogenicity implies that the state of tumor-intrinsic signaling (Wnt/CTNNB1-driven EMT) may prevent RT-mediated immune priming or function of existing TILs, explaining discordant responses. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected therapy_response (response to combined radiation + immunotherapy) because the mechanistic path centers on treatment interaction (RT-induced immunogenicity vs. EMT/Wnt-driven resistance), making a therapy-response proxy the most direct, testable clinical outcome. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded bladder-specific agents (BCG) and tissue-specific assumptions (urothelium vs. breast) from the core hypothesis to keep focus on the generalizable mechanism (CTNNB1-driven EMT undermining RT+immunotherapy efficacy) and to comply with the limit of ≤2 core molecular variables; those factors are retained as secondary modifiers for downstream exploratory analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

Activation of CTNNB1 drives an EMT program that creates an immune-excluded, immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and modulates radiation-induced immunogenic effects, resulting in diminished efficacy of combined radiotherapy and immunotherapy. Specifically, tumors with high CTNNB1 activity and corresponding EMT signatures will exhibit reduced cross-priming and effector CD8+ T cell activity, lower MHC-I presentation, and altered chemokine landscapes that hamper TIL recruitment and function, thereby yielding inferior therapy_response to RT+IO compared with tumors with low CTNNB1 activity and EMT. This effect is mechanistically linked to reduced dendritic cell recruitment (e.g., BATF3-DC–supporting chemokines such as CCL4/CXCL9), increased immunosuppressive signaling, and potential radioresistance pathways, and is expected to be observable across solid tumor contexts independent of tissue origin when CTNNB1/EMT states are matched. Exploratory modifiers (e.g., tumor tissue context or BCG-related urothelial activation) may modulate the magnitude but not the direction of the effect.
