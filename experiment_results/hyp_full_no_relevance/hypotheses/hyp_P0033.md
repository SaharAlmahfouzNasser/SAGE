# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, the key question is why bladder tumors with apparent high immunogenicity (MSH6-associated genomic instability and dense local immune organization) can nonetheless show divergent or poor responses to immune checkpoint therapies. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSH6 → genomic instability/neoantigen axis (Core Path 1) and the eIF4E → translational reprogramming / immunotherapy-biomarker axis (Core Path 3), together with the immune-infiltrate/Immunoscore concept implied by the synthesis (to motivate an imaging readout). These provide a mechanistic tension: high neoantigen load (MSH6) should favor response, whereas eIF4E-driven programs may blunt it. 3) Tier 2 support: HDL-C (systemic metabolic modulation), urine/SDC2–TFPI2 methylation concepts (noninvasive readouts of tumor state), SARIFA-positivity (spatial microenvironment architecture), and pZVR (independent prognostic signal) all reinforce that metabolic, epigenetic, and spatial factors can modulate immune phenotypes or be useful as orthogonal biomarkers, supporting the plausibility of context-dependent immunotherapy outcomes. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (immunotherapy response proxy) because the Ontologist's central question explicitly concerns divergent responses to immunotherapy and because MSH6 and eIF4E mechanistically speak to neoantigen generation vs. immune-evasive translation programs, respectively. 5) Deliberately excluded items and why: Although urine DNA methylation biomarkers and SARIFA-type spatial biomarkers are important translational assets (and appear in Tier 1/2), I excluded them from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic focus tight (max 1 imaging feature + ≤2 anchor genes). They remain in secondary_modifiers to be tested as modifiers or noninvasive correlates rather than drivers of the primary mechanistic claim?

## Working Hypothesis

Among bladder cancers with TLS-like lymphoid aggregates, high MSH6 deficiency creates a large neoantigen burden, which would predict responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade, but concurrent upregulation of eIF4E-driven translational programs promotes immune-evasive states that suppress antigen presentation and effector T cell function. Therefore, TLS-high, MSH6-deficient tumors with elevated eIF4E activity will exhibit reduced immunotherapy response compared to TLS-high, MSH6-deficient tumors with low eIF4E activity. Importantly, lowering eIF4E activity or disrupting its downstream translation should restore antigen presentation and T cell–mediated tumor control, converting a non-responder into a responder, thereby establishing eIF4E-driven translational reprogramming as a functional modifier of neoantigen-driven immunity in this context. The hypothesis also implies that TLS density alone is insufficient to predict response without accounting for the tumor’s translational state (eIF4E activity).
