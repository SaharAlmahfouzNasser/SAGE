# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some TIL-rich tumors fail to achieve effective anti-tumor immunity? The single-path analysis links promoter-associated TGF-β activity → induction/expansion of Tregs → observation of Tregs in prostate cancer, and also connects tumor context with LAG3 modulation and RASSF1A inactivation. This suggests a mechanistic axis whereby tumor-intrinsic RASSF1A loss facilitates a TGF-β–driven, Treg-rich, LAG3-dominated immunosuppressive microenvironment that could neutralize the apparent benefit of high TILs. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TGF-β → Tregs → presence in prostate cancer (immunoregulation axis) and (b) RASSF1A → inactivated in prostate cancer (tumor-intrinsic modifier) together with LAG3 as the immune-checkpoint effector node. I built on these because they provide a tumor-intrinsic (RASSF1A) → stromal/immune (TGF-β/Tregs) → checkpoint (LAG3) mechanistic backbone. 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: contextual differences in LAG3 (protective in BLCA, low in READ) and the documented role of TGF-β in promoting Tregs support the idea that tissue context and checkpoint regulation modulate how immune infiltration translates to function. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: The mechanism directly implicates immune suppression and checkpoint-mediated dysfunction, which most immediately predicts altered response to immune-based therapies; therapy response is therefore the most clinically actionable endpoint for this mechanism. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded imaging features TLS-like aggregates and TSR to keep the hypothesis focused on a single, generalizable histologic immune readout (TIL density). I excluded other genes/diseases from the core hypothesis (e.g., BLCA/READ as disease modifiers and ultrasound as an imaging method) to avoid diluting the mechanistic question; those contextual modifiers remain for downstream analysis?

## Working Hypothesis

In tumors with RASSF1A inactivation, does TGFB1 (TGF-β) signaling drive enrichment and/or functional polarization of intratumoral regulatory T cells, leading to LAG-3–mediated suppression of effector T cells and an immunosuppressive microenvironment? If so, high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density will fail to predict positive response to immune checkpoint blockade in these RASSF1A-inactivated, LAG3-high tumors, whereas in RASSF1A-intact or LAG3-low contexts, TIL density will more accurately predict therapy response.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
