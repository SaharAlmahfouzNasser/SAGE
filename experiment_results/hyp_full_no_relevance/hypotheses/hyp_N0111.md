# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do TLS-like lymphoid aggregates not uniformly confer a survival advantage in breast cancer—i.e., what tumor-intrinsic factors convert histologic TLS presence from a beneficial structure into a functionally impotent or even permissive state? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the CXCL pathway path (PTEN → PI3K → chemokine milieu) and the TIL cluster C4 / mTORC1 path (PTEN → PI3K/AKT → mTORC1 negatively correlates with TLS-associated TIL phenotypes). These link PTEN loss to altered chemokine-driven recruitment, endothelial/antigen-presentation changes, and a specific TIL phenotype tied to prognosis, providing a direct mechanistic route for TLS dysfunction. 3) Tier 2 support: PAK, YAP/TAZ, and TLR/NO paths strengthen the plausibility that PTEN-driven signaling remodels cytoskeleton, mechano-transduction, and innate inflammation—each capable of disrupting immune cell trafficking or APC function, thereby weakening TLS efficacy. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) was selected because TCGA-BRCA provides robust OS data and the question addresses a durable clinical consequence (not immediate therapy response). 5) Deliberately excluded: I excluded listing many individual chemokine genes or HLA alleles in the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (max 2 anchors). Exploratory/co-expression modules (e.g., turquoise module) and cross-tumor context links (glioma/prostate-specific chains) were deprioritized as they are less directly mechanistic in breast cancer context?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are prognostically beneficial in breast cancer when the tumor microenvironment supports effective recruitment and organization of B and T cells, efficient antigen presentation, and proper endothelial maturation. I hypothesize that PTEN loss with concomitant mTORC1 hyperactivation reprograms tumor and stromal compartments to suppress TLS function. Specifically, PTEN loss will be associated with reduced CXCL13 and related TLS-supporting chemokines in TLS-positive tumors, lower MHC class II and co-stimulatory molecule expression on dendritic cells within TLS, and altered HEV/endothelial markers that hinder TLS maturation. In addition, PTEN loss and high mTORC1 activity will amplify PAK, YAP/TAZ, and TLR/NO signaling, further disrupting immune cell trafficking and APC function. Consequently, TLS-rich tumors with intact PTEN will show improved overall survival, whereas TLS-rich tumors with PTEN loss and high mTORC1 will not exhibit the TLS-associated survival benefit. In the TCGA-BRCA cohort, TLS-rich PTEN-intact tumors will have better OS than TLS-poor PTEN-intact tumors, while TLS-rich PTEN-deficient tumors with high mTORC1 activity will have OS similar to TLS-poor tumors. This defines a TLS dysfunction phenotype that could be targeted by PI3K/mTOR pathway modulation to restore TLS activity and anti-tumor immunity in a subset of breast cancers.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.

**Prediction 2:** In functional experiments, restoring or enhancing the anchor gene's signaling will improve immune-mediated tumor control in imaging-feature-positive models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
