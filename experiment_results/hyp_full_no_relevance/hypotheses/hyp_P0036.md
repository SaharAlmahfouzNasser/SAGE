# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist's synthesis, the key puzzle is why tumors with genomic features that should increase immunogenicity (MSH6 loss / high TMB) nonetheless fail to generate effective local anti-tumor immunity or durable disease control. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSH6 → (mismatch repair deficiency → elevated neoantigen/TMB) path because it provides the genomic basis for expected immune recognition, and on the eIF4E → (translation reprogramming → immune-modulatory outputs) path because it offers a plausible tumor-intrinsic mechanism that can actively suppress or reshape local immune organization. These two together let me ask why neoantigen potential does not always translate into functional local immunity. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: GARS1 and urine-DNA methylation (epigenetic state) suggest additional, non-genomic tumor states that modulate the immune microenvironment; AI-based histology biomarkers support the feasibility of capturing immune-organization phenotypes from H&E (e.g., TLS features) and integrating them with molecular data. 4) Why I chose the endpoint PFI: The central problem concerns failure of local immune control leading to earlier progression despite apparent immunogenicity — progression-free interval directly captures time-to-disease-progression and is sensitive to immune-mediated control, making it the most relevant clinical endpoint for this mechanistic question (and it is available in TCGA clinical data). 5) What I deliberately excluded and why: I limited the hypothesis to two anchors (MSH6 and eIF4E) to keep the mechanistic claim sharp; I excluded treating TMB as a primary anchor (it is a downstream consequence of MSH6 and is retained as a secondary_modifier) and excluded less relevant exploratory paths (HDL-C, PSA, CSF-AD biomarkers, cross-cancer Immunoscore claims) because they are indirect or cross-disease and would dilute the mechanistic focus?

## Working Hypothesis

In breast cancer (TCGA-BRCA), TLS-like lymphoid aggregates indicate local immune organization that would be expected to suppress progression in tumors with MSH6 deficiency. However, high eIF4E activity in these TLS-positive, MSH6-deficient tumors dampens chemokine and antigen-presenting gene programs essential for TLS maturation and effective local T cell priming, leading to ineffective TLS function and earlier disease progression. Clinically, TLS-positive, MSH6-deficient breast tumors with high eIF4E expression will exhibit shorter progression-free interval (PFI) compared with TLS-positive, MSH6-deficient tumors with low eIF4E; conversely, reducing eIF4E/translation in this context should restore TLS functionality and delay progression. Experimental testing will involve: (1) stratified analysis of BRCA samples for TLS status, MSH6 deficiency, and eIF4E levels with PFI as the endpoint; (2) mediation analyses linking eIF4E-driven suppression of chemokine/antigen-presentation gene sets to TLS maturation status and PFI; and (3) in vitro assays (breast cancer cell lines with MSH6 knockdown and eIF4E modulation) assessing chemokine/antigen-presentation output and lymphocyte engagement, to provide mechanistic support for translation-dependent TLS modulation.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
