## Working Hypothesis

In pretreatment urothelial carcinomas treated with cisplatin-based regimens, the predictive value of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) for therapy response is contingent on the tumor's intrinsic DNA repair capacity and its differentiation state. Specifically, high TIL density predicts favorable cisplatin response predominantly in tumors with low ERCC1-mediated nucleotide-excision repair and/or a non-luminal differentiation program (reflected by a lower KRT20 signature). Conversely, tumors with high ERCC1 expression and strong luminal differentiation (high KRT20) and with intact ERCC2 and TP53 signaling will exhibit resistance to cisplatin despite abundant TILs, because efficient repair and a differentiation-driven immune-excluded microenvironment limit immunogenic cell death and TIL effectiveness. To test this, we propose an MGX (molecular-genomic-exposure) rule that integrates: (i) imaging-based TIL density as a measure of immune presence, (ii) ERCC1 expression as a proxy for NER-driven repair capacity, (iii) KRT20 signature as a differentiation-state modifier, and (iv) secondary modifiers including ERCC2 and TP53 status (repair/checkpoint integrity), FGFR3 signaling as an additional differentiation-associated pathway, and immune effector markers (CD8A, GZMB) for validation of cytotoxic potential. The anchor pair remains ERCC1 and KRT20, while CD8A and GZMB serve as molecular validation of effector activity, and CD8A is kept in secondary modifiers rather than anchors to preserve the 1–2 anchor constraint. The hypothesis yields testable predictions: (a) high TIL density combined with low ERCC1 and/or low KRT20 will associate with higher rates of pathologic complete response (pCR) or major radiographic response to neoadjuvant cisplatin; (b) high ERCC1 expression and high KRT20 (with intact ERCC2/TP53) will predict poor response despite high TILs; (c) ERCC2 or TP53 loss-of-function will shift the expected outcome toward greater chemosensitivity in contexts of high TIL density and/or altered differentiation. Feasible testing approaches include collecting pre-treatment biopsies with matched clinical outcomes, performing quantitative digital pathology for TIL density, assessing ERCC1/ERCC2 expression or function (RNA/protein), profiling KRT20-associated differentiation, sequencing for ERCC2/TP53/FGFR3 status, and measuring immune-effector markers (CD8A, GZMB). The MGX score will be evaluated for predictive performance against standard clinical predictors, with validation in independent cohorts. This framework provides mechanistic plausibility (DNA repair capacity × differentiation state modulating immune-driven clearance) and a concrete path to clinical stratification for neoadjuvant therapy.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
