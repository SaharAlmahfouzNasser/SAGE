## Clinical Question

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-rich bladder tumors exhibit variable responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors because functional anti-tumor immunity requires both an permissive microenvironment and antigen presentation competence. Specifically, high TGFB1 activity drives stromal fibrosis and immune exclusion, potentially isolating TLS structures from productive tumor-immune cell interactions; concurrently, a urine DNA methylation signature indicative of tumor promoter hypermethylation silences antigen-presentation programs, reducing TLS-driven immunogenic signaling. Therefore, TLS-high tumors with concomitant high TGFB1 activity and a positive urine DNA methylation signature will have poorer ICI responses than TLS-high tumors with low TGFB1 activity and/or a negative urine methylation signature. Conversely, TLS-high/TGFB1-low and urine methylation-negative tumors are predicted to perform as well as or better than TLS-low tumors in response to ICIs. This hypothesis tests whether the combination of TGFB1-driven stromal context and tumor-intrinsic epigenetic silencing modulates the functional relevance of TLS in guiding immunotherapy outcomes.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TGF-β signaling promotes stromal fibrosis and immune exclusion, limiting effector T cell–tumor cell interactions and potentially dampening ICI efficacy even when TLS structures are present.

**Prediction 2:** Tumor promoter hypermethylation can suppress antigen presentation and other immune-evasive programs; a urine DNA methylation signature reflects tumor-intrinsic epigenetic states that may influence TLS functionality without requiring invasive biopsy.

**Prediction 3:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
