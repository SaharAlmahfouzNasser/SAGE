# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist identified a tension between strong local immune organization (Immunoscore / TLS-like aggregates) and poor therapeutic benefit in some MIBC cases; the key puzzle is why TLS/TIL-rich tumors sometimes fail to respond to immunotherapy. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TGF-β signaling (core path linking MIBC aggressiveness to fibrogenesis and immune suppression) — chosen because it provides a mechanistic axis (stromal/ECM remodeling and immune exclusion) that can nullify local immune structures; (b) Immunotherapy biomarker (core path linking cohort-level immune features to therapy response) — chosen because the clinical question is immunotherapy sensitivity; (c) Immunoscore-like concepts (core path linking spatial immune metrics to prognosis) — chosen because TLS/TIL imaging features are the measurable immune context we aim to explain. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: AI-based biomarkers and histo-genomic integration provide methods to detect functional immune exclusion; MSI and antigen-presentation axes (HLA) inform baseline tumor immunogenicity; PTGDS and CD8A/CD3 provide modulatory pathways and cell markers that can modify the core interaction. ## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), TLS-like lymphoid aggregates indicate an attempted localized anti-tumor immune response. However, when tumor TGFB1 expression is high, TGF-β–driven stromal remodeling and fibrosis create an immune-excluded microenvironment that suppresses effector T cell function within or near TLS structures, despite PD-L1 (CD274) upregulation. Consequently, TLS-high tumors that are TGFB1-high and CD274-high will have a lower objective response rate to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. This creates a patient subset that may benefit from combined TGF-β–targeted therapy with PD-(L)1 blockade or stromal remodeling to restore immune access and improve response. The hypothesis is testable by evaluating the interaction among TLS-like aggregates (imaging feature), TGFB1 expression (anchor gene), and CD274 expression (anchor gene) as predictors of therapy_response in MIBC treated with checkpoint inhibitors, with preclinical validation of the TGF-β–mediated mechanism and potential mitigation by combination therapy.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
