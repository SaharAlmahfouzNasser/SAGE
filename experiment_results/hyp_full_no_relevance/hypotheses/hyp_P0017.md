# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Immunologically 'hot' NMIBC (high TIL density) does not uniformly benefit from immune checkpoint therapies — why does immune infiltration fail to translate into clinical response in a subset of tumors?
2) Tier 1 paths used and why: I built on the MSI/dMMR path because mismatch-repair defects directly raise neoantigen burden and are mechanistically linked to immunotherapy responsiveness; I also used the TMB path because mutation load quantifies neoantigenic potential and can modulate the functional capacity of infiltrating T cells. I deprioritized Immunoscore as a primary anchor to keep the hypothesis sharply molecular (neoantigen supply) while using an H&E-derived immune metric (TIL density) as the imaging readout.
3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: eIF4E and GARS1 provide mechanistic modifiers of tumor immune phenotype (translation programs and noncanonical immune signaling that can shape antigen presentation or immune suppression); urine DNA methylation biomarkers and CTCs offer noninvasive surrogates for tumor state and could identify discordant cases (TIL-high but low neoantigen load); HDL-C and MSH6 add metabolic and gene-specific nuance to immune–genomic interactions. These supporting paths justify testing whether neoantigen supply (MSI/TMB) conditions the effectiveness of local immune infiltrates.
4) Why I chose therapy_response as the endpoint: The core tier-1 paths repeatedly converge on biomarkers (MSI/dMMR, TMB, Immunoscore) that are most relevant to predicting benefit from immunotherapy, making therapy response the most mechanistically pertinent and clinically actionable endpoint for NMIBC.
5) What I deliberately EXCLUDED and why: I excluded using Immunoscore as a primary anchor to obey the Focusing Rule (max 2 anchors) and because TIL density on H&E is a simpler, directly measurable imaging surrogate in many clinical cohorts; I excluded wide panels of additional genes (e.g., SARIFA, broader metabolic panels) from the hypothesis sentence to avoid a model-driven, unfocused claim — these sit in secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte density does not predict benefit from immune checkpoint blockade when tumors lack mismatch-repair deficiency and have low tumor mutational burden, because neoantigen load is insufficient to sustain effective T-cell–mediated tumor clearance. Therefore, NMIBC patients with high TIL density and high neoantigen burden (as indicated by MSI/dMMR or elevated TMB) will have improved responses to immunotherapy, whereas those with high TIL density but low neoantigen burden will exhibit poor responses.

**Prediction 3:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
