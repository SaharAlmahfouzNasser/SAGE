# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that NMIBC response to intravesical BCG is linked to immune activation (PD-L1, Immunoscore) and systemic tumor antigenicity (bTMB), yet noninvasive readouts (blood bTMB, urine methylation, CTC transcripts) show discordant predictive performance. Why do some TIL-rich tumors nonetheless fail BCG? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) bTMB↔PD-L1/BCG path — suggests interplay between systemic neoantigen load and local adaptive checkpoint induction; (b) urine-DNA methylation/CTC (ADNP) liquid-biopsy path — provides noninvasive tumor-state readouts but was relegated to secondary modifiers to keep the mechanistic core sharp. These Tier 1 paths were chosen because they directly explain an immune activation (BCG) versus immune suppression (PD-L1) tradeoff modulated by tumor antigenicity (bTMB). 3) Tier 2 support: MSI, TP53, ER status, HPV status and Immunoscore components (CD3/CD8) provide plausible modifiers — e.g., MSI elevates neoantigen burden (supporting bTMB effects), TP53/ER may alter immune visibility or methylation signatures, and Immunoscore analogues validate immune-density–based prognostication. 4) Endpoint choice: therapy_response (BCG) was selected because the core mechanistic question concerns why intravesical immunotherapy succeeds or fails and because BCG response is the most clinically actionable endpoint for NMIBC management. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded urine methylation panels, ADNP/CTC markers, and broader systemic markers from the core hypothesis to maintain the Focusing Rule (max 1 imaging feature + ≤2 anchors). Those entities are included as secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses rather than cluttering the primary mechanistic claim?

## Working Hypothesis

Expanded hypothesis: In NMIBC, high TIL density coupled with elevated bTMB predicts a robust neoantigen-driven immune milieu; however, if PD-L1 expression is also high, the T cell compartment shifts toward an exhausted phenotype that undermines BCG-induced anti-tumor immunity, leading to poor therapy_response (BCG). Conversely, NMIBC tumors with high TIL density and high bTMB but low PD-L1 expression maintain functional, non-exhausted T cells and exhibit improved therapy_response to BCG. In a subset of patients, even when PD-L1 is elevated, addition of PD-1/PD-L1 blockade could reinvigorate exhausted T cells and restore BCG sensitivity, improving therapy_response. The predictive utility of TIL density for BCG response is thus context-dependent and is enhanced when combined with (a) bTMB as a proxy for neoantigen load, and (b) PD-L1 status as a determinant of adaptive immune resistance. Tier2 modifiers (MSI, TP53, HPV status, ER status, and Immunoscore components CD3/CD8) will modulate the strength of these associations, refining risk stratification. A composite biomarker integrating TIL density, bTMB, PD-L1, and the Tier2 modifiers will provide a more accurate forecast of which patients will respond to BCG alone versus those who will require PD-1/PD-L1 blockade to achieve optimal therapy_response (BCG). Secondary noninvasive markers (urine DNA methylation panels, ADNP/CTC signatures) will be explored as supportive modifiers to enhance real-world applicability but are not essential to the core mechanism.
