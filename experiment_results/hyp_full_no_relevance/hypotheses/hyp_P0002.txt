What it is
- TSR is a morphological, spatial biomarker computed from the relative areas of tumor tissue versus surrounding stroma on H&E slides.
- It does not require additional stains or molecular data; it relies on image analysis of standard pathology images.

Why it’s prognostic in MIBC
- Higher stromal content (i.e., higher TSR value, or “stroma-high”) at the invasive front has been associated with worse outcomes in several cancers, including bladder cancer, and can independently predict survival when combined with clinical-pathologic factors.
- It leverages the spatial relationship between tumor and stroma, a feature obtainable directly from routine H&E images.

How to measure from H&E images
1) Acquire representative slides
   - Use invasive front areas from cystectomy specimens or high-quality TURBT sections that include tumor-stroma interfaces.
2) Delineate regions
   - Identify and segment the invasive tumor region and the surrounding stroma within the tumor-containing field.
3) Compute areas
   - Calculate area_tumor and area_stroma (preferably using digital image analysis; color deconvolution can help separate tissue components).
4) Calculate TSR
   - TSR = area_stroma / (area_tumor + area_stroma)

5) Interpret
   - Classify as stroma-high vs stroma-low using a validated threshold for your cohort (commonly around 0.5, but thresholds vary by study and must be validated locally).
