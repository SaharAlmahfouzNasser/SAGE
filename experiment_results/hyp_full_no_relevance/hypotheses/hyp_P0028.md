# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, the key puzzle is why some bladder tumors that appear immune-infiltrated still fail to benefit from immunotherapy or progress despite favorable molecular features. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) MSH6 as a determinant of mismatch-repair driven neoantigen landscape and (b) tumor epigenetic state captured by urine DNA methylation biomarkers as a readout of antigen-presentation/immune-visible epigenetic silencing. I chose these because they form a causal axis linking tumor-intrinsic antigenicity (MSH6 → neoantigen quality) and epigenetic control of antigen presentation (methylation) to the effectiveness of local immune structures. 3) Tier 2 support: GARS1 (metabolic/immune signaling), HDL-C (systemic metabolic modulation of immunity), and ER/TP53 interactions provide plausible modifiers that can shape immune competence or exhaustion in the microenvironment and thus modulate the core MSH6–methylation–immune axis. 4) Why this endpoint: I selected a therapy_response proxy (immunotherapy benefit) because the central puzzle explicitly concerns failure to benefit from immune therapies despite immune infiltration; therapy response is the most mechanistically informative and clinically actionable endpoint for this question. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I omitted Immunoscore as an explicit anchor to keep the hypothesis focused on a single image-derived feature (TLS-like lymphoid aggregates) and tumor-intrinsic molecular drivers (MSH6, methylation). I also excluded weaker/less specific paths (PSA, pZVR, CSF AD biomarkers) from the core claim and placed them in exploratory consideration to avoid diluting mechanistic focus?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in bladder tumors modulate local anti-tumor immunity in a manner that is contingent on tumor-intrinsic antigen presentation and epigenetic state. Specifically, TLS-high, MSH6-intact tumors with a low urine DNA methylation signature (reflecting active antigen-presentation pathways) will exhibit robust neoantigen visibility and T cell effector activity, leading to favorable therapy response to immune checkpoint blockade. Conversely, TLS-high tumors with high urine DNA methylation (reflecting epigenetic silencing of antigen-presentation genes) or with MSH6 deficiency will show impaired antigen presentation and generation of dysfunctional neoantigens, driving T cell exhaustion and poor therapy response. Thus, therapy_response to ICIs in bladder cancer is best predicted by a three-way interaction: TLS status (presence of TLS-like aggregates) × MSH6 function (intact vs deficient) × urine DNA methylation burden (low vs high), rather than TLS status alone.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** intact MSH6 supports neoantigen generation;

**Prediction 2:** low methylation preserves antigen presentation gene expression;

**Prediction 3:** TLS provides a microenvironment for T cell activation. When any component fails (MSH6 loss or high methylation), even TLS-rich tumors may fail to present neoantigens effectively, leading to exhaustion and poor response.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
