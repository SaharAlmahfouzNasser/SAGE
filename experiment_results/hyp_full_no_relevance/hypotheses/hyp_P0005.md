# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that some MIBC tumors with high systemic neoantigen load (bTMB) and inflammatory signatures still respond variably to immunotherapy; why does high mutational load fail to translate into effective anti-tumor immunity in a subset of patients?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), S100A9-driven myeloid inflammation establishes an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that functionally suppresses tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, resulting in reduced TIL activity and low TIL density despite high bTMB. Consequently, MIBC tumors with high bTMB and high S100A9-driven inflammation accompanied by low TIL density will show poorer immunotherapy responses than tumors where high bTMB coexists with preserved TIL density and activity.

**Prediction 2:** as modifiers to refine risk strata, while keeping them outside the primary hypothesis. # 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
