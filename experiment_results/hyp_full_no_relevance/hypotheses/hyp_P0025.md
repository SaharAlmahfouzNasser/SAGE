## Clinical Question

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder tumors with TLS-like lymphoid aggregates, the predicted benefit from immune checkpoint blockade is governed by the interaction between two mechanistic axes: (1) antigenicity driven by mismatch repair status of MSH6 and the resultant neoantigen load, and (2) translational control via eIF4E that shapes the tumor secretome and antigen presentation capacity. Specifically, TLS-rich, MSH6-deficient tumors (high neoantigen burden) with low to moderate eIF4E activity will mount effective T cell priming and effector function and thus respond to checkpoint blockade. Conversely, TLS-rich, MSH6-proficient tumors with high eIF4E activity will exhibit impaired antigen processing/presentation and an immunosuppressive secretome, leading to poor checkpoint blockade response despite TLS presence. In this second scenario, co-targeting the translational program (e.g., reducing eIF4E activity or modulating its downstream pathways) or enhancing antigen presentation can restore tumor immunogenicity and resensitize to checkpoint inhibitors. Therefore TLS density is a necessary but not sufficient predictor; robust prediction requires integration of MSH6 status/neoantigen load, eIF4E activity, and antigen presentation machinery (MHC I, TAP1/2, β2M). Secondary biomarkers (cfDNA/CTC-derived neoantigen dynamics, HDL-C levels, and methylation markers SDC2/TFPI2) and AI-driven integrative models will improve patient stratification and identify candidates for combination strategies (checkpoint blockade plus translation modulation or epigenetic/secretome-targeted therapies).
