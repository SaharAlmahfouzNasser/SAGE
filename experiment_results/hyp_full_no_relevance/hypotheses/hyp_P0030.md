# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In bladder cancer, high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density is associated with improved overall survival (OS) only when MSH6 mismatch repair is intact. In MSH6-deficient tumors, high TIL density does not confer the same OS benefit. Secondary hypothesis: among MSH6-intact, TIL-high tumors, positive stool DNA methylation for SDC2 (and/or TFPI2) attenuates the OS benefit, indicating that tumor DNA shedding and associated systemic immune modulation can undermine effective anti-tumor immunity. This three-way interaction (MSH6 status × TIL density × stool SDC2/TFPI2 methylation) persists after adjustment for MSI status and is potentially modulated by Immunoscore as a secondary immune context marker. Mechanistically, intact MSH6 may support neoantigen-driven, effective T cell responses, whereas MSH6 deficiency can promote immune dysfunction (e.g., exhaustion, impaired antigen presentation) whose benefit is blunted in the presence of detectable stool methylation reflecting higher tumor shedding and/or burden.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
