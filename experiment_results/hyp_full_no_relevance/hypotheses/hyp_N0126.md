# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

PSA density (PSAD) reliably predicts disease reclassification, yet clinical discordance occurs — why does PSAD sometimes misclassify risk? The Ontologist suggests inflammation and EMT-associated loss of E-cadherin could decouple PSA production and gland architecture from true tumor invasive potential, producing PSAD–reclassification mismatches. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) PSAD → disease reclassification (direct clinical predictor), (b) inflammation → prostate cancer (modulates PSA production and gland remodeling), and (c) EMT → E-cadherin ↓ → worse prognosis (mechanism linking cell plasticity to aggressiveness). These provide a mechanistic chain linking stromal/inflammatory context and EMT to PSAD performance. 3) Tier 2 support: PSAD as a benign-versus-malignant discriminator and centrality of PSA levels underpin the biomarker framework; cross-cancer EMT links (e.g., lung) generalize the EMT→invasion concept; TP53 as a broad modifier suggests mutational context may further modulate outcomes. 4) Endpoint choice (PFI): disease reclassification is intended to predict progression risk; progression-free interval maps directly to that clinical consequence and is more sensitive than overall survival for earlier shifts in management. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded Tier 3 exploratory paths (Barrett’s esophagus, polyps, breast-tissue transitional-stage notes) because they are tangential to prostate PSAD biology. I also excluded overloading the core hypothesis with multiple genes or speculative mutational drivers (e.g., TP53) to preserve mechanistic focus; such factors are listed as secondary_modifiers for downstream testing?

## Working Hypothesis

In localized prostate cancer, the predictive accuracy of PSA density (PSAD) for progression-free interval (PFI) is conditional on the tumor microenvironment and EMT status. Specifically, high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) and low E-cadherin (CDH1) expression—indicative of an inflammatory/desmoplastic milieu and EMT activation—will attenuate the ability of PSAD to reflect true invasive tumor burden, leading to PSAD-based misclassification of progression risk. Conversely, tumors with low TSR and preserved CDH1 expression will show PSAD as a more faithful surrogate for tumor burden and progression risk. These effects should manifest as significant PSAD × TSR and PSAD × CDH1 interactions in time-to-progression analyses, with inferior discrimination (lower AUC) and higher misclassification rates for PSAD-based decisions in the high-TSR/low-CDH1 subgroup. If we incorporate TSR and CDH1 into a combined PSAD-TSR-CDH1 metric, predictive performance for PFI should improve over PSAD alone, reflecting a more accurate accounting of non-tumor PSA sources and EMT-driven tumor aggressiveness.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
