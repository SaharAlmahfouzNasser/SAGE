# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Trials and observational data show that intravesical BCG and local immune structures (e.g., TLS/TILs) do not uniformly produce durable responses in NMIBC—why do some immune-infiltrated tumors still fail to respond to BCG? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on Core Path 1 (BCG-driven local immunity/biomarkers) and Core Path 3 (MSH6 as an MMR/prognostic factor) plus Core Path 2's logic linking mutational burden to immune activation (bTMB). These provide a mechanistic chain: MSH6 → altered mutational/neoantigen load (bTMB) → capacity for TLS-mediated priming under BCG. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: TP53/ER and systemic metabolic/hormonal signals (SHBG, IGF1) can modulate tumor proliferation and immune contexture, potentially shifting the threshold of neoantigen-driven T cell activation; pZVR and cross-cancer microenvironment signals (SARIFA) further suggest non-genomic modifiers of immune efficacy. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected a therapy-response proxy (BCG response) because the core problem is mechanistic failure of local immunotherapy despite immune presence; therapy response is the most direct, actionable endpoint for translational validation and trial stratification. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded PD-L1 from the core mechanistic pair to keep the hypothesis focused on the antigenicity axis (MSH6 → bTMB) interacting with organized local immunity (TLS). PD-L1, TP53, metabolic/hormonal markers, and cross-cancer histopathologic signals are listed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up rather than being included in the two-anchor core claim to maintain mechanistic clarity and testability?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates enable robust anti-tumor immunity after intravesical BCG primarily in NMIBC tumors where neoantigen load is elevated due to MSH6 deficiency and PD-L1–mediated inhibition is not prohibitive. Specifically, TLS-high NMIBC with MSH6 loss will display higher bTMB and greater BCG-induced immunologic priming, resulting in superior clinical response compared with TLS-high tumors that are MSH6-intact or that express high PD-L1. In contrast, TLS-high tumors with intact MSH6 (lower bTMB) or with high PD-L1 expression will show attenuated responses, highlighting a genomics-guided stratifier (MSH6 and PD-L1) for BCG benefit. The framework uses two Tier 1 anchor genes (MSH6, PD-L1) and treats bTMB as a mediator/phenotypic readout linking MSH6 status to neoantigen burden and TLS functionality during BCG therapy.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Gene expression does not equal protein activity; phospho-protein or functional assays are needed for confirmation.

## Alternative Directions

- PD-L1: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- TP53: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
