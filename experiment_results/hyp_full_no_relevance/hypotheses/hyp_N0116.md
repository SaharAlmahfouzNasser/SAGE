# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The ontological path links EPB41L2-driven cytoskeletal/adhesion programs to an ECM-remodeling/inflammatory output that prognostically matters, yet FGFR3-associated biology co-occurs with signals of better outcome. Why do stroma-rich, EPB41L2-associated tumors sometimes progress while others do not — could FGFR3 status modulate the deleterious stromal/ECM program? 2) Tier 1 path used: the single provided path (EPB41L2 → underlying risk → Osteopontin → prognosis → FGFR3) is my foundation because it directly connects adhesion/cytoskeletal perturbation to an ECM/inflammatory effector and a countervailing FGFR3-related favorable signature. 3) Tier 2/supporting evidence: empirical links between stromal remodeling and progression, and known roles of FGFR signalling in promoting differentiation/less invasive states, support the mechanistic plausibility that FGFR3 could blunt ECM-driven invasion; osteopontin (SPP1) is treated as the principal mediator and placed in secondary_modifiers. 4) Why PFI: the chain implicates ECM remodeling/invasion and thus risk of progression/recurrence more directly than late mortality, so progression-free interval (PFI) is the most targeted clinical endpoint to test the mechanistic question. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded imaging features tied to immune organization (TIL density, TLS) because the path centers on ECM/cytoskeletal programs; I excluded naming osteopontin in the core hypothesis to keep the core variables to the allowed maximum (osteopontin/SPP1 is included as a secondary_modifier for mechanistic follow-up). I also excluded other ECM genes not present in the provided path to avoid inventing unsupported anchors?

## Working Hypothesis

In tumors with a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR), elevated EPB41L2 expression drives an ECM remodeling and inflammatory program that accelerates tumor progression, resulting in a shorter progression-free interval (PFI). This adverse TSR/EPB41L2 effect on PFI is mitigated when FGFR3 signaling is high, which promotes a more differentiated, less invasive tumor cell state and counteracts the ECM-driven progression program. The impact of the TSR/EPB41L2 axis on PFI is further modulated by SPP1 (osteopontin) signaling as a secondary modifier, such that higher SPP1 amplifies progression risk in the TSR/EPB41L2-high, FGFR3-low context. This framework yields a testable mechanistic axis linking imaging features (TSR) with the cytoskeletal regulator EPB41L2, ECM remodeling, and FGFR3-mediated differentiation, with PFI as the primary endpoint.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
