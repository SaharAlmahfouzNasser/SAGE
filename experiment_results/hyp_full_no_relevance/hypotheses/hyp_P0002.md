# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Stroma-high tumors at the invasive front reliably predict worse outcomes, but the mechanistic basis—i.e., why a higher stromal fraction causally drives poorer survival—is unresolved. 2) Basis for core choice: From the single-path TSR evidence, the most direct mechanistic link is extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling; COL1A1 and FN1 are canonical matrisome effectors likely to alter tissue mechanics, ECM density, and biochemical cues that can impede immune infiltration and anti-tumor activity. 3) Supporting evidence (Tier 2-like): Other matrisome members (COL3A1, LAMA2) and T-cell exhaustion markers (PDCD1, CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT) provide a plausible chain from ECM remodeling → immune exclusion/exhaustion → poor outcome. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) is selected because it is the most robust, clinically meaningful endpoint available and least prone to ambiguous surrogate definitions in retrospective cohorts. 5) Exclusions and rationale: I deliberately excluded proliferation markers and hormone/HER2 signaling genes to keep the mechanistic focus on ECM-mediated immune exclusion; immune markers were relegated to secondary_modifiers because they support the mechanism but would exceed the 2-gene anchor limit if placed in the core hypothesis?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In muscle-invasive bladder cancer, tumors with high stromal content at the invasive front (high TSR) exhibit elevated expression of COL1A1 and FN1, driving extracellular matrix remodeling that creates a physical and biochemical milieu that excludes and impairs effector immune cells, leading to reduced anti-tumor immunity and poorer overall survival (OS), independent of standard clinicopathologic factors. Predicted testable outputs include: (1) a positive association between TSR and COL1A1/FN1 expression at the invasive front across MIBC cohorts; (2) tumors with high TSR and high COL1A1/FN1 show signatures of immune exclusion and reduced immune effector activity, reflected in transcriptional and spatial metrics; (3) the TSR–COL1A1/FN1–immune exclusion axis remains significantly associated with OS after adjusting for age, stage, treatment, and molecular subtype; (4) a multivariate ECM/invasion gene signature including COL1A1 and FN1 provides additive prognostic value beyond TSR alone. Experimental approach (feasible steps): - Analyze retrospective MIBC datasets with matched imaging-based TSR, transcriptomics, and survival data to assess correlations between TSR, COL1A1/FN1, and immune exclusion metrics (spatial proximity of immune cells to tumor front, exhaustion signatures). - Validate COL1A1 and FN1 protein expression and localization at the invasive front using immunohistochemistry or multiplexed imaging in independent tissue cohorts. - Employ spatial transcriptomics to quantify ECM remodeling genes and immune cell localization at the invasive front versus tumor core. - Assess immune infiltration and functional status via deconvolution and pathway analysis, focusing on exclusion/exhaustion phenotypes without naming T-cell genes in the core hypothesis. - Conduct in vitro assays with bladder cancer–associated fibroblasts and bladder cancer cell lines to test whether COL1A1/FN1-rich matrices reduce T-cell migration and cytotoxicity, and whether ECM stiffness correlates with immune cell impairment. - If feasible, explore in vivo models where stromal COL1A1/FN1 expression is modulated to observe effects on immune infiltration and tumor growth, providing causal evidence. - Perform sensitivity analyses adjusting for tumor purity, sampling site (invasive front vs core), and bladder cancer subtypes to confirm robustness of the association.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
