# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 synthesis raises the question of why some bladder tumors with apparent aggressive microenvironmental features nonetheless differ markedly in prognosis — specifically, do interactions between tumor-intrinsic DNA repair status (MSH6) and stromal remodeling/metastasis programs (GREM1-driven BMP antagonism) determine whether a stroma-rich phenotype leads to dissemination and poor outcome? I also excluded exploratory Tier 3 conceptual parallels (CSF AD biomarkers, pZVR, Gleason analog) because they are cross-disease analogies not mechanistically grounded in the Tier 1 axis?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) amplifies the oncogenic impact of GREM1-driven BMP antagonism specifically in tumors with MSH6 deficiency, leading to enhanced epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), stromal remodeling, and intravasation, which translates into poorer overall survival (OS). The highest risk is predicted for tumors that are TSR-high, GREM1-high, and MSH6-low. This axis operates through augmented TGF-β signaling and BMP pathway cross-talk, promoting metastatic competency and detectable circulating tumor cells (CTCs). Noninvasive readouts—SDC2/TFPI2 methylation in cfDNA and urinary markers (creatinine, microalbuminuria)—are expected to correlate with TSR-GREM1-MSH6 status and metastasis risk, serving as complementary biomarkers. Secondary modifiers (CTC burden, TGF-β activity, SDC2/TFPI2 methylation levels, urinary biomarkers) will act as mediators or parallel readouts, reinforcing the mechanistic link and providing feasible targets for therapeutic stratification. The hypothesis predicts: (1) a significant TSR × MSH6 × GREM1 interaction effect on OS with the TSR-high/MSH6-low/GREM1-high group having the worst outcome; (2) higher CTC counts in this group; (3) EMT and invasion markers elevated in models with MSH6 deficiency and GREM1-driven BMP antagonism in a stromally rich environment; and (4) improved prognosis with therapeutic strategies that simultaneously attenuate stromal remodeling and exploit DNA repair vulnerabilities.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
