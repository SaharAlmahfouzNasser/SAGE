# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

In muscle-invasive disease treated with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, why do some tumors with high pre-treatment TIL density fail to achieve clinical response? The single path links chemotherapy-induced cell death, KRAS signaling, and therapy-associated mutagenesis, suggesting KRAS status may modulate the immunogenic consequences of cytotoxic therapy and PD-1 blockade. 2) Tier 1 path used: the only presented path (ClinicalTrial → pembrolizumab+chemotherapy → chemotherapy induces cell death → cell death ↔ KRAS → drug (cisplatin) induces genetic mutations). I built on the mechanistic backbone that KRAS signaling can alter cell-death programs and that chemotherapy can both induce immunogenic cell death and cause mutagenesis. 3) Tier 2 evidence: there are no additional tiers provided; supporting plausibility comes from known biology that KRAS activation can dampen pro-immunogenic apoptosis/necrosis and that platinum agents (e.g., cisplatin) cause DNA damage that can create or select for resistant clones. These support a model where KRAS alters the balance between immunogenic cell death and therapy-driven evolution. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: the path centers on a clinical trial of pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy—hence response to this combined therapy is the most directly relevant and actionable endpoint. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded placing cisplatin-induced mutagenesis or generic DNA-repair genes into the core variables to keep the hypothesis focused; those processes (chemotherapy-induced mutagenesis, immunogenic cell death programs, emergent resistant clones) are noted as mechanistic modifiers in the notes but not forced into the primary hypothesis to satisfy the Focusing Rule?

## Working Hypothesis

Activating KRAS mutations modulate tumor-intrinsic immunogenicity such that high baseline TIL density does not predict response to pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in muscle-invasive tumors. KRAS-driven signaling attenuates immunogenic cell death and promotes immune evasion under cytotoxic therapy, lowering response rates in KRAS-mutant, TIL-high tumors compared with KRAS-wildtype, TIL-high tumors.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
