# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 synthesis highlights two tension points—non-invasive methylation signals (tumor DNA shed detectable in urine; exemplified by SDC2/TFPI2) that mark tumor burden/invasiveness, and mismatch-repair biology (MSH6) that modulates tumor antigenicity. The puzzle is why histologic evidence of local immune organization (TLS-like lymphoid aggregates) does not uniformly translate into durable control or longer progression-free intervals in bladder cancer. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the urine-DNA methylation biomarker path (non-invasive detection, SDC2/TFPI2) and the MSH6 prognostic path because together they mechanistically link tumor shedding/epigenetic state and antigen-presentation/neoantigen landscape to immune efficacy. These two axes can plausibly determine whether TLSs are functionally productive or epiphenomenal. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: TGF-β and SARIFA (tumor–stroma/invasion-front biology) provide a microenvironmental mechanism that could promote methylation-associated invasiveness and suppress TLS function; grading/architectural aggressiveness supports the idea that methylation-positive tumors are more invasive and more likely to shed DNA. 4) Why PFI as the endpoint: Progression-free interval best captures the clinical consequence of local immune control versus early invasive/recurrence biology (it is more sensitive than OS to local recurrence dynamics that urine methylation and TLS interplay would influence). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded casting stool-based SDC2/TFPI2 assays as-is (they informed the methylation principle but require bladder-specific validation) and omitted adding more genes into the core claim to keep the hypothesis mechanistically sharp. Other candidates (PTEN, GLU/metabolites, broader methylation panels) are placed as secondary modifiers for follow-up rather than in the core hypothesis?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates promote effective anti-tumor immunity in bladder cancer only when tumors retain functional MSH6 and exhibit low promoter methylation of SDC2. In TCGA-derived bladder tumors, TLS-rich cancers with MSH6 loss or high SDC2 promoter methylation will show shorter progression-free intervals, whereas TLS-rich cancers with intact MSH6 and low SDC2 methylation will have longer PFIs.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
