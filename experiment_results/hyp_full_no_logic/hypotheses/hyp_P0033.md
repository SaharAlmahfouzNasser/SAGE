## Clinical Question

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in bladder tumors predict superior response to immune checkpoint blockade predominantly when the tumor harbors mismatch repair–deficiency-driven neoantigen load (MSH6 deficiency/MSI-H) and when eIF4E-mediated translation is low, limiting expression of immune-evasive proteins and preserving antigen presentation. This defines a two-axis biomarker model (MSH6/MSI status × eIF4E activity) with a predicted hierarchy of responses: TLS-high, MSH6-deficient, low-eIF4E tumors show the strongest therapy_response; TLS-high with MSH6-deficient but high eIF4E activity or TLS-high with MSH6-proficient but low eIF4E activity show intermediate responses; TLS-high with MSH6-proficient and high eIF4E activity, or TLS-low tumors, show attenuated responses. Anchors MSH6 and eIF4E jointly determine whether TLS-associated local immunity translates into durable anti-tumor activity, enabling a clinically actionable stratification that combines histology with molecular and translational readouts.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
