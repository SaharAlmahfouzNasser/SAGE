## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in NMIBC create a microenvironment that can support anti-tumor immunity when tumor cells retain functional MSH6 and express low PD-L1. Loss of MSH6 drives mismatch-repair–associated hypermutation, increasing neoantigen burden but also upregulating PD-L1, which promotes adaptive immune exhaustion within TLS and undermines BCG-induced immunity. Therefore, TLS+ NMIBC tumors will have heterogeneous therapy_responses to intravesical BCG determined by the MSH6/PD-L1 axis: TLS+ tumors with intact MSH6 and low PD-L1 expression will show a higher therapy_response, whereas TLS+ tumors with MSH6 deficiency and high PD-L1 expression will show reduced therapy_response despite TLS presence. Secondary modifiers such as higher bTMB and MSI status may modulate the strength or direction of this effect and could enhance predictive power when combined with TLS status and the two anchors. The hypothesis generates specific, testable predictions: (1) among TLS+ NMIBC tumors, therapy_response to BCG is higher in MSH6-intact/PD-L1-low cases than in MSH6-deficient/PD-L1-high cases; (2) MSH6 loss correlates with increased bTMB and PD-L1 expression within TLS+ tumors; (3) incorporating bTMB/MSI as modifiers improves prediction of therapy_response beyond TLS and the two anchors alone; (4) Immunoscore nuance (density/composition of immune infiltrates) interacts with TLS status to shape the magnitude of the MSH6/PD-L1–dependent effect. Experimental approaches to test this include: retrospective multi-omics analysis of NMIBC cohorts treated with BCG to quantify TLS-like aggregates (via multiplex IHC for CD3, CD8, CD20, CXCL13, etc.), assess MSH6 status and PD-L1 expression (IHC and/or sequencing-based assays), compute bTMB and MSI status (NGS panels or exome data), and correlate with therapy_response using models that include interaction terms (MSH6 × PD-L1) while adjusting for confounders; validation in an independent cohort; prospective analyses integrating spatial transcriptomics and single-cell profiling of TLS regions; and mechanistic in vivo testing in orthotopic bladder cancer mouse models with MSH6 KO to evaluate BCG response and TLS formation, with optional PD-1/PD-L1 blockade to dissect causal circuitry.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TLS are associated with local anti-tumor immunity but their effectiveness depends on the intrinsic tumor phenotype;

**Prediction 2:** MSH6 function (MMR) governs tumor mutational burden and neoantigen load, which can drive PD-L1–mediated adaptive resistance within TLS-rich microenvironments;

**Prediction 3:** In NMIBC, BCG efficacy relies on robust local immune activation, which may be blunted when tumor-intrinsic checkpoints (PD-L1) are upregulated in the context of MSH6 loss. The two-anchor-gene constraint (MSH6 and PD-L1) with a single imaging feature (TLS-like aggregates) yields a focused, mechanistic hypothesis that is testable in retrospective cohorts and amenable to prospective validation.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
