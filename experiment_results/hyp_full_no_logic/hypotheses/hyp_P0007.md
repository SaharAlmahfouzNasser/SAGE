# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

SARIFA-positive and TGF-β–high MIBC tumors exhibit pronounced stromal remodeling yet show heterogeneous benefit from immunotherapy — why does a stromally inflamed/ remodeled microenvironment sometimes prevent, rather than permit, effective anti-tumor immune responses? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TGF-β as a fibrogenic, immunosuppressive driver (core mechanistic mediator of ECM deposition and immune exclusion) and (b) SARIFA (tumor–adipocyte/ stromal interaction phenotype) as the microenvironmental readout that captures fibro-adipocyte remodeling linked to invasion and altered immune contexture. I built on these because together they provide a mechanistic axis (cytokine-driven stromal remodeling → SARIFA microenvironment) that plausibly blocks immune effector access. 3) Tier 2 support: pZVR, PSA and metastasis-linked signals reinforce the clinical consequence (nodal spread / worse outcome) of the stromal state; SDC2/TFPI2 methylation and AI/MSI-pathways illustrate noninvasive and computational routes to measure/scale biomarkers and validate the stromal signal; these support testable stratification and deployment strategies. 4) Why therapy_response as endpoint: the core translational problem is immunotherapy failure in a subset of MIBC despite apparent microenvironmental changes — demonstrating a mechanistic cause of checkpoint blockade resistance has immediate therapeutic implications (patient selection, combination trials with anti–TGF-β or stroma-targeting agents). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded adding TLS/TIL density as a core imaging variable and PSA/SDC2/TFPI2/pZVR/MSI/AI as anchor genes to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the stromal mechanism (TSR + TGFB1 + SARIFA). Those excluded items are listed as secondary_modifiers because they are valuable for validation, measurement, or staging but would dilute the mechanistic claim if placed in the core hypothesis?

## Working Hypothesis

High stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) in MIBC, when accompanied by elevated TGFB1 signaling and a SARIFA-positive fibro-adipocyte remodeling phenotype, creates both a physical stromal barrier and a suppressive biochemical milieu that limit effector T cell infiltration, TLS formation, and anti-tumor function, thereby reducing response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. This effect is most pronounced in tumors with TSR-high/TGFB1-high/SARIFA-positive status and is predictive of poor objective response rate and shorter progression-free and overall survival on immunotherapy compared with TSR-low or TGFB1-low/SARIFA-negative tumors. Importantly, these tumors may be resensitized by combination therapy that disrupts TGF-β signaling or remodels the stroma (e.g., anti–TGF-β agents, CAF-targeting strategies) in addition to immune checkpoint blockade. A composite biomarker score integrating TSR (imaging/histology), TGFB1 pathway activity, and SARIFA status will stratify patients and guide therapeutic decision-making, enabling testing of combination regimens in prospective trials.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
