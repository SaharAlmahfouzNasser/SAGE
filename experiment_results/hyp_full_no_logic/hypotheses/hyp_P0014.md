# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a recurring paradox — markers of immune infiltration (Immunoscore/TILs) and noninvasive methylation signatures (SDC2/TFPI2) both predict NMIBC outcomes, yet some TIL-rich tumors still progress. Why does apparent immune infiltration sometimes fail to translate into durable control (longer progression-free interval)?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, high TIL density predicts prolonged PFI only when invasion-front stromal signaling remains intact; promoter methylation of SDC2 and TFPI2 disrupts stromal proteoglycan signaling and promotes ECM remodeling at the invasion front, creating an immune-excluded microenvironment. Specifically, SDC2/TFPI2 promoter methylation leads to reduced stromal signaling and increased invasion-front remodeling (e.g., via MMP activity), which mislocalizes CD8+ T cells away from tumor nests and impairs their cytotoxic function, resulting in shorter PFI despite apparent immune infiltration. This hypothesis yields testable predictions: (i) among TIL-high NMIBC tumors, those with SDC2/TFPI2 promoter methylation will have shorter PFI than unmethylated counterparts; (ii) spatial analyses will show T cells predominantly in stroma rather than tumor nests in methylated tumors; (iii) experimentally inducing demethylation or preventing SDC2/TFPI2 silencing will restore invasion-front signaling, enhance T cell–tumor contact, and prolong PFI. Feasible approaches include multi-omics cohort analyses (TIL density, methylation status, PFI), spatial transcriptomics/immunofluorescence for TIL localization, and in vitro/in vivo models employing targeted methylation/demethylation of SDC2 and TFPI2 to assess ECM remodeling and T cell behavior.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** NMIBC outcomes depend on invasion-front biology and immune contexture; immune infiltration alone (TIL density) often predicts better prognosis but can be overridden by immune-excluded microenvironments;

**Prediction 2:** SDC2 is a cell-surface proteoglycan involved in cell–ECM interactions, while TFPI2 inhibits proteolytic remodeling; promoter methylation likely downregulates these genes, promoting ECM/invasion-front remodeling and potentially reducing T cell access to tumor nests;

**Prediction 3:** integrating TIL density with epigenetic markers of invasion-front remodeling provides a testable axis (epigenetic–stroma–immune) that could explain paradoxical PFI outcomes and identify patients who remain at risk despite apparent immune infiltration.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.
- Multi-modal validation pipeline: computational -> spatial -> functional, providing mechanistic depth beyond association.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.
