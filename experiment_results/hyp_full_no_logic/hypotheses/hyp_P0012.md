## Working Hypothesis

Primary: In muscle-invasive bladder cancer, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates predict immunotherapy benefit only when the tumor harbors a high clonal neoantigen burden and intact MSH6 function. Loss of MSH6 shifts the neoantigen landscape toward subclonal or lower quality neoantigens and may impede efficient antigen presentation, thereby blunting TLS-driven T cell priming and reducing response to checkpoint blockade despite high TMB. Consequently, TLS-high, TMB-high tumors with MSH6 deficiency will have inferior objective response rates and shorter progression-free survival compared with TLS-high, TMB-high, MSH6-intact tumors. Secondary, testable assertions: (a) among TLS-high tumors, the strongest predictor of response is the interaction of TMB and MSH6 status; (b) subclonal neoantigen burden and reduced MHC I pathway activity will mediate diminished response in MSH6-deficient tumors. These hypotheses will be tested using integrated genomic (WES for TMB, MSH6 sequencing/IHC), neoantigen clonality analyses, and antigen presentation readouts in ICI-treated MIBC cohorts, with noninvasive modifiers (urine DNA methylation, CTC signatures) explored as adjunct biomarkers.
