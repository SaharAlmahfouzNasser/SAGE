# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 identified that TMPRSS2/ERG-driven oncogenic signaling is linked to elevated PD-L1 on urothelial cancer cells, yet intravesical BCG both requires local immune organization to work and can modulate PD-L1 — so why do some immune-infiltrated (TLS-rich) bladder tumors still fail BCG? 2) Tier 1 paths used: CORE PATH 1 (TMPRSS2/ERG → oncogenic signaling → PD-L1) and CORE PATH 2 (PD-L1 expression on cancer cells) form the mechanistic backbone because they directly connect tumor-intrinsic drivers to checkpoint expression that could blunt local immune structures. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: (a) BCG binds urothelial cells and elicits local immunity, (b) BCG can up-regulate PD-L1, and (c) PD-1/PD-L1 biology in carcinoma provides the functional rationale that PD-L1 can neutralize antitumor T cell activity generated by TLSs. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (BCG response) because the ontologist paths explicitly center on BCG–PD-L1 interactions in urothelial carcinoma; a therapy-response endpoint directly tests the clinically actionable question. 5) Deliberate exclusions and why: I treated the edge reading “PD-L1 up-regulate expression of BCG therapy” as a misannotation and excluded it as mechanistic evidence; I also excluded broader imaging modalities and late-fusion multimodal ideas (Tier 3) to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the PD-L1–TMPRSS2/ERG–TLS–BCG axis?

## Working Hypothesis

In urothelial carcinoma, intravesical BCG efficacy depends on TLS-driven local anti-tumor T cell activity. However, tumors that harbor TMPRSS2/ERG–associated signaling (or other ERG-family overexpression) upregulate PD-L1 and functionally suppress TLS-derived T cells, leading to reduced BCG response. Consequently, TLS-rich tumors that are TMPRSS2/ERG-positive and PD-L1-high will exhibit lower therapy_response to intravesical BCG compared with TLS-rich, TMPRSS2/ERG-negative tumors. This effect identifies a biomarker-defined subset that may require combination therapy (BCG plus PD-1/PD-L1 blockade or disruption of the TMPRSS2/ERG–driven signaling axis) to restore TLS efficacy. Three testable predictions follow: (1) In TLS-high tumors, TMPRSS2/ERG positivity and PD-L1 expression correlate with poorer BCG responses; (2) In preclinical models, PD-1/PD-L1 blockade rescues BCG efficacy in TMPRSS2/ERG-positive TLS-rich tumors; (3) In vitro, TMPRSS2/ERG overexpression augments PD-L1 induction in urothelial cells upon BCG exposure and diminishes T cell–mediated cytotoxicity, which is reversible by pathway inhibitors or PD-1 blockade. The study will employ a biomarker-driven approach, integrating TLS density, PD-L1, TMPRSS2/ERG status, and clinical response, to stratify patients for future interventional trials.
