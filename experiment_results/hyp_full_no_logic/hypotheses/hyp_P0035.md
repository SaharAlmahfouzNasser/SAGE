# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, noninvasive tumor readouts (urine-DNA methylation and liquid-biopsy signals) do not always track with clinical progression in bladder cancer — why do some tumors with low urine/ctDNA signals still progress rapidly? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the urine-DNA methylation biomarker path (direct tumor epigenetic shedding into urine) and the MSH6 prognostic path (tumor DNA-repair status driving mutation/necrosis/inflammation). These provide a mechanistic axis linking intrinsic tumor biology (MSH6) to the quantity/quality of tumor material shed into bodily fluids. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: stromal and microenvironmental modifiers (represented here by GREM1 and systemic host state/HDL-C from Tier 2) can alter tumor architecture, necrosis, and vascular/permeability features that modulate shedding; cross-compartment methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2) illustrate that compartmental detection sensitivity is context-dependent. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: Progression-free interval (PFI) was chosen because the question centers on surveillance/detection fidelity and early progression risk — i.e., when noninvasive biomarkers fail to flag tumors that will progress. PFI is the most relevant clinical outcome for surveillance utility. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded CTC/ctDNA from the core variables to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on urine-based detection and a single tumor-intrinsic modulator (MSH6); other Tier1/Tier2 entities (PSA, broader metabolic markers, TGF-β, CSF AD biomarkers) were excluded because they are either cross-disease confounders or insufficiently specific to the mechanistic puzzle of urine-shedding vs. progression?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, the prognostic utility of urine-DNA methylation for progression-free interval (PFI) is context-dependent and modulated by the tumor microenvironment and intrinsic DNA-repair status. Specifically: (1) A high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) reduces tumor DNA and cell shedding into urine, leading to lower urine-DNA methylation signals even in biologically aggressive tumors. (2) MSH6-deficient tumors, due to MSI-related instability and necrosis/inflammation, shed more DNA and yield higher urine methylation signals regardless of TSR. (3) Consequently, the predictive performance of urine-DNA methylation for PFI is attenuated in high-TSR, MSH6-proficient tumors (false negatives), whereas it is improved in low-TSR or MSH6-deficient contexts (true positives). Overall, TSR and MSH6 status interact to condition the relationship between urine methylation and tumor aggressiveness, explaining discordant noninvasive signals and progression risk.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
