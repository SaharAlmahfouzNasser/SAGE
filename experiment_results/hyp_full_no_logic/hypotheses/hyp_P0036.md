# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist identified convergent noninvasive signals (urine DNA methylation, stool SDC2/TFPI2, and blood CTCs) for bladder cancer, but it is unclear why methylation-based urine assays sometimes fail to identify biologically aggressive tumors and thus mis-estimate progression risk. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the urine DNA methylation path (direct anatomical shedding), the stool SDC2/TFPI2 methylation path (epigenetic marker relevance and assay design), and the CTC/liquid-biopsy path (systemic tumor shedding as an alternative detection axis). These jointly suggest that tumor–stromal architecture and shedding dynamics modulate assay sensitivity. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: TGF-β and GREM1–linked stromal/fibrotic remodeling (Tier 2) plausibly alter physical barrier properties and extracellular matrix composition, which can reduce tumor DNA release into urine while promoting other dissemination routes; metabolic/urinary modifiers (GLU, creatinine, microalbuminuria) and AI-based accessibility frameworks further support that host and assay-context factors change biomarker performance. 4) Why PFI was chosen: Progression-free interval is the most relevant clinical endpoint for testing whether noninvasive assays (used for surveillance/early detection) correctly stratify risk of recurrence/progression; PFI is more sensitive than OS for biomarker-driven surveillance decisions and is available in many clinical cohorts. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded naming TGF-β or CTCs from the core hypothesis sentence to keep it sharply focused (max 1 imaging feature + ≤2 anchor genes). ## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, the tumor-stroma ratio (TSR) of the primary tumor governs urine DNA shedding dynamics, thereby modulating the detectability of methylated SDC2 and TFPI2 in urine. Specifically, a high TSR (stroma-rich tumors) increases desmoplastic and ECM barriers, reducing tumor DNA release into urine and potentially selecting for tumor subclones with lower SDC2/TFPI2 methylation in shed DNA. Consequently, urine-based methylation assays will have reduced sensitivity for detecting aggressive disease in high-TSR tumors, leading to an underestimation of progression risk when relying on these markers alone and a relatively longer observed progression-free interval (PFI) than the true risk. Conversely, low TSR (tumor-dominant) tumors exhibit higher DNA shedding and higher detectability of SDC2/TFPI2 methylation in urine, correlating with a higher risk of progression (shorter PFI). Integrating TSR measurements with urinary SDC2/TFPI2 methylation status will improve risk stratification for PFI and therapeutic response. Secondary modifiers such as circulating tumor cells (CTCs) and additional multi-omics signals can further enhance predictive performance, but the core hypothesis remains that TSR governs urinary methylation signal sensitivity and, hence, the prognostic value of urine methylation assays. The primary testable predictions are: (a) across TSR strata, the relationship between urine SDC2/TFPI2 methylation and PFI will be stronger in low-TSR tumors than in high-TSR tumors; (b) adding TSR to a model with urine methylation markers will improve discrimination (e.g., AUC, C-index) for PFI; (c) a multi-modal panel incorporating TSR, urine methylation, and CTCs will yield superior prognostic performance compared with any marker alone.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
