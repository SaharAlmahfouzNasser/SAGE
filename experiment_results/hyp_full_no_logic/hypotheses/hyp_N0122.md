Other exploratory items (PSA density, finasteride, READ) are listed as secondary_modifiers for future cross-cancer comparisons but are not part of the central hypothesis?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In high-risk NMIBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates exist but functionally fail to support effective anti-tumor immunity when LAG3 expression is high, due to T cell exhaustion within TLS niches. Consequence: TLS-rich NMIBC tumors with high LAG3 will demonstrate reduced response to standard intravesical immunotherapy compared with TLS-low or low-LAG3 NMIBC. Secondary hypothesis: Therapeutic strategies that block LAG-3 or otherwise alleviate exhaustion within TLS will restore cytotoxic T cell activity in the TLS microenvironment and improve therapy response. Testable predictions: (a) Within TLS, high LAG3+ T cells co-localize with exhaustion markers (PD-1, TIM-3) and show reduced cytotoxic gene signatures (e.g., GZMB, PRF1) relative to TLS with low LAG3. (b) Patients with TLS-rich, high-LAG3 NMIBC exhibit lower objective response rates and shorter recurrence-free survival after intravesical immunotherapy than TLS-rich, low-LAG3 or TLS-poor NMIBC. (c) Ex vivo or in vitro assays treating NMIBC-derived T cells with LAG-3 blockade will enhance proliferation and cytotoxic function specifically in TLS-associated T cells. (d) Spatial transcriptomics will reveal distinct TLS microdomains where exhaustion programs predominate in high-LAG3 contexts, contrasting with more functional TLS zones in low-LAG3 contexts. Implications: If true, patient stratification by TLS architecture and LAG3 status could guide use of LAG-3–targeted or combination immunotherapies in high-risk NMIBC.”

## C. Functional Validation

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
