# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that immune context (Immunoscore/TILs), mismatch-repair biology (MSH6/MSI), and systemic dissemination (CTCs) each predict outcome, but these readouts sometimes conflict—i.e., lymphocyte-rich tumors do not always show durable therapy responses. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSH6/MSI (MMR → neoantigen/immune biology) path and the CTC/liquid biopsy path because they provide complementary tumor-intrinsic and systemic-dissemination axes that could mechanistically modulate immune effectiveness. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: TGF-β and GREM1 (stromal remodeling and EMT/metastasis) can suppress local immune function or facilitate dissemination (raising CTCs), SARIFA captures invasion-front stromal states that may exclude effective T cell activity, and urine methylation/AI-biomarkers offer orthogonal readouts to triangulate tumor state. 4) Why therapy_response as endpoint: The key translational question raised by the Ontologist is why immune-rich tumors sometimes fail clinically—this most directly maps to predicting response to immune therapies (therapy_response), which is actionable. 5) What I excluded and why: I excluded using Immunoscore/MSI simultaneously in the core hypothesis to maintain the required 1 imaging feature + ≤2 anchors focus; MSI, Immunoscore, TGF-β, GREM1, SARIFA and urine methylation are relegated to secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses to avoid an overfitted, multi-variable primary claim?

## Working Hypothesis

High TIL density alone fails to predict favorable ICI response in bladder tumors with MSH6 deficiency and high CTC burden, because MSH6-driven MSI creates a neoantigen landscape while tumor-intrinsic programs promote T cell exhaustion and systemic dissemination. Clinically, a combined model incorporating TIL density, MSH6 status, and CTC burden will outperform single biomarkers in identifying bladder cancer patients most likely to benefit from immunotherapy.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** retrospective analysis across multiple bladder cancer cohorts treated with ICIs to assess the predictive value of the combined biomarkers and interaction terms (TIL×MSH6, TIL×CTC, MSH6×CTC) for objective response rate, progression-free survival, and overall survival;

**Prediction 2:** prospective validation in an independent cohort with standardized assays. Measurements: TIL density by standardized pathology scoring or quantitative image analysis; MSH6 status by IHC and/ or targeted sequencing; CTC burden by validated enrichment and enumeration methods; MSI status by PCR/NGS; confounders (PD-L1 expression, tumor mutational burden, histology) to be accounted for in multivariate models.

**Prediction 3:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
