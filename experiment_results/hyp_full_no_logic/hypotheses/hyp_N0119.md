# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Pembrolizumab plus platinum chemotherapy should benefit immune-infiltrated (TIL-high) muscle-invasive bladder tumors by combining cytotoxic tumor killing with PD-1 blockade, but some TIL-rich tumors fail to respond. 2) Tier-1 path(s) used: the single path linking pembrolizumab+chemotherapy → cell death and KRAS → cell death: I build on the observation that KRAS signaling modulates cell-death programs and therefore could alter chemotherapy-induced immunogenic cell death and downstream immune-mediated tumor clearance. 3) Tier-2/supporting evidence from the path: chemotherapy (cisplatin) induces cell death and can cause therapy-induced mutations that affect clonal evolution and immunogenicity; these processes could interact with KRAS-driven cell-death modulation to change antigen release or immune visibility. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response because the clinical question is whether KRAS status changes sensitivity to the combined pembrolizumab+platinum regimen (most directly assessed by response rates), and because the path emphasizes treatment interactions rather than long-term survival. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded adding other genes or broader mutational burden as anchors to keep the hypothesis mechanistically focused on KRAS as the modulator of therapy-induced immunogenic cell death; other factors (e.g., therapy-induced mutagenesis, cisplatin pharmacodynamics) are listed as secondary modifiers for later exploration?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer treated with pembrolizumab plus platinum chemotherapy, baseline high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density predicts greater objective response rate (ORR) overall, but KRAS-mutant tumors within the high-TIL subgroup will exhibit a diminished benefit compared with KRAS-wildtype tumors. This arises because KRAS mutations may modulate chemotherapy-induced immunogenic cell death (ICD) and subsequent T cell–mediated tumor control, reducing the synergistic efficacy of PD-1 blockade and platinum chemotherapy in KRAS-mutant tumors. Therefore, there is a significant interaction between TIL_density (high vs low) and KRAS_mutation status (mutant vs wild-type) on ORR, such that ORR_highTIL_KRASmut < ORR_highTIL_KRASwt, with KRAS status having a lesser or no impact on ORR in TIL_low tumors. Secondary exploratory hypotheses include that therapy-induced mutational processes and cisplatin exposure further modulate this interaction and could influence ICD markers and neoantigen dynamics; these will be evaluated but are not required for the primary mechanistic test.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
