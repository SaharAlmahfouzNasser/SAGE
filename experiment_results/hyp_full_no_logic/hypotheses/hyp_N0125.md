## Working Hypothesis

In breast cancer, loss of epithelial integrity driven by CDH1 downregulation promotes EMT and remodeling of the tumor microenvironment (including ECM/stromal activation with COL1A1 and FN1), which increases chronic antigen exposure and T cell stimulation. This environment induces an exhaustion-like program in tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells, marked by elevated LAG3 expression. Consequently, tumors with low CDH1 and high LAG3 harbor an infiltrate that is numerically present but functionally impaired, correlating with poorer overall survival despite robust TIL density. The strength and detectability of this axis vary by molecular subtype and spatial context, with the highest LAG3 enrichment in CDH1-low, ECM-rich tumor regions. If surrogate blockade of LAG3 or combination immunotherapies are applied, these CDH1-low/ECM-rich tumors may show restored T cell function, suggesting a context-dependent, targetable immune dysfunction linked to epithelial state.
