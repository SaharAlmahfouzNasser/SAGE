# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, the key puzzle is why some NMIBC tumors that are immune-infiltrated (high Immunoscore/TILs) nonetheless fail to mount durable anti-tumor responses or benefit from immunomodulatory therapy (e.g., BCG). 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on Core Path 1 (TMB/NDRG1 link to immunogenicity), Core Path 2 (Immunoscore / SOX2 stemness shaping immune contexture), and Core Path 3 (MSH6 as an independent prognostic/MMR factor). These converge on a mechanism in which tumor-intrinsic stemness (SOX2) and DNA-repair/MMR state (MSH6) modulate whether immune infiltration translates to effective anti-tumor immunity. 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: NDRG1 (ties to mutational processes/TMB), DPYD and PDIA2 (metabolic/ER-stress axes that can influence antigen processing/presentation), IGF-II and systemic metabolic markers (host milieu shaping tumor phenotype), and MSI status/AI-based measurement strategies all support plausible modifiers of the SOX2–MSH6–immune interaction and give routes for measurement/stratification. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected a therapy_response endpoint (response to immunomodulatory therapy/BCG) because the clinical puzzle centers on why immune infiltration does not necessarily lead to clinical benefit; therapy response is most directly relevant to decision-making for NMIBC (e.g., BCG or checkpoint-based approaches). 5) What I excluded/deliberately limited: I excluded multi-gene models and kept the hypothesis tightly focused on one imaging readout + up to two anchor molecular features to preserve mechanistic clarity. I deliberately excluded broader panels (full metabolic marker set, KRAS, pZVR, SARIFA, and an expanded list of DNA repair/metabolic genes) from the core claim to avoid conflating measurement/implementation issues with the central mechanistic question; these are placed in secondary_modifiers for follow-up interrogation?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, high TIL density does not predict favorable therapy_response to intravesical immunotherapy when SOX2-driven stemness suppresses MSH6-dependent antigen presentation, leading to neoantigen heterogeneity and immune evasion. Clinically, tumors that are TIL-high and SOX2-high with MSH6-low will exhibit poor therapy_response to BCG/immune-based treatment and will require combination strategies that target cancer stemness or restore effective MSH6-mediated antigen presentation to convert immune infiltration into clinical benefit.
