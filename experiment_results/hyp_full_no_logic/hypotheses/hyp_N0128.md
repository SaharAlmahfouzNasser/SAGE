# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

CEACAM5 is upregulated in tumors and correlates with higher immune cell infiltration and MSI-H, yet high immune infiltration does not always translate into effective anti-tumor immunity or therapy response. 2) Built on the single provided path linking CEACAM5 → higher immune infiltration → MSI-H because it directly connects a tumor-expressed adhesion molecule to an inflamed, potentially immunogenic genomic background. 3) Supporting evidence (Tier 2 equivalent): MSI-H tumors are known to be immunogenic (high neoantigen load) and yet clinical responses vary, implying tumor-intrinsic modulators (like surface adhesion molecules) could alter immune cell localization/function. The ultrasound→gene expression edge was treated as a negative control (no causal effect). 4) I chose therapy_response as the endpoint because MSI-H and immune infiltration are most clinically relevant to immunotherapy responsiveness, making this the most problem-focused outcome. 5) Deliberately excluded additional genes or speculative immune checkpoints to keep the mechanistic question sharp; ultrasound and claims that ultrasound alters gene expression were excluded because the path states no evidence of causation?

## Working Hypothesis

CEACAM5 upregulation in tumor cells promotes an immune-inflamed microenvironment characterized by high Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte (TIL) density, but creates either retention of T cells at tumor cell surfaces or induction of T cell exhaustion/suppressive signaling, resulting in dysfunctional anti-tumor immunity. Consequently, tumors with high CEACAM5 expression and high TIL density will exhibit reduced objective therapy_response to immunotherapy compared with CEACAM5-low tumors with similar TIL densities. This effect is particularly pronounced in MSI-H tumors, where high neoantigen load coexists with CEACAM5-mediated modulation of immune Engagement. Predicted testable features include: (1) CEACAM5-high tumors show high TIL density but increased exhaustion markers (e.g., PD-1, LAG-3) and altered spatial distribution (peritumoral clustering with limited intratumoral cytotoxic synapses); (2) CEACAM5 expression correlates with poorer therapy_response to checkpoint inhibitors after adjusting for TIL density, tumor mutational burden, and PD-L1; (3) functional assays where CEACAM5 manipulation alters T cell activation in co-culture and CEACAM5 overexpression impairs cytotoxic killing in vivo under immunotherapy pressure.

- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
