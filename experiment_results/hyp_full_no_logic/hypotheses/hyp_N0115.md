# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why does extracellular-matrix/matricellular remodeling sometimes predict poor outcome but not uniformly across tumors? The single Ontologist path suggests EPB41L2-driven cytoskeletal/matrix signaling elevates matricellular programs (osteopontin) that shape prognosis, with FGFR3 status marking a distinct prognostic subclass. 2) Tier 1 path used: EPB41L2 → underlying risk factors → Osteopontin → prognosis → FGFR3; I built on EPB41L2 (upstream driver of cell–matrix signaling) and FGFR3 (prognostic subtype modifier) because they define a clear mechanistic axis connecting tumor intrinsic signaling to outcome. 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: matricellular/ECM remodeling genes and increased stromal fraction (e.g., NABA_CORE_MATRISOME members) provide a mechanistic link between the molecular cascade and a histologic readout; stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) on H&E is a practical morphological surrogate for this remodeling. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) was chosen as the primary endpoint because the ontologist path centers on prognosis and OS is the most robust, clinically meaningful endpoint available in TCGA-BRCA. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded adding proliferation, immune, or many ECM genes into the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (they can be tested as modifiers). I placed Osteopontin (the central matricellular mediator in the path) in secondary_modifiers rather than as a core anchor to meet the 2-gene limit and preserve focus on the upstream driver (EPB41L2) and the modifying receptor state (FGFR3)?

## Working Hypothesis

EPB41L2-driven perturbation of cytoskeletal–matrix signaling promotes matricellular remodeling, including upregulation of Osteopontin as a secondary modifier, which leads to an increased stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) on H&E and drives more aggressive tumor behavior; this remodeling is modulated by FGFR3 status. Consequently, high TSR will predict poorer overall survival specifically in EPB41L2-dysregulated tumors, whereas FGFR3-altered tumors exhibit a remodeling-insensitive, comparatively favorable prognosis.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** in silico analyses stratifying tumors by EPB41L2 dysregulation and FGFR3 status to correlate TSR, Osteopontin expression, MATRISOME signatures, and OS across cohorts;

**Prediction 2:** in vitro models with cancer cells and fibroblasts where EPB41L2 or FGFR3 are manipulated to assess effects on Osteopontin secretion and ECM remodeling markers;

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
