# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

BCG efficacy depends on local immune priming, so why do some tumors with evident local immune structures fail to respond to BCG? The KG suggests a chain where ERG (TMPRSS2/ERG) cooperates with PI3K activation to upregulate PD-L1, potentially creating an immunosuppressive niche that undermines BCG-driven immunity. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) TMPRSS2/ERG → cooperativity with PI3-kinase pathway activation → PD-L1 upregulation (mechanistic backbone explaining tumor-intrinsic immune suppression), and (b) BCG → upregulation of PD-L1 on target cells (links therapy to PD-L1 dynamics). I built on these because they directly explain how tumor signaling can neutralize local immune stimulation. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: PSMA enrichment in cancer stem cells (links a resistant subpopulation that may persist despite immune activation), androgen-deprivation/prostate context (therapy-driven selection pressures that can alter signaling), and the general node that PD-L1 expression on cancer cells mediates immune evasion—these reinforce why PD-L1 elevation would blunt BCG benefit. 4) Why I chose therapy_response as the endpoint: the KG centers on BCG immunotherapy and PD-L1 modulation, so a therapy-response proxy (BCG response) is the most relevant, testable clinical outcome. 5) What I deliberately EXCLUDED and why: I excluded androgen-deprivation and PSMA as anchor genes to keep the mechanistic hypothesis sharp (max 2 anchors); they remain as secondary modifiers for follow-up. I also excluded broad pathway components (e.g., AKT, mTOR) from the core claim to avoid diluting the central ERG→PI3K→PD-L1 mechanism?

## Working Hypothesis

In TLS-high breast cancer tumors, ERG-driven activation of the PI3K pathway induces upregulation of PD-L1 on tumor and/or immune cells within TLS structures. This PD-L1 upregulation creates an immunosuppressive TLS microenvironment that attenuates TLS-mediated immune priming and reduces response to BCG-like immunotherapies. Consequently, TLS-high tumors that are ERG-positive and PD-L1-high will show poorer therapy response compared with TLS-high, ERG-negative or PD-L1-low tumors. This effect may be modulated by additional Tier-2 modifiers (e.g., PIK3CA gain-of-function, PTEN loss, TMPRSS2 expression, PSMA expression). Importantly, inhibiting the PI3K pathway or blocking PD-L1 is predicted to restore TLS functionality and improve therapy response, providing a testable mechanism for combination strategies that re-engage TLS-driven immunity.
