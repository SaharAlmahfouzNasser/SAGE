# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Provisional, testable mechanistic hypothesis (NMIBC with cisplatin-based therapy; imaging feature: Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte density): Higher intratumoral TIL density and the presence of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates create a pro-inflammatory tumor immune microenvironment that enhances cisplatin-induced immunogenic cell death and antigen presentation, thereby improving clinical response as measured by progression-free interval (PFI) or a therapy-response proxy. This effect is mechanistically linked to: (i) robust DNA damage signaling and repair gene status ( Anchor genes to be confirmed; provisional anchors: ERCC1 and BRCA1) which modulate the extent of tumor immunogenicity after cisplatin, (ii) activation of interferon-gamma signaling and antigen presentation pathways (e.g., IFNG, HLA genes, CXCL9/10, CXCL13) that recruit and activate cytotoxic T cells, and (iii) TLS formation enabling coordinated adaptive immune responses. The central mechanistic question is: through which combination of DNA repair capacity and immune microenvironment features does cisplatin efficacy get amplified in NMIBC? Primary endpoint: progression-free interval (PFI) or a validated cisplatin-response proxy; Imaging feature must be TIL density (exactly one chosen). Secondary considerations include potential mediators such as tumor mutational burden, PD-L1 expression, and TLS score, which may modify the strength of the TIL–cisplatin efficacy link. Experimental readouts should integrate histopathology (IHC for CD3/CD8, TLS markers), transcriptomic signatures (IFNG signaling, antigen presentation), and functional assays (immunogenic cell death markers) to establish causality.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Tumor immune contexture, including TIL density and TLS presence, correlates with improved prognosis and therapeutic responses across multiple cancers, and can modulate chemotherapy efficacy by promoting anti-tumor immunity.

**Prediction 2:** Cisplatin and other DNA-damaging agents can induce immunogenic cell death, increasing antigen release and presentation, thereby engaging T cells if the microenvironment is permissive.

**Prediction 3:** DNA damage response pathways (e.g., ERCC1, BRCA1) influence both repair capacity and immunogenic signaling, potentially shaping the magnitude of chemo-immunomodulation. By aligning imaging features (TIL density) with underlying DNA repair and immune pathways, the hypothesis offers a testable mechanism for personalized cisplatin responsiveness in NMIBC.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
