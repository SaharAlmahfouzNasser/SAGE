## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are present but do not guarantee improved progression-free interval (PFI) when tumors exhibit a SOX2-driven cancer stem cell program and high MSH6 expression (MMR-proficient). The proposed mechanism is twofold: (1) SOX2-induced stemness alters the tumor secretome and stromal remodeling in a way that impairs TLS maturation and function, reducing T cell activation within TLS niches (e.g., decreased CXCL13/T follicular helper signaling, increased immunosuppressive cytokines such as TGF-β, and dysfunctional dendritic cell education), thereby transforming TLS from an active anti-tumor microenvironment into a dysfunctional or exhausted structure. (2) High MSH6 expression results in proficient DNA repair and a relatively lower tumor mutational burden/neoantigen quality, diminishing TLS-driven neoantigen presentation and cytotoxic T cell priming within TLS. The combination—SOX2-high and MSH6-high in TLS-rich tumors—creates a high-risk subset in which TLS presence is insufficient to prevent progression, yielding shorter PFI despite TLS localization. This model accommodates the observed clinical heterogeneity and provides testable predictions about TLS maturation state and immune activity beyond mere TLS presence. Secondary modifiers (NDRG1, DPYD, PDIA2, SDC2, TFPI2) are anticipated to modulate the degree of TLS maturation and immune exclusion, thereby refining risk stratification within TLS-rich tumors. Testable predictions include: (a) among TLS-positive NMIBC, SOX2-high/MSH6-high tumors show shorter PFI than TLS-positive SOX2-low or MSH6-low tumors; (b) TLS maturation markers (e.g., CXCL13, CD21, BTNL2) and functional T cell signatures are reduced in SOX2-high/MSH6-high tumors; (c) higher expression of secondary modifiers correlates with impaired TLS maturation and worse PFI within TLS-positive, SOX2-high/MSH6-high cohorts; (d) neoantigen burden and TCR clonality patterns align with MSH6 status and TLS functionality. Feasible testing approaches are outlined below.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TLS presence is commonly associated with anti-tumor immunity, but TLS maturation and function—not just presence—predict clinical benefit;

**Prediction 2:** cancer stem cell programs (SOX2) rewire tumor-immune interfaces and secretome, often promoting stromal remodeling and immunosuppression;

**Prediction 3:** MMR proficiency (MSH6-high) tends to yield lower mutation/neoantigen burden, potentially dampening TLS-driven antigen-specific responses. In NMIBC, reports linking high MSH6 to worse outcomes support a paradox where proficient repair and lower neoantigen generation, coupled with stemness-driven immunomodulation, yields TLS-rich yet ineffective anti-tumor immunity.
