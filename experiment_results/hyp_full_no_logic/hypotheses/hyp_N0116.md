# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The path links inflammatory signaling (S100A9) to PD-L1–mediated immune suppression, raising the question why some tumors with abundant lymphocytes nevertheless fail to respond to PD-1/PD-L1–targeted therapy. 2) Tier 1 path used: the core S100A9 → PD-L1 axis (signaling network → S100A9 → PD-L1) because it provides a direct mechanistic route by which upstream inflammation could sustain checkpoint ligand expression and blunt T cell function. 3) Tier 2/supporting evidence: EPB41L2, SPP1 (Osteopontin) and FGFR3 link the risk-factor/inflammatory milieu to prognosis and may modulate myeloid or stromal contexts that reinforce S100A9-driven immunosuppression; these are retained as secondary modifiers. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: I selected therapy_response (PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint blockade response) because PD-L1 is the proximal functional mediator in the path and the clinical puzzle concerns immune-evasion despite lymphocyte presence. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded other imaging features (TLS-like aggregates, TSR) and placed EPB41L2/SPP1/FGFR3 in secondary_modifiers to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the mechanistic question of how S100A9-driven PD-L1 expression functionally decouples TIL abundance from checkpoint therapy benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In hepatocellular carcinoma, high intratumoral S100A9 drives myeloid-cell–mediated paracrine upregulation and maintenance of CD274 (PD-L1) expression, thereby functionally uncoupling Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte (TIL) density from effective cytotoxic T cell activity. Consequently, tumors with high TIL density and elevated S100A9/CD274 will show reduced therapy_response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade compared with tumors that are TIL-high but S100A9-low or CD274-low. Secondary modifiers EPB41L2, SPP1, and FGFR3 are expected to modulate the magnitude of this effect by shaping the myeloid/stromal milieu that sustains PD-L1–mediated suppression. The hypothesis yields testable predictions: (a) in PD-1/PD-L1–treated HCC cohorts, the interaction of high TIL density with high S100A9 and CD274 expression predicts poorer therapy_response; (b) disrupting S100A9 signaling or PD-L1 reduces this resistance; (c) EPB41L2, SPP1, and FGFR3 status will stratify response within the TIL-high/S100A9/CD274-high category.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** expression often correlates with immune infiltration but does not universally predict benefit from PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, indicating additional regulatory layers. S100A9 is a damage-associated molecular pattern protein/alarmin that drives myeloid-cell recruitment and MDSC polarization, and can induce PD-L1 upregulation through TLR/NF-κB–type signaling in myeloid and possibly tumor cells.

**Prediction 2:** anchor the core mechanism; imaging feature (TIL density) captures the inflammatory context; and therapy_response as the endpoint aligns with clinical relevance in HCC treated with checkpoint inhibitors. Secondary modifiers EPB41L2, SPP1, and FGFR3 are plausible modulators of myeloid/stromal interactions and could refine the predictive model.

**Prediction 3:** In retrospective cohort data, the prognostic benefit of the imaging feature will be modified by the anchor gene expression level (significant interaction term).


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
