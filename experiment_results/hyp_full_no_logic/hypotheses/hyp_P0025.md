# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a tension—MMR defects (MSH6/MSI-dMMR) increase mutational burden and often T-cell infiltration, yet immune contexture (Immunoscore/TILs) does not always translate into effective anti-tumor immunity or predictable therapy benefit. Why does immune infiltration sometimes fail to produce clinical response in MSH6/MSI tumors?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer with MSH6-driven MSI/dMMR, high TIL density indicates robust immune infiltration but does not universally predict checkpoint inhibitor (IO) therapy response. IO efficacy depends on (i) clonal neoantigen burden (favoring clonal over subclonal antigens) and (ii) intact antigen presentation (e.g., preserved HLA class I function, absence of B2M/HLA loss). Therefore, TIL density is a context-dependent predictor: it will correlate with therapy_response only in tumors with high clonal neoantigen load and preserved antigen presentation; in MSHand MSI-dMMR bladder tumors with subclonal neoantigens or impaired antigen presentation, high TIL density may not translate into clinical benefit. This rationale justifies combining TIL density with measures of neoantigen clonality and antigen presentation status to predict IO response, and incorporating noninvasive modifiers (urine DNA methylation, CTCs, AI-based imaging biomarkers) to refine patient selection. Testable predictions include: (a) among MSH6/MSI/dMMR bladder cancers treated with IO, responders will have higher clonal neoantigen burden and intact HLA presentation compared with non-responders, even when TIL density is high; (b) TIL-high tumors with low clonal neoantigen burden or evidence of HLA loss will show poorer therapy_response; (c) a composite model integrating TIL density, neoantigen clonality, and HLA status will outperform TIL density alone in predicting therapy_response. Feasible study design involves a bladder cancer cohort with known MSH6/MSI/dMMR status treated with IO, with data on TIL density, neoantigen clonality, HLA/B2M status, and noninvasive biomarkers, enabling multivariate modeling and validation in an independent set.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
