# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

NMIBC prognosis reflects both histologic aggressiveness and the local anti-tumor immune response, but some morphologically high‑risk or TIL‑rich tumors still recur or progress — why does immune infiltration sometimes fail to prevent progression? 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) tumor proliferative burden (MKI67) vs. immune effector capacity (CD8A) — the key mechanistic axis where high proliferation may outpace or blunt CD8+ T‑cell control; (b) histomorphologic risk (Stage/Grade/CIS/budding) that signals invasion potential and microenvironment remodeling that can favor immune escape. I built on these because they directly address when/why immune presence (imaging TILs) does not translate to tumor control. 3) Tier 2 supporting evidence: cytotoxic/effector transcripts (IFNG, GZMB) and chemokines (CXCL9, CXCL10) indicate functional anti-tumor immunity, while checkpoints (PDCD1/CD274) and tumor architectural features (CIS, budding) can modulate effectiveness of CD8 responses; these support the core mechanistic interaction but are secondary modifiers. 4) Endpoint choice: PFI (progression-free interval) was selected because the clinical problem motivating NMIBC risk stratification is recurrence/progression to muscle‑invasive disease rather than overall survival in early-stage NMIBC; PFI aligns with the decision points for surveillance and adjuvant therapy. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded adding more than two anchor genes in the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (other immune/cytotoxic/ checkpoint genes are relegated to secondary_modifiers). I also excluded alternative imaging features (TLS-like aggregates or TSR) to focus on the most directly relevant, widely assessable H&E metric — TIL density?

## Working Hypothesis

Refined hypothesis: In NMIBC, the protective association between tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TIL density) and progression-free interval is context-dependent on tumor proliferative burden and CD8+ T-cell functional status. Specifically, high TIL density predicts longer PFI when MKI67 (proliferation) is low and CD8A (CD8+ cytotoxic cells) expression is high, reflecting effective cytotoxic control; conversely, when MKI67 is high and/or CD8A is low, high TIL density does not confer protection and may correlate with shorter PFI due to proliferation-driven immune evasion or T-cell dysfunction. This context dependence can be captured by an integrated NMIBC-IMES (Immune–Proliferation–Effector Score) that combines: TIL density (imaging feature), MKI67, CD8A, and functional/chemokine markers (IFNG, GZMB, CXCL9, CXCL10) with checkpoint indicators (PDCD1, CD274). Primary aim: test interactions between TIL density and (i) MKI67 and (ii) CD8A on PFI; secondary aims: assess whether functional markers and checkpoint expression modify these interactions. The study will classify tumors into risk strata with distinct PFI trajectories and explore potential for guiding intensified surveillance or adjuvant therapy in NMIBC.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
