# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Pretreatment TLS presence and organization should predispose MIBC to benefit from intravesical BCG, but not all TLS-rich tumors respond — what molecular or spatial constraints determine when TLS amplify BCG-elicited anti-tumor immunity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the Ontologist's core paths that TLS presence/density PREDICTS BCG_response, TLS proximity-to-tumor modulates immune impact, and spatial organization metrics reflect effective local immunity. These provide the mechanistic backbone linking morphology/spatial context to therapeutic amplification by BCG. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: chemokine drivers of TLS (CXCL13, CCL21) and markers of B-cell zones (CD20, CD21) were listed as supporting entities; these explain how TLS form and organize to position effector and antigen-presenting cells near tumor epithelium. I therefore selected chemokines as mechanistic anchors because they directly mediate TLS recruitment/maturation and can explain TLS functionality. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: BCG response (recurrence-free outcome or categorical response) is the most clinically relevant and proximal endpoint for this biomarker concept — the stated goal is to predict and explain differential BCG efficacy, not long-term OS. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broad spatial metrics and multiple immune-subtype markers from the core hypothesis to keep it mechanistically sharp (max 1 imaging feature + up to 2 anchor genes). Metrics like Ripley’s K, tumor-budding, and additional IHC markers (CD3, CD8) are retained as secondary_modifiers/assays for validation but not in the core mechanistic claim?

## Working Hypothesis

Pretreatment TLS-like lymphoid aggregates located proximal to the tumor boundary form a chemokine-rich niche dominated by CXCL13 and CCL21 that supports local antigen presentation and rapid recruitment/interaction of B cells (CD20+/CD21+ zones) and helper T cells, thereby amplifying the intravesical BCG–driven anti-tumor immune response. When TLS are present and CXCL13/CCL21 signaling is high, patients will exhibit superior therapy_response to intravesical BCG, with lower recurrence rates and longer recurrence-free survival; when TLS are sparse, poorly organized, or CXCL13/CCL21 signaling is low, BCG-induced immunity is insufficient, leading to poorer therapy_response. Specific, testable predictions include: (1) pretreatment TLS density and proximity to tumor correlate positively with favorable therapy_response; (2) pretreatment expression levels of CXCL13 and CCL21 correlate with TLS maturation/organization and predict therapy_response beyond TLS density alone; (3) ex vivo or in vitro disruption of CXCL13–CXCR5 or CCL21–CCR7 signaling diminishes BCG-stimulated immune activation in bladder tissue models; (4) CD20/CD21 prevalence within TLS serves as a surrogate for TLS functionality and strengthens the predictive value of CXCL13/CCL21. Experimental approaches will include retrospective tissue analyses (IHC for TLS markers, CXCL13, CCL21; CD20/CD21; CD3) with digital spatial quantification, RNA in situ hybridization or spatial transcriptomics for chemokine/cell-type localization, and correlation with therapy_response (responder vs non-responder) and recurrence-free survival; prospective validation could utilize similar assays in a new BCG-treated cohort, supplemented by ex vivo bladder tissue cultures to test chemokine-blockade effects on BCG-driven cytokine production and cell recruitment.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
