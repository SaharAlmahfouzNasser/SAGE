# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

In muscle-invasive disease treated with pembrolizumab plus cytotoxic chemotherapy, why do some tumors fail to convert chemo-induced tumor cell death into effective T cell–mediated clearance? 2) Tier 1 paths used: Core Path 1 (clinical testing of pembrolizumab in muscle-invasive patients) and Core Path 2 (synergy of pembrolizumab + chemotherapy leading to cell death) provide the treatment context and mechanistic expectation that chemo can create immunogenic tumor cell death that PD-1 blockade should exploit. I built on Core Path 3 (KRAS mutational context) as a tumor-intrinsic modifier that can reprogram death/immune signaling. 3) Tier 2 support: links between KRAS signaling and cell-death programs (KRAS → apoptotic/necrotic bias), the PD-1/PD-L1 axis (PDCD1/CD274) as the immunotherapy target, and cisplatin’s DNA-damage/immunogenic effects support a mechanism where KRAS alters chemo-induced immunogenicity and PD-L1/T cell dynamics. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (pembrolizumab + chemotherapy response) because the core paths are therapeutic (trial + combo therapy) and the mechanistic question asks why response is variable; therapy_response is the most proximate, testable clinical readout for that puzzle. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broad, non-specific ideas like “drug-induced new mutations” as primary modifiers because they are diffuse and not directly testable in the initial focused hypothesis; I also withheld tissue-context claims (e.g., colon-specific KRAS biology) from the core hypothesis because the ontologist flagged cross-cancer context as uncertain and because the hypothesis targets muscle-invasive disease specifically?

## Working Hypothesis

Activating KRAS signaling in muscle-invasive bladder cancer dampens chemo-induced immunogenic cell death and impairs T cell–mediated anti-tumor immunity, thereby reducing the therapeutic efficacy of pembrolizumab in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy. Specifically, KRAS-mutant tumors will exhibit (a) attenuated IFN-γ–driven upregulation of PD-L1 (CD274) and diminished antigen-presentation signaling after chemotherapy, (b) lower intratumoral TIL density following chemo+ipi (immune checkpoint blockade) treatment, and (c) inferior therapy_response compared with KRAS-wildtype tumors that possess high baseline or chemo-induced TILs. Secondary modifiers such as PDCD1 (PD-1) expression on T cells and downstream KRAS/MAPK signaling will modulate the magnitude of this effect. Testable predictions: (1) KRAS-mutant, TIL-low tumors will show the least response to pembrolizumab+chemotherapy; (2) post-treatment biopsies will reveal reduced TIL density and lower CD274 induction in KRAS-mutant tumors; (3) pharmacologic or genetic dampening of KRAS/MAPK signaling or restoration of antigen presentation will enhance chemo-immunotherapy–driven TIL recruitment and improve therapy_response in KRAS-mutant models.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** upregulation is often IFN-γ–driven and may reflect an active immune microenvironment; however, in KRAS-mutant contexts, impaired antigen presentation and T cell trafficking can decouple PD-L1 expression from effective T cell–mediated killing, leading to poor response to pembrolizumab-containing regimens.
