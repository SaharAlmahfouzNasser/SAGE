# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis the key puzzle is: why do some bladder tumors that appear immunologically inflamed (high local lymphoid organization / Immunoscore) and show systemic tumor activity (detectable CTCs) nevertheless fail to respond to therapy? This implies a disconnect between local immune presence and effective anti-tumor immunity, potentially mediated by tumor-intrinsic genomic features?

## Working Hypothesis

Core hypothesis: In bladder cancer, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates indicate localized anti-tumor immunity, but their predictive value for immunotherapy benefit is contingent on tumor MSH6 status. Specifically, TLS-rich tumors that are MSH6-proficient will exhibit higher responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) than TLS-rich tumors that are MSH6-deficient. In the latter, MSH6-driven mismatch repair perturbations foster subclonal neoantigen heterogeneity and rapid immune editing, selecting for antigen-loss, dissemination-competent clones detectable as circulating tumor cells (CTCs), thereby overriding TLS-associated immune advantage and yielding reduced ICI response. This core axis can be read as: TLS presence + MSH6 proficiency predicts benefit; TLS presence + MSH6 deficiency predicts limited benefit, particularly in CTC-positive tumors. Secondary modifiers modulate this axis by shaping the tumor microenvironment and immune visibility: TGFB1 and GREM1 promote immunosuppressive/fibrotic stroma that impairs TLS function and antigen presentation; epigenetic silencing signals (SDC2, TFPI2) reduce tumor antigen visibility; HDL-C reflects systemic metabolic state that can influence immune surveillance. Integrated model expectations: (1) TLS-rich, CTC-negative, MSH6-proficient tumors demonstrate the strongest ICI responses; (2) TLS-rich, CTC-positive, MSH6-deficient tumors show attenuated responses due to dissemination-competent, immune-evasive subclones; (3) modifiers TGFB1/GREM1/SDC2/TFPI2/HDL-C shift the probability of response within TLS+/MSH6 strata. Testable predictions include interaction effects between TLS, MSH6 status, and CTCs on response rate and progression-free survival, which are further modulated by the modifier panel. Endpoints to prioritize: objective response rate, duration of response, progression-free survival under ICI therapy, with validated composite scores combining TLS density, MSH6 status, and CTC burden plus modifier biomarker readouts.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
