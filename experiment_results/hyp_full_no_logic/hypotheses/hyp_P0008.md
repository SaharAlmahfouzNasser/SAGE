# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 highlights two apparently opposing axes—MSI/dMMR confers high neoantigen load (predicted ICI sensitivity), while TGFB1-driven fibrosis creates a desmoplastic, immunosuppressive microenvironment; the puzzle is why MSI-high MIBC tumors sometimes fail to respond to immune checkpoint inhibitors. 2) Tier 1 paths used: CORE PATH 1 (TGFB1/TGF-β → fibrogenic, immunosuppressive stroma) and CORE PATH 2 (MSI/dMMR → high neoantigen/T-cell recognition). I built on these because they provide a clear mechanistic tension between intrinsic tumor immunogenicity and extrinsic stromal/immune exclusion. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: TP53/ESR1 contextual biology can modulate proliferation and immune visibility; SDC2/TFPI2 methylation and HDL-C/metabolic state offer noninvasive or systemic modifiers of tumor microenvironment and inflammation that could influence stromal remodeling or immune recruitment. These support testing modifiers of the TGFB1–MSI interaction. 4) Why therapy_response as the endpoint: the central puzzle concerns functional uncoupling of neoantigen load from clinical benefit of immune checkpoint blockade, so therapy response (ICI sensitivity) is the most mechanistically relevant and actionable endpoint. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I deprioritized the AI-accessibility axis and many Tier-3 exploratory cross-disease links from the core mechanistic sentence to keep the hypothesis sharp (they remain important as translational/implementation follow-ups and are listed in secondary_modifiers). I also excluded adding >2 genes in the core claim to avoid a correlative, overfitted model and to focus on a testable mechanistic interaction between stromal density (TSR) and MSI status?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, MSI/dMMR tumors defined by MSH2 deficiency that also exhibit a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) driven by TGFB1 signaling will show reduced response to immune checkpoint inhibitors due to a TGFB1-mediated desmoplastic and immunosuppressive microenvironment that physically and functionally excludes tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells from neoantigen-rich MSI/dMMR cells. Therefore, in the subset of tumors with both MSI/dMMR (MSH2-positive deficiency) and high TGFB1-associated stroma (high TSR), ICI efficacy will be attenuated. Introducing a TGF-β pathway inhibitor to disrupt the stromal barrier should restore T cell–tumor contact and synergize with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, improving therapy_response compared with ICI alone. This hypothesis relies on TSR as the single imaging feature and TGFB1 and MSH2 as the two anchor genes; MLH1 and other modifiers are acknowledged as secondary modifiers and not required for the core testable claim.

**Prediction 1:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
