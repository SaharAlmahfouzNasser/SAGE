## Working Hypothesis

Pretreatment TLS-like lymphoid aggregates organized at the tumor–stroma interface prime and sustain intravesical BCG-induced anti-tumor immunity in MIBC, whereas similar total TIL loads lacking organized TLS do not mount durable, effective responses. Specifically, a high TLS score that includes (i) density of TLS-like aggregates, (ii) evidence of organization such as germinal centers, B-cell and T-cell compartmentalization, and (iii) proximity of TLS to the invasive tumor margin, will be positively associated with objective response to BCG (complete or partial response) compared with TLS-poor tumors with comparable overall TIL density. Mechanistically, organized TLS provide local antigen presentation, a niche for T follicular helper and germinal center–like activity, sustained CD4+/CD8+ T-cell priming, and reduced local immunosuppression, thereby enhancing IFN-γ–driven, Th1/Tc1 responses following BCG exposure. In TLS-high tumors, we also expect upregulation of local cytokine and chemokine circuits (e.g., CXCL13, CXCL9/10, IFN-γ–responsive genes) and greater retention/activation of cytotoxic T cells at the tumor interface. Conversely, TLS-poor tumors would show weaker local priming and more dispersed, short-lived TIL activity, translating to poorer BCG responsiveness. This hypothesis yields testable predictions about the predictive value of pretreatment TLS architecture for therapy_response, independent of total TIL density, and suggests TLS maturation status as a potential biomarker for patient stratification.
