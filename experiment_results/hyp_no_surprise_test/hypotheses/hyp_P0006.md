# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlighted that immune-contexture metrics (Immunoscore/TLS) and genomic repair status (MSH6) both predict outcomes but do not always align — i.e., some immune-infiltrated tumors fail to respond to immune therapies. Why does immune architecture (TLS) sometimes fail to translate into productive anti-tumor immunity in MIBC? 2) Tier 1 basis: I built on CORE PATH 1 (immunotherapy biomarker / immune-contexture) and CORE PATH 2 (MSH6 prognostic role). These provide a mechanistic axis: TLS/Immunoscore capture local immune organization, while MSH6 status determines neoantigen load and MMR-driven immunogenicity; their interaction plausibly governs whether TLS are functionally productive. 3) Tier 2 support: Systemic/metabolic modifiers (HDL-C, ALB/GLB, GLU/urinary markers, PSA-like signals) can modulate immune competence or inflammation and thus may modify the TLS–MSH6 interaction; these are retained as secondary_modifiers to test effect modification. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (immunotherapy response proxy) because the core mechanistic question centers on when immune-organization (TLS) confers sensitivity to immune checkpoint blockade—this is a therapy-specific, actionable endpoint directly informed by Immunoscore and MSH6-driven neoantigen biology. 5) Deliberate exclusions: To keep the hypothesis sharp I excluded multi-gene lists and broader systemic markers from the core claim (they are in secondary_modifiers). I also excluded less-direct Tier3 signals (CSF-AD biomarkers, ABH) since they lack a direct mechanistic link to TLS–MMR interactions in MIBC?

## Working Hypothesis

Are TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in muscle-invasive bladder cancer functionally competent to sustain anti-tumor immunity only when tumors harbor MSH6 loss that elevates neoantigen burden, enabling productive TLS-mediated antigen presentation and T/B cell activation? Clinically, TLS-rich MSH6-deficient tumors will exhibit higher objective response rates, longer progression-free survival, and improved overall survival to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade than TLS-rich MSH6-proficient tumors, whereas TLS-poor tumors will show limited benefit irrespective of MSH6 status. The magnitude of this TLS–MSH6 interaction may be modulated by systemic/metabolic modifiers (e.g., HDL-C, albumin/globulin ratio) and urinary metabolic markers, which can act as effect modifiers or confounders and should be modeled prospectively. The hypothesis predicts a testable interaction term between TLS status (density and maturity) and MSH6 status on immunotherapy outcomes, with improved immunological readouts (e.g., local T/B cell activation, clonal TCR expansion, neoantigen-specific T cell responses) in TLS-rich/MSH6-deficient tumors validated by spatial-omics and functional assays.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
