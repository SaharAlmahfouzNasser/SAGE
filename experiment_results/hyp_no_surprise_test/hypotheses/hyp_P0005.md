# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some tumors with favorable immune architecture still fail to achieve better clinical outcomes?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), the prognostic impact of a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) on overall survival (OS) is contingent on tumor genotype, specifically MSH6 status and DPYD expression. TSR-high tumors that are MSH6-deficient (loss of function or MSI-H phenotype) and exhibit high DPYD expression will create a stromal milieu that promotes immunosuppression and metabolic adaptation, enabling immune escape and reduced sensitivity to perioperative therapy, resulting in poorer OS. Conversely, TSR-high tumors with intact MSH6 or low DPYD expression will show a less immunosuppressive stromal environment and greater chemosensitivity, yielding OS comparable to TSR-low tumors. This defines a three-way interaction (TSR × MSH6 × DPYD) that remains significant after adjusting for standard clinical covariates (age, sex, stage, nodal status, performance status) and treatment variables (neoadjuvant/adjuvant chemotherapy, surgical margins). The hypothesis predicts: (1) a significant three-way interaction term predicting OS across cohorts; (2) within TSR-high tumors, the worst outcome in the MSH6-deficient, DPYD-high subgroup; (3) correlative immuno- and metabolic signatures (e.g., reduced CD8+ T cells, increased Tregs/M2 macrophages, altered pyrimidine metabolism gene programs) accompanying the genotype–stroma–host context; and (4) mechanistic recapitulation in model systems where manipulating MSH6 and DPYD alters stromal-immune crosstalk and chemoresistance. These relationships should be detectable in retrospective cohorts and validate in prospective samples, with complementary in vitro and in vivo experiments guiding causal interpretation.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TSR as a stromal biomarker captures ECM remodeling and immune cell localization that influence prognosis but is not universally predictive, suggesting context dependence;

**Prediction 2:** MSH6 deficiency (a component of the MMR pathway) is linked to increased mutational burden and neoantigen load yet can be associated with immunosuppressive microenvironments and altered DNA repair–linked metabolism; DPYD encodes dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase, a key regulator of pyrimidine catabolism that can modulate nucleotide pools and influence sensitivity to pyrimidine-targeting therapies and metabolic adaptation;

**Prediction 3:** the host systemic state (nutritional/inflammatory status) and epigenetic/metabolic signaling in the tumor–stroma axis can further modulate outcomes, supporting a genotype × stroma × host context framework.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
