# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that immune-contexture metrics (Immunoscore/immunotherapy biomarkers) often predict outcome in MIBC, yet microenvironment phenotypes like SARIFA (adipocyte–CAF interactions) associate with metastasis and may modulate immune activity. The puzzle is: why do some TIL-rich tumors fail to translate immune infiltration into durable immunotherapy benefit? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the Immunotherapy biomarker and Immunoscore paths because they establish that T cell infiltration and spatial immune architecture are expected to predict IO benefit; I also used the SARIFA path because adipocyte–CAF programs and associated stromal remodeling (linked mechanistically to TGF-β) provide a plausible axis that could suppress or spatially exclude functional T cells. These three together create a mechanistic hypothesis that stromal/adipocyte-driven TGF-β signaling undermines the functional benefit of TILs. 3) Tier 2 support: MDK, PTGDS and EV-mRNA signals support noncanonical stromal/tumor signaling and systemic biomarker readouts; methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2) and metabolic markers indicate that epigenetic and systemic context may further stratify tumors where stromal programs dominate. These support testing combined tumor/stroma molecular readouts with imaging. 4) Why therapy_response endpoint: The Ontologist emphasis is on immunotherapy biomarkers and immunoscore applicability; the translational question of greatest clinical impact is predicting which patients will respond to checkpoint blockade (therapy response proxy). This endpoint directly tests the mechanism (immune infiltration + stromal suppression → IO resistance). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded using multiple immune markers (CD3/CD8 separately), full ECM/CAF gene lists, and broad metabolic panels from the core hypothesis to keep it focused (max 2 anchor genes). Those are placed in secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density is necessary but not sufficient for response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. A SARIFA-like adipocyte–CAF program drives elevated TGFB1 signaling and PD-L1 (CD274) expression, causing spatial exclusion and functional suppression of CD8+ T cells within the tumor microenvironment. Consequently, tumors with high TIL density but active TGFB1 signaling and high CD274 will exhibit poor IO response. The hypothesis predicts that a combined readout of TIL density and TGFB1/CD274 status will more accurately predict immunotherapy nonresponse than TIL alone. Moreover, in tumors with this stromal program, inhibition of TGF-β signaling will restore T cell penetration and cytotoxicity and enhance the efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. Secondary modifiers (MDK, PTGDS, SDC2, TFPI2) and EV-mRNA/metabolic methylation signatures will further discriminate IO responders from nonresponders within the high-TIL cohort and may guide stratified therapy decisions.
