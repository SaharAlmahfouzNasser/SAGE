# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, some MIBC tumors with high mutational signals (TMB/bTMB/HRD) respond to checkpoint blockade while others with comparable mutational metrics do not; why does genomic immunogenicity sometimes fail to translate into effective anti-tumor immunity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) the TMB → immunotherapy response path (establishes mutation-derived neoantigen load as a driver of response), (b) the HRD score path (links DNA-repair deficiency/TP53-driven genomic instability to neoantigen quality and load), and (c) the bTMB path (provides a blood-based surrogate for tissue mutational signals). These converge on a mechanistic question about whether DNA-repair–driven neoantigens enable organized local immune structures that permit response and whether bTMB captures that biology. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: TP53 and genomic instability provide a mechanistic source of HRD-associated neoantigens; PD-L1 and MSI are additional immune-context modulators that could influence whether an antigen-rich tumor elicits TLS formation and productive immunity; liquid biopsy feasibility supports using bTMB for non-invasive stratification. 4) Why I chose therapy_response as the endpoint: The core paths center on predicting and monitoring checkpoint inhibitor responsiveness (immunotherapy), so a therapy_response proxy is the most direct, clinically actionable endpoint for validating predictive performance and mechanism. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded exploratory/metabolic and poorly linked paths (GLU/urine biomarkers, pZVR, broad prognostic markers) because they lack clear mechanistic connection to immune priming/TLS formation in the TMB–HRD axis; I also limited anchors to HRD and bTMB to keep the hypothesis sharp and mechanistic (TMB, TP53, PD-L1 moved to secondary_modifiers)?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), TLS-like lymphoid aggregates enable effective antitumor immunity and predict favorable therapy_response to checkpoint inhibitors only when tumor neoantigen quality arises from homologous recombination deficiency (high HRD_score). Specifically, TLS-positive tumors with high HRD_score will show higher therapy_response rates than TLS-positive tumors with low HRD_score or TLS-negative tumors, due to HRD-driven clonal neoantigens that sustain intratumoral antigen presentation. Conversely, tumors with high bTMB but low HRD_score will have abundant circulating mutations but insufficient TLS-driven immune organization, resulting in lower therapy_response despite high bTMB. This framework predicts that TLS presence and HRD_score interact to modulate immunotherapy responsiveness, with PD-L1 and TP53-associated genomic instability acting as modulators of this axis; bTMB serves as a blood-based surrogate that may reflect the mutational burden but does not substitute for HRD-driven neoantigen quality in predicting TLS-mediated responses.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
