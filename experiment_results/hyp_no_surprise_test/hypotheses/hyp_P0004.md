## Clinical Question

## Working Hypothesis

The survival benefit associated with TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in breast cancer is contingent on tumor antigenicity and ER proteostasis. Specifically, TLS-rich tumors with MSH6 deficiency (which elevates neoantigen load) and low PDIA2 expression (reduced ER proteostasis burden, enabling efficient antigen processing/presentation) will exhibit enhanced neoantigen visibility, robust antigen presentation, and CD8+ T cell–mediated anti-tumor immunity, translating into improved overall survival. Conversely, TLS-rich tumors with intact MSH6 (lower neoantigen burden) or high PDIA2 expression (enhanced ER proteostasis that dampens antigen processing/presentation) will show diminished or no TLS-associated OS benefit. Therefore, there is a three-way interaction: TLS presence × MSH6 status × PDIA2 expression, predicting the largest OS advantage in TLS-positive/MSH6-deficient/PDIA2-low tumors and attenuated benefit in TLS-positive/MSH6-proficient/PDIA2-high tumors. Secondary modifiers (PTGDS, pZVR, HDL-C, glucose, microalbuminuria, SARIFA) may modulate the strength or direction of this interaction by shaping systemic inflammatory/metabolic milieu and the invasive-front microenvironment, thereby influencing TLS functionality and immune effectiveness.
