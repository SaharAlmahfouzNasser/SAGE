A strong, well-supported prognostic biomarker that fits your constraint (morphological and spatial features from H&E) is the tumor-stroma ratio (TSR).

What TSR is
- TSR quantifies the proportion of stroma (connective tissue) relative to tumor cells within the invasive tumor region on H&E-stained slides.
- It is a purely morphological/spatial measure derived from standard pathology images, requiring no molecular assays.

Why TSR is prognostic in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC)
- A higher stromal content (stroma-rich tumor) generally correlates with worse cancer-specific and overall survival in multiple solid tumors, including bladder cancer, reflecting a pro-tumor microenvironment.
- TSR captures tumor microenvironment architecture (not just tumor cells), adding prognostic information beyond traditional histologic grade or stage.

How to measure TSR from H&E images
1) Digitize slides or use high-quality scans of the cystectomy or TURBT specimens with evident invasive bladder cancer.
2) Delineate the invasive tumor region (and exclude non-invasive/overall non-tumor areas).
3) Within the invasive tumor area, segmentate or classify areas as tumor epithelium vs stroma. This can be done:
   - Manually by a pathologist (time-consuming but accurate for small studies).
   - Automated/digital pathology with color deconvolution and tissue segmentation (recommended for larger cohorts).
4) Compute TSR as:
   TSR = Stroma area / (Stroma area + Tumor area) within the invasive region.
5) Interpret and report:
   - Treat TSR as a continuous variable for modeling.
   - Or dichotomize into stroma-high vs stroma-low using a cutoff (cutoffs vary across studies; common practice is around 0.5 or determined empirically via survival analysis in your cohort).
6) Validate:
   - Correlate TSR with outcomes (overall survival, cancer-specific survival, progression-free survival) in your MIBC cohort.
   - Adjust for clinical covariates (stage, grade, Node status) if building a multivariate prognostic model.

Practical considerations
- Ensure consistent sampling of the invasive front and avoid necrotic or non-representative areas.
- Standardize imaging and color normalization to reduce measurement variability.
- Consider interobserver reliability if part of the workflow involves manual segmentation.
- TSR is compatible with retrospective image archives and can be integrated into image-based prognostic models or combined with other image-derived features (e.g., spatial organization of tumor cells) for improved risk stratification.
