# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do tumors with high stromal proportion (high TSR) consistently show poorer outcomes in MIBC — is the stroma simply a passive correlate or does it actively create a microenvironment that impairs anti‑tumor control? 2) Tier 1 path used: single provided path (TSR → stroma proportion → prognosis); I built directly on this path because it gives the strongest mechanistic backbone linking a morphologic spatial measure to outcome. 3) Tier 2 evidence: while no explicit Tier 2 gene/path data were provided, published mechanistic work (CAF activity, collagen/ECM remodeling, and immune exclusion) supports the claim that stroma can be biologically active and immune‑suppressive; these concepts motivate the mechanistic framing rather than additional gene anchors. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) was chosen because it is the most robust, clinically definitive endpoint available for prognostic biomarker studies and aligns with prior TSR literature in solid tumors. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded molecular/gene anchors because none were provided by the Ontologist (to avoid inventing genes); I also excluded multi‑feature models and therapy‑response endpoints to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on mechanism linking morphology to survival?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) measured at the invasive tumor front reflects a cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF)– and extracellular matrix (ECM)–dense microenvironment that actively promotes immune exclusion and tumor invasion, thereby accelerating progression. This stromal biology leads to poorer overall survival (OS) independent of standard clinicopathologic factors (stage, grade, age, and treatment). Furthermore, TSR identifies a biologically distinct, stroma-driven high-risk subgroup of MIBC patients who may benefit from stroma-targeted or combination therapies that aim to normalize the ECM and restore anti-tumor immunity. The hypothesis will be tested by: (i) demonstrating that higher TSR correlates with reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration and with elevated CAF/ECM markers at the invasive front; (ii) showing that TSR remains independently prognostic for OS after adjusting for confounders; and (iii) validating the finding in an independent cohort and exploring mediation by immune exclusion rather than direct tumor intrinsic factors.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
