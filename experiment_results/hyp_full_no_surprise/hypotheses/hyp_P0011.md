# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, the key puzzle is why some MIBC tumors that display genomic instability (high HRD signatures) nevertheless fail to exhibit the expected sensitivity to DNA-damaging therapies — i.e., what microenvironmental or systemic factors blunt HRD-driven chemosensitivity? 2) Tier 1 paths used and why: I built the hypothesis on the HRD/genomic-instability axis (HRD score) because it directly predicts DNA-damage therapy sensitivity, and on systemic lipid biology (HDL-C) because it offers a plausible, measurable metabolic modifier that can supply lipids/antioxidant capacity to tumors. SARIFA/TAF (tumor–adipocyte interactions) from Tier 1 informs the microenvironmental mechanism (adipocyte-derived lipid support) but was not included as an anchor to keep the hypothesis focused. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: MSH6 and TP53 (DNA-repair/instability modifiers) could modulate baseline HRD signals; immune-context and EV/radiomic signals (MDK, KRT17, radiomics/pro-PSA analogs) provide complementary readouts and potential confounders of therapy response. 4) Why I chose therapy_response as the single endpoint: HRD score has immediate mechanistic/clinical implications for platinum/PARP sensitivity, so therapy response is the most actionable endpoint for translational testing. 5) What I deliberately EXCLUDED and why: I excluded SARIFA as an explicit anchor (used only mechanistically) to respect the 1–2 anchor limit and keep the hypothesis sharp; I also deferred many Tier 2/3 exploratory markers (pro-PSA, EV metabolic renal markers, pZVR, PRAD cross-links) to secondary_modifiers or future studies because including them would make the statement unfocused and model-driven rather than mechanistic?

## Working Hypothesis

In MIBC, a high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR) creates an adipocyte-rich microenvironment in which HDL-C–mediated lipid transfer mitigates DNA repair vulnerabilities associated with a high HRD_score, thereby reducing sensitivity to platinum/PARP therapies. MSH6 and TP53 status modulate the degree of this lipid-mediated rescue, such that HRD_score-high tumors with high TSR and elevated HDL-C exhibit lower therapy_response than HRD_score-high/TSR-low tumors, with potential restoration of sensitivity if lipid supply or stromal–tumor metabolic crosstalk is disrupted.
