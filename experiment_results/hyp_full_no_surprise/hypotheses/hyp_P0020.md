# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Among bladder cancer patients, why do some tumors fail to respond to cisplatin-based chemotherapy despite apparent immune infiltration or early-stage biology — and can a tumor-intrinsic ER-stress signature (represented by PDIA2) explain immune dysfunction and chemo-resistance that also impacts patient functional outcomes? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) the cisplatin–urothelial carcinoma–bladder cancer chemotherapy axis to anchor the question in cisplatin therapy response, and (b) PDIA2 as a prognostic biomarker in NMIBC to posit a tumor-intrinsic mechanism (ER stress/protein-folding) that could modulate immune microenvironment and chemo efficacy. These paths link treatment, tumor biology, and prognosis. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: links between pelvic cancer therapies (neoadjuvant ADT) and erectile dysfunction, plus the contextual association of chemotherapy with bladder cancer, motivate studying how systemic/therapy-related factors and baseline functional status intersect with tumor biology; this supports testing whether PDIA2-associated ER stress impairs anti-tumor immunity (reflected by imaging TIL density) and thereby modifies chemo response. 4) Why choose therapy_response as the endpoint: the mechanistic question centers on cisplatin efficacy (a treatment-outcome problem) rather than long-term survival; therapy response is the most direct, actionable endpoint for assessing cisplatin effectiveness and for designing possible interventions (ER-stress modulators or immune priming). 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded non-mechanistic, patient-level confounders (age, comorbidity) and numerous ancillary molecular candidates from the core hypothesis to keep it sharply focused on one imaging feature (TIL density) and one molecular anchor (PDIA2). Prostate cancer/ED and ADT are retained as secondary modifiers to account for clinical context but not as core variables, because they complicate the mechanistic claim and would dilute testability?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC tumors, co-expression of high PDIA2 and low HLA-DQB1 defines an ER-stress–driven, immune-evasive state that suppresses immunogenic cell death and antigen presentation, thereby reducing sensitivity to cisplatin-based chemotherapy in tumors with low TIL density. Clinically, NMIBC tumors that are PDIA2-high and HLA-DQB1-low with low TIL density will exhibit poorer therapy_response to cisplatin, identifying a subset that may benefit from ER-stress–targeting or immune-priming strategies.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
