# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-rich breast tumors will exhibit improved therapy_response to adjuvant immunotherapy after neoadjuvant chemotherapy only when tumor-intrinsic programs are favorable. Specifically, high expression of MDK and/or KRT17 within TLS-rich tumors remodel TLS into dysfunctional, myeloid-dominated niches that suppress cytotoxic T cell activity, nullifying the expected benefit of TLS. In contrast, TLS-rich tumors with low MDK and/or low KRT17 will maintain supportive TLS architecture (mature germinal center–like structures, CD8+ T cells, and pro-immunogenic myeloid balance) and show enhanced therapy_response to adjuvant immunotherapy. Tumor-derived EVs carrying MDK-related cargo will contribute to TLS remodeling toward immunosuppression, whereas EVs from MDK/KRT17-low tumors will preserve TLS functionality. Systemic host state, captured by a composite index including LMR (lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio) and HRR (a defined host inflammatory/immune-resiliency ratio), will modulate TLS effectiveness: favorable host state amplifies TLS-associated benefit, while an unfavorable state dampens it. Metabolic/hormonal factors (HDL-C, IGF-1) will further influence TLS–tumor interactions by shaping myeloid and T cell function and should be considered as secondary modifiers. Collectively, the presence of TLS will predict therapy_response only within a defined molecular and systemic context; TLS+/MDK-low/KRT17-low patients will have the highest likelihood of benefiting from adjuvant immunotherapy, whereas TLS+/MDK-high and/or TLS+/KRT17-high cases will be resistant, unless mitigated by favorable host/systemic factors.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** is linked to basal-like features and altered tumor-immune interactions. Both can influence the TLS milieu and immune cell composition.

**Prediction 2:** further modulate immune cell activity and TLS remodeling. In breast cancer, leveraging TCGA-BRCA as a discovery dataset allows integration of molecular (MDK, KRT17, EV signatures), histologic TLS features, and treatment outcomes to define the context in which TLS predicts therapy_response.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
