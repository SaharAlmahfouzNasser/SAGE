# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a DNA-repair → immunogenicity axis (MSH6/MMR, HRD/TP53) that should sensitize tumors to PD‑1 blockade, yet many DDR-deficient bladder cancers fail to respond—why does organized local immune architecture (TLS/TILs) not always translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, MSH6 deficiency increases neoantigen burden and promotes the formation of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates, creating a spatially organized immune niche. However, high PD-L1 expression within TLS microenvironments leads to local T-cell dysfunction, diminishing the efficacy of anti-PD-1 therapy. Therefore, TLS presence predicts positive response to PD-1 blockade only when intra-TLS PD-L1 expression is low (or absent). Conversely, TLS-rich tumors with high intra-TLS PD-L1 exhibit blunted responses despite abundant neoantigens. The predictive biomarker is a spatially resolved TLS–PD-L1 axis, operationalized as TLS density plus intra-TLS PD-L1 level, which stratifies patients into likely responders versus non-responders to anti-PD-1 therapy. DDR modifiers such as MSI status and HRD score, as well as TP53 status and HRR-related features, may modulate neoantigen load and immune contexture but are secondary modifiers rather than core determinants. Testable predictions include: (1) among MSH6-deficient tumors, TLS-positive/low intra-TLS PD-L1 regions will show higher ORR and longer PFS with PD-1 blockade than TLS-positive/high intra-TLS PD-L1 regions or TLS-negative tumors; (2) the TLS–PD-L1 axis will outperform overall PD-L1 or total mutational burden alone as a predictor of response; (3) spatial mapping will reveal concurrent T cell activation signatures in TLS with low PD-L1, and exhaustion signatures in TLS with high PD-L1. Experimental validation will integrate spatial proteomics/transcriptomics, region-specific IHC for PD-L1 and TLS markers, and functional assays in organoid or murine models, complemented by retrospective clinical data analyses and prospective stratification pilots.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
