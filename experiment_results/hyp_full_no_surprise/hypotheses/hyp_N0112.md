# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder/urothelial carcinoma, PTEN loss drives hyperactivation of the PI3K-AKT pathway through mTORC2 (RICTOR)-dependent phosphorylation of AKT at Ser473. This AKT S473 hyperphosphorylation enhances mTORC1 signaling and robustly suppresses autophagy initiation and flux via impaired ULK1-Beclin1 axis, leading to reduced autophagic activity under cellular stress. The consequent autophagy suppression correlates with increased invasive capacity, EMT-like traits, metastasis, and poorer clinical prognosis (e.g., higher stage, lymphovascular invasion, shorter disease-free survival). This prognostic effect should be detectable as: (i) PTEN loss or mutation associated with elevated p-AKT Ser473 and reduced autophagy markers (LC3-II flux, Beclin1/ULK1 activity) in tumor samples; (ii) a negative association between autophagy flux and metastatic/recurrence outcomes; (iii) a positive interaction between PTEN loss and autophagy suppression in predicting adverse prognosis. Importantly, disrupting mTORC2-AKT signaling (e.g., RICTOR knockdown or selective attenuation of AKT Ser473 phosphorylation) or pharmacologically enhancing autophagy in bladder cancer models should restore autophagic flux and attenuate invasive/metastatic phenotypes, supporting a causal role for the PTEN/mTORC2/autophagy axis in prognosis. While this axis is framed around prognosis, these mechanistic insights may identify context-dependent therapeutic angles (e.g., combining mTORC2/AKT modulation with autophagy-directed strategies) for bladder cancer, with prognostic biomarkers serving as a primary outcome.
