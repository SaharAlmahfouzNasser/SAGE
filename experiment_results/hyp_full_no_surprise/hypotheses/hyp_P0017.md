# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that local immune architecture (Immunoscore, leukocyte fraction) and MMR status (MSH6) jointly influence NMIBC prognosis, but immune-rich tumors do not always exhibit favorable outcomes—why does organized local immunity (TLS) sometimes fail to prevent progression in NMIBC? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the Immunoscore/leukocyte-fraction axis (immune contexture) together with MSH6 (MMR/genomic instability) because these provide a direct mechanistic link between tumor immunogenicity, local immune organization, and clinical outcome. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: systemic/metabolic and noninvasive readouts (HDL-C, urine DNA methylation, stool SDC2/TFPI2) and AI-derived integrative signatures plausibly modulate or report on immune competence and tumor visibility, and thus can act as modifiers or measurement tools in follow-up studies. 4) Why PFI: For NMIBC the key clinical problem is recurrence/progression rather than immediate OS; progression-free interval is the most relevant, sensitive endpoint to capture the clinical consequence of failed local immune control. 5) What I deliberately excluded and why: I excluded exploratory/weakly connected paths (CSF AD biomarkers, EV-MDK comparisons, PNI as primary anchors) and did not include multiple immune-checkpoint or other gene-level modifiers in the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharply focused on how MSH6-related genomic context modulates TLS effectiveness via leukocyte functional state?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates are associated with longer progression-free interval (PFI) in NMIBC only within a functional immune context defined by intact MSH6 status and a sufficiently high intratumoral leukocyte fraction. Specifically, MSH6-proficient tumors with TLS + high leukocyte fraction will show the longest PFI, whereas MSH6-deficient tumors or tumors with low leukocyte fraction will not derive the same TLS-associated benefit. TLS presence in the setting of MSH6 deficiency may even coincide with shorter PFI due to elevated neoantigen burden driving T-cell exhaustion within TLS. Therefore, there is a significant three-way interaction (TLS status × MSH6 status × leukocyte fraction) that predicts PFI in NMIBC, reconciling variable TLS prognostic value observed across tumors and enabling a stratified prognostic model using only existing TLS imaging features and Tier 1 anchors.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** assemble a cohort with histology-scored TLS, MSH6 IHC/sequence data, and leukocyte fraction (via pathology estimates or transcriptomics).

**Prediction 2:** Define TLS status (present/absent) and categorize MSH6 as proficient/deficient.

**Prediction 3:** Model PFI with Cox regression including TLS, MSH6 status, leukocyte fraction (continuous or categorized), and all two-way and three-way interaction terms.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.
