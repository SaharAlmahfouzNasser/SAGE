# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

NMIBC tumors with apparent immune infiltration (TILs) or otherwise favorable classification still recur or progress; why does immune presence fail to translate into durable tumor control in a subset of cases? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built the core mechanistic hypothesis on MSH6 (MMR → neoantigen load → immune recruitment) and E-FABP (tumor lipid-handling/metabolic programs linked to metastatic/aggressive behavior). These two provide opposing influences on immune efficacy—MSH6 can increase immunogenicity and TIL recruitment, whereas E-FABP-driven metabolism can create a metabolically suppressive microenvironment that impairs T cell function. 3) Tier 2 support: urine-DNA methylation biomarkers offer a noninvasive readout of tumor epigenetic state that can contextualize immune/metabolic phenotypes; HDL-C/CDKN2A link systemic lipid state and cell-cycle control as potential modifiers of tumor aggressiveness; HRR (inflammation/nutrition index) can alter host immune competence; AI-based integrative methods can discover interactions among these layers. 4) Endpoint choice rationale: Progression-free interval (PFI) is the most clinically relevant outcome for NMIBC (recurrence/progression risk and timing drive management), and captures failures of local immune control. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded PSA, SARIFA, and cross-cancer grading analogies from the core hypothesis because they are cross-contextual or less specific to NMIBC biology; I also omitted TLS-like aggregates and TSR as the imaging feature because TIL density maps more directly onto the MSH6 → neoantigen axis and is measurable on H&E for NMIBC. I kept the hypothesis tightly focused (1 imaging feature + 2 anchors); other entities were placed as secondary modifiers for follow-up testing?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, the protective association between high Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocyte (TIL) density and longer progression-free interval is contingent on MSH6-driven neoantigen generation, but E-FABP–driven lipid metabolic reprogramming in tumor cells creates a metabolically suppressive microenvironment that impairs T cell effector function. Consequently, NMIBC tumors with high TIL density and elevated E-FABP expression will exhibit shorter PFI than tumors with low E-FABP, irrespective of MSH6 status, indicating that metabolic state can override neoantigen-driven immune benefit; urine-DNA methylation biomarkers and other modifiers (HDL-C, CDKN2A, HRR) can refine risk stratification as secondary modifiers.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
