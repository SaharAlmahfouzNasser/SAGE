# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Clinical trials combine PD-1 blockade (pembrolizumab) with chemotherapy expecting that chemo-induced tumor cell death will increase antigen release and make TLS-rich tumors highly responsive to anti–PD-1, yet some immune-infiltrated tumors fail to benefit. 2) Tier 1 path built on: the single provided path links chemotherapy/pembrolizumab → cell death → KRAS signaling → treatment outcomes, suggesting KRAS activity can modulate cell-death pathways and the immunogenic consequences of cytotoxic therapy. ## Working Hypothesis

In TLS-rich breast tumors, activating KRAS mutations attenuate the beneficial association between TLS presence and response to combined PD-1 blockade plus chemotherapy. Specifically, KRAS-driven signaling in TLS-rich tumors reduces immunogenic cell death and downstream antigen presentation within TLS structures, leading to weaker T cell priming and lower objective response rates to pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy compared with TLS-rich KRAS-wildtype tumors. Consequently, the predictive value of TLS for therapy response is diminished in the context of KRAS mutations, whereas TLS-rich KRAS-wildtype tumors retain higher response rates. This hypothesis can be evaluated by (i) comparing response rates and duration of response across four groups defined by TLS status (TLS-rich vs TLS-poor) and KRAS mutation status (mutant vs wildtype) in breast cancer cohorts treated with PD-1 blockade plus chemotherapy, with adjustment for confounders; and (ii) validating mechanistically that KRAS mutation dampens markers of immunogenic cell death and T cell activation within TLS-like microenvironments.
