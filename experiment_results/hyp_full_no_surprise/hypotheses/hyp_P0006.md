# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Tier 1 synthesis highlights a paradox: MSH6/MMR-driven MSI should increase neoantigen load and immunogenicity, yet MSI/MSH6 status alone does not universally predict favorable biomarker-guided outcomes or immunotherapy benefit in MIBC. Why does high mutational neoantigen potential sometimes fail to translate into effective anti-tumor immunity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) the MSH6-as-prognostic-factor path because MSH6 loss is the canonical MMR perturbation linking genomic instability to neoantigen generation, and (b) the MSI→AI-biomarkers and (c) immunotherapy-biomarker paths because they frame MSI as an immunogenic signal whose clinical utility depends on immune-context features that imaging/AI can capture. These together motivate testing an immune-organization requirement for MSI-driven benefit. 3) Tier 2 support: PTGDS, pZVR, PSA and HDL-C provide orthogonal, cross-cancer biomarker signals that could modulate tumor inflammation, stromal composition or systemic milieu and thus act as secondary modifiers of the MSI–immune axis; they justify exploring confounders and effect modifiers beyond MMR. 4) Endpoint choice: I selected therapy_response (specifically immunotherapy/ICI response proxy) because the mechanistic question centers on why neoantigen-generating MMR defects do not always yield clinical benefit from immune-based therapies—therapy_response is the most biologically direct and actionable endpoint for this question. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded Tier 3 concepts (Immunoscore, HDL-C as primary mechanistic anchors) from the core claim to keep the hypothesis sharply focused on the interaction between tumor genomic immunogenicity (MSH6/MSI) and local immune organization (TLS); Tier 2 candidates were retained as secondary_modifiers for downstream analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

MSH6-deficient/MSI-high muscle-invasive bladder tumors realize their neoantigen-driven immunogenic potential to elicit a clinically meaningful immunotherapy response only when the tumor microenvironment contains TLS-like lymphoid aggregates. Specifically, MSI-H/MSH6 loss will be associated with higher therapy_response to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors primarily in TLS-rich tumors, whereas MSI-H/MSH6 loss in TLS-poor tumors will show attenuated or no improved response despite elevated neoantigen load. Tier2 modifiers (PTGDS, pZVR, PSA, HDL-C) modulate this axis by shaping inflammatory signaling, stromal remodeling, or systemic metabolic milieu, thereby influencing TLS formation/maintenance and local antigen presentation. Consequently, the predictive value of MSI/MSH6 for immunotherapy response will be strongest in TLS-high tumors and will be further stratified by Tier2 biomarker levels, with a measurable interaction between MSI/MSH6 status, TLS presence, and Tier2 modifiers on therapy_response.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
