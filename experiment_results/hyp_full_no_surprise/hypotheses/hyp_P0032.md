## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density does not uniformly translate into improved tumor control when tumors display concurrent homologous recombination repair deficiency (HRD) and loss of MSH6. Specifically, TIL-high tumors that are HRD-high and MSH6-deficient will exhibit qualitative changes in neoantigen landscape and chromosomal instability that promote immune evasion or ineffective cytotoxic activity, resulting in worse overall survival (OS) compared with TIL-high tumors lacking HRD/MSH6 defects. A panel of 4–6 secondary modifiers (SDC2, TFPI2, TP53, ALB/GLB, HRR, GREM1) will further stratify TIL-high bladder cancers into two prognostic groups: (i) TIL-high with HRD/MSH6 deficiency and adverse modifier signals (e.g., TP53 alterations, dysregulated ALB/GLB balance, pro-tumorigenic GREM1 expression) predicting poorer outcomes, and (ii) TIL-high without HRD/MSH6 defects or with favorable modifier profiles predicting relatively better outcomes. This framework implies that prognosis and therapeutic planning should integrate TIL metrics with tumor intrinsic instability (HRD score, MSH6 status) and selected host/environmental modifiers, guiding strategies that combine immune modulation with targeted interventions addressing genomic instability or host factors. Testable predictions include: (a) in large bladder cancer cohorts, the hazard of death for the TIL-high/HRD-high/MSH6-deficient subgroup will exceed that of TIL-high/HRD-low and/or MSH6-intact subgroups after adjusting for confounders; (b) within the HRD-high/MSH6-deficient cohort, adverse secondary modifiers (e.g., TP53 mutations, high GREM1 expression) will associate with progressively worse outcomes; (c) functional studies will show reduced T cell–mediated cytotoxicity in HRD/MSH6-deficient contexts unless therapies targeting HRD-associated vulnerabilities or enhancing antigen presentation are used.
