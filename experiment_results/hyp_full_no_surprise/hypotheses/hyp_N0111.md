## Working Hypothesis

In tumors with high tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and intact TP53, mTORC1/2 inhibition (everolimus/rapamycin) induces autophagy that, in the setting of functional TP53, promotes TP53-dependent tumor cell death. In the same TIL-rich context, high LAG3 expression marks exhausted T cells that dampen cytotoxic activity and blunt overall anti-tumor efficacy of mTOR inhibitors. Therefore: (a) TP53 WT, TIL-high, LAG3-low tumors will show superior objective response to mTOR inhibition compared with TP53 WT, LAG3-high or TP53-mutant tumors; (b) in tumors where autophagy is pharmacologically or genetically blocked, the cytotoxic effect of mTOR inhibition will be reduced, supporting autophagy as a required mediator; (c) LAG-3 blockade will reinvigorate TILs and synergize with mTOR inhibition to enhance tumor control, especially in LAG3-high tumors; (d) combination therapy will yield measurable increases in autophagy markers (e.g., LC3-II flux, p62 degradation), TP53 pathway activation (p53 stabilization, p21), and effector T-cell activity (IFN-γ, granzyme B) in the tumor microenvironment; (e) a composite biomarker signature integrating TIL density, TP53 status, and LAG3 expression will predict response to mTOR inhibitors and to the proposed combination therapy, enabling patient stratification. To test these predictions, a stepwise experimental plan is proposed: (1) in vitro mechanistic studies using TP53 WT and TP53-mutant tumor cell lines co-cultured with activated T cells engineered to express varying LAG3 levels, assessing autophagy flux, TP53 pathway activation, cell viability, and cytotoxicity with and without LAG-3 blockade; (2) patient-derived organoid models with autologous TILs to evaluate response to rapalogs ± LAG-3 inhibition, with readouts including organoid viability, autophagy markers, and TIL activation; (3) in vivo murine models (syngeneic and TP53-genetically manipulated) to test monotherapy and combination therapy, with endpoints including tumor growth, survival, flow cytometry/immunohistochemistry of TILs, autophagy markers in tumor cells, and expression of exhaustion markers; (4) exploratory analysis of retrospective datasets to correlate TIL density, TP53 status, and LAG3 with rapalog sensitivity; (5) development of a translational biomarker assay combining TIL density, TP53 mutational status, and LAG3 expression to guide patient selection for clinical testing of mTOR inhibitors and LAG-3 blockade.
