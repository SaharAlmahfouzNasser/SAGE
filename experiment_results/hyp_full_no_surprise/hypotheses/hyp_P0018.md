# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist frames a key paradox — some NMIBC tumors show organized, high immune infiltration (Immunoscore/TLS-like features) and detectable tumor-derived urine methylation signals yet still recur or fail to benefit from immune therapies. Why does spatial immune organization not always translate to effective anti-tumor immunity? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) the urine-DNA methylation biomarker path as the noninvasive tumor-detection anchor and (b) the immunotherapy/Immunoscore paths that emphasize spatial T-cell organization and immune-checkpoint biology. These together define a situation where tumor presence is confirmed noninvasively while immune architecture suggests immune engagement — a precise scenario to ask why response fails. 3) Tier 2 support: Metabolic-immune links (HDL-C) provide a plausible systemic/modulatory mechanism that can impair T-cell function; liquid biopsy (CTCs/EV-RNA), conventional markers (KRT17, MDK) and leukocyte fraction offer complementary measures of tumor burden, aggressiveness, and systemic immune competence that could modify the core interaction. I place these as secondary_modifiers to test mediation/moderation effects. 4) Why this endpoint: I selected therapy_response (immunotherapy response proxy) because the core mechanistic tension concerns why organized immune infiltrates do not yield therapeutic benefit — a causal, clinically actionable question for stratifying immunotherapy in NMIBC. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded exploratory Tier 3 items (CSF/AD biomarkers, stool methylation SDC2/TFPI2) from the main hypothesis because they are cross-disease extrapolations with weak mechanistic linkage to NMIBC. I also excluded listing the urine methylation panel directly as an anchor gene to keep the hypothesis focused on immune-mechanism (imaging + immune genes); the urine panel is retained as a secondary modifier to represent tumor detection/burden?

## Working Hypothesis

In non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), the presence of TLS-like organized immune infiltrates indicates immune recruitment but does not guarantee effective anti-tumor activity if tumor-intrinsic PD-L1 (CD274) expression suppresses CD8+ T cell effector function. Specifically, NMIBC tumors with TLS-like architecture and high CD274 expression will exhibit diminished CD8A+ cytotoxic function (low granzyme B, perforin, IFN-γ; reduced proliferative/memory markers; impaired TCR clonal expansion) compared with TLS-high tumors with low/moderate CD274. This functional suppression will correlate with poorer therapy response to immune-modulating strategies (e.g., checkpoint blockade or intravesical immunotherapy proxies) despite positive urine DNA methylation signals indicating tumor presence. Furthermore, systemic metabolic context, particularly elevated HDL-C, will moderate T cell metabolism and function, exacerbating or unveiling resistance in TLS-high/CD274-high tumors. Secondary modifiers (urine DNA methylation panel as tumor-detection/burden readout; CTCs/EV-RNA; leukocyte fraction; tumor markers KRT17, MDK; and DPYD status) will influence the magnitude of the PD-L1–mediated decoupling by altering tumor aggressiveness, systemic immune competence, or treatment-related toxicity risk, thereby acting as mediation or moderation factors in the primary relationship. Collectively, the strongest predictor of immunotherapy-like response in NMIBC will be the interaction between TLS presence and tumor CD274 expression driving CD8+ effector impairment, with HDL-C and secondary modifiers shaping the magnitude of this effect.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
