# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: The clinical efficacy of intravesical BCG in high-grade non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) depends on the in situ formation of TLS-like lymphoid aggregates within the bladder mucosa after TURBT. TLS formation is more likely when residual tumor burden is low, enabling sufficient antigen deposition and local cytokine/chemokine signaling to recruit and organize B and T cells, dendritic cells, and follicular helper cells into ectopic lymphoid structures. These TLS-like aggregates support effective antigen presentation and targeted cytotoxic responses, yielding durable local control and prolongation of progression-free interval (PFI). Conversely, in patients who do not form TLS-like structures post-BCG, even with BCG exposure, local immune orchestration remains suboptimal, leading to higher rates of recurrence and earlier progression to muscle-invasive disease. Clinically, TLS status after TURBT+BCG will stratify patients into responders vs. non-responders, guiding escalation strategies (e.g., early re-resection, intensified or combination immunotherapies, or systemic therapy) to improve PFI. Secondary modifiers (e.g., cisplatin-based regimens or other intravesical agents) may modulate TLS formation and should be explored as adjuncts to BCG in TLS-poor tumors.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** BCG exerts local immune activation in NMIBC, but heterogeneity in response suggests a need for organized adaptive responses at the mucosal site.

**Prediction 2:** TLSs are organized microenvironments that concentrate antigen presentation and effector lymphocytes, and their presence correlates with robust anti-tumor immunity across cancers and with favorable responses to immunotherapies.

**Prediction 3:** In NMIBC, residual tumor burden post-TURBT influences antigen load and the inflammatory milieu; lower residual disease may provide a more conducive setting for TLS development and effective immune priming.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
