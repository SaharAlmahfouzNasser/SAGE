# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights a tension between robust, noninvasive tumor signals (urine DNA methylation; CTC-derived ADNP mRNA) and variable clinical behavior in NMIBC — specifically, why some tumors with apparently favorable local immune features still progress. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on (a) liquid-biopsy/CTC ADNP (Tier1) because it provides a circulating readout of dissemination potential, and (b) DPYD (Tier1) because it implicates tumor/host pyrimidine metabolism as a plausible mechanism for metabolic immune suppression. These two directly address dissemination and local immune-modulatory metabolism. 3) Tier 2 evidence that supports the claim: HDL-C and HRR suggest systemic metabolic/host-inflammation states can modulate immune surveillance; AI-based biomarker frameworks support integrating histologic immune features and molecular assays to resolve discordant cases. These provide plausible modifiers and a path to measurable integration. 4) Why PFI as the endpoint: Progression-free interval best captures the clinically relevant failure mode in NMIBC (progression to higher stage/recurrence) that both local immune control and early dissemination would influence, and is a measurable endpoint for surveillance-driven biomarkers. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded the urine-DNA methylation signature from the two anchor genes to keep the mechanistic hypothesis focused (it remains a key secondary modifier for diagnostics). I also excluded broader exploratory metabolic panels (ALP, TG, IGF-1) and cross-cancer immunoscore constructs from the core claim to avoid diluting the mechanistic question; these are retained as secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

In NMIBC, TLS-like lymphoid aggregates do not uniformly prevent progression because a subset of tumors exhibit (i) high tumor DPYD expression driving pyrimidine catabolism that metabolically suppresses local anti-tumor immunity, (ii) detectable ADNP+ circulating tumor cells indicating early dissemination potential, and (iii) a adverse systemic metabolic/inflammatory state that further dampens immune surveillance. Therefore, TLS-high tumors with elevated DPYD expression and detectable ADNP in blood will have shorter progression-free intervals (PFI) compared with TLS-high tumors with low DPYD expression and no detectable ADNP+ CTCs. Modulation by systemic factors (HDL-C, HRR) and AI-integrated biomarker frameworks will influence the strength of this association, such that high HDL-C and favorable HRR attenuate risk in TLS-high/DPYD-high/ADNP+ contexts, while unfavorable host states exacerbate risk. This yields testable predictions: (1) DPYD expression in tumor tissue positively correlates with progression risk among TLS-high NMIBC; (2) ADNP+ CTCs independently predict shorter PFI and amplify risk in TLS-high/DPYD-high tumors; (3) pyrimidine metabolite excess (uracil/ dihydrouracil/ dihydrothymine) in urine/plasma associates with shorter PFI, particularly in TLS-high tumors with DPYD overexpression and ADNP+ CTCs; (4) a composite biomarker score integrating TLS metrics, DPYD status, ADNP CTCs, and pyrimidine metabolites provides superior PFI stratification compared with any single marker.

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
