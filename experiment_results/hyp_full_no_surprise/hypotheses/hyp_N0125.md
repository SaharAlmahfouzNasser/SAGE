# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some tumors with evidence of epithelial–mesenchymal transition (transitional states, E-cadherin loss) progress to overt pathological invasiveness while others do not, and could microbial triggers (Klebsiella) and TP53 mutation gate this progression via stromal remodeling? 2) Tier 1 paths used: the core mechanistic spine links EMT → invasive cancer and highlights TP53 mutation and E-cadherin (CDH1) as central molecular events that modulate invasiveness; I built the hypothesis on these because they provide a clear, testable molecular mechanism for invasion. 3) Tier 2/supporting evidence: imaging–invasion links (ground glass nodules predict pathological invasiveness), prognostic associations of E-cadherin, and the concept of transitional epithelial–mesenchymal states reinforce a model where cell-intrinsic (TP53, CDH1) and microbe-driven EMT alter tumor–stroma relationships. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) was chosen because it is the most robust, clinically meaningful endpoint available in TCGA-BRCA and will capture the net effect of molecular + microenvironmental drivers of invasion. 5) Exclusions/deliberate simplifications: I excluded explicit inclusion of imaging immune features (TIL/TLS) and dietary/bladder-specific signals (sugary beverages, bladder cancer) from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic question focused on EMT–mutation–stroma interactions in tumor invasion; microbial exposure (Klebsiella), CIS, ground-glass imaging correlates and EMT as a process are relegated to secondary_modifiers for follow-up testing?

## Working Hypothesis

Does Klebsiella-driven EMT interact with loss of CDH1 and TP53 mutation to promote desmoplastic remodeling and increase stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR), thereby enhancing invasion in breast cancer? If this interaction exists, tumors with high TSR and concurrent CDH1 loss and TP53 mutation in TCGA-BRCA will have worse overall survival, supporting a microbe–EMT–mutation axis driving aggressive, stroma-rich invasion.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
