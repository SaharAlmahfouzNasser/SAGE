# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In tumors with high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR), TP53 deficiency creates reliance on PROS1-mediated signaling to sustain proliferative clones via TAM receptor pathways. PROS1, produced by tumor and/or stromal cells, engages TAM receptors (TYRO3, AXL, Mer) on cancer and/or stromal cells, amplifying downstream proliferative signaling (e.g., AKT/ERK) and supporting clonal persistence after local therapy. This axis is strengthened in TSR-high environments due to enhanced stromal support and paracrine PROS1 availability. Consequently, tumors that are TSR-high, TP53-mutant, and PROS1-high will exhibit accelerated clonal expansion, higher local recurrence after treatment, and shorter progression-free interval (PFI). MKI67 (Ki-67) expression, while correlated with proliferation, acts as a secondary modifier that modulates magnitude rather than being essential to the core axis. Positive surgical margins and the index lesion contribute to microenvironmental context, increasing the likelihood that PROS1-mediated signaling promotes early progression but are not required for the axis to operate.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** TSR-high, TP53-mutant tumors will show higher PROS1 signaling activity and TAM receptor engagement than TSR-low counterparts;

**Prediction 2:** In vitro, PROS1 knockdown or TAM axis blockade will attenuate proliferation of TP53-deficient cells in stromal-rich settings;

**Prediction 3:** In vivo, disrupting PROS1-TAM signaling will reduce post-therapy clonal regrowth in TSR-high, TP53-mutant models;


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.


# 5. Strengths, Caveats & Alternative Directions

## Strengths

- Interaction-first biomarker strategy — asks WHY the imaging feature sometimes fails, not just whether it predicts outcome.

## Caveats & Risks

- Hypothesis is computationally derived and requires experimental validation.

## Alternative Directions

- MKI67: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
- Positive surgical margins: Could serve as an alternative mechanistic axis if the primary hypothesis is not supported.
