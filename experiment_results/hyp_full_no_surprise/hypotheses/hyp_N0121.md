# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that mismatch-repair features (MSH6/MSI) and circulating-tumor signals (CTCs) each carry prognostic/therapeutic implications, yet TLS-like immune structures in tumors do not uniformly predict benefit; why does local immune organization (TLS) fail to translate into effective immunotherapy response in some patients? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the MSH6 (MMR/MSI) prognostic path because MSH6 loss mechanistically increases neoantigen load and should potentiate immune-mediated tumor control, and on the CTC/ liquid-biopsy path because high CTC burden reflects active dissemination and systemic immune evasion that can negate local anti-tumor immunity. These two anchors (MSH6, CTC burden) directly frame a local-versus-systemic mechanistic tension that can explain variable TLS efficacy. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: GREM1, MDK and EV-mRNA biomarkers provide orthogonal readouts of metastatic propensity and tumor-secreted immunomodulators that could mediate the systemic suppression implied by high CTCs; AI/MSI methods and EV biomarkers offer practical assays to stratify these axes. 4) Why I chose the endpoint 'therapy_response': The central question pertains to why immune organization (TLS) does or does not translate into clinical benefit from immune-directed therapy, so a therapy-response proxy (immunotherapy response) is the most biologically relevant and actionable endpoint. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded using MSI as a separate anchor to avoid redundancy with MSH6 (MSH6 status operationalizes MSI in this context) and excluded introducing additional Tier 1/Tier 2 genes within the hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp; remaining candidates (GREM1, MDK, EV-mRNA, PSA) are listed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up analyses?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates in bladder tumors contribute to immunotherapy responsiveness only within a permissive systemic context. Specifically, TLS presence predicts higher therapy_response to immune checkpoint blockade when (a) tumor immunogenicity is high due to MSH6-associated neoantigen load (MMR-deficient context) or (b) the circulating tumor cell (CTC) burden and tumor-derived systemic immunomodulators are low. Conversely, TLS-rich tumors with high CTC burden and/or elevated circulating immunomodulators (GREM1, MDK, EV-associated mRNA) exhibit diminished TLS functionality and reduced therapy_response despite TLS presence. Therefore, combining TLS assessment with MSH6/MSI surrogate status, baseline CTC burden, and plasma levels of GREM1/MDK and EV-mRNA signatures will yield a superior, testable predictor of immunotherapy benefit than TLS status alone. The hypothesis implies testable sub-questions: (1) in TLS-rich tumors, does MSH6 loss associate with higher ORR to ICIs compared with MSH6-intact TLS-rich tumors? (2) does high CTC burden or elevated GREM1/MDK/EV-mRNA negate TLS-associated benefit? (3) can a composite score integrating TLS density/organization, MSH6 status, CTC burden, and EV/mRNA biomarkers outperform single variables in predicting therapy_response? Endpoints should include objective response (RECIST/iRECIST), duration of response, PFS, and OS where feasible.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Retrospective analyses in immunotherapy-treated bladder cancer cohorts to correlate TLS score with therapy_response stratified by MSH6 status and CTC/EV/mRNA biomarkers.

**Prediction 2:** Prospective cohort with baseline tissue and liquid biopsies to validate a composite predictor.

**Prediction 3:** Mechanistic studies using bladder cancer organoid/co-culture systems and, where possible, humanized mouse models to test how CTC-derived factors and EVs influence TLS formation/function and T cell–mediated cytotoxicity in the presence of ICIs.
