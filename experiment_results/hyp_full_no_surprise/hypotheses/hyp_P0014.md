# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that urine DNA methylation (tumor epigenetic programs) and MSH6-linked mismatch repair status are central axes in NMIBC, yet patients with apparent favorable local immune organization still recur or progress. The puzzle is: under what molecular conditions does local immune organization (TLS) fail to protect against progression?  
2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on CORE PATH 1 (urine DNA methylation biomarkers) because it provides a non-invasive readout of tumor epigenetic states that can mediate immune-evasive programs; and CORE PATH 2 (MSH6 prognostic factor) because MMR deficiency/MSH6 alters neoantigen load and immune contexture. I selected TLS-like lymphoid aggregates as the imaging feature because TLS formation is the most mechanistically linked histologic correlate of local antitumor immunity and intersects with both epigenetic and MMR biology.  
3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: systemic/metabolic context (HDL-C) and MSI/immunotherapy cross-cancer lessons suggest that host metabolism and genomic instability modulate immune benefit; radiomics/pro-PSA and liquid-biopsy ADNP provide orthogonal assays to validate discordant cases where TLS presence does not equal durable control. These support testing interactions between TLS, methylation, and MSH6.  
4) Why PFI endpoint: Progression-free interval is the most disease-relevant clinical endpoint for NMIBC (recurrence/progression risk drives management), is well-captured in TCGA/clinical cohorts, and directly reflects the clinical problem identified (patients with apparent local control still progress).  
5) What I excluded and why: I deliberately excluded broader AI-based composite biomarkers from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic question sharp; HDL-C, pro-PSA, ADNP, SDC2/TFPI2 and MSI are placed as secondary_modifiers for follow-up because they are supportive but would dilute the focused test of whether epigenetic immune evasion and MSH6 status explain TLS failure?

## Working Hypothesis

Among NMIBC tumors with TLS-like lymphoid aggregates, concurrent epigenetic silencing of antigen presentation and immune pathways detected by urine DNA methylation biomarkers and MSH6-associated mismatch repair defects negate TLS-associated antitumor immunity, leading to shorter progression-free intervals. Consequently TLS-high tumors that are urine methylation–high and MSH6-altered will have worse PFI and may benefit from epigenetic priming combined with immunotherapy rather than surveillance alone.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
