# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that immune-context biomarkers (Immunoscore/TLS), DNA repair status (MSH6/MMR), and systemic/metabolic signals (HDL-C) jointly influence prognosis and immunotherapy responsiveness in MIBC; the key puzzle is why histologic evidence of organized immune infiltration (TLS/Immunoscore) does not uniformly predict benefit from PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) Immunotherapy biomarker path (supports testing immune-context features as determinants of response), and (b) MSH6 prognostic path (links MMR/neoantigen generation to immune visibility). I built on these because they provide a direct mechanistic axis—antigen generation (MSH6/MMR) → local immune organization (TLS) → checkpoint-mediated modulation (CD274). 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: MSI/TMB relate to neoantigen load and are proposed modifiers of TLS efficacy; Immunoscore and TIL metrics operationalize immune organization; HDL-C and radiomic/pro-PSA signals provide orthogonal metabolic/imaging context that may modulate immune infiltration or be useful as noninvasive covariates in AI models. 4) Endpoint choice: therapy_response (PD-1/PD-L1 blockade response proxy) was chosen because the core axis concerns immunotherapy biomarkers and the clinical question is which immune/tumor features enable conversion of local immune organization into treatment sensitivity. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded broader AI-derived signature technology and numerous tertiary exploratory cross-cancer markers (e.g., PSA, PTGDS, stool methylation markers) from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic question sharp and testable; these remain for secondary analyses or model-building but are not in the primary mechanistic statement?

## Working Hypothesis

TLS-like lymphoid aggregates promote efficient local antigen presentation and priming of tumor-specific CD8+ T cells, but this process yields potent anti-tumor activity primarily when tumor neoantigen burden is high due to MSH6 deficiency (MMR loss). Consequently, TLS presence will predict favorable response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade predominantly in MSH6-deficient, high-neoantigen tumors, whereas in MSH6-intact tumors or contexts of CD274-driven immune exclusion, TLS may not predict response, indicating that combined assessment of TLS, MSH6 status, and CD274 expression improves patient stratification for therapy.
