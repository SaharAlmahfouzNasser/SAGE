# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do some tumors with high histologic immune infiltration fail to derive benefit from immunotherapy or show discordant biomarker signals? The Ontologist's tiers highlight DDR defects (HRD score) and MSH6/MSI as core modifiers of tumor immunogenicity, with liquid biopsy signals as complementary readouts. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on the HRD score path (CORE PATH 1) and the MSH6/MSI prognostic path (CORE PATH 2) because they provide mechanistic, and clinically actionable, axes linking DNA repair defects to neoantigen burden and immune engagement. I kept CTCs/EVs as a secondary modality to preserve hypothesis focus. 3) Tier 2 support: TP53-driven genomic instability and the EV-derived MDK/KRT17 signatures support mechanisms by which DDR status shapes tumor aggressiveness, antigen heterogeneity, and non-invasive detectability; AI-based biomarkers provide a translational route to integrate these signals. 4) Endpoint rationale: I selected a therapy_response proxy (immunotherapy response) because MSH6/MSI and DDR features have direct mechanistic and clinical implications for checkpoint inhibitor sensitivity, making therapy response the most problem-relevant endpoint. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded CTCs/EV markers from the core two-anchor hypothesis to avoid exceeding the 2-anchor limit and to keep the mechanistic claim crisp; exploratory Tier 3 signals (PSA, pZVR, GLU/creatinine, PNI) were also excluded because they are indirect or cross-disease and would dilute the mechanistic focus?

## Working Hypothesis

In tumors with high baseline TIL density, functional anti-tumor immunity during immune checkpoint blockade is contingent upon preserved antigen presentation and DDR status. Specifically, tumors harboring MSH6 deficiency together with a high HRD_score exhibit elevated neoantigen heterogeneity but concurrently upregulated immunosuppressive and antigen-presentation–defective programs. This leads to TILs that are abundant but functionally exhausted or ineffectual, resulting in poor immunotherapy response despite high TILs. Conversely, when antigen presentation capacity is intact (no B2M/HLA loss) and DDR-driven neoantigen load is high, high TIL density will more accurately predict favorable response. Therefore, a combined biomarker framework that integrates (1) TIL density (imaging/IF), (2) MSH6 status and HRD_score, and (3) antigen presentation integrity (B2M/HLA LOH, MHC I expression) will superiorly stratify patients for immunotherapy outcomes. Secondary liquid-biopsy signals (EV-derived MDK and KRT17) and AI-derived biomarker readouts will enhance non-invasive stratification, particularly for identifying cases with functional antigen presentation deficits not captured by tissue biopsy alone. Clinically, patients with high TILs but impaired antigen presentation may require DDR-targeted plus immune-modulatory combination strategies to convert: (a) high neoantigen burden with (b) preserved antigen presentation into meaningful checkpoint inhibitor responses.

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)

- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
