# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

What molecular mechanisms determine whether immune structures translate into clinical benefit?

## Working Hypothesis

In breast cancer (and applicable solid tumors), TLS-like lymphoid aggregates establish a local adaptive immune niche that can convert HRD-associated neoantigen burden into effective anti-tumor activity, but only when antigen presentation remains coherent. Specifically: (A) TLS-high tumors with high HRD_score and intact MSH6 will exhibit heightened clonal neoantigen presentation, robust TLS-driven cytotoxic and helper T cell responses, and superior sensitivity to DNA-damaging therapies (platinum agents) and PARP inhibitors, reflected in higher objective response rates and longer progression-free survival. (B) In TLS-high tumors with high HRD_score but MSH6 disruption, impaired antigen presentation or increased neoantigen heterogeneity will blunt TLS-mediated recognition, yielding attenuated therapy responses despite prominent TLS and HRD features. (C) TLS-low tumors will show comparatively poor responses regardless of HRD or MSH6 status due to lacking organized immune architecture. (D) Secondary modifiers (TP53-driven genomic instability, CTC-derived MDK/KRT17 signals, perineural invasion, and AI-derived host/measurement modifiers) further modulate the TLS–HRD–MSH6 axis by impacting immune infiltration, antigen presentation, tumor cell shedding, and assay interpretation. Testable predictions: (1) TLS_high/HRD_high/MSH6_wild-type tumors have the highest objective response rates and longest PFS on PARP inhibitors or platinum therapy; (2) TLS_high/HRD_high/MSH6_mutant tumors show intermediate responses; (3) TLS_low tumors show consistently lower responses irrespective of HRD/MSH6; (4) mechanistic readouts will show coordinated activation of cGAS-STING/type I IFN, upregulated antigen presentation pathways (HLA-I, B2M, TAP1/2) and TLS-associated chemokines (e.g., CXCL13) in TLS_high/MSH6_wild-type contexts; and a discordance of these signals in TLS_high/MSH6_mutant contexts. Feasibility: testable via integrated clinical multi-omics and spatial analyses, complemented by preclinical models manipulating MSH6 status in HRD-high backgrounds and assessing PARP inhibitor/dna-damaging agent responses and TLS formation.

# 2. Why This Gene — Not Others

## Primary hypothesis gene(s)

**HRD_score:** Selected as a core mechanistic driver from Tier 1 knowledge graph analysis.

**MSH6:** MSH6 (MMR gene/MSI axis) provide a mechanistic backbone linking tumor-intrinsic DNA repair defects to neoantigen load and immune engagement; I built on these because HRD predicts DNA-damage sensitivity and MMR perturbations (MSH6) can alter mutational/neoantigen landscapes that modulate immune recognition.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** At the tissue level, tumors with the imaging feature but low anchor gene activity will show immune cells confined to structural peripheries rather than infiltrating tumor nests.

**Prediction 2:** In functional experiments, restoring or enhancing the anchor gene's signaling will improve immune-mediated tumor control in imaging-feature-positive models.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
