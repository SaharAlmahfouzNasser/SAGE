# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

Why do high intratumoral TIL densities sometimes fail to translate into improved survival in bladder cancer? The Ontologist's Tier 1 paths point to systemic metabolic state (HDL-C) and tumor genomic instability (HRD score) as plausible modulators of immune efficacy, suggesting the puzzle is about what conditions permit TILs to be functionally anti-tumor. 2) Tier 1 paths used: (a) HDL-C — chosen because lipid metabolism and HDL biology can directly modulate T cell function and systemic inflammation, providing a parsimonious systemic modifier of immune competence; (b) HRD score — chosen because genomic instability is a strong determinant of neoantigen burden and thus of the antigenic stimulus TILs require. I built on these two because they form a clear mechanistic dyad (immune competence × antigenicity) that can explain variability in TIL prognostic value. 3) Tier 2 support: KRT17/MDK, MSI, EV mRNA, CTCs and methylation markers support alternative mechanisms for tumor aggressiveness, antigen shedding, and diagnostic accessibility; they strengthen the plausibility that tumor-intrinsic biology and liquid-biopsy signals could confound or refine the proposed TIL–HDL–HRD interaction. 4) Endpoint rationale: Overall survival (OS) was chosen because it is the most robust, clinically meaningful outcome available across cohorts and is appropriate to test whether the proposed biological interaction has durable patient-level impact. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded the CSF/AD biomarker axis from the core hypothesis despite being Tier 1, because its cross-domain link to bladder tumor immunity is indirect and would dilute mechanistic specificity; I also excluded other Tier-3 exploratory markers (pZVR, stool methylation) from the core claim to keep the hypothesis sharply testable?

## Working Hypothesis

The prognostic impact of high tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density on overall survival (OS) in bladder cancer is conditional on systemic lipid-mediated immune competence and tumor antigenicity. Specifically, high HDL-C enhances T cell metabolic fitness and effector function, while elevated HRD score increases neoantigen burden, enabling TILs to recognize and control tumor cells. Consequently, patients with high TIL density will exhibit the greatest OS benefit when both HDL-C is high and HRD score is elevated; in contrast, low HDL-C or low HRD attenuates or abolishes the TIL–OS association. This three-way interaction (TIL density × HDL-C × HRD) defines a biologically coherent subset with favorable survival and potential heightened responsiveness to immune therapies.

# 3. Clinically Testable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Retrospective multi-cohort analysis using BLCA datasets with quantified TIL density (pathology or digital pathology), baseline HDL-C, and HRD scores; model OS with Cox regression including main effects and the three-way interaction; adjust for confounders (age, stage, smoking, treatment).

**Prediction 2:** Stratified analyses: assess TIL–OS within HDL-C-high/low and HRD-high/low strata; test for interaction significance.

**Prediction 3:** Replication in independent cohorts with similar data.


# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
