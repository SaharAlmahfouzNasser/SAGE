# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist highlights that immune-contexture readouts (Immunoscore®, immunotherapy biomarkers) do not uniformly predict outcomes in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC); why do some TIL-rich tumors fail to respond to immunotherapy?

## Working Hypothesis

In muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), high systemic HDL-C reconfigures the tumor lipid microenvironment such that abundant tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (high Immunoscore) do not translate into effective anti-tumor immunity. This HDL-C–driven remodeling promotes immunosuppressive stromal and myeloid states (e.g., M2-like tumor-associated macrophages and PTGDS/prostaglandin signaling activity), leading to impaired CD8+ T cell effector function and reduced therapy_response. Consequently, the predictive value of Immunoscore alone is attenuated in the context of elevated HDL-C, revealing a HDL-C × Immunoscore interaction that stratifies responders from non-responders within high-TIL tumors. Secondary modifiers—SARIFA-driven tumor–adipocyte/stromal remodeling, PTGDS expression, and AI/radiomics features reflecting metabolic-immune crosstalk—will further refine prediction and provide noninvasive readouts of the underlying biology. Testable predictions include: (1) among Immunoscore-high tumors, higher HDL-C will associate with lower therapy_response rates to ICIs; (2) tumor tissues from HDL-C–high cases will show increased PTGDS expression, prostaglandin signaling activity, and M2-like macrophage markers with reduced CD8+ cytotoxic signatures; (3) in vitro and ex vivo models will demonstrate that HDL-containing media promote immunosuppressive phenotypes and blunt T-cell activation in the presence of bladder cancer–associated myeloid cells; (4) radiomic features tied to SARIFA/adipocyte remodeling will correlate with HDL-C levels and predict treatment outcome. This framework yields testable, clinically actionable insights into metabolic–immune crosstalk in MIBC.

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
