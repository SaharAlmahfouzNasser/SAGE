# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

From the Ontologist synthesis, TIL/immune signals and DNA-repair (MSH6) and stromal/metastasis drivers (GREM1) all bear on prognosis, yet they do not always align — i.e., some TIL-rich or MMR-deficient tumors still have poor outcomes. Why is immune infiltration sometimes ineffective? 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on MSH6 (core DNA mismatch-repair / neoantigen → immune attractor) and GREM1 (BMP-antagonist → stromal remodeling / metastasis) because together they provide a plausible mechanistic axis that can explain when immune presence fails. 3) Tier 2 support: EV cargo (MDK, KRT17) supports a tumor-secreted program that could reinforce metastatic/stromal phenotypes; methylation markers (SDC2/TFPI2) and metabolic/renal signals (GLU, creatinine, microalbuminuria) supply orthogonal, noninvasive readouts to stratify these states; PSA/pZVR represent additional cross-cancer/proteomic signals to test generalizability. 4) Endpoint choice: Overall survival (OS) — chosen because it is the most robust, ultimate clinical outcome available in the datasets and best captures the net effect of competing tumor–immune–stromal programs. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I excluded MDK and KRT17 from the core hypothesis to keep the mechanistic claim sharp (they are placed as secondary_modifiers as noninvasive EV-readouts). I also excluded exploratory/indirect signals (HDL-C, CSF-AD biomarkers) from the main claim because their links are too distal?

## Working Hypothesis

In bladder cancer, tumors that are MSH6-deficient and TIL-rich will exhibit variable overall survival depending on GREM1-driven stromal remodeling. Specifically, among MSH6-deficient, TIL-high tumors, those with high GREM1 expression/signature (indicating active BMP antagonism and stromal remodeling) will have poorer OS than those with low GREM1 expression. This implies a mechanistic uncoupling between immune cell presence and effective anti-tumor activity mediated by the stroma. The hypothesis predicts a significant interaction between MSH6 status, TIL density, and GREM1 expression in predicting OS, even after controlling for standard clinical covariates. Secondary, noninvasive readouts (EV cargo MDK, KRT17; methylation markers SDC2/TFPI2; metabolic/renal signals like GLU/creatinine/microalbuminuria; PSA/pZVR) will stratify this axis and enable monitoring of state transitions (immune-attractor state vs. immune-suppressive/stromal-dominated state). Mechanistic corollaries include: (1) GREM1-high TMEs will show reduced cytotoxic T cell activity markers and ECM/fibroblast activation signatures; (2) BMP signaling modulation will influence T cell function and tumor control in co-culture/in vivo models; (3) interventions targeting BMP/GREM1-driven stromal programs will enhance the efficacy of immune-directed therapies in GREM1-high, MSH6-deficient contexts.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
