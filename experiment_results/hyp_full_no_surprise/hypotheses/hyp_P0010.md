# 1. Clinical Question & Core Hypothesis

## Clinical Question

The Ontologist identified that lipid/metabolic programs (HDL-C), stromal–adipocyte phenotypes (SARIFA), and mismatch-repair status (MSH6) co-occur as candidate biomarkers in MIBC; the core puzzle is why MSH6-linked immunogenicity (which should favor immune checkpoint responses) fails in some tumors that instead show adverse outcomes. 2) Tier 1 paths used: I built on MSH6 (DNA MMR → immunogenicity) and HDL-C (tumor lipid metabolism → immune modulation) because they form a direct mechanistic tension: MSH6 deficiency predicts higher neoantigen load and ICI sensitivity, while tumor-associated lipid handling (HDL-C-related) can create a metabolically immunosuppressive niche. I selected the imaging surrogate 'stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR)' to capture the SARIFA/stroma–adipocyte context morphologically. 3) Tier 2 evidence supporting the claim: MSI status and AI-based biomarker paths support the link between MMR defects and ICI response; PTGDS and TGF-β paths support paracrine inflammatory/lipid signaling and stromal fibrosis that can reinforce metabolic immune evasion; lymph node metastasis provides a clinically relevant readout of aggressive biology. 4) Why therapy_response was chosen as the single endpoint: MSH6/MSI biology has the strongest direct mechanistic and clinical link to immune checkpoint responsiveness, and the key translational question is when predicted ICI sensitivity is negated by microenvironmental metabolic/stromal features. 5) Deliberate exclusions: I limited anchor variables to MSH6 and HDL-C to keep the hypothesis sharp; SARIFA (though core) and other supporting nodes (PTGDS, AI signatures, TGF-β, nodal status) were relegated to secondary_modifiers to preserve mechanistic focus and testability. I excluded broader prognostic endpoints (OS/PFI) to avoid conflating response mechanisms with downstream survival effects?

## Working Hypothesis

Primary hypothesis: In tumors with high stromal-to-tumor ratio (TSR), HDL-C–driven adipocyte-like stromal metabolism creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment that blunts the enhanced immunogenicity conferred by MSH6 deficiency, resulting in reduced objective therapy_response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade compared with MSH6-deficient tumors that have low TSR or low HDL-C pathway activity. The effect of MSH6 deficiency on ICI response is therefore moderated by TSR/HDL-C status, such that: (1) MSH6-deficient, low-TSR/low HDL-C tumors exhibit the strongest ICI responses; (2) MSH6-deficient, high-TSR/high HDL-C tumors show attenuated responses; (3) adding stromal/metabolic modulation could restore responsiveness in the high-TSR/MSH6-deficient subset. This interaction should be observable as a significant MSH6 × TSR/HDL-C interaction for therapy_response (ORR) and related clinical endpoints, after adjusting for MSI/TMB and other confounders. Secondary modifiers (SARIFA, MSI status, PTGDS, lymph node metastasis, AI signatures, TGF-β) may influence the magnitude of the interaction and can be explored to refine the predictive signature.

# 4. Minimum Viable Validation Plan

## A. Retrospective Cohort Analysis

**Data source:** TCGA-BLCA (Firehose Legacy), ~400 patients
- Cox PH model with interaction term for the imaging feature and anchor gene
- Kaplan-Meier 2x2 stratification

## B. Tissue-Level Validation

**Methods:** Multiplex IF / IHC / spatial transcriptomics

**Key readouts:**
- Immune structure markers (e.g., CD20, CD3, PNAd)
- Effector cell localization (CD8) relative to tumor nests
- Anchor gene protein / phospho-protein expression
- Stromal features (collagen density by SHG, if applicable)

**Goal:** Confirm that the proposed mechanism operates at the spatial / tissue level.

## C. Functional Validation

**Models:** Ex vivo organotypic cultures, 3D co-culture, syngeneic mouse models

**Perturbations:** Genetic (KO/rescue) or pharmacologic modulation of anchor gene

**Readouts:** T-cell infiltration, cytotoxicity (granzyme B / IFN-gamma), tumor growth / survival

**Goal:** Establish causal relationship between anchor gene activity and immune efficacy.
