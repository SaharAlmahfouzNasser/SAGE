Genomic biomarkers
- Nectin-4 expression: Enfortumab vedotin targets nectin-4, so tumor nectin-4 expression is a biologically relevant factor for potential activity.
- Tumor mutational burden (TMB): Higher TMB has been associated with better responses to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in urothelial cancer.
- APOBEC mutational signature: Common in bladder cancer and linked to higher neoantigen load, potentially correlating with immunotherapy responsiveness.
- DNA damage response (DDR) gene alterations: Mutations in DDR pathways (e.g., BRCA1/2, ATM, CHEK2, FANCD2) may influence sensitivity to DNA-damaging therapy and immunotherapy, though the predictive value for this exact combo is not yet established.

Histopathological biomarkers
- PD-L1 expression: PD-L1 status on tumor and/or immune cells is frequently assessed as a potential predictor of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor benefit, though predictive value is variable.
- Tumor immune microenvironment: Dense CD8+ T cell infiltration, presence of an “inflamed” gene expression profile, and higher immune gene signatures often correlate with better immunotherapy responses.
- Molecular subtype and histology: Basal/squamous-like or other inflamed subtypes, and histologic features with immune infiltration, may be associated with differential responses.
- Histologic variants and immune context: Certain variants (e.g., sarcomatoid components) and features like lymphovascular invasion can influence response and toxicity in some settings.

Metabolic biomarkers
- Baseline 18F-FDG PET uptake (SUVmax) and metabolic response: Higher baseline metabolic activity and changes during therapy have been explored as surrogates of tumor biology and response, but no standard thresholds exist for eligibility.

Combined and dynamic biomarkers
- Immune transcriptomic signatures: T-cell–inflamed or interferon-γ–related gene expression profiles can enrich for patients more likely to respond to immunotherapy.
- Integrated scores: Composite biomarkers that combine nectin-4 status, PD-L1, TMB, and immune gene signatures may better predict activity than any single marker.
- Blood-based markers: Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) dynamics (baseline levels and early clearance) can serve as an early surrogate of treatment effect; peripheral immune cell profiling (e.g., CD8+ T cells, exhaustion markers) is also studied.

- Consider RNA-based immune gene expression profiling to gauge an inflamed tumor microenvironment.
- Evaluate metabolic activity with 18F-FDG PET and monitor ctDNA for baseline levels and early on-treatment changes.

Notes and caveats
- No validated, universally accepted biomarker panel specifically for neoadjuvant EV + pembrolizumab in MIBC exists yet. Most actionable biomarkers in this space come from broader urothelial cancer immunotherapy and nectin-4–targeted therapy data.
