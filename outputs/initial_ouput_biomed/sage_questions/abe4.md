Short answer: There isn’t strong, universal evidence to routinely de-escalate or omit adjuvant therapy in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) patients solely because they achieved a pathologic complete response (pCR) after neoadjuvant therapy. Decisions should be individualized, and many clinicians still consider adjuvant therapy in high-risk settings or within clinical trials.

What the current evidence suggests
- Standard of care: For fit patients with MIBC, neoadjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy followed by radical cystectomy is the mainstay. After surgery, decisions about additional (adjuvant) therapy depend on post-surgical risk features.
- pCR after neoadjuvant therapy: Achieving ypT0N0 (pCR) is associated with excellent outcomes, but relapse risk is not zero. Whether this alone warrants omitting adjuvant therapy is not established.
- Adjuvant immunotherapy: Trials like CheckMate 274 showed disease-free survival benefit with adjuvant nivolumab after cystectomy in high-risk urothelial carcinoma, regardless of neoadjuvant therapy status. This supports use of adjuvant immunotherapy in selected high-risk post-surgery patients.
- Adjuvant immunotherapy in general populations: The IMvigor010 study (atezolizumab) did not demonstrate a clear benefit in an unselected post-cystectomy population, so adjuvant immunotherapy is not universally adopted for all patients after cystectomy.
- De-escalation in pCR specifically: There are limited, retrospective data and no robust prospective evidence that omitting adjuvant therapy based only on achieving pCR after neoadjuvant therapy is safe or equivalent in terms of long-term outcomes. This is an area of active investigation and often discussed in trial contexts.

Clinical implications and practical guidance
- Individualize decisions: If you’ve achieved pCR, your absolute risk of recurrence is lower, but not zero. The decision to give or withhold adjuvant therapy should consider:
  - Postoperative pathologic features beyond pCR (e.g., lymphovascular invasion, nodal status, margin status)

- Monitoring vs. therapy: For some very low-risk patients or in specific trial settings, careful surveillance without adjuvant therapy may be discussed, but this is not the standard of care outside trials.
